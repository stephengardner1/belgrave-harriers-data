Tributes to John Jeffery, 1944-2004

2004-12-08

John's was the first friendly face I saw at the end of the London Marathon and I felt honoured to have his support. Even last week at the relays, he shouted at me to catch the group of runners in front, which motivated me even more to try hard. I'm sure he will be greatly missed by all at Belgrave.

Louise Cooper

Very sad and shocked to hear the news about John Jeffery. He would always greet me with "Hello Noisy". I will always remember the times we would run around the course supporting the teams together. He will be sadly missed.

Iwill always remember John at the time when I first joined BAF and was present at the World Cup at Crystal Palace. John was supportive and just a nice guy.

David Anderson

Roger Alsop

Having just returned from our holiday we were looking forward to seeing John to tell him all about it as we always exchanged information about places we had both visited. What a shock for us to read our e-mail about the loss of John, not only a friend but also a wonderful supporter of Belgrave Harriers. Our thoughts are with Maureen and his family at this sad time.

Reg Hopkins and Pat

Iwas shocked to hear of John's death. He was well liked and respected by all who knew him. He was an understanding and sympathetic listener who commanded the respect of the many athletes he dealt with. His time as England Assistant Team Manager, Administrator of the SCAAA and his part in organising the Crystal Palace major meetings meant that he knew more people outside Belgrave than in - and that's saying something!

He will be sorely missed by the Club and replacing him will be no easy matter. But now is the time to give praise to his huge contribution towards our premier place in British athletics. Our teams owed much to his presence.

John and Maureen stayed with us here in Sydney a couple of years ago and we look back on that visit with fond memories. Our deepest sympathies go to Maureen who suffers the biggest loss of all.

Clive Shippen

Iam shocked and deeply saddened to learn of the death of John Jeffery. To me, John represented everything that is great about Belgrave: determination, dedication, energy, and, above all else, fun. What a loss to us all! My sincerest condolences to his family.

Justin Chaston

Iwould like to send condolences to John's family and to all at Belgrave for this sad loss.

Igot to know John well when I took over as Team Manager for Newham and Essex Beagles. I remember him congratulating me at end of my first match when we finished second (to Belgrave of course) and that was typical of his warmth and friendliness. I've tried hard over the years to get teams together that could beat Belgrave, but have generally failed due to the excellent management structure that you have. John was an integral part of
that. He knew the sport, he understood athletes, and he cared.

Ishall miss teasing him (and him teasing me) and our games of 'cat and mouse' about the respective strengths of our teams. It's all so sudden and such a shock. I wish I'd spoken to him at Sutton Park.

Condolences from all (including Tony and Mick) at Newham and Essex Beagles.

Bob Smith, Newham & Essex Beagles

Such shocking news. I’m still trying to come to terms with it - like a huge number of others I am sure, especially his family of course. As if being joint manager of the British League team wasn’t enough, he was always willing to help officiate at other Track & Field fixtures where Belgrave teams were taking part. He will be sorely missed.

Rex Bale

Earlier this year my own club experienced a similar great loss when one of our own passed away suddenly and unexpectedly .... Andy Paul

Iwas very saddened to hear of the passing of John. He was certainly a supporter of athletics from the grass roots up to the international stage and he was always a regular spectator at the Lauriston Paarlauf on Christmas Day no matter what the weather.

In my days of sometimes covering the British League for AW he was always helpful and often came up with an interesting 'nugget' about his Belgrave charges.

Until reading Alan's fine obituary I didn't realise the extent of his performances which even these days would have stood him in good stead. A 49s 400m and a 49min 10-miles must be quite unique.

Pete Mulholland

Iwas so shocked to hear about John. He was always very friendly and always made time to talk to me and find out what I have been up to. He will be deeply missed.

Sincere condolences to you all.

Matthew Fraser Moat

John was the epitome of Athletics and the finest of advertisements for the sport, and it's the more sad since he should have had far more time on this earth to continue to enjoy everything about athletics.

For myself, since I knew him quite well, and on behalf of Kingston & Poly members everywhere, I extend to Bels and all its members, and to his family, our heartfelt wishes at this saddest of times.

David Barrington, Kingston Athletic Club & Polytechnic Harriers

Ifirst came across John when I was running as a 14 or 15 year old in open races around London. His encouraging words meant a lot to a nervous boy prior to these races and he soon became my friend. As I got older and improved I was lucky to travel on teams abroad where John was the team manager. His knowledge of the sport was invaluable and his 'appropriate' comments just prior to one’s race meant a lot. We ran together on several occasions during these trips. He was good company.

John, you will be greatly missed by me and all who knew you. Sleep peacefully

Billy Dee
