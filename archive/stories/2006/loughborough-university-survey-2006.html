Loughborough University Survey

2006-04-20

Study conducted by The School of Sport and Exercise Sciences at Loughborough University


The School of Sport and Exercise Sciences at Loughborough University is conducting a study into the effects of athletes' (romantic and marital) personal relationships on sport performance. Anecdotal evidence reveals the recognition placed by coaches (e.g., Sven Goran Eriksson) on the positive impact athletes' partners have on sport performance. Also limited empirical evidence suggests that close friends can help reduce stress and lower injury frequency.

In an attempt to answer "Is an athlete's relationship with their partner an added stressor or can it actually help the athlete perform better?", data will be collected from both the athlete and their partner at two different time points during the athlete's season (off-season and mid-season). This will allow us to explore the manner in which the added pressures of sport affect the ebb and flow of the relationship and in turn the athlete's competitive performance.
The data will be collected employing a method (email, telephone, mail or internet) that best suits yourself and your partner. All information provided for the purpose of this investigation will be COMPLETELY CONFIDENTIAL and not accessible to third parties.

The knowledge generated from this investigation aims to,

(a) inform athletes (and their coaches) about the impact personal relationships have on performance,
(b) suggest ways to form harmonious and stable personal relationships that are likely to help athletes achieve their best performances, and
(c) advise on how to deal effectively and successfully with relationship conflict, tension and disagreements.
