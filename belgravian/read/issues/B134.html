EDITORIAL

Dear Fellow Members, By the time this edition is published, the track and road season will be with us and it is to be hoped that the Winter has not been spent in idleness by those wishing to do well in these spheres. Our field events men are already hard at it as witness our successful open meeting at Battersea Park on March 2nd. This event does great credit to its organisers and it is a valuable contribution, giving performers competition during the close season.

Our old friend, P.C. Fred Stone, was seen by many on a television programme recently when receiving an award in recognition of his bravery during police service. Harry Evans, as sprightly as ever, also appeared on the programme to recount some tales of their younger days.

We needn't be too disappointed with the cross country season, for like so many other clubs we were victims of a flu-ridden Winter. It cannot be too strongly emphasized, however, that a season over the country demands both sufficient groundwork and sensible racing. So many seem to forget that the real tests come after Christmas; to feel race-weary when the new year begins is fatal. Likewise it is equally fatal to expect to commence one's season in January with the Southern and National in mind. Under such circumstances it is not possible to produce top performances in both events.

Our walkers continue winning in merciless fashion and we must not lose sight of that great driving force behind them, Jack Goswell. He must be the Stampfl of the walking world in his ability to bring out the best in his men. To Eric Hall and Stan Vickers, who have never walked better we offer our congratulations. It may sound fantastic but Eric, now one of our "greats" won a 2 mile walk handicap in 1950, off 650 yards! So go to it, you novices.

Fred StoneHarry EvansJack GoswellStampflEric HallStan Vickers

MATCH v. MITCHAM, MET. POLICE AND GUILDFORD & GODALMING A.C. at BANSTEAD. DEC.28TH

Out of our terrific numerical strength of cross country runners we could only muster thirteen, including three juniors, for this away fixture. The venue is set in the midst of Surrey's grand scenery and provides a complete change to the normal terrain over which we run. I have little doubt that all who took part had an enjoyable afternoon. John Davies, who has been a regular participant in cross country fixtures this year, led home the Belgrave contingent in 8th place, closely followed by a pack of Belgravians in 10th, 11th, 12th, 14th, 15th, 17th and 18th positions. This good bunching enabled us to secure a narrow win in the team race, with Mitcham second, Met. Police third and Guildford fourth. Ken Jones was performing for the Police and ran well to finish fourth in the individual placings. Bob Glover, a strong runner, deserves special mention - he is continually improving and without doubt will greatly assist our teams before many seasons are over - stick to it Bob!

Report · page 4

SURREY COUNTY C.C. CHAMPIONSHIP AT REIGATE. JAN.4TH

This race was held at the same venue as the South of the Thames Junior in November and the course consisted of a mixture of grassland, firm paths and hills, some of which were very steep.It could well be described as a good sporty course.The finishing time indicated a distance well in excess of the scheduled seven miles which produced some grumblings from the competitors, especially those whose training had previously been of a sketchy nature. It would seem that we are doomed to take team positions outside the first three in this race for some years to come unless we can split the strength of South London and Herne Hill and the recent rise to power of Walton A.C. Indeed, we have not won this event since 1952 nor been placed since 1955. Only Baulch and Gebbett ran true to form and finished in positions which deserve any special mention. Baulch especially was in fine fettle to finish 11th behind such names as Firth, Caulder, Barrett, Towl, Maynard, Clark and Driver, all of whom are in international class in one sphere or another. Dave was indeed unlucky to miss his selection for the County team but should fare better next year. Eric Gebbett eventually finished 20th after having been up to 15th at one stage - it seems strange to have to report that the extra distance was his downfall.Charlie Walker in 33rd place was very disappointing - perhaps he should not forecast his running before the race - it always seems to be fatal. Len Bishop and Eddie Short should have been at least ten places higher than their 42nd and 43rd, and Peter Hitchcock should also have been better positioned than 52nd. It is obvious that we have the talent but it is not put to the best use when major races are run. Instead of rising to the occasion we fall further and further from grace. Do we do enough training to keep up with modern conditions?

MATCH v. ORPINGTON A.C., ISLE OF SHEPPEY AND OLD CROYDONIANS AT ORPINGTON JAN.11TH

The Cross Country Secretary sent out numerous cards directing runners to this fixture but the nett result was a turnout of four which was extremely disappointing and an insult to our hosts. The race took place over good grassland over a distance of five miles and Robin Weate and J.Dooley ran together to finish second and third. Gerry Lines made one of his rare appearances and showed welcome form to finish 9th. Musselbrook rose to the occasion in answering the card sent to him by appearing in person to complete our team in 17th position. Orpington took the team honours with 22 pts., Bels..were second with 31, Sheppey scored 44 and Old Croydonians amassed 75. On the whole an enjoyable afternoon out for the few stalwarts who supported this fixture. It should of course be mentioned that we had another away race against Westminster Bank on this day which split our forces but nevertheless there were many of our members who stayed away from both.

WALKING NOTES

We entered a team in the Essex Beagles open 3¾ miles youths and juniors race. Nic Milsum walked very well to win the race in 28.56 and Bryan Cornish was 8th in 31.18. Unfortunately we lost both Terry Clark and John Knifton who came under the judge's ban when in 2nd and 4th positions thus ruining any chances of winning the team race. The same day (Jan.4th) E.A.Smith finished third in the London Business Houses 7 mile championship in 55.52. We also had a match versus Met.Police at "The Warren" Hayes and in a field of eighteen Stan Vickers was first home in 56.05. R.D.McMullen was second and R.C.Hall third. Scoring eight a side we won with 43½ pts. to 93½.

On Jan 11th at Imber Court the Metropolitan Police held their open 7 mile walk and in a field of 195 Stan Vickers led for the whole way to win in the record time of 50.14 which was 78 secs. faster than the previous best. With Eric Hall 4th, R.D.McMullen 16th and George Checkley 19th we easily won the team race from Met.W.C. and Highgate. It was very pleasing to see the large number of Club supporters stationed around the course - their presence was well rewarded.

CLUB 10 MILES C.C. CHAMPIONSHIP (MACHER SHIELD) JAN.25TH

This year's race,held on a cold damp day, ran true to form with Charlie Walker, who always performs so well over our own courses, the winner by the comfortable margin of 47 seconds. Dave Baulch took second place, 53 seconds ahead of Eric Gebbett who had earlier in the season won the seven miles championship. This was the order which had been maintained almost throughout the race and the fact that, for the second year, the course was inadvertently shortened by missing out the lap of the starting field in the middle of the race, obviously made no difference to the result. The back markers in the handicap could well complain, for if the full distance had been covered there is little doubt that Walker and Moore would have been among the prizes. John Moore (the second), in his first season with us, has made his mark upon us and will soon be taking a leading place in our races and should bolster up our teams both on track and country. His fourth place in this event was well earned, for he has been a keen performer since he joined us. Archie Bedford returned for one of his rare visits and, by finishing fifth, proved he still has the capacity to gain a place in our first teams. Eddie Short ran well to take sixth place and that "little potterer" Joe Dooley followed close on his heels with Jim Heathfield, only a few yards behind. Bob Plummer, better known for his exploits over the marathon distance, took advantage of the handicapper's generosity to win the handicap by a good margin. Another long marker, Reg Bolem, took second place and John Hilton, a newcomer, made good use of his 9½ mins. start to secure third place.

SOUTH OF THAMES SENIOR C.C. CHAMPIONSHIP FEB.1ST

This race was held from Belgrave Hall in honour of Les Cohen who this year is President of the South of the Thames Cross Country Association. He must have been proud of the efficient manner in which this event was organised and our thanks go out to Arthur Whitehead who took all the responsibility for course marking and the provision of starting and finishing pens upon his experienced shoulders. The number of barring clauses in this event precluded the fielding of our strongest team, but on paper our lads, with a mixture of experience and youthful exuberance, appeared to have a chance of a place. Unfortunately we were again to be disappointed as we could finish no better than 9th in the team race with the mammoth score of 461 pts. George Flanagan was the only one of our runners who excelled himself and he should be pleased with thirteenth position. John Moore, by finishing 52nd, gained good experience in a large field and Robin Weate, in his first year with us, showed that he is possessed of more than his fair share of pluck to finish 88th. Robin, still a junior, always gives of his best and is a great asset to us - with regular and correct training he should follow in the footsteps of that courageous little man, Bert Footer, who did so much for Belgrave in the years preceding the last war. Bob Glover, also in his first season, improves with every outing and in finishing 111th can be said to have performed well and should benefit from this experience. Ken Jones, running in the colours of the Metropolitan Police A.C. ran very well to take 33rd place and will be a tower of strength in these races when we have his services.

SOUTHERN COUNTIES SENIOR C.C. CHAMPIONSHIP at Parliament Hill Feb.15th

A return to the course over which the National was held last year saw one of the biggest fields ever set off on the long trek of nine miles over hilly grassland with a fair proportion of mud and slush. Toward the end of the first of the three laps Eric Gebbett was our first scorer way back in 49th place followed closely by Charlie Walker 55th. Then followed a string of runners before we again saw a Belgrave vest filled by Eddie Short who had John Moore and Jumbo Masters close on his heels, all of them in the 130's. Pete Hitchcock, Clive Shippen and Len Bishop were next, in the 170's while the rest of our men were back in the 200's. The next view of the field was soon after the first lap had ended, and Walker had moved up to Gebbett and Short and Moore had also progressed about fifteen places and were running well. Masters was making very heavy weather of it and was slipping back steadily, Hitchcock and Shippen were 152nd and 167th respectively with Len Bishop and D.Baulch in close attendance. At the conclusion of the second lap Walker had left Gebbett and was now 44th but was having great difficulty in making any further progress. Although Gebbett was slipping back gradually, Short and Moore were still making up ground and were now 103rd and 104th. Our other men however, were making little or no improvement with the exception of J.Dooley who had started very slowly and was now beginning his late excursion through the field. On the last lap Moore made up another ten places and Hitchcock put in a good run to move up nearly thirty places. Dooley seemed to enjoy himself at this stage and must have passed about fifty runners in the last three miles. The others seemed to have got themselves well bogged down. The final count gave us ninth place with the astronomical score of 591 points, which must have been our lowest place and highest score for a good many years. The impression one gets from this fiasco is that most if not all of our runners are short of the type of training needed to keep pace with the leading performers. On examining the final positions one cannot fail to notice how many athletes, possessed of far less ability than our lads, finished well up the field and assisted their clubs to secure good places - the moral is obvious.

In the youths race Belgrave were 10th.The only other news we have is that C.Thompson was 26th and J.Thresher 48th.

COUNTY 10 MILES WALKING CHAMPIONSHIPS - FEB.22ND.

The splendid performances of our walkers in county championships gives every promise of Belgrave repeating last year's record of winning all three major R.W.A. titles. Again those two great ambassadors of the sport, Eric Hall and Stan Vickers, retained their titles in a convincing manner. A feat probably never equalled by any other club was that of ten Belgrave walkers gaining their county colours.

Surrey at Walton-on-Thames. A splendid race was won in immaculate style by Eric Hall who was just over one minute ahead of Dave McMullen.Then followed three other Belgravians which meant that the team race was won with the minimum score of ten points. It is interesting to note that our"B" team finished in third position which gives a startling indication of Club strength.

1st Belgrave "A" 10 pts. 2nd Surrey W.C."A" 39 pts. 3rd Belgrave"B"

Middlesex at Enfield A full team plus two individuals, as Cadogan A.C., represented the Club, and right well the performed to finish second team to Metropolitan Walking Club, beating Highgate, Polytechnic and many other fancied clubs in the process. A particularly fine effort was that of Ted Smith in finishing fourth and George Checkley sixth.

Kent at Hayes This might be described as Stan Vickers benefit, which he won for the third year in succession (not running!), walking completely on his own, with the ease and style which have become the standards whereby all others are judged. His time was 80.29.

A. Hiding.

Harry Shields made his mark by leading others astray on the pretext of having tea - result - we had four minutes to swim 200 yards, sprint half a mile and jump six feet. It was a good job we were fit or we'd have missed that train.

CROSS COUNTRY SECRETARY'S NOTES

We have reached the end of another cross country season and I regret that I can raise little or no enthusiasm for our performances. When the results of the major championships are considered it has been our worst year since the war and probably our worst since the early 1930's. Take a look at the following. South of Thames Junior 7th Surrey County 4th South of Thames Senior 9th Southern Counties 9th National 15th I realise that many clubs would be only too pleased to have such a record but it should be remembered that we have one of the largest memberships in the country and should therefore produce a higher standard in our teams. I am convinced that the talent is with us but the spirit to succeed is missing so far as cross country races are concerned. The whole atmosphere changes, however, when the road relay races start. Why can't we carry the enthusiasm shown on the Brighton road into the harder grind of the mud baths. It was a great joy to see us go from 18th to 2nd in the Southern Brighton relay last October; this proves that we have first class performers. This type of performance, however, is not repeated in the large fields of the major cross country races against the very same opposition. Why is this??? I feel that we are neglecting the well balanced training that is necessary to attain the fitness required for good cross country running. Two or three outings each week is not enough - daily training is essential, with a basic amount of really hard work combined with as much speed work as possible. We have all seen the Piries and Drivers at work at Tooting and know that such methods succeed; their followers too, benefiting similarly, even though missing the same limelight, but nevertheless becoming fit enough to bring their clubs into good positions in open races. These lads are no different to any of our members; they are all flesh and blood with the thrill of athletics in their bodies.Perhaps we fail because the concerted training runs in large packs at least three times a week no longer take place. Individual training on Saturday mornings is not necessary - we must all get together and use our efforts for the benefit of our fellow members and the Club as a whole. I am firmly of the opinion that it is quite wrong for an athlete to train continuously for months on end without an occasional race to test his ability. Our Club matches, for the past few years, have been very poorly supported, especially those away from Wimbledon. In our heyday everyone turned out, and it was not necessary for the Secretary to have to remind members to turn up, the fixture card alone was sufficient to guarantee at least thirty runners at every match. These races are planned to fit in with the advance of fitness throughout the winter season and from my own experience they can be very helpful in assessing ones progress. So many of our members fight shy of races on the score that their performances in the championships will suffer but just look at these performances and you can see that this contention is far from justified.I hope we shall all give our training considerable thought in the Summer months with a view to a considerable improvement next Winter. Now I feel that I must attack those members who habitually ignore all the communications I send out. Do they realise the hours spent in writing cards and letters for their benefit and also the cost to the Club for stationery and postage? I have been disgusted with the ignorance shown by many members in taking no notice whatsoever of my cards, especially those regarding the Ilford and Bexley road relays. In the former event we failed to finish a "B" team through the absence of a number of members who failed to give me sufficient notification of their inability to attend. At Bexley we finished only one complete team out of three just because the following failed to write or phone me despite sufficient warning :- R.Linstead, S.Charlton, S.Boreham, G.Lines, D.Manning and R.Braine. In consequence those members who ran in incomplete teams turned out with no object but to finish the course, and spent money on fares unnecessarily. In addition the Club spent 10/- per team on entrance fees. I can only hope that the people concerned have sufficient conscience to avoid a recurrence. Out of this season come a few bright prospects. In John Moore, Ken Jones and Robin Weate we have a acquired runners of high quality and Club Spirit and the improvement of George Flanagan, Peter Hitchcock, Clive Shippen and Bob Glover promises well for the future. It is around these boys that our future teams will be built, plus the experience of those diehards who go on for ever. The foregoing remarks may seem rather direct and caustic but they are intended for the good of the Club and I hope you will all take them in the right spirit. It is my ambition that Belgrave should again rise to the top of the cross country world and I want everyone to analyse their approach to Club matters and resolve to do everything in their power to bring the "Claret and Gold" to the fore for many years to come.

R.W.A. 10 MILES CHAMPIONSHIP MARCH 15TH

A.A.H.

We were strongly represented in this race, held at Victoria Park, Hackney on March 15th, fielding three teams - a goodly proportion of the 227 starters. Moreover most of our men reported fit and well, although we should have liked to have seen Bob Newson, Syd James and Roy Switzer in the field. The course consisted of five laps of approximately two miles each on level roads within the park and as expected the pace was fast and furious, and many had cause to regret their earlier speed work as they neared the end of the race. Our Stan Vickers, the Championship holder, started at a tremendous pace and at one mile had a lead of about thirty yards over a small group consisting of Eric Hall, Matthews (Sutton Coldfield) and Norman Read(Steyning), the Olympic 50 kms. Champion. At three miles Stan's lead had been reduced by Eric and Matthews, whilst Read had fallen back slightly and already there were some huge intervals between walkers. Gradually Hall and Matthews overhauled Vickers and this trio were together for quite a while, each having his nose in front momentarily. As the last lap was approached Matthews showed signs of his earlier efforts and faded out somewhat, eventually to finish sixth. Our Stan and Eric continued together, lapping numerous competitors in their progress. This passing of other walkers who had been lapped nearly proved Eric's undoing for as our two passed a group of three walkers, one on each side, one of these walkers stepped to one side (presumably to allow the faster men through) but only succeeded in knocking Eric off balance and out of rhythm - and this with less than half a mile to go. But for this the race would have been even closer for the individual title. However, we were delighted to have the first two home, repeating last year's effort. Well done, Stan and Eric! Meanwhile, what of our other team men? Dave McMullen walked a very even race indeed and was always in the first twenty. His 17th position at the finish was one of the soundest pieces of team work he has given us, and they have been many. He was in our winning team in 1947 and it says much for his efforts that he can again score all these years later. We hope to see him emulate some of his Father's performances over 20 miles in the near future. Ted Smith and George Checkley found the start a very fast one indeed and early on they seemed well back, but towards the finish they were gaining many places and claiming their rightful positions. Ray Hall did well on a course that was never to his liking and we look forward to a first class "20" from him soon. The nature of the course and the speed at which the race was walked, combined with very cold conditions towards the end, gave rise to many disqualifications. We lost Nick Milsum and Terry Clark of our first team men, but their turn will come. Nick was particularly unfortunate for he was our fourth man, on the last lap and, in the view of many, walking splendidly as he came through the field. Of our others Ray Middleton, D.J.Stevens and J.Keown show promise for the future and allin all we were well satisfied that all our walkers of A, B and C teams carried the Club colours with great credit on this difficult day. Naturally we were proud indeed to retain our hold on the London Shield and to have the first two men home. They were also first and second in the Inter Counties Race, and Eric Hall, Dave McMullen and Ray Hall were in the Surrey team which was placed third. Result :-

"THIS IS YOUR LIFE"

A.A.H.

On March 3rd, a popular Belgravian Vice-President, Frederick Mark Stone ("Rodney"), found himself the centre figure of the well established B.B.C. television programme. Rather unwillingly he was persuaded to leave the audience for the platform by Eamonn Andrews, when Paul Bennett V.C., Mrs. Stone, Fred's two sons and many former colleagues and friends from all stations of life (including our own Past President, Harry Evans) outlined those assets of character, courage and sportsmanship which have endeared him to such a wide circle. The programme culminated in the presentation of a testimonial cheque by the Commissioner of the Metropolitan Police, Sir John Nott-Bower on behalf of the members of that great body to "our Rodney", who has been well described as the "bravest Metropolitan Policeman ever". Mention was made during the programme of his winning of the Kings' Police Medal for Bravery in jumping from Westminster Bridge to save life, of the award of the Royal Humane Society Medal for life saving, of supreme courage during an air raid which brought the award of the British Empire Medal, and of his success in preventing a smash and grab raid (when on sick leave and in plain clothes) which resulted in Fred being beaten over the head with an iron bar - but his man did not get away! His courage and devotion to duty gained recognition when he received the George Medal from the hands of Her Majesty the Queen - but his severe injuries unfortunately resulted in his discharge from the Police. Fred was in many of our long distance walking teams in the early 1920's and was a great worker for the Club always, and it was pleasing to see such a combination of many virtues (and many could only be lightly touched upon) on television, exemplifying a life - with many more years yet to run, we trust - which could well serve as a model for the younger man of service, courage and personality. A.A.H.

PORTRAIT GALLERY

What stranger than to have represented Great Britain in the Olympic Games, gained international vests for track running and yet be denied a single Club Championship during a distinguished career lasting almost twenty years? Such is the case of Bill Lucas our present cross country secretary. Unlike some stars, who shot into their orbit from the blue, but who faded into obscurity with equal rapidity, Bill progressed steadily in the post war years, representing the Club on every possible occasion and proved that good track running is not adversely affected by cross country racing. 1946 saw Bill win the South of the Thames Junior C.C. race at Cheam and the following January he gained his County vest by finishing fourth in the Surrey C.C. Championship. He also finished well up in the South of the Thames Senior race and in the same year finished second in the Surrey 3 miles track race. Although not listed amongst the probables for the 1948 Olympic Games, Bill sprang a surprise by gaining third place in the A.A.A. 3 miles and earned selection for the 5000 metres in the Games, where he ran well, probably the best of our representatives. Two international races were to come for Bill, when in 1950 he finished second in the 5000 metres in a triangular match at the White City, and at the same venue, versus U.S.A. and Benelux Countries, was third in an event of the same distance won by Ashenfelter of U.S.A. For his great three mile time of 14m.11.6s. in the A.A.A. Championships of the same year Bill received the coveted Tommy Green Trophy for the best performance of the year by a Belgravian. Bill's record in the London to Brighton relay is second to none; he was consistently in the team from 1947 to 1954. Possibly his best performance was in giving Max Jones of Birchfield 31 secs. start on the last stage in 1948 and finishing twenty yards in front of him to give us a great win. On four occasions Bill had the honour of handing the baton to the Mayor of Brighton after finishing first on the last stage of this classic relay race. Although he took a long time to prove he meant it, Bill retired in 1955, only to increase his Club activities even moreso in official capacities. Rather outspoken, but that is what is needed in our Club, Bill has earned the respect of all Belgravians for good work. Perhaps the greatest tribute one can pay him is that he moves with the times and recognises today's runners for what they are worth, one never hears him make unfavourable comparisons between present and past and few know of his distinguished war career when he was decorated for service with the R.A.F.
