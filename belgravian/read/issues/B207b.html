Editorial · page 3

NATIONAL CHAMPIONS

With greater interest being shown by Belgravians in indoor competition we waited for the results of the 1984 AAA's Indoor Championships with more than usual interest - and were not disappointed. As a result of wins by Gary Staines in the 3000m and Ernest Obeng in the 60m there was a brief moment when Belgrave members held a total of six National Titles. What with Stuart Paton's 83 1500m indoor win, John Gladwins 1500m UK Champs title and Paul Richley's 83 Junior 3000m win our increasing strength in middle distance events was well underlined. Add to this Carl Lawton's 83 35km National walk title and you have some measure of our current strengths.

It is perhaps disappointing that these performances have not been widely publicised in the main Athletics Journals. It clearly takes time for our up and coming members to gain the general recognition they deserve. One sure fire way of attracting attention seems to be by winning team competitions whether on the road, country or track. Well we started 1984 in grand style when a team of twelve sprint and middle distance men travelled to the Wellington International Relays in Ostend and came away as only the second winning British team in the 28 years of the event.

Our main target for this summer must be to gain promotion to Division 3 of the British League preferably as the clear winners of Division 4. Our pole vault and jumps teams rate second to none and the throws have been considerably strengthened by the formidable Ken Onuaguluchi who has wintered well under Mike Winch's tuition. Where we have gaps; high jump, hurdles, and back-up in the sprints and throws, we have a crop of most promising youngsters who we hope will be eager to experience the excitement of representing our Club in British League competition.

The new Belgrave Hall Gymnasium will be opened in mid-July and with Mike Winch and Judy Oakes at the helm will provide exceptional facilities for building up our sprint and field men. We might have the occasional problems but these are to be expected. The progress is still inexorably upward!!!

grapevine

There can't be many runners qualified to compete in 3 Countries. Chris Steer, who won the Canadian Marathon back in 1969, now lives in Seattle, USA, but has retained Belgrave membership(and UK citizenship!). In March Chris was back on holiday and ran in several races to make first reserve for the 12 stage relay. But, back in November he won the Canadian Vets C.C.Champs and the next month led home his Seattle based Club to win the USA Vets C.C. Team title. Our man's been quite busy!!!

Our 12 stage relay provided a good get together for members of our past winning teams. Gerry North, Tony Fairclough, Chris Steer, Colin Pearson, Pat O'Connor and Trevor Hart could all be seen in animated conversation. The subject was Trevor's 'See Through Outfit'. He says it suits his mature image. Pat reckons he got it from Oxfam.

More news from across the Atlantic is that Peter Hardwick has run a very respectable 2.29.55 marathon in Ottowa. It's hard to believe this is the same athlete who was so slow round the Club 10 five years ago that he helped to pick up the flags on his last lap! Peter is rewarded with 14th place on our all-time list - one place ahead of Gerry North.

If you hear the sounds of a melodeon with the jingling of bells and the banging of sticks around the Epsom area keep an eye open for Alan Mead, the Southern League T & F Sec. He has by all accounts discovered hidden talents as a Morris dancer. It seems a more refined form of exercise is now preferred to track reps.

Since retiring out West Gordon Doubleday is busier than ever with time-keeping duties in and around Bath. He still manages to travel back to officiate at Belgrave fixtures. He has also joined Somer AC and is building up their activities with another Belgravian - Bob James - who will be remembered by those active in the late 1960's. Gordon still manages to address by hand all the 750 envelopes for despatching the Belgravian.

John Bicourt has made a second TV advert for Olympus Sports.

Ken Onuaguluchi, Derrick Williams and Keith Smith were among Belgrave athletes attending a sunny Crystal Palace on April 24th to provide background realism for a film called 'Gulag' which centres around the Moscow Olympics and involves political intrigue. It is due for general release later this year.

Kevin Sturman spent 3 weeks before Xmas out in Greece selected as a mobile 'Look alike' for star Jason Connery, son of you know who! This film is an epic entitled 'The first Olympics' and Virginia McKenna, Bill Travers and Benedict Taylor are the stars . It looks as though we should be including Equity membership forms when we sign new members!

Glandular Fever has struck-first, Junior John Warren who had already had his share of bad luck with a troublesome knee injury, and second Gary Staines who succumbed immediately after the 12 stage relay. Next might be Stuart Paton who was seen inspecting Gary's throat in some detail after that particular race.

Alan Painter showed all the signs of a misspent youth on Dec 3rd when he landed a touch in the 1 o'clock at Chepstow and took the Denmark Pot in the evening. But he must have found time for training as he put one over the Hon. Handicapper in our 7½ mile CC Champs the same afternoon!

BELGRAVE HALL DEVELOPMENT

£45,000 BELGRAVE HALL DEVELOPMENT GETS THE GO AHEAD

The Belgrave Hall Development will be nearly complete as this issue of the Club Mag. lands on your doormat. But it was touch and go whether or not it would proceed at all because the first tenders ranged from a staggering £48000 to an incredible £74000!! With only £31000 raised at the end of November 1983 things looked desparate. But, not for the first time our good supporters -The GLC- came to our rescue. A hastily prepared proposal, a presentation to the GLC's Arts & Recreation's Sports Committee(chaired by Peter Pitt) and we were given a maximum £14000 Grant for our size of Club.

So, once again we went out to tender on a slightly reduced sheme and this time 2 quotes were within budget. The Finance and Trustees gave the 'Go Ahead'; the General Committee quickly approved and the contract was let against a 72 page Specification prepared by our Architect - David Lawson RIBA -. By a piece of good luck our rush proved worthwhile as the contract period which spans from 1st March to May 31st ends just 1 day prior to June 1st when VAT becomes chargeable on building works of the type we are undertaking. (Little did anyone dream that the spring Budget would bring in this unwecome bit of legislation). If we had delayed the development any further it would have cost a further £6000 for the VAT and would have effectively put and end to our plans for several years.

The Hall Development represents the largest single investment ever by Belgrave and is a measure of our commitment to the future. Once the loans totalling £11000 have been paid off the Hall will be a source of substantial revenues which will be used to finance a range of ambitious projects each aimed at helping members to enjoy our Sport and to give everyone the best chance to develop their athletics carrers to the full.

A lot of Members have put in a great deal of very hard work to see this development through and to raise the funds for this project. We owe each and everyone a great vote of thanks. One practical way will be for you to support all Hall activities organised by Bill Couzens and the 'Social' team and to use the Gym and Leisure facilities which will be available after JUNE 1984. Keep in touch for the date of the GRAND OPENING in June/ July.

52 YEARS AGO

With the 1984 Olympic Games to take place in Los Angeles, U.S.A. many will recall the 1932 Games held in that city. It was an expensive operation for Great Britain and a long journey for the team which travelled by sea and overland. We did not send our full complement - but Belgrave were represented.

There had been no walking events in the previous Games at Amsterdam, prior to that walks had been held over the shorter distances. After strong representation by the Walking World it was decided to hold a walk on the road over a longer distance - 50 Kilometres. Our sole representative was Belgravian Tommy Green, undisputed winner of many famous place to place walks including the London to Brighton. Tommy won the race of his career in 4hrs 50m. 10secs. 7m 12secs. ahead of J. Dalinch, Lativia (now part of U.S.S.R.), probably the biggest winning margin in any Olympic Athletic Event. He presented the Club with a cup bearing his name, awarded annually to the best performance by a member.

On the track Tom Hampson (Archilles Club) scored a great win in the 800m to crack the 1m 50sec. barrier by recording 1m 49.7sec. (rounded up to 1.49.8secs as times over this distance were only given in fifths of a second). Tom was a second-claim member of the Club and did much of his training at Battersea Park.

Both these Champions have passed on but their performances are remembered with pride and admiration

In Memorium

T.H. SCRIMSHAW

High on the International Honours Board at Belgrave Hall is the name of Tommy Scrimshaw. We are sorry to learn that he died in March at the age of 73. He had been admitted to hospital for treatment when his condition worsened and he died.

In the early thirties Tommy was one of the country's leading Half Milers, as his many International vests show. He also represented the A.A.A. on a number of occasions, also the County of Middlesex. He was a tower of strength in Club teams. One event popular in those days was the One Mile Medley Relay (880, 220, 220 and 440 yards), with Tommy on the first leg a good lead from him would give his team a great advantage. In 1933 he narrowly missed winning an A.A.A. Championship being beaten on the post.

To reach the heights he did meant serious and devoted training and he prepared himself thoroughly for races at the top level. During the winter he would enjoy a spin over the country, and on Sundays would take part in an all day stroll over tough going in the Surrey Hills. He was also a regular at the Club's Social Functions.

His representative appearances took him all over Britain and his gift for making friends brought him a wide circle of friends. Amongst these were countless Belgravians and many had the pleasure of meeting him over the last few years when he came along to Wimbledon to see the lads in action.

The news of his death will bring back happy memories of the joy and fun which his running and friendship brought us. We extend our sympathies to his wife and family.

A number of Belgravians attended his funeral at Randalls Park, Leatherhead.

Tommy Scrimshaw

HON. SEC'S. REPORT

A 'FIRST' FOR TED STIMPSON

I am sure members will wish to congratulate Ted Stimpson on being elected President of Middlesex County A.A.A.

Ted, who was a Cross-Country International and a member of Belgrave Winning National Teams, was also member of the renowned Cadagon A.C. (which - not to get too involved - was a closed club within Belgrave for members who had a Middlesex Qualification) at the time when clubs could only affiliate to one county association. He was a member of many winning Cadagon teams in the Middlesex Champs. often on the same day as the rest of Belgrave were winning the Surrey Championship!!

Although past General Secretary, Alf Harley, was President of Middlesex C. A.A.A. it was in his R.W.A. capacity that he was so elected. Ted is the first 'nominated committee' member of Belgrave to be elected to this office - well done - have a good year.

WELCOME RON SEVERN

Experienced Officials are few and far between and it take years of hard graft in Club Athletics to produce the kind of experience which can help sustain and create a Club's vitality. Club Officials generally come up through the ranks and naturally remain in the Club for a lifetime. Sometimes however Officials find that they are unable to carry on for various reasons and it mainly moving house that causes the biggest problems. Belgrave have been most fortunate in gaining the membership and support of Ron Severn who moved to Wimbledon from High Wycombe last year and has taken on the job of Assistant Secretary.

In an athletics career which he says has been notable for it's mediocrity, he first began running while in the Navy where he won medals for cross-country and boxing. After a lapse of several years he joined Wycombe Phoenix Harriers, where he says his level of non achievement continued. He did however become in turn; Secretary, Trustee and Chairman of Wycombe Phoenix eventually giving up in the late 1960's. His two satisfying achievements were a Road Runners Club 2nd Class certificate(at 10,15, and 20miles), and his association with the once famous Wycombe 'Furniture-Five' Road Race. When Ron first became involved with it, it was a small, fairly local event. When he left it , it had become a major Southern race which all the top Clubs contested from as far afield as Bristol, Portsmouth and of course Belgrave.

In private life Ron is a solicitor, a chartered accountant and is a Company and Commercial accountant with his own practice. He is retained by a major firm of Chartered Accountants in London. We welcome him to our management team and hope that he will be able to devote his undoubted knowledge of the Athletic scene as well as his personal talents to the continuing development of Belgrave.

AAA'S - BIG SHAKE UP PROPOSED - BILL LUCAS INVOLVED

At present Athletics in the U,K, & N.Ireland is controlled by 19 separate bodies and a quick glance at Belgrave's letter heading will confirm that we are affiliated to 12 of them in order to conduct the affairs of our members. From time to time plans have been prepared aimed at simplifying the organisation of the U.K's Athletics activities. The last major report on this topic was prepared 15 years ago by the Byers working party and although it recommended the setting up of a single Governing Body little progress has been made due to the reluctance of the various Area Bodies to relinquish power.

Recently pressure from certain Clubs has led to the AAA's setting up another working party to study the thorny problem of re-organisation. Chairing the working party is Dr Mike Turner -ex English International and of particular interest to Belgravian's is that our own Past President Bill Lucas was included in the total of 10 members. Other members include; Dave Moorcroft plus representatives from the BAAB, Welsh AAA's, Northern Counties AAA, Midland AAA's, and the AAA's itself.

Not surprisingly the Turner Report follows closely the Byers Report and proposes a simpler Administrative Structure. Belgrave has set up a 5 man Study Group to review the recommendations as they affect us. What they conclude and how BEL'S use their 5 votes at the AAA's E.G.M. which is to be held in June will be reported in the next Belgravian.

RON LANGHEIM - FUND RAISER

In 1979 Ron Langheim announced a record profit of £279 for the Club Draw which he organises along with the Dinner and Dance. Over the years this profit has increased until in 1983 Ron was able to point to a figure of £429. Over the last six years Ron has raised a total of nearly £2000 for the Club by this means and we owe him a big vote of thanks.

In addition Ron has organised the Dinner and Dance for the last 4 years but sadly he is to step down and this year Brian Pritchard and Bill Couzens are taking over. They have booked Bourne Hall - A really delightful place in the heart of Ewell Village. Dining and Dancing facilities are excellent and we strongly recommend you start saving now for the 3rd Friday in November.

WALKING

It has been a good winter, almost a clean sweep -the Enfield being the slight upset. Richard Dorman has regained form temporarily lost and backing up strength is certainly on the up. Team spirit seems to be also at an excellent level. One just hopes that we can put it together for this year and all the year. Stephen Marshall's form should be an inspiration to all, two years ago his time was 95 minutes in the Belgrave 10, this year 75 mins, a 20 minutes improvement! Again mentioning Richard Dorman all his races have been done on trips down from Nottingham - not for him a ½ hour journey. Mike Scamell has promised to train this year! Newcomer John Llewellin, he won the handicap at Enfield, with a sub 56 for 7 in his 1st Open., Neil Foster, although at College in Liverpool has managed trips back for races as well as various meetings up North, and Michael Feighan another with great prospects have all been well up in the finishing orders..

Those of you who competed with George Williams will be pleased to know that his return has now gone unnoticed. Although not quite as fast as he used to be. As a result of his rejuvenation he has formed a Post Office walking section in Bournemouth.

By the time this is published Graham Seatter who 6 years ago was one of our leading lights will have re-appeared in competition. He made his return to England in December and has been training hard since. With the possibility of another addition in a few months one can be fairly certain that nobody can guarantee a team place.

Although shorter distance events should prove no problem in turning out teams, we could still have difficulties in the ultra distances but not if we can all pull together.

On a similar front we are not doing too much on the Younger Age Groups. We did have two girls teams in the Nationals but 2 out of 7 is not all that good.

YOUNG ATHLETES

Reports by Ian Horton

This is the third winter season under the 'new management' and again it has proved to be the most successful to date. It was obvious from the beginning that in order to create strong sections in each age group, it would be necessary to build at the base with the Colts. Therefore, requiring a five year term. Vests could be seen buzzing at no less than 18 separate venues. We have learnt a lot at the Downs. Probably the most important aspect is that each age group runs over the same course distance at separate times, therefore providing information which can be correlated for broader use.

ROAD AND COUNTRY

Reports by.......Don Anderson. C.C.Captain.

Looking back at the Road and Country results for the 1983/84 season it can be seen that performances were patchy but better than in recent years. We started off very badly in the first Surrey League match and the Surrey Road Relay, but ended up in 3rd position in Division 1 of the Surrey League behind Aldershot and Herne Hill. On the road we picked up our first team relay medal -in the Cambridge Harriers Bexley race - for nearly 10 years. On the Country our peak performance came from our middle distance and speed men who travelled to the Wellington International Relays at Ostend in March and not only became the 2nd ever British Club to win the Men's race but did so in a time exactly 1 minute inside last year's winning time.

We can improve on these performances next season by keeping our Road Running side going during the summer and I look forward to all those not actively involved in our track teams to contact Tony Grant who will this summer be looking to get teams out at major races in the hope that we can pick some more team medals. Finally a word of thanks to Tom Carter, Colin Pearson and Gordon Biscoe for all their support during the winter season.

Belgrave First

25th MARCH 1984 OSTEND 28th WELLINGTON INTERNATIONAL RELAY

Last year Blackheath became the first British Club to win the famous Wellington International Relay organised by the Hermes Club Oostende. Last year we were 6th and Team Manager-Leo Coy- was determined that we would produce a much better performance this year. But other teams had the same idea and both Aldershot and Blackheath seemed confident that they had brought the men to win. The organisers were not so sure and predicted a close race between Dutch and German teams who were fielding several Internationals. Before the race our strong group of loyal supporters were busy looking for bookmakers to get a sidebet on our'Unknowns' but without luck.No doubt the odds on us will be poor and the chance to supplement costs via the bookies have disappeared for next year. On to the race itself; The turf was heavy and rough after overnight rain.With 16 International teams and 39 Club teams the first stage was like the charge of the Light Brigade. Mark Sinclair kept well out of trouble away from the rails and by the end of the 800m stage had worked up to a useful 7th place. Stuart Paton zipped through the next 600m leg handing over to Dave Mote in 3rd place and the Team Captain kept us in that place for his 200m stint. Mike Allayne is big and tough and there was no way he was going to lose a position on his 350m leg. Pat Lyttle tied up a bit over the next 300m but although we slipped to 4th the field in front was being pulled back. The next stage changed everything. Leo had predicted that the 3 long legs would settle it and it was John Gladwin who put the issue beyond doubt on the 6th stage of 1200m. His sustained pace hauled in the 3rd place man almost immediately and he drifted round the tight turn at full stretch to take the lead in front of a full and clearly impressed grandstand of spectators. By the time he handed over to Gary Staines on the next 1200m leg we were 9secs. clear and Gary, followed by Kevin Sturman(1100m) - Paul Richley(800m) - Richard Addison(400m) - Guy Sagar(400m) - and Derrick Williams(200m) each maintained that margin as the strolled round lapping up the multi-lingual commentary which was very compimentary about their efforts. And so we were all pleased -not because the winning time was exactly 1 minute faster than last years- but because our men ran for each other and were impressive as a Team!

The evening was quite something with the supporters laying on the right kind of celebration in an English owned bar. As far as the other goings on we refer you dear reader to the Centre Pages of this Edition

GREAT IMPROVEMENT IN 12 STAGE PERFORMANCE

7th APRIL 1984 WIMBLEDON BELGRAVE/BROOKS/SOUTHERN 12 STAGE RELAY

To the outside world this race was the same well organised affair that it has always been. To us things were very different. Gone were Haig whiskey to be replaced by new sponsors BROOKS who have agreed to at least a 3 year term. Add to this the decision by the Southern Counties to at last make this event an Official Area Championship and we had an air increased expectation. Clive Shippen, who in Gordon Biscoe's absense, masterminded the whole affair valiantly dealt with over 70 entry applications and gradually whittled the field down to 45 with no 'B' teams. Bill Couzens had moments of panic as first the Police insisted on a new course which had to be measured only to find the day before the race that road works made it impassable. So, at the 11th hour we reverted to the old course and everyone was delighted as it enabled times to be compared with previous years. Generally as we count down to the race injuries occur and people drop out. This year the team was decided on the Sunday before and Chris Steer who delayed his return to the USA remained as reserve. Only Kevin Sturman and John Bicourt were unavailable and our youngest ever team(with the exception of 'Super Lol') did all that we had hoped for running 8 minutes faster than last year, qualifying for the National 12 stage, and producing a time that would have been good enough for 2nd place 4 years ago. Inevitably every leg was discussed in great detail for the following few days. With all our track stars on the short leg we achieved the distinction of being the only team with 3 men under 16 mins on the short stage,with Mark Sinclair only just outside. But for most the outstanding run was from Andy North, who,having shaken off a niggling injury which has dogged him for 3 years, has shown rapid progress. Of the long leg men Peter Gilbey showed how rapidly he has recovered from his injury as he ran 2nd fastest time behind Oliver Foote. Many of the team knew they could have run better and it would not have taken much to close the two min. 39sec gap between our 10th and Elliott's 3rd place. Maybe next year!!!!

BRITISH LEAGUE BITS

TRACK AND FIELD

The British League has decided that from 1985 there will be 8 Teams in Div.1 and that 6 teams will be retained in each of Div,s 2 to 5. The big worry was the ability to keep a reasonable timetable particularly in the throws. Our experience in 1983 with 7 teams in Div. 5(a once off exercise) showed that the javelin was often still in progress after the relays had finished. No doubt the BAL Officials feel they can sharpen up in this area. So, in 1984 the top 3 teams in Divs 2 to 5 will all be promoted and only 1 team will be demoted. Two extra teams will enter Div 5 raising the total number of British League Clubs to 32.

Belgrave men set 3 new Div 5 records last year. Ernest Obeng posted a new 100m best with 10.6, Derrick Williams a new long jump record with 7.50, and our 4x400m squad of Stuart Paton,John Gladwin,Guy Sagar and Dave Mote set a classy time with their 3.17.8. For anyone wishing to get their name on the Div 4 record book these are the targets you'll have to beat.

Leo Coy points out that John Bicourt achieved the rare distinction of spanning 14 years of League competition for Belgrave. In 1969 he was a member of our team that contested the first National League(as it was then known) and in 1983 he made a welcome return to our steeplechase squad. In addition to John there are still several members who are actively helping the Club in one way or another; Snowy Brooks, Charlie Gillett,Tony Fairclough, Bill Couzens,Laurie O'Hara, Alan Mead, Bob Taylor, John Martin and Lionel Mann: and sharing the honours are ex-Surrey AC National League competitors-Bill Treharne, Dave Mole and Bob Bridges.

TRACK AND FIELD RANKINGS 1983

Clubs. In 1981 Belgrave ranked 35th , in 1982 we were 21st and we knew that our 1983 performances were good enough to improve on that. And so we awaited the 1983 Track and Field Rankings with a good deal of pleasant anticipation. This year John Lunn's rankings of the top 200 Clubs was based on the 3 best performances for each event excluding the 10000m. Leading the rankings for 1983 were Haringay with 48,839 pts followed by Birchfield with 47,813 and Shaftesbury with 47,093. Belgrave moved up to an excellent 9th place with 43,833 pts. Our rivals in this years British League Div.4 will provide stiff competition judging by their 1983 performances;

Aldershot 16th 42,541, Enfield 14th 43,251, Sheffield 15th 43,013, Windsor S&E 22nd 41,649, and Woodford Green 42, 017 in 19th spot.

Our local rivals did not fare so well with Epsom & E 23rd, H.H.H. 28th, Hercules Wimbledon 57th, Croydon 60th,Sutton & Cheam 78th and S.L.H. 89th.

Individuals. Stuart Paton ranked 9th in the Scottish 400m with his 48.9 mark; 5th in the 800m with 1.49.9, 5th in the 1500m with 3.43.27 and 7th in the mile with 4.05.9. Mark Lindsay Bayley's 15.35 110mH time gave him 4th spot in the Scottish Lists while his 56.1 400mH was good enough for 11th place and his 6.65m long jump was also sufficient for 11th spot.

In the UK Rankings Ernest Obeng featured in 8th place with his 10.28(W) 100m while Colin Gaynor's 10.56(W) made 17th spot in an injury plagued season. Ernest avoided the big 200m races because of back injury and Colin's early season's 21.68 was good enough for 38th place. In the 800m John Gladwin's 1.46.46 was good enough only for 10th place in this star packed event and Stuart's time made 35th. In the 1500m the order was reversed with Stuart in 22nd spot followed by Gary Staines in 42nd(3.44.94) and John 44th with 3.45.09. In the mile John was 30th with a 4.02.2 timing. Tim Kay our Irish Guards steeplechaser was top for us with 9.08.7 in 46th place while MLB made 40th in the 110mH. Our field men showed great improvement. In the high jump Jim Gregg ranked 21st with 2.05,while in the long jump Derrick Williams filled 11th spot with 7.52 and followed this with 29th in the triple jump with 14.98m(W). Our Pole Vaulting strength was evident with Phil Lovell in 7th place with 4.90m and Mark Shippen in 17th with 4.40. Dave Dawson(another of our Irish Guards) made 24th in the shot with 14.96 while Ken Onuaguluchi finished in 10th position in the discus with 52.46m. His guest appearance in the first Southern Counties rep match and 52.60 throw seems to have gone unrecorded.

Our Youths also performed with distinction. Philip Goedluck ranked 20th in the 200m with 22.5, and Richard Allison's 400m County record of 49.9 was worth 18th spot one place behind a certain Ade Mafe.

We look forward to further improvements this coming season with particular attention to those moving up to junior and senior ranks. Good Luck!!!!

INDOOR ROUND UP

compiled by Steve Williams

The 1983/1984 Indoor Season saw a number of tremendous performances from Belgrave track and field men including AAA Championship victories for Ernest Obeng and Gary Staines, AAA Under 20 medals for Philip Goedluck and Sean Richards in the Youths Championship, a club indoor best vault from Phil Lovell - all capped by Gary's double International selection for Great Britain for his first senior 'vests'.

It hadn't been Gary's long term objective to go for the AAA 3000 metres but a last minute decision to test how the Winter's training was going provided a bonus when he took the title from Billy Dee and ten others in a pb and new club indoor best of 8.04.72. Gary thus added the Senior Championship to his AAA Under 20 victory of two years previously. As a result of this fine win he was selected for the Indoor Internationals against the GDR at Cosford on 1st February and France in Vittel on 4th February over the same distance. In the first match, the East Germans wound up the race from half way and Gary couldn't quite hold on in the closing laps. He modestly said he had been outclassed on the day but he was less than 6 seconds behind the winner and recorded another good time of 8.07.3( in even 2.42 splits ) for a very creditable senior debut. Just 3 days later in France, on an acutely angled track under a low glass roof which created an uncomfortable muggy atmosphere, Gary, like most of the Brits, could find nothing against the fresher home opposition and he finished 3rd in 8.19.99.

While Gary was taking the longest track event in the AAA's, Ernest Obeng made it a double celebration for Belgrave by winning the shortest event, the 60 metres, in 6.79 ahead of Buster Watson and Cameron Sharp. Ernie was even a shade faster in the semis with 6.77 - 4/100ths away from his best. This result confirmed Ernie's supremacy on the boards for he had already beaten Sharp in 6.8 the week previously at the Cosford Games on 7 January.

Phil Lovell and Colin Gaynor also took the opportunity to sharpen up indoors, competing in both the Cosford Games and the AAA's. Phil - in transitional stage between 170lb and 175lb poles - vaulted an indoor best of 4.75 for 5th place in the Cosford using the softer pole before going out at 4.90. In the AAA he warmed up with the 175, looking for a competitive height, but he encountered problems and had to revert to the old pole to clear 4.50. He couldn't then get it together to go any further and finished in 9th place but Phil says he hopes to go over 5.10 or even 5.20 when he gets used to the extra tension of the 175.

Colin Gaynor shaped up with 7.0 and 6.99 over 60m but was unfortunately eliminated in the preliminaries of both events.

Not to be totally outdone by their seniors our more than promising young sprint triumvirate of Philip Goedluck, Sean Richards and Andrew Rodney were also in action to considerable effect.

Phil ran some exceptional 200m times for a Youth indoors; 23.0 to win the Youths event at the Cosford Open on 19 November and 23.15( 23.04sf) for a marvellous silver medal in the AAA Youth Championship on 17/18 February. We have no knowledge of any faster indoor times by a Belgravian so Phil's 23.04 goes down as a club best. Hardly a stride behind Phil in the AAA's was Sean Richards with 23.28 for a fine 3rd place. Sean also went one better in the 400m with a silver medal in 51.67 - a magnificent double effort. Over 60m Andrew Rodney three times recorded 7.2. during the winter and peaked in the AAA Junior preliminaries with a fast 7.12 electrical timing.

All in all a season to whet the appetite for the summer ahead.

WITH THE LADIES

Ladies Secretary

During the winter we have been based at Belgrave Hall on Saturday afternoons and a fair number have been coming along either for cross country training or a gentle jog across Wimbledon Common. Sunday mornings has seen us at Battersea Park and again a good crowd has been coming along for training with Frank Simmons. My thanks to Frank and to Ted Weldin and Graham Thom for their help and taking some of the work load off Frank. Words, cannot express my gratitude for the work done by Harry Porter and David Sharpe. Our circuit training sessions started later than we intended, but thanks to the help of Merton Council we were able to use a School gymnasium in Wimbledon. We always had a good crowd coming along to be put through their paces and finishing off the sessions with a game of Netball (really a cross between Netball, Basketball and at times all-in-wrestling). Definitely not for the nervous or faint hearted. We have four District Cross Country Champions. Kim Marsh started the ball rolling with an emphatic win in the first ever Hammersmith & Chelsea Schools event at Wormwood Scrubbs. Gail Porter completed a hat-trick of wins in the Wandsworth Schools (this time in the Intermediates). Mary Anna O'Connell did well to win the Chelsea Schools as did Jackie Stone in the Morden Schools who also beat girls two years older than herself. Although we did not have as many turning out in Cross-Country races as we did last year we did have regular attendances with good performances in the Girls from Jackie Stone, Isobel Young, Christina Gauge and Mary Anna O'Connell before she was injured. In the Juniors, because of the injury to Gail Porter, Davina Cameron was our only regular middle distance and cross country competitor and she was always trying hard. The other runners came along for training purposes and it seemed they enjoyed doing this, as there was no pressure on them. Injuries affected Gail Porter, Joanne McGroder and Mary Anna O'Connell and 'flu', mumps and various other illnesses hit everybody at some stage during the season. Rosemary Honeychurch was our only regular Senior Cross-Country runner, but she always battled away well in races and gave of her best, always turning up for training with an infectious enthusiasm that is very refreshing. Leaving the best till last, our walkers have been doing well in the Walking League and various other races that they have been in The Record Book has been completely rewritten by Gail Porter in the Juniors and Rachael Parrott in the Girls. Rachael now holds all four Girls Records and could probably lower these even more. There was good backing from Joanne McGroder, Kim Marsh, Isobel Young, Nikki Marsh, Charlotte Christou, and once again Rosemary Honeychurch improved on her times. 8½ year old Sarah O'Connell showed a lot of determination. The resurgence of interest in Walking has been helped by the enthusiasm of David Sharpe. Our trips to Southend, Highams Park and Birmingham were very enjoyable despite the bad weather on the last two. Thanks to all who have helped and supported the Ladies Section and I feel that a special thank you should go to Sue Parrott and Margaret O'Connell for their help and suggestions. Also, of course, thanks to Harry for driving the Mini-Bus, to training sessions and meetings as well as to Sue and Ken Parrott, Arthur O'Connell and Peter McGroder for providing transport as well. Harry, will probably be happier when he has a new navigator. Sorry Harry. We now look forward to a good summer season and hope that we are able to challenge for promotion this year. Support is welcome on Southern League trips and details of these and our other league matches appear later in this report.

Ladies Secretary

VETS. CORNER

Apart from his successes in Area Championships Laurie O'Hara, who is now in his 52nd year, has once again put together a string of very impressive performances which confirm him as our top Veteran once again. On the 7th October in the Windsor Half Marathon he finished in 5th place overall and first O/50 with 1hr 14. On the 3rd December he was 8th in the Eastcote 6 mile Open C.C. race, while on New Year's day he once again ran the London Wall 10 mile road race and placed 33nd overall and was first O/50 with a remarkable 53.40 timing. He ran two other Half Marathons during the winter finishing in 4th place in the Harrow race on the 6th November in 1:12:30, and in 6th place in the Hillingdon event on the 25th March 1984 in 1:13:05. In both races he won the vets titles. The Hillingdon race was only 2 days after he had finished in 29th place in the Feltham 5 miler in a time of 26.19. No wonder he made our 12 stage relay team. Each year we say Laurie must start to slow down. To save speculation he could perhaps drop us a line so that the other Vets can get down to some serious training!!!

Laurie O'Hara

Having been a member for 57 years(so far) and living as I do in the sticks, I always look forward to receiving my Belgravian.

John Thresher

From; John Thresher. Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada.

I have just received the recent Belgravian. It was really heartwarming to read of the successes and the new young Belgravians coming through. I still receive the Athletics Weekly so I was well aware of the exploits of John Gladwin and Stu' Paton, but the British League and the 25x1 mile relay results were so well worth reading.

I was pleased of course to see my Club mile record surviving into 1984 making it a round 20 years. I predict now however that 3 Belgravians will break 4 minutes for the mile in 1984 and I wonder if Gary Staines will beat the two favourites to become the first.

I had a good Spring(1983). In May I ran 32.16 for an accurate 10km road race in a temperature of -6deg C! and then 2:33:10 for a marathon in June. I injured myself in the marathon and haven't raced since. I don't intend to run another marathon and just want to run again. Time is running out as I am now turned 44!!

I enclose a small donation towards the Belgrave Hall Development fund. Best wishes, good health and happiness to all the Bels.

John Thresher.

Ranjit Bhatia

From; Ranjit Bhatia. Delhi, India.

The new President of Belgrave is part of the gentry at Belgrave Hall to whom I owe so much. It is really through their guidance and encouragement that I have managed to survive as an athlete for 32 years now. I like to feel that is an achievement of a sort. As the years add on the annual list of attainments gets gradually diminished. But so what? Running can be more fun as we get older than ever it was.

I am delighted to read about the renovations at Belgrave Hall. I shall be sending a donation towards the project. I always think of the wonderful fraternity at Belgrave Hall which represents the best aspects of the Athletic movement

I was running reasonably well last season and managed a 1:23 half marathon on October 25th. This spurred me on to more training and then suddenly everything came to a halt due to pressure of work. If my plans for Los Angeles materialise I hope to be in the UK in July and aim to run for the Bels Vets. I am delighted to see that Bill Lucas was recently President of the Southern Counties AAA's. All good wishes to fellow Belgravians. Warmest regards.

Ranjit Bhatia.

" It is always pleasing to hear from our overseas members and good to see that Ranjit is going strong. Our newer members will not be aware that back in March 1960 Ranjit set a new record round the 5¾mile road circuit of 27min 56 secs. Our stars of today will need to go a bit to beat that. Ranjit is also involved on the admin. side and in October 1983 was manager of an Indian team that toured Japan. In between all that he is Professor of Mathematics at Delhi University. Once a year he visits the UK to take his place as the Indian represenative at Oxford University on the Rhode's Scholarship Selection Panel. We look forward to welcoming him to our rejuvinated Belgrave Hall this summer." Ed.
