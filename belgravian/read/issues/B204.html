DEVELOPMENT PLANS

Some 5 years ago and with £4,500 in the bank our Committee decided to invest everything in improving the social facilities at Belgrave. Two years later we were broke and hoping we had made the right decision. Now the Hall is a changed place and makes a fine meeting place for all members and their families. What's more, the bar revenue, plus the efforts of several loyal and hard working members have results in a situation where we find ourselves now with over £6,000 in the bank. In Memorium HARRY SHIELDS

Jack Goswell

Following the tragic death of Dave King, Belgrave H. suffered its second great loss in successive months by the passing of Harry Shields early on Sunday morning, 25th October 1981, just seven weeks after completing 50 years membership of the Club.

HARRY SHIELDS AT THE 'BANSTEAD' TURN DURING THE 1955 SWC CROYDON AND BACK. Jack Goswell is seen recording.

From early days "athletics" and BELGRAVE in particular, and love of the English countryside played a large part in Harry's life. Rising through the ranks eventually to become a member of one of the finest Cross Country teams in the country at that time, winning English National Cross Country team medals and National London-Brighton Relay winning team medals.

In the late forties he turned his talents to Race Walking and with the great tenacity for which he was noted, he became a regular stalwart of Belgrave teams in the fifties. He was member of the first Club team which won all the National Championships in one year, so becoming one of the few people, if not the only one, to hold winning team medals for English Cross Country, National Relay and National Race Walking and after a number of London - Brightons, was awarded the Centurion Medal number 235 after the Brighton and Back in 1955.

Despite a busy Athletics career Harry assisted in the administration of his sport, serving for many years as Ass. Hon. Sec. to his great pal Alf Harley for the Club. He also held the same position for the South of the Thames Cross Country Assn. of which he was President in 1960/61 and represented this body on the Southern Counties C.C.A. of which he was President in 1957/58. He was also a member of the Southern Counties A.A.A. Committee for many years and also served as an R.W.A. Judge.

In late 1968 Harry had a setback in health which took a couple of years to overcome, and after early retirement, and never being an "onlooker" rather a "participant" he turned his main attention to Bowls and Painting, of which his talents were amazing and had to be seen to be believed. In the last couple of years all his paintings in Art School Exhibitions were snapped up, and during his last few months the local "Kent Messenger" requested an interview after seeing some of his paintings in a London Transport Exhibition.

So passes a great stalwart of Athletics, many people share with me pleasant memories of strolling days in his company. I remember he was an authority on "Trees" a subject we could always be given a lesson on if we mistook one, which needless to say were numerous. We are all poorer by his passing, but enriched by knowing him and enjoying his company over the years and our sympathy to his wife Betty and his two daughters in their great loss.

Jack Goswell

TOM REYNOLDS

We regret to record the passing of Tom Reynolds, the well-known timekeeper who died in October at the age of 82. Although not a member of our Club (he was a member of Surrey W.C. and Surrey A.C.) he was one of those people who belong to athletics and his services were at the disposal of many clubs and organisations. Amongst these was our own Club and he was a frequent visitor to the Hall, and as recently as last July he was calling times at our 20 miles Open Run. He was also in contact with many of us through his keen and devoted service to Surrey County A.A.A. for which he worked hard. These last two words sum Tom up very well - whatever he was doing for athletics he worked hard at it.

A WORD FROM THE PRESIDENT

Derek Crookes

A busy six months have passed since I was elected President, and whilst I do not wish to repeat in detail results which have appeared in my feeble attempts at Newsletters, or which appear elsewhere in this magazine, I would like to take up just a few lines of the Belgravian.

Our Walkers obtained 3rd place in the National 100km, 1st place in Belgrave Open 7-Mile Walk and, in fact, all the London 7s, the Southend 6-Miles and the Met. Police 11km. Carl, their busy Sectional Secretary - as well as Membership Secretary of the Club - and Barbara, his wife, are expecting Lawton - Mark III - in October, sincere congratulations!!

On the Country, our Junior Team of Alistair Horton, Stuart Paton, Paul Richley and Mark Waller retained the Junior Team Race in the Surrey Country C.C. Stuart was 4th in the Scottish National and Paul 1st in Surrey Schools. We duly obtained promotion from Surrey League Div.II back to Div.I, but a black Saturday must surely have been the Southern Championships when we finished NO TEAMS AT ALL. Another season of hard work has been done by the Sectional Secretary, Gordon Biscoe.

Our boys, colts and youths have made steady progress, under the guidance of Ian Horton, a definite asset to the small management band of Belgrave. He has been assisted by Colin Pearson and Luke Sexton.

Our Ladies, to be honest, have enjoyed little success, but with Harry Porter joining in the administration side to help Derek Jones, who has worked single-handed for a long time, we hope for better things in the summer.

Looking to the Track we have-on-paper a very good Southern League Mens Team. The question is - "Can we change paper into reality"?

Luckily, we only have a limited number of fixtures at Battersea Park. Why do I say this? - Because, despite of numerous appeals, we still look like being very short of officials. Please look at your Fixture Cards and if you can make one or more of the meetings to help in an official capacity - please do so.

Besides those mentioned above I would like to thank our Treasurer, Jim Heathfield, and all the other members of committee for their support so far this year and look forward to the same support for the rest of the club year.

Derek Crookes (President)

HOW IS THE PRESIDENT CHOSEN

Derek Crookes

It was agreed, some years ago, that a brief explanation of how the Club President is chosen should appear in the Belgravian every few years. Basically the choice of President - as with all other Club Officers - is a matter of decision for the Annual General Meeting, but obviously any member being put up for the most important job in the Club should be well briefed before hand. Firstly - the job. This consists of being present at most of the important fixtures:- Track, Cross-country, Road, Socials on almost each weekend of the year, plus many Wednesdays in the summer, giving a speech at the Club Dinner & Dance and Chairing Club Committee Meetings. Secondly - how to choose the right man or women for the job. It is felt that the past Presidents of the club know more about the job than anyone else, so it is the custom for them to meet in June each year to discuss possible names for President and give a recommendation to committee. If committee approves the past presidents recommendation, the prospective president is approached to obtain his willingness to undertake the job and his name is put forward at the A.G.M. Of course, the choice of President is not a closed shop and members are invited to give any recommendations to the General Secretary or Present President, for discussion at the Past-Presidents Meeting.

Derek Crookes (Hon. Gen. Sec.)

BELGRAVE SUPPER - NUMBER ONE

Charlie Manning.

23 January 1982. That was the day of a very special evening which could, and should, become as significant as that day in 1887 when Belgrave Harriers was born.

What,you may well ask, has that got to do with "the Club Supper?".

Well, it appears to me that the Club Supper showed we have reached maturity. Of course in the past we have had hundreds of social events from Theatre to Fancy Dress, from Bingo to Photographic Competitions, from Jumble Sales to Brighton Relays, from Butlins Weekends to Rambles, from Formal Club Dinners to County and Southern Dinners etc. etc. etc.

But, this particular evening was a singular success. It depended on the mixture of old and young, of cantankerous and nieve, of doers and critics. It needed this amalgam which can only come with age.

It was just like a family meal, noisy, boisterous, good humoured and comfortable. One felt as easy as at home - and frankly - that was what it was "at home", the Bels, at home.

In the kitchen were the Mums - Gwen (Couzens), Mary (Crookes) Maureen (Pritchard) and Misses Ruth (Sinclair) and friend plus Karen (Crookes). We can't ignore what all their husbands did, plus, Colin Pearson at the Bar. I'm bound to have missed someone out, so, sorry - very sorry - and thank you very much and thanks to all of you who made such a night possible.

You've taken it to the top of the charts. Its Number One. Please don't alter the formula - make it number one again and it will run as long as Belgrave Harriers. Because that was what it was, Belgrave Harriers, at Home.

Charlie Manning.

WALKING

It can hardly be said that we have had a bad winter in so far as results go. The weather during December and early January obviously did not help proceedings. Team wins at Chippenham, Luton, Enfield, Wimbledon and Bexley in the Opens and the G.L.C. 10. We only missed out at Corby due to the lack of a 4th man. Fortunately we did not have to rely on the same 4 in the team events with 7 individuals picking up the 'pots'.

Unfortunately we seem to be suffering the general trend in walking, that being a reduction in the numbers of competitors and officials. On the officials side we have lost over the last few months Harry Shields and Syd Spencer, and although not a member of the club, Tom Reynolds. They were all long standing members of the walking world who, when their competing days were over, stayed around to put something back. These are people that the club is drastically short of and we need replacements. Having had the situation of starting, timekeeping, competing and recording in a recent race at Wimbledon I cannot stress the need for those that have hung up their boots and come forward and lend a hand even if only occasionally.

We did eventually collect National medals last year, in the 100kms. fortunately the classification rules worked in our favour but we were still third. Thanks must go to the members of Belgrave who helped out at the feeding station in the rather deplorable conditions.

On to this year, with the prospects of a full team turn out in most Nationals we can look forward to overall better performances than last year. We lost the McSweeney Trophy last year due to the failure of having any under 20 age group teams. To overcome that this year we must somewhere find some new recruits. All suggestions welcome.

OCTOBER 10th 1981 - CHIPPENHAM TO CALNE

Gordon Doubleday

Run into debt with the Subscription Secretary and you will soon get a reminder and the debt can be paid. Run or walk yourself into an Oxygen debt, and if not soon replenished, you are literally in dead trouble. History has it that this vital substance was discovered at Calne and nowadays every October walkers come puffing in after the six miles slog from Chippenham.

This years race, held on the 10th October, attracted the usual good entry and the race got under way in a fine drizzle. This year instead of dashing around to overtake the field I was in a car a few yards ahead of the start and was able to start my watch and the car got away smartly to reach the finish before the competitors had time to lose their breath.

A fine win for Phil Vesty (Leic) in the second fastest time ever, followed home by Mike Parker (Brighton). Carl was first home in 6th place and Richard three places down. Fine performances from John Hall and Newnham to close the team in for yet another win of the C and T Harris Trophy. With five more Belgrave finishers the locals saw plenty of Belgrave vests.

"THE FULHAM DENTIST"

This was the nickname given to the late Harry Evans, a past President, and a leading Judge in his day. He was so called because he 'pulled' so many out. His reputation was high and his decisions firm but fair. In 1909 he won the inaugural Garnet 10 miles cup race, with the club team in second place. A baker by trade he would work overnight amongst hot ovens and would then strip off and take on the best. His memory is perpetuated in the Harry Evans Cup awarded to the individual winner of our annual Open 7 miles walk.

This years event, the 62nd, was held on 28th November and, once again the weather was kind to us. 98 walkers set off at a signal from our President, Derek Crookes. After one lap of the hills Phil Vesty, Leicester's young star was leading from Dave Jarman of SWC and Adrian James of Enfield. Carl led the rest of the field with our lads battling it out to be in our scoring team. After two laps Richard was coming through, John Hall was giving his usual sound performance with the still improving John Newnham going confidently.

That's how it was at the finish except that Richard had caught Carl and they came in together. The scratch team race was ours with Steyning pipping Surrey for second. Mike Scamell was our next man in and took the prize awarded to the first Belgravian not gaining another award. Following in are two faces of the past, double Olympian Eric Hall, beating the hour and Richard Tanner who has been off the scene for a couple of years.

No Belgravians featured in the handicap but in the London Business Houses championship in conjunction Messrs. E and J. Hall and John Bromley were 2nd team representing the Civil Service and John Dunsford led home third team for London Transport.

Derek Jones

LETTERS TO THE EDITOR / More on Battersea Park Track

Tom Morrell

Dear Bill,

May I congratulate you on the No.203 edition of the Belgravian although most of the names are unknown to me, I read every word, its really a life line to those like me, out in the sticks.

I would refer in this respect to the article - Where are they now? by Gordon Doubleday.

I well remember running on the three lap track at Battersea, the hundred yard straight started from the first bend, the track of cinders was like a dust bowl after two or three days dry weather. The hundred yards was laned by iron spikes having loops on top through which was threaded white tape, usually 2/3 ft.high.

The club always held a Spring Meeting (open and club events) usually on the last Saturday in April, for the meeting held on April 30th 1927, we had the following races and entries - Open Hundred Yards Handicap, entries 83; Club 100 yds Handicap, 28 entries; for the 880 yds. Open Handicap, 116 entries, for a 1-Mile Open Novices, 26 entries; a 300 yd. Open Handicap, 86 entries; 26 entries for a One Mile Club Handicap; 2-Mile Club Handicap Walk, 31 entries; Club Long Jump 4 entries; altogether a total of 400 entries, the programme was priced at three pence (old money). I have the programmes for 1927/8/9. I believe the last such meeting was held in 1929.

Of those members named in the pre 1938 list, they were entered into their respective events - K.B. O'Kell, A.E. Duffett, L.L.King, F.R.Webb (Ashcombe A.C.) and myself.

My earliest programme is dated August 26th 1926, this is for a Club Handicap Meeting, four events, 89 entries, only three entries from the 1938 list - A.E.Duffett, L.L.King, and myself.

Regards to all,

Yours sincerely, TOM MORRELL

OTHER BATTERSEA PARK TIT-BITS OF THE 20's

When the track was not available, in the 1920's Belgravians would train round the gas holder, at the Battersea Gas Works, behind the Battersea Dogs Home, on a cinder path.

In the 20's and 30's Belgrave always had a large crowd of spectators at all our meetings, practically all round the railings, and two or three deep along the straight.

Made to measure spikes were £1.2. 6./£1.13 pence (new money). These were made by Sandy Law of Wimbledon, a shoemaker and member of the club.

In the 1920's Bill Fish was general factotem of the club, preparing the track, knocking in the spikes to tape the lanes in the hundred. He would also guard the gate to prevent unauthorised people coming on to the centre of the track. He would also sell programmes.

Those better off would have a massage after training, cost 1/- (5p new money). At one time the then new dressing room had three tables.

The Steeplechase

John Bicourt

The Steeplechase By John Bicourt

Steeplechasing is a specialist event. It is essential but not sufficient to be a good flat runner. The better your technique the nearer to your potential you can run. You should be no more than 12-15 secs off your best 1,500 flat time, over the jumps (26-30 secs for 3k s/C.)

If you don't practice hurdling you won't be very good at it. Do it at least twice a week. Once at the end (and beginning too) of a flat session and once as a main session where you try to run sections of the race distance at ideal racing speed.

Begin by running short distances many times. As you become accustomed to the effort of clearing hurdles reduce the reps and increase the distance, e.g. 10-20 x 100m over three hurdles at race speed .... 6-12 200m over four same speed ...... 4-8 x 400m five hurdles ..... 3-6 x 600m 5 hurdles on lap ..... 2-4 x 800m ..... 1-3 x 1,000m. Recover same distance run. The aim is to run strongly and within yourself and at a constant pace. ONLY GRADUALLY INCREASE the distance over a period of weeks. Naturally you may want to run an occasional long rep. to help give you the feeling for the race. But there is no point in struggling around a 1,000m if after 400m you are hopelessly tired and your technique is blown!

NEVER set off too fast in the 'chase. You will pay for it dearly in the later stages. A steady, even pace, will pay dividends at the finish. More people 'die' in the steeplechase than in any other track event. DO NOT RUN 3,000m S/c if you are a youth or first year junior you will do more harm than good.

Practice water jumping only over a full water jump. Start 30m out and run 30m beyond. Be committed, accelerate into it - your increase in speed helps to carry you over the twelve feet - keep low, drive hard off the far side of the bar, leave the driving foot on the bar as long as possible (so you make a big stride in the air) and don't stop for a rest when you land! ONCE YOU CAN COMFORTABLY CLEAR THE WATER JUMP - only one foot should get wet - you can take the jump in your longer reps. Remember when clearing water jump and hurdles to keep your arms in the running action ..... don't throw them up all over the place. PRACTICE, PRACTICE, PRACTICE.

Before all track sessions and particularly for hurdling do ten - 15 minutes stretching. You'll be surprised how much it helps you. Do them too at home when ever you can. Good flexibility and mobility are essential to maximum performance.

Steeplechasing is a technique event just like any of the jumps or throws. Only the most exceptional flat runners have any kind of success without proper preparation. Assume that you are NOT exceptional.

I cannot at this point tell you specifically how to train for the flat, but take note of some basic requirements:

Even in season do plenty of steady, easy running // when running flat out have good recovery // at race-pace speeds you can reduce the recovery until you increase the distance. Make all interval/rep training progressive. YOU STRESS THE BODY TO MAKE IT ADAPT TO THE DEMANDS, ONCE IT CAN HANDLE THE DEMANDS YOU MUST INCREASE THE STRESS TO LIFT IT TO A HIGHER LEVEL OF PERFORMANCE// Always follow 'hard' sessions with one or two days easy running.

Have a regular, consistant and progressive training programme with a goal in mind ... then work towards it ... good luck.

John Bicourt.

ROAD AND COUNTRY

Gordon Biscoe

It is hard to write enthusiastically over this last season when the sum total of our notable achievements has been winning the Surrey Junior title; second place in the Surrey League Div.II and fifth place in the South of the Thames Junior. Of course it has been good to note the steady improvements of Leo Lyons; Tony Binda; Mick Gleave and Simon Williamson, as well as the exploits of some of our Juniors as recorded elsewhere in these pages, but in the main all these efforts have been in the form of individual achievements. Our problem is still the same one which has plagued us for quite a while - we are just not getting it together as a team on the day, when it matters! While it would be foolish to say we could not do with some 'new blood' in the team, it is not through lack of ability that our performances are so poor, but perhaps a change of mental attitude that is required. Cross country running is still basically a team sport and it is a team that we were successful in in the past and should be able to do so again. When I keep receiving 'phone calls asking "what's on next week" or listening to the reasons why people can't turn out for various races, I tend to wonder just how much forward planning some runners do, or in fact just what is their training all in aid of. It seems ironic that we currently have thousands of people from all walks of life trying to 'run' marathons while a club of our size and reputation can't field more than six runners in the National or in the Bexley Road Relay!

In January we lost probably the most talented Senior road and country runner to join us for a considerable while when Graham Macky returned home to New Zealand. Not only did Graham's performances in the 14 months he was with us, have that 'touch of class' but his friendly disposition and uncomplicated approach to the sport made him very popular with all Club members. I'm sure all would join me in saying 'many thanks Graham for your efforts, it was a pleasure to have you with us and can we hope to see you in Belgrave's colours again one day'!

JUNIORS WIN AGAIN - SENIORS DISAPPOINT / JANUARY 2nd 1982 - WIMBLEDON COMMON - SURREY COUNTY C.C. CHAMPIONSHIPS

With Clive Shippen as the current County President we followed with tradition and acted as hosts for this year's Championship. Coming as they did in the middle of the atrocious weather and with Wimbledon Common under snow and ice, it required several course inspections by the Club President and C.C.Sec. approaching the day to work out various options should some of the proposed routes be impracticable. Fortunately a partial thaw did take place and everything was able to proceed as planned albeit in a mud bath. Special mention should be made of those who helped prepare and mark the course that day, and when you consider that several were out on the common without a break from 10.00 a.m. to nearly 5 p.m. it must raise serious doubts as to the wisdom of holding five Championships (i.e C, B, Y, J and S) on the same day!

In the Senior race it was not one of Graham Macky's best performances and although our team packed well in the 50's and 60's its the wrong part of the field to be if you want to win medals, hence we could only finish 7th out of the 12 teams closing in.

It was, therefore, left to our Junior team to provide our highlight of the day in a very close finish with Sutton & Cheam. Without John Gladwin and Mark Sinclair or John Searle and with Olly Foote not yet able to score for us it was left to the 'old firm' of Paton, Richley and North to form the spear-head of our scoring four with Alistair Horton rising to the occasion with an excellent run to close our team in. Thanks are also due to Mark Waller who turned out inspite of only just recovering from flu and to Giles Thrush drafted into the team at the last minute. Congratulations then to all the team for a fine effort in retaining the title won last year.

Report · page 42

E.OBENG

Ernie Obeng left our cold shores early in January to join coach Malcolm Coomber in Florida and to have a crack at the U.S. indoor circuit which, by then, had already got underway. After several races it was clear that Ernest was not firing on all cylinders and he wrote to say that he would concentrate on training for the outdoor season. His present plans include returning for the European circuit this summer and he hopes to be able to help us in our fight for promotion to the British League.
