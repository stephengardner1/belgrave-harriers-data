EDITORIAL

Congratulations! Ray Middleton. Our leading walker continued the tradition of Belgravians being amongst Games walking medals by winning a well-earned "silver" in the 20 miles walk at the British Empire and Commonwealth Games in Jamaica. His usual steady start left him in 5th place at the 9 miles mark. "I then knew I was in with a good chance for a medal" he said, "I made my effort, moved through and I knew I'd got one". It all sounded so simple. What he didn't say was how he felt about getting within 30 seconds of the winner, team mate Ron Wallwork! As a medal winner,Ray now joins that illustrious pair of Belgravians Tommy Green (Olympic Gold) and Stan Vickers (Olympic Bronze and European Gold). He must also be hottest favourite for this year's"Tommy Green" Cup award.

Whilst appreciating Middleton's grand performance, a thought should be spared for another Belgravian who narrowly missed going to Jamaica and whose performances this season have put him in World class. We speak of Geoff North. Geoff scored a fine win in the Inter-counties 3 miles in a Championship Best Performance time of 13 minutes 18.8 seconds and deservedly represented Britain against U.S.S.R. Two disappointing performances afterwards were just sufficient to squeeze him out by the narrowest of margins. With next season to consolidate his new form, he stands every chance of renewing his acquaintance with the Mexican senoritas in 1968! We are sure he can do it.

Meanwhile our thoughts remain with Ray Middleton, who will shortly carry our flag again in the European Championships at Budapest, where the competition will be much tougher.

HON SEC'S NOTES

A.A.H.

We were indeed sorry to learn of Jack Crump's death in April and Athletics has lost one of its greatest workers. In his very early days Jack, with his brother Bob, was a member of Belgrave until they decided to throw in their lot with a club nearer their home, London Vidarians W.C.

Some months ago Jack confided to the writer that he had been inspired and encouraged throughout his long career in athletics by the thought of some of our earlier Belgrave enthusiasts - men like Parker, Belchamber and Horwood - who had impressed him in those early 1920 days with their love of the sport and their Club.

Belgrave owed much to Jack Crump's efforts in so many ways. He was always very welcome at our Meetings and functions, and always helpful to us. We mourn his passing.

We have recently been advised that one of our oldest Life-Members died earlier this year on the eve of his 91st birthday. F.A.Bool was Hon.Secretary of the Club from 1898 to 1903 and was made a Life-Member in 1905. He lived in Chelsea all his life and his daughter tells us that he followed the fortunes of Belgrave until the end.

A copy of a Club News Sheet issued in May 1901 has recently been sent to us - and this was written and issued by the above mentioned F.A.Bool. It tells of the fine entry of 30 for the Club race of the month, a half-mile handicap, held, of course, on the road along the Embankment. 27 runners started and the winner proved to be W.Berridge, off 35 yards. Scratch man was an old friend of many of us, the late George Still.

One item reads "members will be pleased to hear that Sgt.J.Cox is on his way home from the front"!

We are indebted to the son of W.Berridge for this News Letter, found among his fathers papers.

All members should note the formal Notice of Annual General Meeting, given on another page, as required by ruling of our constitution. No other notice of this Meeting will be circulated. We hope for the usual good attendance of members to demonstrate the vitality of the Club and the interest of its members in all its activities.

As a Club with freehold Headquarters to maintain and improve, and a record of competition in Championships and other events wider than that of any other club, we must give thought to MONEY .

We do ask for (a) the prompt payment of Subscriptions, which become due at the Annual General Meeting, (b) generous donations from our Life-Members, Vice-Presidents and others, (c) support of our excellent and rewarding Football Scheme details of which may be had from our Hon.Treasurer and (d) full blooded co-operation with Frank Webb in his efforts on behalf of the Club when he circulates details of our Derby and Christmas Draws.

Greetings to all members come from Bernard Eglington now serving in Malta where between duties he is acting as "talent-spotter" for Belgrave.

George Piddington's imminent move into Hampshire will rob us (partially at any rate) of one who has made great efforts for our Boys and for coaching within the Club. We do thank him most sincerely for all his hard work and honest endeavour,and we hope to see him quite regularly in the future.

Our revered Harry Evans, now in his 86th year, has had a patch of ill-health, but is now happily recovering. He was able to be with us at the Club Track and Field Championships and again at our Inter-Club Walking Competition at Battersea Park. Harry's regularity of attendance at our races, both Summer and Winter, is proverbial; and an example to younger generations of officials and athletes.

A Club Champion of former days (1927 and 1928 to be explicit) Jim Cooper Clark showed up recently and promptly requested re-election. We hope to see him frequently at our events although he no longer moves with the speed that astounded us at one time !

Incidently we do like to keep in touch with former members; and welcome them as Honorary or full members, so if you know of any, sign them on the dotted line !

When this copy of the "Belgravian" reaches you, we shall be about to begin a new winter season. Can our cross-country runners reach a peak next March to win the English Championship? It could be. We certainly have the men, and if all could and would give of their best at the same time, the title could once again be Belgraves.

Our walkers haven't the same strength in depth as obtained in recent years but with the return of Eric Hall to the South, and with the help of our two other Internationals in Ray Middleton and Ray Hall, we hope to remedy affairs and build up as powerful a team as ever we have known in the past. What about it Stan Vickers ? We understand there's no surplus weight!

A corner to acknowledge George Beecham's solid worth to our walking teams over many years. Again this year he scored in our winning team for the NIJMEGEN Shield (teams of eight), the ninth occasion he has done so.

ROAD RELAY ROUND-UP

3 FIRSTS — 2 SECONDS — 1 FIFTH

What an anti-climax the 1966 Spring road relay season proved to be ! The loss of the revered National London - Brighton road relay knocked the bottom out of the usual "spring spirit" and most leading club men were unable to conjure up the enthusiasm for events that were once used as valuable pointers to Brighton form. The excitement of seeing two dozen athletes fighting to prove their worthiness for inclusion in the select twelve was, alone, an occasion worth waiting for. But this year there was nothing at the end of it and the short season seemed to fizzle out like a damp squib. Perhaps next year a new form of National road relay will help to remedy the situation.

As usual, the Ilford and Southampton/Bournemouth relays clashed the week following the National cross country championships. Last year we stretched our resources in an endeavour to win both, but although taking the Southampton event by a 2½ minutes margin could only finish a close 4th at Ilford. Re-arranged teams could certainly have won both. Because of this we attempted to do the same this year but finished one place further down in each event; both teams being affected by injuries and Thresher, Hart and Reilly all being unavailable. At Ilford, Clive Shippen and Gordon Biscoe were called up into the 'A' team and this alone meant the difference between 1st and 5th places. It was good to see Ranjit Bhatia showing some of his old form again and with Jack Brown and Pat Newell also putting in useful appearances the team was not unlike those of 7 or 8 years ago.

At Southampton Chris Steer produced some of his best running to date, being within 3 seconds of fastest time on stage 2 and taking us from 7th to 3rd. Gerry North was also in great form, taking nearly a minute off Hyman, and Belgrave to within 20 seconds of the lead. Despite the acquisition of Dai Davies the Welsh Champion, and fastest stage times by Wiggs and Ellis, our old rivals TVH could still only get within 47 seconds of us at the finish.

At the Cambridge Harriers relay at Bexley, a great effort by Geoff North (who for the second successive year shared the fastest time of the day) helped Belgrave to a good win over the two teams who pipped us on the final stage last year - Blackheath and SLH.

The following week we beat both Portsmouth and TVH at Cranford in the "Valley's" annual promotion but a full strength Bristolian quintet rose above their Bournemouth form to beat all three of their former conquerors. Geoff North was second fastest of the day, ahead of Strong,Tulloh and Wiggs, and Adrian Major proved himself capable of holding his own in any of our first teams by turning in a notable sub-17 minute run.

Our best outing was undoubtedly at Blackpool where we won against tough opposition for the 3rd consecutive year. In 1964 and 1965 the race was for teams of six runners but this year the number was reduced to four and it was felt that this would tend to favour our rivals. However, which ever runners we pair with the North brothers it seems to make little difference and this time Steer and Fairclough paved the way for Geoff and Gerry to clinch another win. Of the remaining runners Bill Dance was the fastest. The rate of this man's improvement since joining us is most gratifying and he has surely now run himself into our 'A' teams.

The road relay season closed on April 30th with the Boro' of Enfield relay, which resulted in another victory for the BELS in record time. Our 'B' team were 5th, and with Bill Kerr doing a great 17:46 "come back" and Bill Dance anchoring in 17:55 we had two men faster than Major and Steer in the 'A' team

So with three wins in six races our runners showed they were in their usual tip-top Spring form. Had there been a "Brighton" this year the selectors would have had their usual difficult task, but the ironical thing is that this year the collective form of our best twelve runners was so much better than our leading rivals. If the abandonment of the race had been postponed for a year we would have had an excellent chance of being the first and last holders of the National Relay Title (1951 and 1966).

GEOFF WINS WIMBLEDON CUP

'5¾' victory in record time

At one minute to 3 o'clock on April 2nd 1966 the Club Record for the 5¾ miles road race championship stood at 27 mins 15 secs. - credited to Gerry North. During those tense moments before the 50 starters poured down Lauriston Road, one athlete appeared to be extraordinarily on edge - even wanting to know the exact position of the start line; it was plain to see he really meant business this day, for when it was suggested he might like someone to knock in some starting blocks for him, it failed to raise even the merest smile. When Geoff North reacts this way he is either about to cane all and sundry or, less frequently, has just been caned; as he had not yet run, it became apparent to everyone that he was gunning for brother Gerry's record - an excellent time, set a few weeks after the elder North had won the National Cross-Country title in 1962.

No sooner had Bob Taylor's starting arm dropped than Geoff was clear of the field and 10 yards to the good at the end of the road. After the two small laps had been completed and the Copse Hill stretch approached, North was 100 yards clear and still going away. He was in great form, and providing he could overcome the hills on the return journey, stood a fair chance of clipping the record. At this point a group of five (Hart, Dabbs, Major, O'Hara and Fairclough) were being led along by Chris Steer. Chris and young Adrian Major looked in fine fettle but Laurie O'Hara was showing the effects of recent 'flu'.

Geoff kept his form well on the homeward run and a "break it or bust" sprint down the finishing straight sent up an "ooh!" from the watchers that, momentarily, did not indicate which side of the record his time was. Relief all round (Gerry of course wasn't there!) as it was announced that Geoff had beaten the record by just ONE SECOND!

His fast pace had really kept the others at it and the battle reached its climax up Cambridge Road and the steep "kiss of death" where Adrian Major romped past Fairclough, Steer and O'Hara like a 2yrs old only to find the experienced Dabbs and Hart just out of reach. After a hard fight over the last mile Trevor got the advantage over Chas. and Adrian clung to his well earned 4th place.

The field had certainly stretched out, but never before have we had as many as eight men under 28:30. With Gerry North, Thresher and Barrow not running (besides other possibles like Newell, Reilly, Dance and Kerr) this surely would have produced our fastest "Brighton" team.

Once again veteran Jack Brown put in another of his stirling performances, shaking up many first team aspirants with his 29:27; and Ranjit Bhatia made the most of his years stay in England by re-appearing in the race for which he once held the record (27:56 in 1960). Another distinguished record-holder to re-appear was Ron Linstead whose 28:25, set 12 years ago when he was 17, stood for 5 years.

Geoff.North's fast run also earnt him 3rd place in the handicap - Chris Fairfield having risen above his usual form to take first prize and Adrian Major a well earned second.

25 YEARS AGO....

From the BELGRAVIAN June 1941:

Previous to the winter 1935/36 Frank Elson had run a small competition in which one forecast how many goals would be scored in the first and sedond divisions of the English Leagues. Each forecast cost one penny and the prizes began at 10/- and then increased to £1 as the scheme grew. This, though seemingly a small thing, proved to be a very valuable addition to our "Building Fund" ........ a committee was formed ........; so it was that in season 1935/36 a very large competition was launched. ....... our wildest dreams were surpassed and the future, as well as the present, Belgravians will owe much to those who so heartily supported us in those days.

As soon as this bad dream (the war) has ended and we get back to civilian life, we are going all out to put Belgrave in such a financial position that we will have the finest headquarters of any club, equipped with baths and showers, indoor excercise facilities, massage room,etc.,etc. and all our very own, by our own efforts. That independence is very dear to all Belgravians and we shall insist on maintaining it.

Chris Martin 2nd in SURREY CO. CHAMPS.

May 21st - Motspur Park

A great display of sprinting was put up by Chris Martin, our only Senior sprinter in the Championships, the climax coming when he chased the international Mel Cheskin (Poly) home in the final of the 220yds. Having already run in the heat and final of the 100yds (10.5/2nd - 10.4/5th) Chris was drawn in the outside lane of the furlong with Cheskin right on the inside. At the gun it was obvious that nobody would be able to touch the Polytechnic Harrier as he steamed through the field to win by 10yds in a new record but Chris fought off the rest to finish a brilliant 2nd in 22.2 - his best ever time.

John Thresher was 5th in the mile, having only been training in spikes for little more than a week. With a bit of luck he should be in top line for the major championship meetings later in the season. Seventh was our 2nd claim Poly member Derek Fernee with 4:15.1 .

The 3 miles was a very tactical affair with no one wishing to take the lead and the field bunching up to three or four deep. After 3½ laps of this Gerry North shot to the front and within 220yds the field was strung right out - the first mile being reached in 4:36. Once again the leading pack slowed up and Roberts (SLH) took the initiative with Bob Holt (Hercules) and Robinson (Achilles) close behind. With all this chopping and changing the inevitable happened and unhappily, Mick Gowan (HHH) was badly spiked and had to drop out. At 2 miles (9:13.8) Gerry took the lead again and this time only Robinson followed. Adrian Major, running steadily near the rear of the field improved his best 2 mile time yet again with 9:32. One and a half laps left and Robinson made his effort and although Gerry closed in the final straight, he had to let his title go to the athlete who so nearly won it last year. Adrian tired over the last couple of laps but ran in a good 9th with another fine effort.

CLUB CHAMPIONSHIPS

June 4th - Hurlingham Park

Saturday, 4th June at the Hurlingham Park Track saw the Belgrave Club Championships Meeting. There was a welcome return of the children's races and this together with the customary efficiency of our "top brass" officials created the ideal setting for our best Championships in years. Blessed with fine weather, although the wind was quite gusty, blowing up clouds of dust from the track, there was a good turn-out of spectators and athletes.

It was obvious we were in for an exciting afternoon when with his very first jump in the first event, Bob Jones upped the Champ.Best with a Long Jump of 23'2½". No sooner had the commotion died down when we were informed by the announcer that Bob, on his second leap, had improved again - this time to the winning mark of 23'5½". Derek Wade held 2nd place for most of the competition until John Mitchell, unable to produce his best form, displaced him with a 22 footer.

Chris Martin started a busy couple of hours by chasing home John in the 100yds, later reversing the result in the furlong. Both were very close races. The next event on his schedule was the quarter-mile for which there were heats. By the time the final came around he was practically dead on his feet but his turn of speed around the final bend was still too much for Bob Nelson and Alan Mead, so he gained himself a second 'pot'.

In the Javelin, Brian King threw over 9'0" further than before to beat Colin Clair by only 1" with Barry Townsend 3rd. Brian's magnificent effort just halted Colin's bid for a treble as he took the discus and shot, both with best distances by Belgravians so far this year.

Geoff North, after one false and one very doubtful start, made all the running in the 880yds, finishing well ahead of Dick Calderwood and Lionel Mann, both of whom had excellent runs. John Bicourt, who ran in 4th, had decided in view of his injury sustained in the Inter-Counties,not to compete in the Steeplechase leaving Alan Black to go it alone for a Meeting Record of 9:30.4 . Bill Dance was 2nd in exactly 10 minutes with John Dear close behind.

Another solo effort for a record was John Thresher's very even paced mile of 4:13.2 . The battle for the positions was much closer, however, Adrian Major fading towards the end with Pat Newell and John Bicourt both getting away from him.

Bob Jones made it a double by winning the Triple Jump from Bob Couillaut and Barry Townsend was an inch too good for Derek Wade in the high jump.

SPOTLIGHT ON C. T. CARTER

C.S.

The scene: Battersea Park Track. The year : 1930. A young blond runner has just finished lapping the track several times and pauses for a walk. His stylish running has not gone un-noticed and a small man with a walking stick hobbles up and enquires "Do you belong to a Club ?" The rosy cheeked youth stops, wondering whether the fellow means football club, youth club or Christmas club. "Er, not a running club,why ?" he replies. The enquirer screws his eyes up yet again, leans heavily on his stick and says "Well you ought to take up running properly, and join one now". This conversation heralded the association of two men whose names were synonymous with Belgrave in the 30's and 40's. The man with the stick was Bert Footer, Belgrave's greatest ever Captain (then injured) and the fair headed novice Tom Carter - the subject of this article.

Tom Carter was born on 18th May 1908 in Putney and was raised, under the care of his grandparents, in Chelsea. Although his grandfather's brother had been a member of Ranelagh Harriers and a contemporary of the well known Sid Thomas, Tom had no aspirations towards athletics. In fact the only competitive event he took part in at school was a walking race, which he duly won !

At the end of 1922 he left school and was apprenticed to a joiner. Keen on football, he belonged to a youth club where he played a game every Saturday and Sunday, soon becoming an established team player.

During these early years Tom also became a proficient ballroom dancer and five times a week danced his way into the night. His urge for competition gained him many successes, but these events were few and far between then and Tom was obliged to engineer his way into closed events. On one occasion he reached the final of the London British Legion Championships, but was disqualified when it was discovered that none of his relatives were members !

Dancing, Football and walking were his main preoccupations and it was not until 1928 that his athletic ability became apparent. The occasion was the Sports Day for Chelsea Youth Clubs, and on the strength of Tom's lasting ability on the soccer pitch his rector suggested he represented the Club in the mile. Not one for half measures, Tom borrowed a pair of spikes,("a little too big") from a work-mate and dutifully appeared at the meeting. After two laps he lost a shoe, but he continued to "just follow the leaders" until the final straight, when he came thro' to win feeling highly elated and none too concerned about his blistered foot. He had won his first mile in fraction under 5 minutes. This display of natural ability led his club friends to persuade him to "gather an odd point" in the 440 yards. Strapping his shoes on more securely this time,he sprinted to the first bend, got the inside berth (no lanes then) and held off the opposition all the way round to score a second win. Carter's canniness had already made it's mark But, most important, these results encouraged Tom to do a little training.

During this year he joined the "Young Imperials", soon becoming their Social Secretary, a position for which his extrovert sense of humour was well suited.

It was at the London Sports Day of the Young Imperials that Tom first rubbed shoulders with a Belgravian - our own Norman Dewberry, who a few days earlier had been placed in the Belgrave 1 mile Championship. Once again Tom produced a final flourish, passing Norman in the straight to finish second and go forward with the winner to the All England Championship at the Crystal Palace. Tom was not over-awed by this premier event and, rising to the occasion, finished 4th, beating the London first string and clocking 4:46 - an excellent time for a novice, particularly in 1929.

Further encouraged, Tom began to visit Battersea Park track and it was here that he met the "little man with the stick", as previously related.

He was not sure which club to join, if one at all; but under some pressure from Tommy Scrimshaw and Ernie Duffett he decided on Belgrave, "mainly because I liked their vest".

On 29th August 1930 Tom Carter was formally elected a member of Belgrave Harriers - a date of great significance for the Club. Only a few weeks after joining he made his mark. Off only 40 seconds in the Yacht 3 miles handicap he finished second! From then on he went from strength to strength, and in 1934 ran his first "National"; Belgrave finishing 3rd and Tom 95th. The following year he was walking on air; a "National" team-winners medal was his, thanks to his 19th position; but there was still the little matter of the four Belgravians that finished in front! In 1936 he put it right by being first Belgrave man home (12th) and gaining a place in the International team

The successes of his long career are too numerous to mention in full. Suffice it to say that wherever Belgrave went Carter went also, and the Club was all-conquering in the 1933-48 era. He obtained his Surrey County Colours at the first attempt and represented the County over the country on 19 occasions. During one of these trips to the"Inter-Counties" an argument developed between the late Jack Crump (team manager) and George Pallett (then Herne Hill) over which of two runners to drop from the team. Tom who was Captain on this occasion, heard all the arguments for and against, and with typical unselfishness stepped in and insisted on dropping himself as he was not yet back to his best after injury.

Footer and Penny were his close rivals for most of the time, but Tom is the proud owner of the rare AAA vest of early design, which neither of the others managed to obtain - and he still raises a chuckle over this..Many of our present active members will be surprised to learn that Carter actually turned down his first ever invite from the AAA because it clashed with an important Club event. He is sorry to see the present clamour for "over-rated" races outside Club fixtures and firmly believes that many"stars" are losing their sense of values. It is fascinating to hear him reminisce and to relate anecdotes concerning innumerable Belgravians, but the one for whom he had most admiration was Bert Footer,"his great determination and personal example put our Club on the map".

In 1938 Tom married Ethel the girl who had keenly supported him and the Club for many years. It is an indication of their keenness that, because of a mix-up in dates and the clash of their wedding day with the well known Plymouth track meeting, they (?) put their wedding back a day!

It was not long after this that Europe became embroiled in war, and the organisation of athletics took a severe knock. Tom had now become a proficient mill-wright and being in a totally reserved occupation was able to help hold together those few members that were left behind. Despite difficulties with the "blitz" and "black-out" he still kept training, and in season 1940/41 had a remarkable sequence of nine wins in nine weeks, generally against the best opposition in the South. His coverage in the newspapers increased week by week. The run of wins was broken on the 10th outing thro' going off course and failing to get back to the front by a mere 2 seconds.

Eventually the war ended and the 1945/46 season found Tom running as well as ever. The "National" was staged once more, with Belgrave defending champions after their win in 1939. Carter had won the Southern title and was expected to make the International team quite easily but, as in 1937 when he was also tipped for a place, he contracted 'flu', ill-advisedly ran, and finished 16th. Nevertheless he had helped Belgrave to retain their title and it was this that mattered most to him.

What were his most satisfying moments? Well,there were many, but one was undoubtedly being a scoring member of our last winning National team (1948) at the age of 40 years. He continued to make our National teams for two further years, but it was perhaps symbolic that as Tom faded so did our team (6th in 1949, 8th in 1950). The second experience he most likes to recount was his win in the "Southern" of 1946. He was invincible at this time, particularly over our own course where the race was held, but he had heard that Doug Wilson (Poly) was planning to run one lap only and use his well known miling speed to break Tom and let in Bingham (Finchley) - Carter was always a very fast starter and would not ease up until he had got clear of the field. Doug Wilson remembers to this day how Tom went out and ran him off his feet after 2 miles - going on to win the title by 32 seconds.

The retirement of Carter from competition coincided with the start of a ten-year lean spell for our runners. It was particularly galling for Tom to witness this, having been a key figure for so long during Belgrave's superiority. He resolved to do something about it personally and embarked on a scheme to nurture a group of boys and youths and to coach them through to Senior status. He was elected Junior Captain and in 1956 became our President. The first of his pupils included Fairclough, Steer and Cocks, who have since proved their worth in the Senior ranks.

Tom's coaching school is still going strong at Battersea Park,where he appears each Tuesday to give them their week's programme. He enjoys being amongst our young members and its quite apparent that they like him to be there too - not just so they can be one of the dozen bodies that are frequently transported home in his car, but because he is young at heart, has a sense of humour, and most of all, understands them.

Tom is now in his 59th year (though he looks 10 years younger) and has a business of his own. His bubbling enthusiasm and Carterian humour are as active now as they ever were, whether it be at track or committee room. He still does a little running, but that irrepressible competitive urge now has to be satiated by the frustrations of golf!

It is 36 years since that fair headed youngster met Bert Footer at Battersea track - Belgrave Harriers have been richer for every one of them.

WALKING

What a great effort by Ray Middleton (see Editorial). His Empire Games silver medal was a just reward for his performances this season and, dare we say it, symbolic of his runner-up record this year. Every time Ray beats the race favourites, someone else seems to pop up and steal the limelight. In the R.W.A. 20 miles he beat Wallwork and Thompson, only for Norman Read of New Zealand to beat him by just half a minute; in the R.W.A. 50 kilos he cracked Nihill, only for Thompson to come through and win; and to crown it all in Jamaica he beat both his former victors, only for Wallwork to take his turn at winning. Altogether this happened on eight occasions between April 20th and August 6th, the victors being Nihill (twice), Read (twice), Thompson, McCullagh, Brooks and Wallwork. Everyone,except Wallwork so far, has been soundly beaten by Ray since their wins. Ray will not have Ronnie along side him in Budapest so will have to wait for an opportunity to continue the trend; perhaps he could try willing himself to believe that every competitor in Budapest is Wallwork - you never know!

Ray Middleton had his usual steady start to the season and, in the four "National" races, improved with each performance - clinching his Empire and European Games places. After the 10 miles, 20 kilos and 20 miles races it looked as if Belgrave were in danger of a complete shut-out for medals in 1966. Team positions of 5th, 4th and 4th did not auger well for the 50 kilos on July 23rd, but rising to the occasion on an oppressively hot day our boys romped home to an excellent win, placing 7 in 23. Middleton was still working up to peak form when the 10 miles and 20 kilos were held, but he began to come into his own on June 11th when the R.W.A. 20 miles was held in the Midlands. Holding the leaders to within a minute for three-quarters of the race, he made his effort over the final 5 miles, permitting only Norman Read the Olympic Gold Medallist to head him home. Some felt that if he'd made his effort earlier he would have clinched a win - who can tell ? There were "ifs and buts" also at Chigwell Row after the 50 kilos, but no-one begrudged the diminutive Don Thompson his win. Jack Goswell is as shrewd a tactician as any and he advised Ray to go out gunning for Nihill right from the start, as he knew that the S.W.C. man was not himself when under pressure It worked. Nihill stretched his lead to 43 seconds at 10 kilos, lost it over the next 10 kilos, and was 75 seconds down at the 30 kilos mark; Thompson being over a minute further behind. It was here that Ray began to blister badly, and his blood soaked sock at the finish was adequate evidence of the courage he had shown during the final stages when he had to fight against pain, the effects of his fast start and his old rival Thompson. Compensation was provided to the full though by our team's great win. HOW I TRAINED

Here is a summary of a debate on the merits of strolling vis-a-vis stripped training.

The main arguments appeared to be whether strolling should take precedence over stripped training and how fast the strolling should be. Our Brighton cup-holder confesses to a preference for speeds of well over five miles per hour. The ex-holder favoured Alf Pope's preference for a steady 4¾ mph (13 minute miles) in which he is supported by Harold Whitlock.

Other and equally important considerations which might have been discussed had time permitted are:- (a) That strolling training can be done from home and is much less likely to attract small children, dogs and offers of a lift, and (b) that it is not always easy for a family man to find time for all day strolling.

Those out to reach Brighton in the first three must of course study seriously the strolling school plan, but those whose aim is as yet less ambitious may take comfort from the fact that most of the men who walked in our team last September, which was certainly the finest eight we have ever fielded, did most of their preparation in stripped training and racing over shorter distances, with very little high speed or long distance strolling.

BELGRAVE '20' — TAYLOR SMASHES RECORD.

TAYLOR SMASHES RECORD.

Those that showed surprise last year when Bob Roath chopped our course record down to 1:45:53 were simply astounded on July 16th this year when 21 years old Graham Taylor of Cambridge Harriers re-wrote the record book with a truly outstanding 1:39:47. This sub 1:40 effort (equivalent to sub 5 minute miles throughout) came just 10 years after the first sub 1:50 performance round this course. What then in 1976?!

Taylor was in a class of his own and his performance will have done our promotion a lot of good. He had a 1:10 lead after one lap, 2:59 after two and a winning margin of 3:42 at the end of the three lap course. His lap times were 32:49, 32:49 and 34:09 - with 2 miles of "stitch" on the final circuit!

Conditions were cool and windy when our President, Bob Taylor, sent the 108 starters on their way and for 2½ miles a sizeable group held the lead; including our Charlie Dabbs. It was at this point that Graham Taylor set off on his lonely way, stretching out the field behind him. Dabbs passed through the first lap in 8th position with 35:06 and was still holding his place a lap later after a second circuit in 36:23. Chris Steer was still well placed after two laps in 35:30 and 37:13 but did not look happy when embarking on his final lap. Our potential third scorer was Doug Brew, running in his first ever '20' and with laps of 37:38 and 38:28 he was sufficiently well up for us to be leading a close fight for second team place.

A lot happened on the final lap. Cambridge Harriers edged up to take second team place and Ranelagh 'B' simply raced through the field to snatch third. For Belgrave, Charlie Dabbs held his form well to finish 6th in Club Record time, but Chris and Doug dropped nearly 20 places each to put us out of the team reckoning.

Charlie Dabbs took the "Lady Fell" shield for the first time but he must have had quite a fright; for Bill Dance joined the race just after the start and made his way through the field to finish an unofficial 6th in 1:47:36 . But for missing out that first 50 yards he would have undoubtedly been Club Champion at his first attempt.

Once again Gordon Biscoe handled the race arrangements, and once again everything went off very well.

BROCKMAN TROPHY — COLIN CLAIR EQUALS JAVELIN RECORD

July 16th - Wimbledon Park

The 18th Annual H.G.Brockman Trophy meeting was held in cold, showery conditions at the Wimbledon Park track. Our field events section continued the trend started a fortnight ago at the 'Ryder' by scoring 27 points to our track men's 15 points. Hurdles events (11 points) not being included in either score. Our overall position was an improvement on the 1965 result by three places and 31 points.

Prior to the Javelin competition Colin Clair had joked about the possibility of his gaining a AAA Grade I Standard. No one was more suprised than he when on his 5th effort the spear reached well over the 200' line and was later measured at 210'2" - a standard and Club Honours Badge by 2" . The most amazing thing was that his other throws were all around his normal distance of 170'- 180'. This one was completely out of the blue. He was placed 3rd behind Travis (Surrey) and Fitzsimmons (Poly) and ahead of R.Lane (Croydon) 204'2" and Colin Smith (TVH) 199'1" .

In the sprints Chris Martin ran only one-tenth slower than his best in the final of the furlong, won by Steane (TVH) with 21.5, and also finished 4th in the heats of the 100yds in 10.4 . Tony Brooks clocked 51.7 and finished 2nd in his heat of the 440yds but could only manage 52.6 and 5th in the final, 45 minutes later.

Our high hurdler Pete Pepe was 4th behind G.Tymms (Surrey) 14.8, Howell (HHH) 15.7 and Wilmshurst (Walton) 15.9 and was unlucky to be given 16.0 when he was inseparable with the 3rd man.

The final result was not certain until the last event, the Javelin, was finished and here Brian King did his duty by beating the nearest Blackheath rival by 4 feet.

The sprints were run into a stiff wind and the times suffered considerably but Jim Vivian romped home in both, looking most impressive in the furlong where he beat a good man in Shurmer of the 'Heath. Bob Couillaut and Lyle Moar filled first two places in the Hop, Step and Jump, held yet again into the wind. These two should both be heading for 45 feet with a little more help from the weather. In the mile race, Chris Wade(LAC) led all the way with Alan Black lying 2nd and a member of Blackheath 3rd. The LAC runner piled on the pressure over the last lap and beat Alan by 10yds but the 3rd man faded and it looked really impressive as Belgrave men streamed past him in the final 100yds to fill 2nd, 3rd, 4th and 5th places.

HERE AND THERE

With so much competition available outside Club fixtures it has been a rare sight to see more than 2 or 3 of our middle and long-distance men at one time. The better runners get these days the quicker they are to plan their season around the multitude of so-called "Invitation" races that are fired, as from a shot gun, right through the Club's fixture lists each year. These races are now so frequent that one wonders whether it will soon go full cycle and be "the trend" to appear at Club fixtures !

It is presumed that our road relay performers do occasionally meet each other during the summer, so it is hoped that many of them will get together during the latter part of the season to prepare for an intense Autumn Road Season (you can always ease off during November!). The fixtures are as follows.

September 10th - Surrey Road Relay (Woking) September 17th - Blackheath Road Relay (Hayes) / Club 5¾ miles road race (non-championship) September 25th (Sun) - "Johnstone" Open Road Relay, 12 stages (Hornchurch) October 1st - Walton Road Relay / Club 3 miles Yacht Handicap October 8th - BELGRAVE H & S.C.A.A.A. ROAD RELAY, 12 stages October 22nd - Bristol — Weston Road Relay

Excluded from the March issue of the "Belgravian" was the result of the International Cross-Country Championship. Although he did not quite repeat his "National" form Gerry North once again scored for the winning England team being 6th scorer in 19th position.

On the following Saturday Gerry was the third Englishman to finish in the Martini cross-country classic in Brussells, his 11th place being 46 secs. behind Khlistov (USSR).

Another late cross-country result was the Junior Inter-County race , in which John Bicourt (15th) and Adrian Major (22nd) were the first two Surrey men to finish.

Ranjit Bhatia is endeavouring to collect material, in connection with a book he is hoping to have published next year, on the life of Dr. Otto Peltzer, the controversial German distance runner of the 1920's, as a tribute to his 10 years work in India as an outstanding coach.

If any of our members have any published information or personal recollections of Otto Peltzer the Editors will gladly pass on to Ranjit the details, however minor they may seem.

It was the day after Geoff North's excellent win in the Inter-Counties '3'. The Sunday morning pack were dressing after their showers and were very full of their activities of the previous night. Geoff listened. His happy face slowly saddened, as he said with a sigh "With all this dedication I can't even remember when I last stayed up to see the dawn break"____ Tough!

John Thresher produced his best ever 5,000 metres performance when he raced in a special invitation event in Vienna on July 14th. Conditions were atrocious and the track waterlogged. Ron Clark was the winner in the slow time (for him) of 13:41.4; Mecsar and Kiss of Hungary were 2nd and 3rd in 13:55 and 13:59 respectively. John was still with Kiss with 600 metres to go but was unable to hold his own in the final rush for the line. Vasic (Yugoslavia), Gerlach(W.Germany), Coyle (Australia) and Dick Taylor (Coventry) all passed him and John finished in 14:05.8; 4.8 seconds behind Taylor and only fractionally outside the European Games qualifying standard.

Latest news of Mike Shingles now happily married in New Zealand is that he is enjoying the life out there and is now back in full training. He certainly won't be able to get the 'Bels' out of his system as he is now employed as a fireman in Wanganui. Mike adds that he and Beverley are expecting their first little 'Kiwi' at the end of this year - shows you what speed training can do!!

Following the photograph in the last'Belgravian' of Arthur Bruce doing some 'shady' training with Charlie Smart, it has now been reported that Arthur has bought himself a new club vest - "My first since 1945", as well as new road shoes. Those who are used to Arthur's normal immaculate appearance are not sure whether this is a sign of a determined'come back' or just to help keep Gerry North in business. (Cont'd on p.37)

HERE AND THERE (Cont'd from p.38)

Thresher had a second B.A.A.B. trip to the Continent on July 30th.This time to La Baulle, a popular sea-side resort in France. Accompanying him was Geoff North. It was intended that John would run in the 1500 metres and Geoff in the 3,000, but on arrival it was found there was no 1500 only a 1000. John plumped for the 3,000, where our boys clashed with the redoubtable Michel Jazy. Geoff led the field for 2½ laps and then John took over, dropping all except Jazy. With 800 metres to go the Frenchman sprinted away to an easy win with Thresher 2nd in 8:14.0 and North 4th in 8:27.0 .

CLUB 6

Not out for a fast time, Gerry North was content to tuck in behind the leading pack for 4½ miles and then do a solo for home. Joe Reilly did the donkey work for the first 3 miles, with Alan Black then taking over. When North broke away Reilly, Fairclough and O'Hara chased hard, dropping Alan in the process, but Black recovered well and showing great determination ran through again only to fail by a mere inch to add a "silver" to his 3 miles "gold". Jack Brown went through the field like a dose of salts with 3 mile splits of 15:40 and 15:31 to beat Ranjit Bhatia who was competing in his last track race before leaving again for India in October.
