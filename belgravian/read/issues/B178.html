EDITORIAL

Yes, believe it or not, you are looking at another edition of THE BELGRAVIAN. Our gazette has survived many difficult periods during it's existence these last 47 years, and the last 18 months has been no exception. This is not the time, however, to dwell on those difficulties. Let us instead tell you of the interesting prospects in store.

First, we are delighted to announce that Alan Mead has offered to take on the Editorship of the gazette. Alan played a part in many editions during the 1960's and his experience and enthusiasm will surely produce a publication to which we can all look forward. The magazine is intended to reflect the results, news and views of the whole membership, and any contributions the membership can make towards a better magazine will be welcomed by the new Honorary Editor at 17, High Ashton, Kingston Hill, Kingston, Surrey.

Second, the first edition of the new Editorship will be the December issue, which it is hoped will be in your hands by the end of that month. So you will have the relative luxury of two gazettes in the span of a month - this issue having been delayed until November.

This edition has not been produced in the form we would have liked. With the need to condense the material for five issues into one much editting was necessary. This was done with the two principal functions of our magazine in mind. They are 1. To distribute Club news of a wide range to the whole membership and thus maintain contact with our distant and non-active members 2. To provide a comprehensive record of Club activities.

We believe that when it becomes impossible to achieve both of these aims then the latter takes priority. We would therefore ask you to bear with us when ploughing your way through the many pages of historical results and hope that you will find the December issue a refreshing contrast.

Alan Mead

Editorial · page 3

Our belated congratulations go out to three Belgravians who achieved selection for the 1972 Olympic Games in Munich.

JOHN BICOURT, who for much of the season was Britain's No 2 steeplechaser, was selected for the 3000 metres steeplechase. Despite a time of 8:38.8 in his heat he was eliminated, as were the other two Britons in this event.

WIL WESCH, who was the life and soul of Belgrave walking teams last year, is now back in Germany. His efforts during the year earned him a place in West Germany's 20 kilometres walk team. Wil finished 16th in 1:35:20.6.

CLIFF BROOKS has shown a marked improvement in his all-round abilities these last two years. His efforts were rewarded by selection for the Olympic decathlon by his native Barbados. Cliff was going well at Munich until the third event on the second day - the pole vault. He was unable to achieve a height, along with many competitors of note (Gabbett, GB, was 4th when eliminated at the vault), and ceased competition with a 7 event score of 4842 (11.19, 22'-4¼", 38'-5", 5'-8.7/8", 49.8, 16.37, 111'-2½").

We are indeed proud of all three.

Spare a thought, though, for the man who has served his Club and Country so well in the past, and so very nearly did so again -- if he had been allowed to.

RAY MIDDLETON was put forward by the R.W.A. as third string for the 50 kilos walk, but only two names were selected by the British Board. Just as it seemed that RAY would fill the vacant third spot the Board announced that Paul Nihill would "double up" and do the 50 kilos as well as the 20 kilos.

They are just the bare facts, but they are enough for us to accord Ray our every sympathy. He also has our admiration for the way he overcame his disappointment and the temptation to retire from competition, and resumed training to carry our colours to the fore once again.

PAST PRESIDENTS' PARS 1971

A.A.H.

I am very grateful to the Club for the honour they accorded me when I became President for the second time, in 1970-71. I have enjoyed my extra year in office, and now look forward to making my contribution from the main body of the Committee, instead of from the platform as for so many years past. It will not be easy to accustom myself to changed circumstances, but no doubt the new President and new Hon. Secretaries will be indulgent towards an old-stager!

There is no doubt that as a Club the Belgrave Harriers are in good heart - the spirit of service to our Sport and our own particular organisation is as good as ever it was. I do confess, however, that I would like to see rather more cutting edge to our competitive ventures, a little more intensity of effort, a little more "devil" in taking on the opposition, a little more long-term appreciation of what we could do if we really made up our minds well in advance.

The standards we set ourselves are, of course, very high, and we know that our failures could spell out relative success to other clubs.

We can easily justify these high standards we have set if everyone will do just that little bit extra in every field of Club life. We have many young athletes of great potential appearing in our colours throughout the whole of the year. A few more good friends in our ranks, some conscientious preparation and determined competition and Belgrave will be the "tops" in all the main branches of amateur athletics.

What a tremendous example Micky Pyer is to us all! Micky started his running with the Club almost fifty years ago and attained a good measure of success in Surrey and Club championships over the country and on the track. Years later he coached many of our younger middle-distance runners, and recently decided to add to his coaching qualifications by studying javelin throwing. He duly qualified, and is now a recognised coach and doing excellent work with the youngsters, which we hope will long continue. He has shown that advancing years are no excuse for any of us, and that we can all add to our usefulness if so minded

What an example to us all!

The Committee has decided that the "Tommy Green" Cup for the best single performance of the year by a Club member should go to John Moullin for winning the Open 100 Miles Walk held in June in the Ewhurst area. John's performance is the second best ever recorded and we congratulate him most heartily on his splendid win - and on holding for a year the Cup presented by our 1932 Olympic winner.

Congratulations also to Past President Jack Goswell, who has been elected President of the Race Walking Association. Jack has given (and still gives) tremendous service to our Club, and to Walking, and his many years of really hard work fully justify his elevation to the Presidency of the R.W.A. This election honours the Club as well as the individual; and in this connection we acknowledge with grateful thanks the additional hard work and support given by Mrs. Joan Goswell over many years.

A young member who deserves the highest praise from us and the heartiest of congratulations is Rodney Heward-Mills. Competing for Somerset in the English Schools' Championships at the Crystal Palace on July 9/10 in the Senior Boys' Triple Jump, he achieved 50' 9" (15.47m.) and set up a U.K. Junior Record and a Championship Best Performance.

He is a student at Millfield School, and we hope that next season we may have his services in at least some senior competitions, and that we shall see him achieve international honours.

We congratulate South London Harriers on attaining their Centenary in November, and wish them well in the future. Over the years they have distinguished themselves on the country and on the track, and have a great record. In the last year or two things have been on the quiet side for them, but we may be sure that the "Irrepressibles" will rise again.

Our own beginnings have a close connection with the South London Harriers, for following an S.L.H. Open track meeting at the Oval in September, 1887, a number of lads who had cycled across from the Westminster area decided on their way home to form their own athletic club. And so the Belgrave Harriers came into existence.

THOSE WERE THE DAYS!

Gordon Doubleday

From Gordon Doubleday:

I was recently given an old copy of the News of the World, dated June 16, 1935. On the front page was a description of the famous "Mile of the Century" held in New Jersey, U.S.A. It was won by Jack Lovelock, the Oxford and New Zealand runner, in 4m. 11.2s. - four yards ahead of the leading American milers including Glen Cunningham, holder of the world record of 4m. 6.7s. (a record next broken by Sydney Wooderson at Motspur Park).

Turning to the other pages, I was hoping for some news of Belgrave, and I was not disappointed. The Whitsun British Games, organised by Joe Binks at the White City, drew a crowd of 20,000, and one of the highlights of the meeting was a Three Miles match between ourselves and Birchfield H.

Earlier in the year, the Bels had ended Birchfield's long run of wins in the National C.C. Championship, by winning at Beaconsfield, and a few weeks later had beaten them again in the London to Brighton Relay.

It was an exciting race, won by our own Jim Ginty, but the Birchfield tail proved stronger than ours, and they beat us by four points. (I believe it was six to score.)

Representative honours were more scarce in those days, but we had a member in the A.A.A. team which beat Cambridge University. Tommy Scrimshaw, already an International, was third in the 880 yards, the winner's time being 1m. 56.2s.

There had been a big Open Meeting at Plymouth, and one of the chief events was a Three Miles Open in which Jim Ginty (already mentioned) and Pat Parker were second and third, the winner's time being 15m. 7.4s. I do not suppose that anybody at that time thought that we would see 10 miles being covered at a greater speed than five minutes a mile; and that the marathon would be done at 12 m.p.h. - the British record was still a shade over 2hrs. 30m., and only a few had covered the distance at over 10 m.p.h.

In the Middlesex Junior Championships, Stan Botwright won the 220 yards in the 17-19 class, and was runner up in the 100 yards. Stan developed into one of our best sprinters, and in 1939 was Club Champion at 100, 220, and 440 yards.

Sussex County held their Jubilee Championships at Preston Park, Brighton, and Joe King retained his two-miles walk title in 14m. 31s., beating the previous record by 3s.

Looking back, we had at the time achieved the unique position of being National Team Champions at Cross-Country and Road Walking (50 kilometres), a feat that no Club had previously achieved, and one that we were able to repeat. When are we going to do it again?

As for the rest of the country, I noticed in the advertisements slimming pills (presumably for the ladies), and, wait for it, Fags 10 for 4d.

Footnote: Jim Ginty and Pat Parker both subsequently gained International Vests over the country. In the 1937 Championship (National) at Stratford-upon-Avon, they gave the team a fine start by finishing third and fourth, but Birchfield gained the title by a very narrow margin of points.

Our President A.D.(MICK)PYER

We have paid ourselves a great compliment in electing MICKY PYER to the highest position in the Club and we can be quite sure that he will do everything possible for the Club, as he has done for so many years past. It is just over fifty years since Micky first drew on a Belgrave vest and impressed himself as a more than useful runner and a valuable member of our teams. In 1922 he won the Club Mile Championship, a win he repeated the following year, and succeeded in taking the Surrey County Championship at the distance also

In the 1923 7 miles Club Cross-Country Championship Mick finished second to Jimmy Flatt; but in 1924 he won the event from A.E. Smith. The 10 miles Championship for the MACHER SHIELD in both 1923 and 1924 were won by Micky from Dick Savage, and Wally Bayliss respectively.

Mick Pyer showed up prominently in our teams at this time and contributed to our early minor championship wins as Belgrave began to assert itself in the cross-country world.

The claims of work took Mick away for a while but on his return he made a very valuable contribution in coaching the younger runners. This he was able to do by reason of his own experience and the AAA qualification he possessed, and in later years he has become a duly authorised Coach in Javelin throwing and many of our boys know how well he understands and promotes the technique of this event even though it was not particularly fashionable in his early days on the track.

Our President, for some years past has attended most of the Meetings--Summer and Winter--in which the Club is concerned, and we can truly acclaim him as our Number One Belgrave Harrier---trebly underlined this year when he proudly carries the title of President.

We wish him well in his year of office, and we may be sure he will exert all his endeavours for the betterment of the Club. The Presidency is in safe hands!.

OBITUARY

A.A.H.

DAVE MARTIN SHEPHERD (23.2.1916 - 20.12.1971)

It is with very real regret that we have to chronicle the passing of Dave Shepherd, after a very short illness; and we extend our sympathetic condolences to his wife, Winn, and her mother (our two lady vice-presidents) and other members of the family on their very great loss.

It is a loss which we feel also, and shall continue to feel for a long time to come. Dave joined the Club in July, 1931, and quickly showed himself to be a real, stolid Club supporter in every way. He trained under the supervision of Dick Savage, and was a member of our junior team which finished fourth in the Southern Counties C.C. Championship in 1934, and was one of our winning Surrey County junior team.

The demands of his career prevented Dave from giving as much time and effort to our activities as he would have liked during the next few years, although he was to be seen at many of our meetings; and in later years Dave really shone as one of our hardest and most enthusiastic workers. As a Social Hon. Secretary, he worked tremendously hard on our behalf; and his technical abilities were at our disposal when the time came for a rented St. John's Hall to become a leasehold, and later a freehold, Belgrave Hall. Dave prepared the first plans embracing the alterations at the rear end of the Hall and the installation of hot water, plumbing etc., and gave us the ammunition to seek planning permissions and building leases for the improvement of our headquarters.

Dave also served as an efficient Running Hon. Secretary for several years, and more recently as a conscientious and energetic Hon. Treasurer.

Small wonder that he was elected a Life Member as a mark of appreciation of his valiant work for the Club over a long period; and that he became President in 1962/63, worthily filling that position.

In addition to his work within the Club, he found time to serve the Club and the Sport by his membership of many other athletic associations. For several he had served on the Southern Counties A.A.A., the Southern Counties Cross-Country Association, the South of Thames C.C. Association, the Surrey A.A.A., and as Chairman of the Surrey County Women's A.A.A. He was one of the four delegates from the South to the English Cross-Country Union; and all branches of our Sport are in debt to Dave for the work he essayed and accomplished. He arranged Suppers, juggled with discs in the funnels at muddy cross-country races, wrote out receipts for subscriptions, advised on hot water problems at the Hall, and generally gave us a life of service, and of good humour, affection and example.

He was, indeed, a "cheerie chappie", of high integrity and great loyalty, with an enthusiastic and burning faith for Belgrave, and a real worker for our Sport in many ways, over many years.

We mourn his passing - without doubt we shall all miss him tremendously - and we feel grateful that we should have had the opportunity of lasting friendship with one we were all very, very proud to know.

R.I.P.

- A.A.H.

OBITUARY H.G.("HARRY") CHURCHER.

A.A.H.

It is with great sorrow that we have to advise the death on June 24th of Harry Churcher at the age of 61 years. Harry carried the Belgrave colours to six AAA Championship wins and with his many other distinctions, carved for himself a place in Belgrave history which may never be surpassed. Our sincere condolences go to his widow, and brother Charlie; and we are glad to have had Harry's help and determination over such a long period in our history.

It was in August 1931 that the writer, having competed in a two miles event at Stamford Bridge Track and leisurely dressing thereafter, was approached by a middle-aged man then unknown to him. He pressed some forms and money into my hand with the words "Here are a couple of lads who will bring fame to Belgrave"-----and disappeared. As a Belgrave holiday trip to Switzerland was imminent the matter passed out of mind temporarily, but a little later I discovered that the mysterious man was W.C. Wheeler, a former Kent walking champion, and uncle of Charlie and Harry Churcher.

His words certainly came true in the years that followed. Charlie became Club Captain and led our teams to many victories; whilst Harry recorded success on success confining himself in the main distances of 10 miles and below, and culminating in Olympic honours in 1948.

In early October 1931 Harry had his first race with us in a Club handicap over five miles, with the competitors starting off their marks. Charlie was first to finish and Harry was third, and recorded the fastest actual time in the field of 28 walkers.

Incidentally on the same day 43 started in the 3 miles Run and the "Belgravian" says "it is a pity that more did not start in this race".

Later in the year Harry won the HORWOOD CUP, with Redgrave second, Speechley third and Harley fourth----the first of many trophies he was to hold over the years.

Harry continued to improve, and in 1933 in the RWA Junior 10 miles Championship (30 teams, 211 starters) at Fort Dunlop, he led the Club team to victory. He finished in third position, the team being completed by DON BROWN (6), BERT FIELD (7), and ALF HARLEY (10).

Numerous successes now came Harry's way and he won the Inter-County, Southern, Surrey, L.B.H. and Club Championships and Open events week after week with almost monotonous regularity. He even won the Joe Lyons Cake twice, given for the most meritorious performance of the week!.

His first placing in AAA Championships came in 1936 when he was third in the 2 miles event. He was second in the 7 miles Championship in 1937, beaten only by J. MIKAELSON of Sweden, later to become an Olympic Champion. 1938 gave Harry a third placing in the 2 miles event, but in 1939 he surpassed himself and had a double Championship win the 2 and 7 mile Championships. Incidently, Joe Coleman was third in the latter event.

The War then descended upon us and most of us found ourselves in uniform and decanted in different parts of the world. Harry was in the RAF and covered his miles on a motor-cycle, the walking track being well out of mind.

In 1946, however, he strove to get back to his previous fitness and form, and experienced much disappointment in so doing. However he persevered and won through, and demonstrated his strength of character and his will to succeed to such an extent that he was again placed (2nd and 3rd) in the AAA "2' and "7' ; Hindmar of Sweden, winning both Championships.

Hindmar was again winner of the 2 miles event in 1947 with Harry second and KRESEVICH of Italy third. In the 7 miles race however, Harry scored a win beating DISALVO and KRESEVICH.

1948----Olympic Year----and Harry scored a double-win in the two AAA Walking Championships and was selected for the British Olympic team. In the Olympic event Harry was somewhat off form----probably due to an early caution, and eventually finished fifth in the 10000 metres track walk. There were 19 competitors from 10 nations.

Harry Churcher was always a quick, short-striding walker and his fluid style sometimes brought forth criticism, and occasionally disqualification. It was interesting however to have the opportunity of seeing a film of Harry walking in slow motion; and to have confirmation that his positive contact and rhythmic balance were maintained even at the highest speeds. In fact his action was the very poetry of motion. In contrast, some long-standing walkers normally regarded as near-perfection were found to be at fault in their action and merited disqualification on the progress displayed in the slow motion films. However, this is by the way.

In the 1949 AAA Championships Harry won the 7 miles event, and was second to BORJESSON of Sweden in the shorter event.

A third place in the 1951 "7" brought to a close Harry's remarkable sequence of AAA Championship placings.

Continued:

(Cont'd.) Harry's successes on the track were many as has been shown. He competed in Sweden, set up records in France and Ireland and held the World record for 5 miles Walking at one time.

So too on the road, at distanes up to 15 miles Harry had tremendous success; and scarcely a week passed without a Churcher win being recorded.

The inaugeral R.W.A. 10 miles Championship at Sheffield in 1947 gave Harry another Championship at Sheffield in 1947 gave Harry another Championship win, and with Jack RUTLAND (2), R.D. McMULLEN (4) and Joe COLEMAN (6) following close behind, Belgrave had an easy win with 13 points.

The following year in Hyde Park, Harry and Belgrave repeated their wins of the previous year; and week after week Harry and Belgrave hit the headlines.

The foregoing is by no means fully comprehensive, and merely gives emphasis to the highlights of a brilliant athlectic career which brought considerable honour to a great sportsman, and to his Club which held him in such high esteem, and will continue to have him in mind wherever the giants of the past are discussed.

A.G.M. REPORT. 1971

As a break with tradition the 84th A.G.M. was held this year on Wednesday September 22nd , a change from the customary fourth Thursday in the month.

There were 59 club members present with apologies from a further 13 , and the meeting was quickly away with the approval of the minutes of the last A.G.M. Our new secretary Peter Hillier , prior to presenting his maiden speech then called upon the members to stand in silence in tribute to a sadly large number of senior members who had passed on during the previous year. Tribute was paid to Jack Chappell , Jim Flatt, W. Isaacs , Ernie Kemp, Fred [illegible]c[illegible]ay and Bill Redgrave.

Peter then presented his report and outlined the club's running,walking, and field event's activities on track, road and country during the last year. He made reference to the sterling performances of several of the club's athletes whose exploits had been duly recorded in the Belgravian during the past year .

Reference was also made to the taking of another club photograph, which resulted in the largest attendance of club members at Belgrave Hall for many years. In conclusion he spoke of the work put in by all club officials and the report was duly adopted.

The much awaited report of the treasurer John Baker followed and he was congratulated on keeping the club out of financial difficulties . John paid tribute to our fund raising stalwarts Bill Couzens and Frank Webb and also called for a vote of thanks to our auditors.

Life membership was next on the agenda and duly elected were Gerry North and Micky Pyer. Micky was soon to be mentioned again when it came to the time for electing our new President. Alf Harley president for the past year proposed Micky and with considerable enthusiasm he was unanimously elected. So Micky pulled off the distinguished double of president and life member in the same year.

Before vacating the chair Alf Harley spoke of his 45 years as a Belgravian and thanked members for making him president twice . He wished Micky all success in his year of office.

Vice presidents were next on the list and duly elected were [illegible].Donovan , T.Hart, Reg Hop[illegible]ins and W.Ashford.

Presentation of the Ryan cup to J.Beaumont and the Shield Cup to R.Hopkins, almost brought matters to a close and with a few minutes discussion on general matters another annual general meeting had come and gone.

FROM 'The Belgravian' 25 years ago and 40 years ago

FROM 'The Belgravian'

25 years ago

DECEMBER 1946 -- "The winter season is now in full swing and we are getting good musters at Belgrave Hall. Our runners will do their utmost to retain their many championships, including the English and Southern titles. The walkers will strive to regain the championships we have held in the past. Our respective captains, Bert Footer and Charlie Churcher, will have watchfull eyes over their men and will be pleased to advise any member on training."

"The new monthly magazine ATHLETICS is now well established and needs no introduction to most members. For those not acquainted with ATHLETICS it is edited by one of our members P.W.(Jimmy) Green: the annual subscription for twelve issues is 10/- post free."

"Heartiest congratulations to Colin Sutton and Jock Martin upon their performances in the 24 hours walk held at the White City on July 14th-15th. Both covered 100 miles within the 24 hours and so qualified to become members of the famous Centurions' Club. Their times were: Sutton 20:40:11, Martin 22:16:57, and both have been presented with a suitably inscribed certificate from the Club. The winner of the event was our Poly member, Percy Reading, who set up three new records viz. 19 hours: 105 miles 466 yards, 110 miles: 19:57:16, 20 hours: 110 miles 410 yards. Percy also beat the unofficial record for a non-stop walk, covering 129 miles 749 yards."

"ETIENNE GAILLY, perhaps better known as 'Steve', is now commissioned in the Belgian Army, and sends his best wishes to his Belgrave friends. 'Steve' joined us in 1943, having escaped from occupied Europe." (Ed: Two years later Etienne was the first marathoner to enter Wembley Stadium at the London Olympic Games, only to be passed by Cabrera and Richards on the track. In 1971 he died tragically in a motor accident in Belgium.)

LITTLE BUT GOOD — Copthall Stadium, July 31, 1971

A small squad of Bels. travelled to Hendon for an informal meeting put on by Shaftesbury Harriers. Several clubs were present, but teams scores, apparently, were not kept. Our men would hardly have been in the running for a team award, there were only seven of them. But they came away with two firsts, two seconds and two thirds on an individual basis, so can be said to have performed pretty creditably.

Philip Gee was second in the 400m. in 52.4s., but went even more strongly in the 800m., to win in 2m. 3.7s. Also in the 800m. were Colin Pearson, 5th in 2m. 09.5s., and Richard Moody 7th in 2m. 10.6s.

New star Jim Rimmer took the 1500m. in 4m. 04.5s., and to prove what a useful performance this was, we can say that Laurie O'Hara was third in 4m. 05.6s.

Those indefatigable field events twins John Martin and Bill Couzens carried our colours in the field. John was second in the shot, with 11.28m., and Bill Couzens third with 9.73m. In the javelin, however, Bill led the way, finishing fourth with 43.08m., and John was fifth, with 39.68m.

BELGRAVE v DJK TRAUNSTEIN — Sunday 1st August '71

Only two representatives of our club turned out for this international meet held deep in the heart of Sussex on the East side of the Brighton Road opposite Bolney.

The day before the race our team, Bob Glover and Charlie Manning, flushed with success from their recent 5,000 metre Club Championship, were out from their training camp and did two laps of the course with Ted Stimpson, Ian and Martin Gillings. On the Sunday morning, after a light breakfast, they went to the tent of the competitors - who had accepted their challenge, after they'd taken the xxxx out of them on their return from training the day before. To Bob and Charlie's surprise the Germans looked too much like runners, having removed their paper hats and set aside their bugle, they appeared in Adidas, shorts, etc.

The pace was hot right from the start. Chas was running with the leading German and Bob alongside the other at the back. They pushed the pace on the first mile out - mostly downhill. At the steep, short, climb to the left onto the long leg they went like a couple of greyhounds with our men still in contact. This was the order as they commenced the long uphill struggle towards the finish. Halfway along this stretch Bob moved to the front - can't you picture it?- the multitudinous voices, "GLOV-ER - GLOV-ER - GLOV-ER". England were now 1st and 3rd. With less than a mile to go the leading German, spying yet another hill, expired and dropped back! Bob, with gentlemanly aplomb, returned to make sure his adversary continued along the course. Chas, well in command at the front eased down at the camp shop (he reckoned that to be the finish) only to be surprised by the remaining German sprinting right down to the tents for the congratulations and considering himself the winner.

Southern Road-Relay — BELS BACK IN THE MEDALS — BELGRAVE & SOUTHERN COUNTIES AAA ROAD RELAY---8th April 1972

Last year's 7th place was our worst ever performance in the Southern Road Relay, so there was a determination afoot to wipe out the memory of that unhappy occasion.

Thanks to Tony Brien we achieved the necessary good start and thereafter we were attacking the leaders on each stage. Only when Bob Holt swept our local rivals away and out of sight on stage eight was it clear that we would be fighting it out for bronze. We won this secondary battle without difficulty, pushing the holders (TVH) into 4th place. It was a good all-round effort by a team showing much verve and all running pretty well to, and above, form.

STAGE 1: A. BRIEN (24:42, 7th fastest).

Tony was always with the main leading group, which allowed just four runners to get away. The evergreen Harland (Camb.) led them all home, 10 secs ahead of our second claim member, Bob Richardson (B'Hth). Tony was involved in a mass scramble for the following positions where six seconds covered six runners. Four of our main rivals, T.V.H., Herc/Wimb., Hillingdon and Medway (all ahead of us last year) finished 14th,15th,and 21st respectively.

STAGE 2: P. CARTON (15:31, 2nd fastest)

Peter set off shoulder to shoulder with Hicks of Aldershot, 42secs behind the leaders. Showing impressive form, the young pair forged their way through the string of teams ahead, passing the front runner in the final stretch. Of the 165 runners who tackled the short stage this day these two were the fastest---Hicks getting home 3 seconds ahead of Peter, with Reading in 3rd place.

STAGE 3: W. WELLER (24:46--5th fastest)

This was Bill's first outing for us in this event and he proved right away what a valuable addition he is to our relay teams. Although he had to give best to Aldershot (Adams 24:15) and Portsmouth (Cain 24:21) he stuck to our forward position very well and opened up a further 10 seconds on Reading.

STAGE 4: W. CURTIN (16:02, 6th fastest)

This was one of Bill's better days. Showing much ferocity he attacked the field in his best style, destroying the Portsmouth runner and closing within 11 seconds of the leader (Aldershot).

STAGE 5: GERRY NORTH (24:32, 3rd fastest)

Much can happen on a long stage, and it was here that Aldershot was pushed right back to 6th place, 1½ minutes down, and out of the medals. Gerry, and Biscoe of Reading, fought out the lead for the whole of the 5½ miles; Gerry giving best by a mere 4 secs at the end. It was a fine run which showed so well what a great competitor this man is. T.V.H. and Herc/Wimb. were still lanquishing back in 8th and 9th places, 2 minutes down.

STAGE 6: C. PEARSON (16:52, 9th fastest

Colin, who has been reserve runner on so many occasions, stepped in here and acquitted himself very well. Although several of the following teams closed up, he gave very little away up front and finished 16 seconds behind the leaders, Reading.

STAGE 7: J. PHELAN (25:08, 10th fastest)

John was another having his first outing in this race, and what an initiation it was! As he waited to start he found himself alongside McAndrew (Reading), Ford (Aldershot), Watson (Cambridge) & D. Holt (H/W). The race was now burning hot. Although John slipped two places, only 61 seconds covered the first five teams---and Hercules Wimbledon were just 15 seconds behind us.

STAGE 8: J. Stow (16:22, 6th fastest)

John was our third junior this day to race over the short stage, and he followed the example of his predecessors by carrying the claret and gold further forward to regain 3rd place. Reading was still in front with Hercules Wimbledon now 9 seconds in arrears.

STAGE 9: P. O'CONNOR (25.00 , 7th fastest)

Its a good job that Pat has a cool disposition. To take off just 9 seconds ahead of a Bob Holt in full flight is an unenviable situation in which to find oneself. Two more internationals, Ashton (Reading) and Thomas (TVH) were also doing much damage, so Pat found himself in a similar situation to fellow Irishman Phelan 40 minutes earlier. He did well to hold third place by passing the Aldershot runner, 3 seconds down. T.V.H. were now 5th, only 17 seconds behind us.

STAGE 10: T. HART (16:07 , 3rd fastest)

Trevor has usually been one of our long stage runners so there were high hopes that he would bite into the lead of the two teams ahead. Unfortunately, Wallis (H/W) was fastest on this stage, but Trevor took 10 to 18secs off the other top four teams to help stabilise our 3rd place. We were now 91 seconds behind Reading and 72 seconds behind Hercules Wimbledon.

STAGE 11: L. O'HARA (24:34, 2nd fastest)

Just 2 months away from becoming a 40 yrs old veteran, Laurie O'Hara was faced with a situation where he had little to gain (Piotrowski and Badgery were well ahead) and everything to lose (T.V.H., Aldershot, Portsmouth and Cambridge were all within striking distance). Laurie rose to the occasion in great style and only Cass of Portsmouth was faster on this stage. We were virtually assured of medals.

STAGE 12: A. BLACK (16:29, 6th fastest)

This is not always the glory stage that it's made out to be. With Reading and Herc/Wimb fighting it out ahead Alan ran a solo effort just hard enough to keep out of trouble from a second scrap going on behind.

And so it was that Hercules Wimbledon, through Halliday, took the lead for the very first time just yards from the tape to win the title from Reading (Bacon) by a mere second after 4 hours of racing. Belgrave were third, 9[illegible] seconds in arrears, and T.V.H. (Standen) just held off a fast finishing Aldershot (Fletcher, 15:49) to take 4th place.

WELLER WINS OUR BIRTHDAY '20'

BELGRAVE OPEN 20 MILES ROAD RACE --------- Wimbledon, 22nd July 1972

Showing some of his most impressive form, Bill Weller put the icing on our 21st promotion with an intelligent win in the open race and added a cherry too by taking the Surrey title held in conjunction. The most notable retirer was Alan Cass of Portsmouth who shot into a 12 seconds lead at 5 miles and stretched it to 22 seconds at 10 miles, only to find himself back on his heels after taking "Copse" for the second time, 4th at the 15 miles mark and in an ambulance a mile or so later. Weller bided his time nicely. He ran with Pratt and Macintosh for 10 miles, took the lead near the end of the second lap and eased away gradually to a 25 seconds margin at 15 miles and on to a 43 seconds winning lead. His team-mates O'Connor and Mann were always well placed but couldn't match the second half running of Ealing's Warren and Case. Colin Pearson produced a very good debut at 20 miling and was rewarded with the Newcomer's prize and a third place team medal in the Surrey Championship. With Clive Shippen doing a "Cass" at 18 miles, Joe Dooley stepped into the breach with a fine run to preserve our Surrey medals. Others forced to withdraw were Hilton, Baker, Paget, Laws and Fairfield. The organisation was as excellent as usual and Gordon Biscoe now becomes our longest serving race secretary for this promotion with six consecutive efforts, one ahead of Arthur Whitehead who initiated the event 21 years ago. Australian Athletics

BY A BELGRAVIAN "DOWN UNDER"

Australian Athletics ~ BY A BELGRAVIAN "DOWN UNDER"

Apart from the well known athletes that are household names throughout the world, the general standard of athletics in Australia is surprisingly lower than one would imagine. Schoolboy times and distances are quite often phenomenal, but once school is behind him, the combined attractions of the beach scene, birds and cars take their toll, and athletics is very often pushed aside for the casual life. The State of Victoria is the most athletics conscious, and they usually manage to keep New South Wales at bay in the annual Interstates Championships.

The set-up in New South Wales is built largely around a league system and every club provides up to six competitors in different grades, ranging, for example, in the 400m., from 48s. men in the "A" grade down to 58s. in the "F" grade. Each grade is, in fact, run as a separate league, and towards the end of the track season excitement mounts as each club strives to become top dog in at least one grade. Every track and field event is covered in all grades, Pole Vault, Hurdles and all, so each meeting is split into two sections; 100m., 400m., 1500m., etc. on Saturday, and 200m., 800m., etc. on Sunday, or in the following week.

These meetings are family affairs, and often last from midday through to early evening, as there are events for boys in age groups from 10 years upwards, and many of these are also graded. Many spectators take a picnic tea and an ice-box full of Aussie beer, and there is general enjoyment all-round. "Old Joe", flogging himself nearly to death in winning the "F" mile, gets just as much applause as the thoroughbreds in the following "A" 100m., where the first three might well dip under 10.6s.

WALKING — THREE NEW CENTURIONS

BY JOHN KEOWN

BY JOHN KEOWN

On June 25/26 in an Open 100 Miles Walking Race promoted by Surrey W.C., three Belgravians, John Moullin, John Morris and John Keown became Centurions when they finished the distance inside 24 hours. By definition, a Centurion is one who, as an amateur, has walked in competition, in Great Britain, 100 miles within 24 hours.

John Moullin joined the ranks of the Centurions in a most spectacular fashion. With a truly magnificent display of fast, controlled walking, he finished first in the race, with a remarkable time of 16hrs. 55m. 44s., only 1m. 29s. outside Frank O'Reilly's world best performance for 100 miles. Frank had already competed in two previous races o[illegible] 100 miles and above when he set his world best time of 16hrs. 54m. 15s. in the Leicester to Skegness 100 miles on July 1/2, 1960. A search through the Centurions roll of honour shows that no one has entered their ranks with such a high-class performance. To get inside 17hrs. at one's first attempt at the distance is indeed an unique achievement which makes John Moullin one of our top ultra-long-distance walkers. Since the Centurions were founded in 1911, only five other walkers have succeeded in qualifying as members by finishing inside 18 hours; Billy Baker (Queen's Park Harriers) in 1926, Tom Richardson (Woodford Green) in 1936, Johnny Henderson (Steyning A.C.) in 1937, Rex Whitlock (Metropolitan W.C., and brother of Harold) in 1952, and Colin Young (Essex Beagles) in 1960. The British track walking records from 80 miles upwards must now be within John's grasp. In conjunction with the 100 miles race was a 100 kilometres race, which John also won, with a time of 10hrs. 21m. 06s.

When asked what special training he did for the 100, John replied: "I did no special training. In fact, I had done no training at all for at least a month, on accoun[illegible] of working till about 9.30 each evening, but I did several races which I used as training for the 100, including the Leicester Mercury '20', Bradford 50 kms., and the National 20 miles."

The other two Johns, who have contested many Brightons during the last twelve years, in a spirit of friendly rivalry, surprised themselves and their mates when, on this occasion, they combined forces to to complete the 100 miles together in 20hrs. 49m. 27s. They walked the entire race shoulder to shoulder, each in turn supplying the necessary encouragement to keep going when the other appeared to falter. As is their normal practice, both trained for this race alone. They covered about 40 miles per week, split into four sessions, during the month prior to the 100. Beforehand, they agreed to try to walk at roughly 5 m.p.h. for at least 60 miles. In fact they reached this mark in 11hrs. 58m.

The race was held on a 9 lap road course around the village of Ewhurst, near Guildford. There were 73 starters, including walkers from Holland, Belgium, W. Germany, Canada and Ireland. Contesting the team race (three to score) were three teams from Dutch clubs, one from an Irish club, and six from English clubs. On the lap, there were four feeding stations, roughly 2¾-miles apart. One of these stations was run by the Wilson brothers and our second-claim member, Cyril Gittins. The course, which was mostly on country roads, was fairly flat for five miles, then , in typical Surrey fashion, the remainder of the lap was rather undulating. Here and there the road surfaces were bad, especially near the side. Each 5 km. point was clearly marked with a large sign. These signs were lit during the hours of darkness, from about 11 p.m. until 4 a.m. The only successful way of walking through the wooded sections during this period was along the white line in the centre of the road. The weather was fine except for three hours during the night when it rained very heavily. The winner said this of the course: "I really enjoyed it, but I think it would have been a bit more pleasant if one could see where one was walking in the hours of darkness."

At 6 p.m. on the Friday evening, the Life President of the Centurions, Ernest Neville, sent the 73 competitors on their way. Alomaine, the Belgian long distance specialist, led for the first lap. John Hedgethorne (Essex Police) and John Moullin passed him at about 14 miles, and completed the second lap together. The Belgravian now took the lead and retained it until the finish. Although feeling a little tired on lap 5, John Moullin reached the 100 Km. mark in 10hrs. 21m. 06s., just one minute and six seconds outside his schedule. He expected to slow down after this point, but was surprised to find that he managed to maintain his pace until the end. The only trouble he experienced during the race was a bit of swelling around one of his big toes.

Morris and Keown, ignoring the early rush, started in a steady fashion. After a couple of laps they were passed by Pat Duncan, who had started the race 20 minutes late. Pat retired around the 40 miles mark. The only trouble the two Johns had in the first half of the race was caused by grit, which Keown seemed to attract like a magnet. Morris, with his detailed calculations of possible finishing times and places, kept his companion's spirits up during the period of heavy rain, when the latter was afraid that an old knee injury might give trouble. Around the 75 miles mark, Morris began to experience some trouble with his back and thighs. This was probably caused by his having ridden a bicycle, for the use of a helper, from Dorking Station to the venue.

No amount of praise can be too high for the helpers. All three successful Belgravians owe a great debt to the people that manned the feeding stations and to those boys from the Club who looked after their individual needs throughout the race. Prominent among these were Carl Lawton and Pat Duncan. Pat, after only two hours sleep, took on the role of helper. They saw all three walkers safely across the finishing line. Although a friend of John Keown's took over as a helper at 8.30 a.m. on the Saturday morning, the greater experience of our Walking Secretary proved invaluable when a certain amount of quick massage became necessary to Morris's back during the last 20 miles of the race. Australian Dave de Bondi, who had kept an eye on his clubmates during the early stages of the race, snatched a few hours sleep and drove over to Essex for a 50 kms. race on the Saturday afternoon. Belgravian Alan Callow helped fellow countryman J.D. Harrison, from Boundary Harriers, Isle of Man, to complete the course.

Our thanks to all these, and to the officials who made the race possible.

At the finishing line to clock Keown and Morris home in 16th and 17th places was a Past President of the Club, Gordon Doubleday. To add to Belgrave's long day of success, the three Johns managed to squeeze into second team place, with 22 points, between L.A.T. Amsterdam (15 points) and R.W.V. Rotterdam (22 points).

Forty competitors completed the distance within 24 hours.

OBITUARY — A.J. STIRLING-WAKELEY

G.S.D.

We were saddened to learn at a recent Committee meeting that Jack Stirling-Wakeley died suddenly last December from Coronary Thrombosis. Always interested in amateur sport, Jack developed an interest in long-distance walking when serving in the Middle East during the 1939-1945 war. On his return home he joined Belgrave, and soon became a regular member of our walking teams, and helped us to win first team medals in the R.W.A. 50 kilometres championship and the London to Brighton walk. He also represented Hampshire in the Inter-Counties road walking championship. His two outstanding performances were in the 24 hours track walk at Motspur Park in 1948, when, going into the lead after 100 miles, he went on to win with 122 miles 1,411 yards to his credit. Three years later in the London to Brighton and Back race, he led from the start to win in 18hrs. 46m. A few hours afterwards he was at his place at the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden, where he was a percussionist, to play in "Die Meistersinger", one of the longest operas! For his Brighton and back performance he was awarded the Tommy Green Cup. He had also held the Club long-distance trophy, and had been awarded a Club Honours Badge for a performance of outstanding merit.

It was a tribute to his keenness and determination that he was able to fit in the long and hard training for these performances with his duties as a professional musician. He was also a qualified physiotherapist. Our sympathies have been conveyed to his wife, Stella. It is problematical whether there will be any more London to Brighton and Back races, so his winning time may stand as a Club record for all time. His winning distance at 24 Hours, however, will stand as a challenge to future Belgrave long-distance aspirants.

He was the first Hon. Editor of the Road Runners Club Newsletter, and also took over the Belgrave Newsletter, started by Percy Wright during the war. The latter he typed and circulated himself - a labour of love, and typical of his ideals.

Jack collapsed at Wimbledon Broadway in the 1950's, and was rushed to hospital to be operated on for a perforated ulcer. He did not compete again. But only the day before his death he performed in matinee and evening productions. - G.S.D.

Feature · page 62

The Men Behind The Trophies

PHOTOGRAPH OPPOSITE---1913 South of Thames "Junior" winners.

Left to Right:- Dick Best (17th), Joe Helm (9th), Harry Parker (15th), Ernie Musselbrook (18th) and Oscar Horwood (16th).

Belgrave was placed third in the South of the Thames Cross-Country Championship in 1904; and our next appearance in team results was in 1913 when our team won third medals in the South of Thames Junior Race - 59 years ago!

This event was held at Weybridge and our scoring team was:- HARRY PARKER 15, OSCAR HORWOOD 16, DICK BEST 17, and ERNIE MUSSELBROOK 18 - five seconds covering the four runners. Marvellous packing, of course; but JOE HELM,who finished ninth, was in the 'B' team and one wonders what happened to the team selection that day!

The photograph shows these five runners, four of them in the claret and gold hoops of the period.

All five former club-mates of ours are now dead. JOE HELM was killed in the 1914-18 war. DICK BEST, until fairly recent years, was to be seen at our track meetings where he had standing instructions to be entered in every event - and he always paid up! HARRY PARKER was to prove himself one of our finest officials ever, and in due time was Club President and later, in the year that he died, President of the English Cross Country Union and Southern Counties C.C.A.

ERNIE MUSSELBROOK must have been the original wearer of a woollen cap - he was never without it! (He presented us with the Cup which bears his name, and is annually awarded for the Junior 1500 M.) OSCAR HORWOOD was another to serve this Club with all his heart and sinew; and became Hon. Secretary in the immediate Post-War (1914/18) period when the Club funds were what you could find in your own pockets! OSCAR became Walking Hon. Secretary and President later and for many years helped the Club in a hundred ways.

Our scoring four of yesteryear are all represented in the range of Club trophies.

DICK BEST gave us, many years ago, the cup for our Half-Mile Championship. HARRY PARKER presented the "Pyramid"Cup for Shot Put and is also remembered in the "Parker Bowl" which goes to the first Belgrave man to finish in the E.C.C.U. Senior Championship. OSCAR HORWOOD presented the Horwood Cup for Walking, which is contested enthusiastically each December and has done much to raise our standards, and ERNIE MUSSELBROOK also gave us a trophy as mentioned above.

So our team of 1913 is remembered 59 years later!

In 1915, at Esher, the third position was again ours with 21 seconds covering the four men who were E. MUSSELBROOK 13, C. ARNOLD 15, J.E. OFFEN 16, J.J. SEXTON 17.
