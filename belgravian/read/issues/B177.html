PRESIDENTIAL PARS

It is with sorrow that we advise the death of F.E. "Fred" McKay - brother of J.R. McKay, Past President, who himself died only a few months ago. Both brothers joined the Club in May 1922 and maintained a close interest in all Club happenings throughout the greater part of their lives. Our condolences are extended to Mrs. F.E. McKay.

We congratulate most sincerely our walkers on retaining the R.W.A. 10 miles championship at Redditch. This was a great victory for sound team support in a large and representative field. It was particularly pleasing to see ALAN CALLOW, our new member from the Isle of Man figuring in our winning team.

Our Cross-country runners met with extreme disappointment in the National championships at Norwich. It certainly was not our day, the redeeming feature perhaps was yet another good run by that model of consistency, GERRY NORTH, who just failed to make the international team on a bitterly cold day.

Our plan for a Club photograph was beset by adverse circumstance but nevertheless a large number of members, covering all ages and a true cross section of the club, did face RON LINSTEAD and his camera on March 13th, and we trust we shall have a valuable record of faces for posterity.

This Club photograph was originally intended for the last Saturday in January when runners, walkers would be present in good numbers at our headquarters. However it was found that the Belgravian could not appear in sufficient time to advise all members of the intended photograph and it was decided to defer it until March 13th which seemed to be the only possible alternative date. Plans were duly made, special invitations extended to Past Presidents and many others; and then the postal strike intervened and our "Belgravians" and invitations to participate could not be sent out as well in advance as we would have hoped.

However, come the day and we were very pleasantly pleased with the numbers at Belgrave Hall. One wondered how many clubs could show such a muster, and with the circumstances adverse at that.

We'll try again sometime! Meanwhile apologies to those whose invitations reached them after the event - we should have enjoyed their company.

Our oldest members present were V.P. Ernest Neville (88 years) Charlie Speechley (83) and 1932 Olympic champion Tommy GREEN (77) - the youngest were still in single figures! Harry CHURCHER winner of seven A.A.A. titles, looked as though he could do it all over again and in support were a miscellany if internationals, former and present club champions, past presidents, and good honest worthy Belgrave Harriers covering a very wide span of years.

The track and field season proper is now upon us, and we trust we shall have an enjoyable Summer season. A few trophies here and there will remind us of our full potential, and we hope we shall distinguish ourselves in Southern League, and other events.

The prime consideration on which so much depends is, of course, complete support in every possible way of our officers and the arrangements they make on our behalf. A full attendance every time will do much to take us to the top and keep us there.

OBITUARY

A.A.H.

J.G. (JIMMY) FLATT

We have to advise the death of our old friend, Jimmy Flatt; and extend our sincere sympathy to his daughter.

Jimmy joined the Club in October, 1921, and his quiet, modest, unassuming manner quickly endeared him to our clubmates of 50 years ago.

In 1922, Jimmy was second in the Club 7-miles cross-country championship, but went one better the following year, when he won the "Blackstaffe" Shield. Second to him on that occasion was Micky Pyer, and third Bert Footer. In 1924, he took third medal in this championship.

He figured regularly in our teams during this rather undistinguished span of years, and led our scoring team home in the National cross-country championship at Beaconsfield in 1923. Jimmy's position was 91st, and our six scored 913 points, giving us 23rd place. Incidentally, this appears to have been the first occasion that we finished a team in this championship, which we were to win emphatically some years later in our history.

In Surrey County cross-country championships, Jimmy was a member of our winning team in 1926, and of our placed teams in 1923 and 1924.

His running career was interrupted by some spells of ill-health, but his affection for the Club was unimpaired, and he was truly one of those who laid foundations for those who followed.

In many years past, he had paid us the occasional visit at Wimbledon, and we were always glad to see the modest little man who had helped so much in the building up of our cross-country traditions, and retained his loyalty to the Club over so many years.

R.I.P.

W. (BILL) REDGRAVE

We have to record with regret the death of Bill Redgrave, a Vice-President of the Club, and a member for over fifty years. Bill was born and lived all his life in Westminster, and even after his retirement from the Army and Navy Stores continued to serve as an agent of our Football Scheme until a few weeks ago.

Bill helped the Club in many ways during his long membership, and many will remember his unfailing appearance at our Meetings to take charge of the programme sales in the days when a printed programme was considered a necessity.

Our sympathy is extended to his son Charlie, who figured in our walking teams at one time, and to Bill's daughter and her husband, Mr. and Mrs. S.L. King. Syd is, of course, a Life Member, and a former Hon. Treasurer and Walking Hon. Secretary.

- A.A.H.

FROM 'The Belgravian' 25 years ago ..... and 40 years ago

FROM 'The Belgravian' 25 years ago .....

JUNE 1946 ------ The first post-war Winter season was a memorable one for the Belgrave Harriers. Our cross-country runners won every open championship. In November we had the first two teams home in the South of the Thames "Junior", and the first three individuals. This in itself was a remarkable feat as all of our pre-war "cracks" cannot run in this race owing to the barring clauses. In the New Year we won the Surrey County Title, whilst our Middlesex members, running for Cadogan A.C., took the Middlesex title. This was not regarded as very extraordinary as it had been done on various occasions before the war. The South of the Thames "Senior" Race resulted in another win for us. Here again, a large number of our best men are barred. The Southern Counties Cross-Country Championship, 7½ miles instead of the usual 10, was held from Belgrave Hall. Our Captain, Tom Carter, gained a well earned and popular victory. With David Main third and four others in the first 20 positions, we gained a 99 points margin over the second team. In fact, our team of 12 was home before any other team had finished its six scoring men! Then to the much awaited English Championship of which we were the proud holders. On March 9th our lads travelled to Leamington Spa and had another battle royal with our famous Midland rivals, Birchfield Harriers. Our team finished in the first 24 positions, and after a breath-taking adding up of the scores we were acclaimed winners with a ten-points margin. We are indeed truly the Champion Club.

Of individuals, Tom Carter deserves our thanks and praise for the successful training and nursing of his team, also of course for his own brilliant running. It was a cause for general regret that Tom did not get another International vest. Len Herbert, by finishing seventh, was awarded an England vest and we congratulate Len on adding his name to the distinguished company of Belgravians so honoured in the past. It is of interest to recall them: A. Allum, H.E.Footer (3), A.W.Penny (3), C.T.Carter, J.Ginty and E.S.Stimpson. Pat Parker qualified for an England vest but chose to run for Ireland. David Main has also entered the ranks of the Internationals as he also ran for Ireland.

MARCH 1931 ------ Arthur Allum merits the congratulations of all upon his splendid running in the Southern Cross-country Championship. He is the first Belgrave man to finish in the first three of this race, and it should not be long befores he improves, even upon this.

Since writing the above Arthur has very nearly exhausted our supply of superlatives by finishing fifth in the National Championship, and so earned a place in the English team. The Club's best wishes go with Arthur to Ireland.

In the International Championship decided at Baldoyle Racecourse, Dublin, Arthur Allum finished ninth in the actual race and was the sixth Englishman to score. Thus England scored a very easy win and we are proud to have supplied one of the victorious team.

Len Fletcher and Frank Elson have assisted the Club in seven of these eight wins; Tommy Green six times; Fred Rickards on three occasions; Harold King, Frank Thorn and Ted Saunders each twice; and Joe King and Jack Field once each.

INTER-COUNTIES Leicester, January 16, 1971

Gerry helps Surrey to title

Lancashire went to Leicester as hot favourites for the inter-counties' title, but as at Leicester four years ago, it was Surrey that ran away with the race - and helping them was our own Gerry North.

Once again, Gerry showed his capacity to rise to the occasion, covering the near 7½-miles course in 35m. 42s., against 35m. 04s. by the winner, Trevor Wright, of Hallamshire and Yorkshire.

Lionel Mann produced a sound run, as usual, to finish 84th in 37m. 06s. for Bedfordshire and Huntingdonshire. But ahead of him was our rapidly improving R.A.F. runner, Tony Brien, who carried Wiltshire colours to 67th place in 36m. 48s. Turning his attention to the mud, also, was our road running member Brian Joines, who finished 287th in 42m. 55s. for Oxfordshire.

Apart from winning the Senior race, Surrey took third place in the Youths' event. Three Belgrave runners finished in the Country's scoring four - Bill Curtin coming home tenth in 18m. 58s. - some 32s. behind the winner- while Peter Carton was one place further back in 18m. 59s., and Tony Binda was the third Surrey man, with 26th place in 19m. 32s. John Stow also won his County colours, but was going through a rough patch when this race came round, and finished 99th in 21m. 11s., nothing like the performance we know he can produce.

A SOUTHERN SILVER!

BELS HIT FORM AT PARLIAMENT HILL

Having slipped out of a place in the Southern Cross-Country in 1970 for the first time in several years, the Bels bounced back into form in the Parliament Hill mud this February, with a highly encouraging second place.

Reading romped away with the title - helped by our old friend Dick Piotrowski in 37th place. Their total of 137 points went quite unchallenged. Our second place was a nail-biting business, however. With 221 points we had a margin of only 4 points to spare over the up-and-coming Hillingdon side, and only 5 to spare over Brighton & Hove.

It was pleasing to be the first Surrey side - ahead of the titleholders, Hercules Wimbledon - and also to take the Oddling Smee Cup, as the first Surrey club to close in a full team of 12 men.

The most heartwarming sight of the afternoon for Belgrave supporters was that of the flying figure of Gerry North, who made a thrilling attempt to recapture his title - and had he not been hit by his old enemy, the stitch, might well have done so. As it was, his fourth place in 46m. 55s. gave us a magnificent start, and with another three men in the first 34, and John Thresher in 42nd, it was a good day for the Bels. John Bicourt made a good recovery from his disappointing show in the Surrey, to finish 34th in 48m. 55s. Lionel Mann was two places ahead of John, with 32nd in 48m. 53s., but both these stalwarts were outpaced by that rising star Tony Brien - 30th in 48m. 48s. A below par Tony Fairclough made up the scoring six - in 79th place.

Pat O'Connor's 82nd place was closer to his real ability than some of his other performances lately, while it was good to see Bill Dance down from Yorkshire to carry the Belgrave colours again. Trevor Hart must have been disappointed with 123rd, but not as disappointed as Guy Monnet - the most sharply improved runner in the club - who could have expected to finish at least 100 places higher than his 151st.

A different Guy might have been seen, however, had it not been for a poisoned tooth. John Kelly was rewarded for his recent improvement with a first run in the Southern.

ROAD RELAYS

It cannot be said that 1971 was a vintage year for Belgrave in the road relay field. But the bright aspects are the emergence of our junior stars as forces in the senior ranks, and the continuing enthusiasm to be found in our ranks. Setbacks are inevitable from time to time, but they can also be the springboard for greater things.

National Relay

BIRCHFIELD BACK - National Road Relay, Sutton Coldfield, May 1, 1971

If it was like old times to see old pals of the Brighton Road, Thames Valley, win the Southern Road Relay, it was doubly like old times to see our London-to-Brighton rivals of the nineteen-thirties, Birchfield, taking the National Relay title.

But it was bad times for the Bels. With our Birmingham friends first in 4hrs. 10m. 58s., we were 4hrs. 21m. 00s. But just to show how fortunes fluctuate in this type of running, Southern winners Thames Valley were only 56s. ahead of us, in 12th place.

On the same lines as the Southern event, the race consisted of a 5.6 miles leg and a 3.1 miles leg, covered alternately, to make a total distance over 52 miles.

Hillingdon led at the end of the first lap, of 5.6 miles, and Coventry at the end of the each of the next 7 laps, except the fifth lap, when Tipton briefly took up the pace-making. Birchfield ended each of the last four laps at the front, but only after a great battle with the reigning champions, Godiva.

Laurie O'Hara got us off to a promising start. Though only 14th, his 27m. 11s. was a good time for the distance, and kept us well in touch with the leaders. Only Trevor Hart of our men could get within half-a-minute of his clocking.

At the end of the second stage, the outlook still looked promising. John Bicourt went round the shorter leg in a useful 15m. 23s., and gave us our best position of the day - eleventh.

Bill Kerr was not at his best, however, on the third leg, falling to 16th, with a time of 27m. 58s. Peter Carton, just turned 18, came into the team, and gave a hint of things to come, returning 15m. 43s. to pick up one place.

Pat O'Connor was a shadow of his real self on the next stage, dropping to 18th, with 28m. 27s. Gerry North was still fighting off the effects of his earlier illness, and took a short stage, rather than his customary long. He ran well in the circumstances, however, to cover his lap in 15m. 09s., and pull back two places.

Next, it was Lionel Mann's turn, but not his day - a time of 27m. 55s. losing one place. Young Tony Binda had an unenviable task in his first race of this calibre - but he did well to hold his position and clock 15m. 52s. on the eighth stage.

Bill Dance stayed in 17th place. with 28m. 05s., and about the only bright speck in the sky at this point was that we were at least ahead of that renowned road relay club, Portsmouth A.C. Tony Fairclough was again fit enough only for a shortage stage, but he caught the Cardiff man to put us in 16th place , clocking 15m. 59s.

Trevor Hart remained in 16th place on the 11th lap, but he put in some good work (by the standards of the day), to return 27m. 38s., and provide Bill Curtin with something to go for on the last lap.

Bill showed his senior teammates a thing or to, passing the Medway and Bristol runners on his way to 14th place, with 15m. 40s.

Southern Road-Relay

BEDFORD STAGGERS THE CROWD - BELGRAVE STAGGER Wimbledon, March 27, 1971

We are used to success in our own Southern Counties 51-miles Road Relay at Wimbledon - with four wins and one second in the five races held up to this year. We had expected to be pressed to win a place this year - with clubs like Reading and Hillingdon beginning to hit the high spots. But seventh was a bit below our hopes.

Sometimes in a relay of this kind it is a blow-out on one or two legs that does the damage. But this was not case here. None of our men ran outstandingly badly, but most were half a minute or so slower than they would be on top form. And that adds up to quite a lot of minutes altogether.

Our old pals of the Brighton Road, Thames Valley pulled one out of the bag to win the race in 4hrs. Olm. Ols., after pre-race favourites Reading had made most of the running, while particular congratulations, perhaps, should go to Cambridge Harriers, third, and Medway, sixth.

As usual, the race consisted of a 5½-mile lap and a 3½-mile, covered alternately.

First leg: John Bicourt took the first stage (5½-miles) for us, and set the tone for most of our men, with a modest 13th place in 24m. 55s. - our local rivals, Hercules Wimbledon heading the field in 23m. 51s.

Second leg: Colin Pearson made a sound, if unspectacular debut in this class of competition, and was unlucky in having to run on his own for most of his stage - holding 13th place with 16m. 49s., while Reading moved into the lead.

Third leg: On a better day, Lionel Mann might have done much to strengthen our position, but as it was he fell back a place in clocking 25m. 07s. Portsmouth took a turn in the lead.

Fourth leg: Bill Kerr pulled us back up to 13th, but can't have been very pleased with his time of 16m. 16s. Reading went back to the front.

Fifth leg: Gerry North was still suffering from the illness which hit him ahead of the National cross-country, but showed his usual dermination in covering his lap in 24m. 18s., our best of the day for the long stage, and can count himself unlucky to pick up only one place on balance. Gerry actually passed two men, but was himself passed by a a shatteringly on-form Dave Bedford, who took Shaftesbury from 16th to 10th with a time of 22m. 20s - cutting no less than 54s. of the lap record previously held by Bob Holt of Hercules Wimbledon (if the 23.12 by Medway's Dick Newble, who finished Bedford's stage in second place is left out of account).

Sixth leg: Bill Curtin made an impressive entry at this level of senior competition, and from this point our fortunes can be said to have been on the mend. Bill clocked our second fastest short-stage time of 15m. 55s, and moved us up to tenth.

Seventh leg: Pat O'Connor - teetering on the brink of a return to top form - picked up another place, with 24m. 45s.

Eighth leg: Tony Fairclough made our position look even more respectable, taking us up to eighth. A time of 16m. 02s is not the vintage Fairclough, but Tony clearly ran up to the limits of his fitness.

Ninth leg: Trevor Hart's 24m. 30s. was our second-fastest long-leg time - but Trevor still fell back to tenth. Such are the fortunes of war. Thames Valley took the lead.

Tenth leg: Bill Dance gave us a boost by pulling us up to seventh, with 15m. 57s., while Reading recovered the lead.

Eleventh leg: Laurie O'Hara held our seventh position, though he might have hoped for better than 24m. 58s. Thames Valley took a 12s lead over Reading.

Twelfth leg: John Thresher went off with some formidable gaps in front of him, and made a bold attempt to close some of them, and at the end was only 23s. behind Medway, and 35s. behind Hercules Wimbledon, and was closing up strongly.

WIMBLEDON CUP for Mann

Double hat-trick for Lionel - Wimbledon Cup, Wimbledon, March 13, 1971

If Lionel Mann wants to go on adding to his list of Belgrave championships, he may before long have to start learning the pole-vault. (There is no club pole vault championship, of course, but it may be necessary to invent one).

Lionel started off the spring road season the way he finished the way he has been going for the best part of a year - with his sixth current club championship in the 5¾mile road run.

Just for the record, Lionel's sideboard now boasts the Lady Fell Shield (20-miles road), the Griffen Cup (5,000m. track), the Tom Carter Trophy (10,000m. track), the Blackstaffe Shield (7 miles c.c.), the Macher Shield (10 miles c.c.), and the Wimbledon Cup, not to mention the Denmark Pot (darts championship).

As in the 10-mile c.c., Lionel only just got home, however, and this time he had a margin of only 1s. to spare over Laurie O'Hara. Trevor Hart showed some improved form, in third place, while youths Bill Curtin, Peter Carton, Tony Binda and John Stow added to their growing reputations with fourth, sixth, seventh and twelfth, respectively.

The race provided a nostalgic moment for youths' coach Ron Linstead, and some others with longer memories. Ron set a record for the 5¾ course in March, 1954, when he clocked up 28m. 24s., at the age of 18. Now after 17 years comes the Linstead coached Bill Curtin to clock 28m. 00s. - also at the age of 18.

By and large, the results of the race were encouraging, after the disappointment of the National Cross-Country . Among a number of good performances was Colin Pearson's 8th place - which put him squarely in the running for the Southern Relay team. Colin only managed second place in the handicap, however - first place going to Bert Stroud, who obviously knows his way around a 10m. handicap. Good to see Pat Newell turning out again - and breaking 30m. yet once more.

The Editor's inability to co-ordinate his contributers' contributions, so that they arrive for the same deadline, means that he is able to relieve our hard working Walking Hon. COMMENT

If British walking has been through a bad patch its certainly equal to the best in the world at the present time. Each week brings a fresh crop of times which, only a year ago would have been considered impossible. Phil Embleton has broken 70 for 10 miles, 88 for 20 Kilometres and best of all the Track 10,000 Metres UK Record with a staggering sub 42 clocking. Over 20 Miles Belgrave look strong enough and our distance men can usually be relied upon but the 20 Kilometres at Luton looks to be the risk if we are to achieve an unbeaten national record this year. Fortunately some of the boys have been playing it a bit carefully and we can only hope that they have timed it right. Wilf Wesch is obviously as fit as ever, so is Carl Lawton. Alan Callow is struggling to right a back injury but Ray Middleton is on the rising fitness trail once more. Barry Ingarfield we hope can show consistently the form he has produced on occasions and with Len Duquemin back in training and eligible for the Post Office John Moullin may also reappear. Dave DeBondi and Pat Duncan are both training hard - so is Dave King. Nonetheless although we have the greatest depth and strength of any club in the world - witness those packs at Wimbledon-in the present competitive climate it is impossible to relax. Reg. Hopkins

We have now in the junior section a very lively Colt team, training and racing with a spirit that would put many seniors to shame, running without inhibitions, and showing a strong loyalty to the Club and the team.

Boys Cross-country championships 27-2-71 - 2½ miles

Although there was only a small field, this was one of the best boy's races for some time with very little difference in the abilities of the first four it was anyones guess who would triumph in this Club championship, and with the prospect of holding for a year that much coveted Jack Hampshire trophy to spur them on, each was out to be the first man home.

Trying to introduce tactics to his aid in his efforts to thwart the others, Reg Hopkins set off at an incredibly fast pace, his object being to open up a big gap and then try to hold them off until the finish. Simon, Dave and Jimmy were content to watch him go, feeling confident that he would soon tire and come back into sight and thinking that the main threat lay among themselves.

On reaching the Windmill they were shocked to find that Reg was not in sight; and the realization that they may have given him too much suddenly brought them to life. The Pace was now desperate, and as it happened saved the day for them. It was at the Queensmere that they caught a glimpse of his back. He was now feeling the early effort, but determined to hold his lead. The moment of truth for them all had arrived. Jimmy was leading the chase with Simon and David hanging on, on reaching the avenue the gap was closed and the race for the finish was on.

Jimmy took the lead and in spite of pressure opened a gap from Simon which he managed to hold till the end. David caught and passed Reg and thought he had him beaten for third place, but he had reckoned without the latter's finish, and lost it on the line. Final positions and times were:

BLACKHEATH HARRIER'S WINTER TRACK MEETING, CRYSTAL PALACE, SUN. 21.3.71

We went to this meeting with just eight club members - and recorded four wins.

This is, I would think, the biggest of the winter meetings, with Clubs from all over the South of England (and indeed a few from the North represented) Most of those who enter are just out to get the feel of things again after the winter recess, and so the competition is none too serious. Nevertheless, some worthwhile performances were recorded, in spite of the fact it was a miserable, cold, windy day.

Our lads performed equally well, Our first win was in of all things the Youth's Shot putt, Dave Raffell reaching 11 metres with the 10lb ball. Dave is a big lad for his age, and a keen sportsman, he is now taking this event very seriously, training most nights with the use of weights. We expect him to do great things this summer, indeed I would prophesy that other things allowing - education etc.,we now have at last a future international field events man in the Club.

Our second win of the day was with Reg Hopkins doing his thing (Fosbury) high jump. Like Dave,Reg is now taking this field event seriously, working to a schedule with weights and excercises and trying to perfect a technique. He managed to equal his personal best of 1.73 (5 ft. 8 ins) in spite of the winter conditions already mentioned. This height would have won him the junior event and placed him 5th in the senior. His ambition is to clear 6 ft. this summer, while still aged 16 years.

You would think we had done well enough with two field event wins but we had forgotten Stephen Crosbie. Although he failed in the sprints, finishing last in his race, he came back with a vengance in the long jump and recorded 5-67m to win the Youths event.

Our interest in the track was equally rewarding, Jimmy McAffee showing tremendous form in coming through the field and taking a commanding lead to win the Youths 1000m in 2m49.2s. Simon Holmes made it a Belgrave one-two finishing second in 2m55.7s. We are so used to seeing Martin Bloice first past the post that we were a little disappointed to see him finish 4th in the Junior 300m in 38.0s. Martin was the first to agree on leaving the track that he sadly needed training having done virtually nothing for weeks. This run shook him up and he has since got down to work and is determined to recapture his old form.

David Harvey ran in the 600m and although not in the first four, his was a very worthwhile effort. His ambition is to break 2.00 for the 800mts this season, I have every reason to believe he will manage it if he continues the way he is going. His time in this race of 1m31.9s seems to point the way.

Our only other competitor was Tony Holmes. Although Tony is not yet in the same class as the other members he is very keen and trains hard in a business like manner. This I'am sure will bring him sooner or later up with the others. He managed, however, 3m17.5. in the Youths 1000m although still only a boy.

(Ed.'s note: Since Reg wrote this, Martin Bloice has been out of action with a broken arm. Before his accident, however, Martin showed promising form for Lambeth in Paris. The latest news is that he is on the mend , so that there is still time for some good performances this summer. As will be seen from reports elsewhere in the magazine, there have been a number of excellent results from our youths since the Blackheath meeting.)

NATIONAL CHAMPIONSHIPS - NORWICH 6th March 1971

YOUTHS

The big day our lads had spent months building up for saw us finish a dissappointing eighth. Once again only Mark Clancy (153rd) ran to form. Bill Curtin (28th) led the team home with Peter Carton four places behind. Tony Binda came 50th, John Stow 175th and Pat Breslin 197th

The whole team except Mark appeared jaded - Bill although physically below par lacked nothing in determination - to beat Peter. A pity Peter wasn't on form, if he had come 5th or 6th one imagines Bill would still have run "eyeballs out" to beat him and perhaps have come 3rd or 4th. Lets hope in years to come Bill can channel his energies into trying to carve up the rest of the field, and not just his clubmates.

Of course afterwards we had the usual 'What went wrong'? questions. The truth is that the team got fit far too early in the season and did not ease right off for the odd week or so now and again. The team manager accepts responsibility for having got the runners to their peak so early but feels that two of the runners in particular ignored advice to ease down at certain periods. The blame for our lack of success must be shared therefore.

On the basis, however that "a man who never made a mistake never made anything" the individuals concerned having learned vital lessons, and are not in the least perturbed now the event is over. There are many seasons ahead to make amands, and this will surely happen.
