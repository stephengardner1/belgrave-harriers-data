Editorial · page 3

Heartiest congratulations to our team placed third in the Amateur Athletic Association's 4 x 440 yards Relay Championship. This is the first time we have ever been placed, and Messrs. Pack, Michael, Herbert and Bird will value their bronze medals.

Our team ran impressively in their heat, and in the final Eddie Pack gave our team a splendid start, unofficial watches timing him to do a shade outside 50 seconds. Dick Michael then took over, and striding magnificently, retained the lead, although our opponents were gaining. L. W. Herbert took the next "leg" for us and despite a plucky effort was passed by J. V. Powell (L.A.C.) the International runner; and the Achilles runner. Alby Bird was our last man, and he could not be expected to give Halford (L.A.C.) and Barnes (Achilles) a start. Bird held his position, however, and was content to ease off in the finishing straight when third place was secure (South London H. were fourth) and first and second teams could not be overtaken.

Our team and all Belgrave Harriers present were delighted. Not only was it an historical moment for the Club, but it shows that we are within measurable distance of an A.A.A. Relay title. A little more improvement and our track teams may well command the respect during the Summer, that our teams are accorded in the Winter months.

* * * * *

In the A.A.A. Championships proper, a week later, our representatives acquitted themselves well. Pat Parker was placed third in the 3 miles race (his time being 14 min. 28 sec.), and earned selection against France the following week. Don Brown was third and Percy Wright fourth in the 2 miles walk, and Eddie Pack finished fourth in the final of the 440 yards championship. His time was 49 1-5 sec. and he is the fastest quarter-mile we have had since the days of George Dunckley. In fact, Eddie's running would have gained him a title in some years.

We were also glad to see J. Dodd, representing the Northern Counties A.A., win the Pole Vault. Dodd hopes to be back again with us shortly, following on his studies in the North, and we shall have a welcome for him.

Club members to gain standard medals, additional to those previously mentioned, were A. A. Bird (880 yards), A. W. Penny (6 miles), L. Cohen (3 miles), A. R. Shaw (Steeplechase), F. A. Walker and A. F. Mitchell (Marathon).

* * * * *

On July 24th we sent a team to Birmingham to compete in the "Waddilove" Trophy track competition for the first time. Birchfield Harriers have been anxious for years that we should participate, and at our first attempt we were glad to become joint-holders with the London A.C. of the handsome trophy. We were successful in winning the Two Miles team race through the efforts of A. W. Penny, L. J. Cohen and E. S. Stimpson; and took second place in the Relay (won by Birchfield), our team being A. A. Bird, R. G. Michael, S. C. Botwright and E. Gray. In addition Bird ran wonderfully well to finish second to J. V. Powell, beaten by a yard and a half in 1 min. 57 3-5 seconds. E. Gray was placed third in the 100 yards.

Final scores were : Belgrave H. and L.A.C., 12 points each; Birchfield H., 10 pts.; the holders, Achilles, scoring 1 point only. All our runners did well, and we returned feeling well satisfied with our first visit to Perry Bar.

Several of our Junior runners, too, have taken the eye and promise well for the future. Our relay team in the 4 x 110 yards Junior A.A.A. Championship qualified for the final and finished close up fourth. B. Saunders and F. Skelley showed up well. Skelley has, of course, earned our congratulations by virtue of winning the Southern Junior Long Jump Championship, and in taking second place in the A.A.A. Junior Championship with a fine leap of 21ft. 6½in.

Several others in our colours showed good style and may be expected to be in the top class when physical maturity arrives. We have had much pleasure in welcoming so many promising junior runners during the Summer season, and if their interest continues, as we have no doubt it will, our future on the track looks very bright.

* * * * *

It is curious that we should have had such a poor season in Southern Amateur Athletic League track events, when otherwise we have enjoyed much success. For example, we finished last in the S.A.A.L. 4 x 440 yards race (the Club Outing the day before may, perhaps, be blamed!) and yet practically the same team took third place in the A.A.A. Championship! Strange, yet, true!

Our comparatively poor form in S.A.A.L. events gives us added interest in the final event of this competition, the One Mile Medley Race, on August 21st. On this result depends whether we continue in Division I, in which we have competed since its inception, or whether we are relegated to Division II next season. Whichever way things go, we may be sure that we shall give these fixtures greater attention next season. If we continue in the upper ranks we shall be out to make sure that our position is not endangered, as has happened this year. If we compete in Division II it will be our earnest endeavour to head the League and regain our lost place. Certainly, this year's poor showing should shock us into doing something better next season.

* * * * *

Congratulations and best wishes to J. C. Edwards, who was recently taken unto himself a wife. Best of luck!

* * * * *

On September 4th the London—Brighton Walk takes place, starting from Westminster at 7 a.m. We shall have several entrants and as usual the Club Long Distance Championship, which carries with it the "Belgrave Cup," will take place in conjunction with the main race.

We shall have a bus on the road, leaving Big Ben at 7-30 a.m., and anyone desiring a seat (5/- fare) is asked to communicate with the Walking Hon. Secretary at once. This is always a good day out, and we hope to have a number of supporters on the road.

Anyone willing to act as cyclist attendant to our walkers is asked to get in touch with the Walking Hon. Secretary.

* * * * *

The Club year is approaching its end, and late in September, on a date to be advised, the Annual General Meeting will take place. It is the duty of all members to attend if they can possibly do so, and ensure that a representative and hard-working set of officials and committee are elected to guide the fortunes of the Club in the ensuing season. This is your responsibility. See that you attend the A.G.M. and carry out the privileges and obligations of membership in full.

ROUND THE MEETINGS.

In the "Kinnaird" Competition, held on May 29th, we failed to gain a point and yet there were several very fine performances by our members. It is to be hoped that one year we shall really be in a position to make a determined effort to wrest the "Kinaird" Trophy from its usual holders, Achilles Club. We have always felt that the system of scoring, and the events comprising the competition, hardly favour us, and we should dearly like to see a competition which included the other events held at the meeting, viz., Steeplechase, Marathon and Team Walk, and the addition of, say, the Javelin Throw. The choice of events is, of course, a matter for the promoters, the Polytechnic Harriers, whose kind invitation to participate we are always glad to accept.

In the "non-Kinnaird" events we did well. Our walkers dead-heated for first place with Woodford Green A.C. In the Southern Counties Steeplechase Championship, we had second and third men to finish in C. T. Carter and A. R. Shaw.

The Marathon was run under tropical conditions and only seven men finished inside standard time. The remainder of the competitors were not permitted to finish on the track owing to the Stadium authorities' restrictions. We were the only club to finish three men (Messrs. A. F. Mitchell, F. A. Walker and F. G. White) and at a committee meeting of the Polytechnic Harriers, the special carved oak team awards were voted to our men. We congratulate them upon finishing the course on an extremely difficult day.

In the County Championships, some of our men were seen to advantage. E. H. Pack won the Middlesex 440 yards championship, and A. A. Bird was third in the 880 yards, and T. D. Wiley third in the 440 yards Hurdles at the same meeting. Percy Wright distinguished himself by taking second place in the Walk, returning excellent time. He has since been invited to represent Middlesex.

In the Surrey championships, Don Brown and Joe Coleman were first and third in the Walk; and L. W. Herbert was narrowly beaten in the 880 yards race. When Herbert has learned to run his first 300 yards a little faster and in company, we shall expect him to beat 1 min. 56 sec. for half-mile.

In the Southern Championships held at Brighton, Brown was second to Cooper in the 2 miles Walk, and Bird finished fourth in the 880 yards, inside A.A.A. standard time.

At the various Open meetings, several of our men have been prominent, notably T. D. Wiley and S. C. Botwright, who have not been content with single wins!

Congratulations to all who have caught the judge's eye! Restrictions of space do not permit a full list of successes, and have also prevented the publication of an article by T. F. Morrell on successes at Woking.

THE CORONATION MEETING AT SOUTHGATE.

On May 15th, at Broomfield Park, the local club, Southgate Harriers, entertained Blackheath H., Polytechnic H. and ourselves in a series of team and relay events. Also on the programme were handicaps open to members of the four competing clubs.

We were more or less outclassed in the sprint events, Blackheath winning both the 4 x 110, and 4 x 220 yards. Also, owing to the earliness of the season, we could not field a team for the 4 x 440 yards.

The 4 x 880 yards was held in conjunction with the Southern Amateur League event at this distance, and although L. W. Herbert gave us the lead over the first stage, we eventually finished in fourth place, beating Mitcham A.C. and Herne Hill H. Southgate pleased the local crowd by just beating the Poly after a very close and exciting race.

In the 1 Mile Team Race, Pat Parker strode round in the lead, only to be beaten up the straight by P. E. Bull, of the Poly H. When the points had been added up it was found that Blackheath and Belgrave each scored 29, with Polytechnic 38, and Southgate 40.

We also fielded a team in the 1 Mile relay open to those who had not competed before that afternoon in the match events—in other words, a reserves race—but an unfortunate blunder at the first take-over resulted in the baton being dropped and so put our team out of the race.

So far, during the afternoon we had not gained many points, but along came our field event men to show the runners how it should be done! We won the Weight Putt with Grassau and Melchior, also the Javelin with George Powell and, once again, Melchior, who, by the way, materially contributed to our total of points.

The result of the match was : Southgate H., 37 pts.; Blackheath H., 34½ pts.; Belgrave H., 25½ pts.; Polytechnic H., 24 pts.

Our runners were more to the fore in the handicap events; mainly due to the fact that we provided the larger portion of the entries, especially in the 880 yards, which was won by Alby Bird off 8 yards. He ran splendidly to beat W. E. Lucas on the tape, so that Belgrave had the first two home.

The souvenir awards which were presented to all winners and runners-up were much appreciated by the competitors, and our best thanks are due to the Southgate Harriers for promoting such an interesting and pleasant afternoon's sport.

Report · page 8

MATCH versus OLD TIFFINIANS A.C. AND OLD MID-WHITGIFTIANS.

We were again blessed with a fine evening on May 27th for our match at Kingston. A very good and keenly contested meeting resulted which was very much appreciated by the spectators.

I am sure, however, that the high-lights of the meeting were our Shot Putters. Grassau, Jones and Bill Sutton monopolised the crowd once they started, and apparently they had not met men of their magnificent physique before.

Points were secured for us by E. L. Kealey (3rd, 100 yards); T. D. Wiley (3rd, 220 yards); A. H. Sampson (3rd, 440 yards); S. Garland and W. Ashford (2nd and 3rd, 880 yards); A. A. Bird and E. Stimpson (2nd and 3rd, 1 mile); E. L. Blyther (3rd, Long Jump); T. D. Wiley (1st, High Jump); F. Grassau and S. Jones (1st and 2nd, Shot Putt).

The Relay was won by our hosts, with our team second; and final scores were : Old Tiffinians, 44 pts.; Belgrave H., 28 pts.; Old Mid-Whitgiftians, 9 points. Thus concluded another most enjoyable fixture at the Old Tiffiinian's hospitable headquarters.

THE INTER-CLUB RELAYS AT TOOTING TRACK.

The first of the contests for the Inter-Club Relay Shield was held at the new Tooting Track. It was also the first time that one of these contests had been held on a Saturday, the usual practise being to run them on week-nights. From our point of view, however, the change was quite a success, although the track was on the loose side. Given time, we are sure this pleasantly situated ground will prove popular with the South London athletes.

In the first event, the 4 x 100 yards, we fielded quite a useful team; and, with F. W. Pritchard running a fine third leg, we had the satisfaction of beating Mitcham A.C., with Epsom beating Croydon for third place.

The 4 x 880 yards saw Dick Michael give us the lead on the first stage, but F. H. C. White was caught and passed by the Mitcham runner. Alby Bird went next and, running with fine judgment, soon overhauled his man, and sent L. W. Herbert off with a useful lead. Herbert ran well within himself and kept off the opposition without much trouble. Epsom again beat Croydon for third place; and their half-milers are beginning to weld themselves into a useful team.

We welcomed Eddie Kealey back into the active ranks for this meeting; while S. C. Botwright also turned out for us in the 4 x 300 yards. Tom Wiley went first and gave us the lead, which F. W. Pritchard and Botwright kept, to send Kealey away with a winning margin. Croydon reversed their placings with Epsom in this event.

Then came the 1 Mile Team race, and although we were without Pat Parker and Les Cohen, our team, led home by W. E. Lucas, ran well to win the team race. Harry Shields caused quite a stir by dashing into the lead when 300 yards from home and this seemed to act as a winning tonic to our team.

We had a few club handicaps at this meeting and in the 220 yards for Juniors, under 19, F. J. Skelley showed he was in sprinting form by winning from the 5 yards mark. G. H. Knight, a newcomer to our ranks, gave him a close race off 15 yards, and the scratch man, B. Saunders, ran well to gain third place.

In the 2 Miles Walk, E. C. A. Heather made a welcome reappearance and, with the handicapper being kind to him, won off 475 yards, with another long-marker in W. G. True, off 650 yards, second. Joe Coleman walked with plenty of strength and determination to finish third.

The 880 yards handicap was interesting because of the fine effort made by L. W. Herbert, off 25 yards. He cut through the field on the second lap, and won in fine style, with E. J. Selby (75) second, and S. Garland, off 45 yards, third.

Our first field event handicap of the season was the Shot Putt and E. L. Blyther (13ft. 6in.) showed improvement over previous performances by taking first place with 38ft. 8in. R. E. Stride, Snr. (16ft. 0in.) was second with 37ft. 4in. and S. Jones (1ft. 0in.) did well with 37ft. 0in. to take third place.

Report · page 10

THE SOUTHERN ATHLETIC LEAGUE.

As already mentioned elsewhere in this issue, the first of the contests in the S.A.A.L., Division I, was the 4 x 880 yards held at Southgate, on May 15th. Owing to the earliness of the season, our team was not at full strength and some of the other competing clubs were in a similar position. Southgate made an excellent debut in the 1st Division by beating the Poly in a close race. The other placings were : Blackheath 3, Belgrave 4, Mitcham 5, and Herne Hill H. 6.

The next event was the 4 x 220 yards at the "Kinnard" meeting at the White City. Sprinters have never been our strong point, and we were not very optimistic about our chances here. As expected, Blackheath were winners, with Herne Hill H. second, and the Poly third. Then came Southgate H., and we just managed to beat our rivals, Mitcham A.C., for last place.

So far, results had not been too encouraging, but with the longer distances to come we were confident that we would be more to the fore.

The 1 Mile team race was held at the White City in conjunction with the Civil Service meeting, and without doubt was the finest event on the programme. Pat Parker soon went to the front and led the field round at a good pace. Out team were running well, and the biggest danger appeared to be Blackheath. Over the last lap Hodges, of Southgate, went in front, followed by Poole, Blackheath, and A. V. Reeve, of the Poly. Pat had dropped to fifth place but by a determined effort down the straight he pegged one back and so finished 4th. W. E. Lucas had run well to gain 8th place, and Tom Carter and Ted Stimpson finished in 12th and 13th positions. We had to wait until the judges had sorted the men out and then came the result : Polytechnic H. 35 pts., Blackheath H 36 pts., Belgrave H. 37 pts. Thus we had lost first place by two points, and yet were placed only third. A closer finish could not be imagined and although scoring one point less than last year, when we were second, we were now one place worse off!

We came then to the 3 Miles team race, which was held at the Herne Hill Track. In the individual race, D. B. Pell, of Herne Hill H., ran a fine race to win from Hodges, of Southgate, with Les Cohen, of our own club, third. We seem always to be the unlucky club when it comes to close finishes in team races; and this was no exception. When the points had been totalled up it was found that Mitcham A.C. scored 31, with Belgrave 32. Of the other members of our team, Tom Carter was 6th, with Arthur Penny 7th, while Harry Shields had an off day in being placed 16th. Thus, if Pat Parker had been there it would have been "in the bag," but I seem to remember other occasions when we have had cause to think of the old saying, "there's many a slip . . . . "

The 4 x 440 yards was held on a Monday evening at the new Ladywell Park track. While not being the best of days on which to hold a sports meeting, the track was also in a very loose state, and the running of the meeting left much to be desired. As to the race itself, Polytechnic H. were the winners from Southgate H., with Herne Hill H. 3rd, Blackheath 4th, Mitcham A.C. 5th, and the "Bels" last. It must be said in fairness to our lads that on the previous day they had been on the Club's outing to Littlehampton and their strenuous exertions "by the sea" had not left them in a very fit state for running. The irony of the thing is that a week later the same team, with Eddie Park, taking the place of Tom Scrimshaw, gained third place in the A.A.A. 4 x 440 yards relay!

The last event in the League programme is the 1 Mile Medley Relay on August 21st, at Herne Hill. We have not been able to ascertain the correct league placings, but are informed that we lead Herne Hill H. and Mitcham A.C. by 1 point for last place. As the bottom club is relegated to the 2nd Division, it looks as if there is every possibility of the "Bels" being the unlucky one. However, the race had not been run yet, so here's hoping! Whatever the result, I am sure our boys will enter upon next season's contests with just the same enthusiasm they have shewn during the present one, which after all is the spirit we want. The results can look after themselves.

As regards the Field Events Section of the League, I understand we are at present leading in this contest, but here again we have been unable to find out the positions in the table. It seems as though this season has not shewn quite the same success as in the past. By this I mean among the clubs generally; and it looks very much as if the whole position will have to be overhauled at the next A.G.M. of the S.A.A.L.

Report · page 11

INTER-CLUB RELAYS AT CROYDON.

The second Inter-Club relay contest was held on June 1st at the Croydon Harriers' ground. Mitcham appeared to have a stronger team and seemed out to prevent a repetition of the Tooting result.

In the 4 x 100 yards Mitcham were first with ourselves just beating Croydon for second place.

The 4 x 880 yards saw a ding-dong battle between the "Mental Hospital" boys and our lads; and over the first leg Frank Webb was just beaten by Leadbeater. P. Dee, the Welsh "crack," went next, with Alby Bird in hot pursuit. The leader, however, was not to be caught and, running in strong style, increased his club's lead. Our Track Captain, Harry Shields, not only caught his rival, but sent L. W. Herbert off with a slight lead. Roberts, the young Mitcham runner, trailed his man well, but nearing home Herbert went away to win in convincing fashion.

Our team in the 4 x 300 yards started off well, F. W. Pritchard giving us the lead which Botwright kept. We then ran G. Camkin, who was making his first appearance in these contests, but although he tried hard enough, Mitcham, and then Croydon, passed him. Tom Wiley was thus given a hopeless task in attempting to catch the leader, but running in determined style, he beat the Croydon runner down the straight to give us second place.

We came then to the Mile team race and, after a good race, Mitcham beat us by two points. They had the first two home in Dee and Brickwood; and although our boys finished 4, 5, 6, 7, it was not good enough!

Thus at the end of the evening the points up to date were: Belgrave H., 29; Mitcham A.C., 27; Epsom H., 12; Croydon H., 12.

Report · page 12

CLUB MEETING ON JUNE 9th.

On this evening we had a triangular match with Imperial College A.C. and the Leigh-on-Sea Harriers. The Club 3 Miles Championship was also decided, and in addition we had two races open to unattached novices on the programme.

Tom Wiley won the 100 yards match, with F. W. Pritchard third. An Imperial College man was second, with Leigh gaining fourth place.

We also had first two home in the 220 yards, S. C. Botwright and F. W. Pritchard doing the needful.

Pat Parker turned out for us in the Mile and won in easy fashion, with A. R. Shaw second. Towell, of Leigh, was third, with Britton, Imperial College, fourth.

Eddie Pack was also a starter in the 440 yards match, but was content to take things easy after his Middlesex County win the previous Saturday. Alby Bird raced into the lead, with Pack following, and they finished in that order, with Leigh filling third and fourth places.

We thus led easily on the track events, but some closer competition was seen in the field contests.

F. Grassau did 37ft. 8½in. in the Shot Putt, and C. A. Melchior gained third place with 31ft. 4in.

The High Jump saw Leigh and the Imperial College gaining the points; and in the Javelin, M. Bradley threw 147ft. 9in., to beat Melchior.

T. C. Jones won the Long Jump match; and also proved the winner of the club Long Jump handicap.

The 3 Miles club championship saw a field of 14, although Pat Parker decided not to compete. He rather wisely preferred to concentrate on the shorter distances until later in the season, and his running in the recent A.A.A. championships proved the wisdom of his decision. Ted Stimpson cut out the pace in the early stages, but a little bunch of runners soon moved up to him, and then J. C. Smart had bad luck in sustaining a fall which put him out of the race. We then saw Les Cohen, Tom Carter, and Arthur Penny move away from the rest; although Dan Patience was running well close behind. In the last lap a hectic struggle ensued. The pace proved too much for Penny, and down the straight Cohen got the better of Carter in a rousing finish. Dan Patience also sprinted past Arthur Penny near the tape, although it seemed as if the latter had thrown his hand in. 1st class standards were gained by A. W. Penny, A. H. Sampson (who ran very well over this distance), H. W. Shields and Arthur Allum. 2nd class medals were also gained by Alf Taylor and R. W. Tobin.

In the club handicaps we saw F. J. Skelley run well to gain second place in the 100 yards, under 19. G. K. Talbot, with 7 yards start, proved the winner. Skelley was off 1 yard, and L. F. Barber, off 6 yards, was third.

A newcomer in A. S. Blunden won the club 100 yards, off 8½ yards, with S. C. Botwright, off 7 yards (a mark he will not see again), second, and Alec Mewes, with 5½ yards, third. The latter certainly seems to run better in club handicaps than he does in open competition, but perhaps he is saving it up. Who knows?

As was generally expected, W. E. Lucas took advantage of his mark to win the 880 yards in fast time. J. H. Chantler ran well to gain second place, with Tommy Morrell third. Apart from the winner, the finish was very close, a bunch of runners making a dash at the finishing line.

In the 2 Miles Walk handicap, L. J. Coleman made good use of his mark; with another long marker in W. G. True, second. Charlie Redgrave made a welcome re-appearance and walked well to gain third place.

With regard to the Novice events, owing to lack of entries we had to cancel the 100 yards, and only three runners turned out for the 1 Mile. The winner was N. Welsford in moderate time, and it really seems a pity we cannot get a better turn-out in these races. I am sure if only the club members would co-operate we should meet with more success. I trust that if we decide to hold these events in the future that all members will do their best to interest any of their friends who are, as yet, not attached to any recognised athletic club.

THE ANNUAL TRACK WALKS.

Our annual promotion of four scratch walks at 5, 3, 2 and 1 miles was held at Battersea Park on June 14th, and nine clubs entered teams. The usual conditions were in force and two men were allowed in each event from each club, no man being allowed to turn out twice in the evening. 18 points were allocated to the winner's club, 17 points to second, and so on, downwards, in each event.

The first race was at five miles and Redman (Metropolitan W.C.) and A. H. G. Pope (Woodford Green A.C.) had a spirited duel over the best part of the journey, which carried them well away from the other competitors. Both men are record-holders and their struggle was worthy of the high reputations both men bear in walking circles. Towards the end of the race, Redman drew on his reserves of strength and won by a distance. Our representatives were C. E. Churcher, who finished eighth, and A. Readman, who suffered disqualification.

THE "DORIS" SHIELD CONTEST.

On June 17th we journeyed to Richmond for the annual "Doris" Shield match with Thames Valley H., and Ashcombe-St. George's A.C.

We were joint holders of the shield with T.V.H., but any hopes we might have had were rudely dispelled with the result of the 100 yards.

Lester and Warner, both of T.V.H., were first and second, with Liffen, of Ashcombe-St. George's, third, and Tom Wiley fourth.

In the 440 yards match we had Eddie Pack and he duly won from A. D. G. White, of T.V.H. They also had the third man in R. J. Green, and we were sadly lacking a second string in this race. Tom Wiley, who ran for us, did his best, but was clearly running beyond his distance.

We fielded a useful team in the 2 Miles team race and Arthur Penny ran away from his opponents to win in 9 mins. 38 secs. J. C. Smart ran well to finish in second place, while our team was completed by H. G. Verney, the ex-Birchfield runner. The team race result was: Belgrave H., 1; Thames Valley H., 2; Ashcombe-St. George's, 3.

With the last event to be decided our chances of even sharing the shield were gone, and Thames Valley consolidated their lead by winning the 1 Mile medley relay. Alec White beat Alby Bird over the half-mile stage, and although Dick Michael ran well in the 440 yards leg, Les. Bennett and S. C. Botwright could not hold the T.V.H. sprinters.

Thus the final points scored were: Thames Valley H., 30; Belgrave H., 22; Ashcombe-St. George's A.C., 14.

In the usual handicaps open to members of the three competing clubs, we saw J. Warner (who was second in the scratch race) receiving 7½ yards start and he won in 9 4-5 secs. A Belgrave junior in W. H. Bowers was second, off 10 yards, with a T.V.H. youngster, also off 10 yards, third. It was in every respect a long marker's race and the back men, to put it frankly, did not stand an "earthly."

A small field was seen in the 2 Miles Walk, there being six Belgrave competitors and one Thames Valley man. Our Walking Hon. Sec., Alf Harley, walked well, however, to win, with Don Brown second, and Harry Churcher, off the scratch mark, third.

A better race was seen in the 1 Mile handicap, and on the final lap it looked as if W. R. King, of T.V.H., off 135 yards, would be the winner. E. J. Selby, of the "Bels," off 115 yards, thought otherwise, however. Tommy Morrell, off 90 yards, also put in a spectacular finish and eventually King was placed third. Frank Webb, off 35 yards, ran well to finish a close fourth in what was quite a fast-run race.

CLUB CHAMPIONSHIP DAY, July 3rd, 1937.

We were certainly blesssed with fine weather on our Championship Day, and the performances, if not brilliant, were sound; and the finishes very close and keenly fought out.

A new holder had to be found for the Boys, under 15, race, owing to the age limit, and A. V. Heitman, the winner, is full of promise.

The newly-instituted 100 yards Junior race gave F. J. Skelley a chance to show his sprinting powers. In view of his successes at the Long Jump, we can certainly expect even better jumping, consequent upon his improved speed.

In the 100 yards championship, E. Gray once again proved the best of our short distance men, and retained his hold on the "Grosvenor Cup." Tom Wiley gained one of his many place medals. He picked up four in all—quite a field day!

We had been promised a good race in the 880 yards, and so it proved. Alby Bird ran finely to win in time which equalled Tommy Scrimshaw's record of 1 min. 59 1-5 sec. L. W. Herbert was a good second; and Dick Michael, the holder, had to be content with third place.

For the first time we held a Junior 880 yards race, and although there was only a small field, it was a most interesting contest. H. G. Neve proved the winner. L. Marchant, who was second, is only 16 years of age, but with careful training should make them all go in a few years' time.

As usual, the Mile provided the spectators with an exciting race, and a field of 18 faced the starter. Les Cohen soon showed that he was determined to keep in front; but W. E. Lucas hung on grimly, while our old war-horse, Arthur Penny, was not far behind. Round the last lap they went, but try as he would, Lucas could not get by, and down the straight Cohen strode away to win in 4 min. 33 secs.

The 220 yards Junior saw the holder, B. Saunders, show a clean pair of heels to his rivals, although G. K. Talbot ran well to finish second, while R. S. Turner followed up his third in the 100, by filling the same position here.

We had a surprise result in the 220 yards championship, for the holder, E. Gray, was beaten a foot by S. C. Botwright, who followed up his recent "Open" successes by annexing the "Elsie Cup." Botwright is a former club junior champion and gives promise of even better performances in the future.

Owing to our date clashing with the Police championships, we were denied the sight of Eddie Pack beating the club record of 51 secs. in the 440 yards championship. The race, however, provided us with the closest finish of the day. Alby Bird and L. W. Herbert came down the straight locked together; and Alby gained the verdict by inches. Dick Michael lost another of his titles here, but ran well to finish a close third. Tommy Scrimshaw made a come-back in this race and gave us all a thrill by leading up to the home straight. We all hope to see Tommy once again racing fit, and he certainly has plenty of racing years left in him yet.

The 2 Miles Walk saw the holder, W. A. Treacher, and Harry Churcher both non-starters. Don Brown, however, showed he is coming back to his best form by beating Percy Wright, with Joe Coleman pipping his young brother for third place. A feature of this race was the number of ex-runners competing (no offence meant!) and even our Percy has been seen tearing up the sprinters!

Our five field event championships saw some good performances, and here again soundness of effort predominated.

F. Grassau was an absentee from the Shot Putt and Bill Sutton gained first place with 35ft. 3in. Ben May gained his first place medal here and should gain encouragement from this.

In the Javelin, C. A. Melchior became first holder of the "Dailey Cup" with a throw of 155ft. 8in.; and concentration on this particular event would see further improvement.

The High Jump saw the holder, H. M. Quackenbos, who is our Junior Captain, retain his title with a leap of 5ft. 2in.; and here again increased height can be expected. "Bud" Hutchinson was second, and with more serious training might show us some of his Canadian form.

S. Jones won the Discus Throw with Bill Sutton as runner-up. In fact one might say "the old firm" did their stuff!

Another "Dailey Cup" was awarded to the winner of the Long Jump, and we are very grateful to the donor for these welcome additions to our club trophy list. F. J. Skelley, with a fine leap of 21ft. 6in., became the first holder of the cup, and E. Gray also jumped well with an effort of 20ft. 5in. The fact of Gray and Wiley being placed proves once again that speed is essential in this branch of sport. Jesse Owens is, of course, of course the most classic example, and I hope our junior long-jumpers will remember this most important factor.

We had another event on our programme which proved quite a success, namely, a 1 Mile medley relay open to Schools. Six teams competed, and the Latymer Upper School ran home winners, due, in the main to a very fine run by their half-miler. This race was in the nature of an experiment and the result was gratifying to those who had urged its inclusion. We hope that next year we shall have an even bigger entry and so help develop the latent talent that these schools must possess. We trust our worthy Hon. Editor was wearing his "old school tie," and are sure he must have been pleased with his old school's victory.

The number of standard medals gained in the various club championhips showed the interest that is taken in these awards, but we should like to see even greater interest, and trust that next year will see more club members entering these events.

THE CLUB OUTING.

A trip to Littlehampton was planned on Sunday, July 4th, and received a fair measure of support, about forty members and friends contributing to the gaiety of the occasion. Clouds threatened and a strong wind did its best to upset the party, but fortunately there was no rain. Football, cricket, rounders, swimming, rock-climbing (ask Footer!) passed the time, and contributed those aches and pains which were a feature of the next day. However, the trip was generally voted to be an excellent day out; and we record our grateful thanks to our Social Hon. Secretary for his work in connection with the outing.

Deserving of special mention is the promptitude and celerity with which Mick Walker met a sudden emergency on the journey outwards. His quickness of thought and physical advantages combined to make him the man of the moment, and to transcend the now almost legendary Colley Hill incident with which the name of Shields is for ever linked.

THE CLUB MEETING ON JULY 14th.

On this evening we had a Junior match with our old friends, the Thames Valley Harriers, and also several club handicaps.

The match events were to be on relay lines, but in the 100 yards event T.V.H. were only able to field two boys, and so we made it a scratch race only. F. J. Skelley won readily enough from W. L. Kuy, of T.V.H., with Belgrave filling the third and fourth positions.

In the 880 yards Team race J. Bavage, of T.V.H., a very useful runner, won in 2 mins. 7 secs. They also had the second lad, with H. G. Neve, N. C. Shrubb, L. Marchant, all of Belgrave, in the 3rd, 4th and 5th positions. Thames Valley thus won the team race, and the score was "one all."

Skelley did not jump for us in the Junior Long Jump, but the winner was a Belgrave lad, A. S. Peverell, who jumps with a good style and should certainly improve with steady training. The Thames Valley boy was second, with R. Dunford third.

The final event was the 3 x 200 yards relay, and over the first leg G. K. Talbot gave us a useful lead. V. D. Wynyard went next, but Bavage, for T.V.H., slowly cut down his advantage. Wynyard held on, to send Skelley away in front of his rival. The latter ran well and did not give his opponent a chance to be on terms.

We thus won the match by 3 events to 1, and are looking forward to the return match later in the season.

In the 100 yards (under 16) handicap, a close final was seen, the scratch man, L. F. Barber, being beaten by inches by P. McMahon, off 8 yards; S. A. Bailey, off 1 yard, was third; with P. Kearney, Jnr., off 7, fourth.

The 100 yards Veterans resulted in a win for A. V. Chetland; with H. H. Baumann, off scratch, second; and Dicky Best, third. Charlie Cook was also in the final, but did not display his customary "dash."

For excitement the 880 yards handicap wanted some beating. First, Harry Shields, off 35 yards, looked like winning, but appeared to have made his effort too soon. Then A. H. Beaumont, off 65, went in front, only to be passed in the last few yards by S. Garland, off 42. The final placings were: Garland, Beaumont, Shields, with W. J. Ashford fourth.

The 220 yards final was also a close affair, a few yards covering the five finalists. E. A. Keyworth, off 17 yards, proved the winner, with Alby Bird, off 12, second; and R. A good race was also seen in the club 2 Miles Walk handicap, J. A. Belchamber, a son of our past President, making good use of his 525 yards start, to lead until half-way round the last lap, where E. C. A. Heather, off 350 yards—a liberal mark—caught him although he only won by a few yards. Joe Coleman walked excellently off 175 yards to gain third place; with his brother "L.J." once again close behind him.

THE R.W.A. 50 KILOS CHAMPIONSHIP.

By G. S. DOUBLEDAY.

This was held over a hilly 5-lap course from De Bohun's Farm, Enfield, nine clubs competing. It was fairly warm at the start, but cooled down considerably and a coldish wind blew towards the finish.

After a couple of laps (12½ miles) the Metropolitan Walking Club had their three scoring men in seven positions, scoring 12 to our 25, but we had our seven men in the first 20 places, so we had plenty to draw upon for our three scoring men. At 18¾ miles M.W.C. led us 16—22; the Whitlock brothers being 1st and 3rd, their third man, Redman, having dropped to 10th. Fred Bentley was 6th. Fred Rickards was having a bad time, and had dropped back a little. Jack Bidgood and Colin Sutton were walking well together for our third scoring position.

After 4 laps (with 6¼ miles to go), M.W.C. had a 4 point advantage. Rickards had recovered and was his old self again, and was coming along just behind Bentley. Bidgood was walking strongly two places behind. It was now seen that barring accident the M.W.C. would win, as with 1st and 3rd and only 3 to score, they held an enormous advantage. However, our men kept pegging away.

H. H. Whitlock recorded his fourth successive win, being followed by D. H. Christie-Murrey, and his brother G. B. R. Then Woodford Green provided the next two men home, but their third man was well down the field. Then came our own trio, Rickards, Bentley and Bidgood, in 6th, 7th and 8th positions, scoring 21 points to the 16 pts. which the M.W.C. subsequently totalled.

Of our non-scoring men, Sutton was always close at hand for our third position, but was content to finish outside first-class standard time ( 5 hrs. 5 mins.) and thus preserve his "Junior" status. His recent performances stamp him as a first-class distance walker. R. Martin, A. Readman and S. C. Roberts all gained second-clas standards (5 hrs. 15 mins.).

Whilst offering the Metropolitan Walking Club our heartiest congratulations on winning the "Horlick Cup" from us (we have held it for the last 3 years), our men's performances indicate that the Cup will soon be back in our possession again.

A feature of the race was that we started seven walkers (more than any other club), and that all seven finished a most difficult course. Our four men who did not take team medals, all gained second-class medals. We are grateful to our scoring trio for once again making a fine show on the Club's behalf, particularly as Bentley has been unable to train seriously owing to business difficulties, and that both Bidgood and Rickards have been off colour.

BELGRAVE HARRIERS v. KENSINGTON ARGYLL A.A.

This fixture was contested at Southfields on July 10th and will not be found on our current list, but was arranged at short notice by one of the K.A.A.A. officials—none other than Percy Wright! Match events were held at 100 yards, 220 yards, 440 yards, 880 yards, Mile Walk and Mile Medley Relay. The programme commenced at 8 p.m., and let it suffice to say that Belgrave ran out easy winners, as expected. The Business House Club's athletic section is now being revived, and we were very glad to give a helping hand. In the Walk, amid strange cries, a Kensington Argyll man won easily—none other than Percy Wright!

At the conclusion of a very pleasant fixture on a well-kept and nicely situated ground, we adjourned to the Pavilion for dancing, etc. Prizes were presented to the placed men in the 880 yard handicap (which attracted sixty entries from members of the two clubs and was won by N. Isaacs, with "Snake" Webb second, and E. Stimpson third) and a speech of welcome was made and seconded—by none other than Percy Wright.

It may be said that all the Belgrave Harriers present enjoyed this Saturday evening fixture; and look forward to another match next season. No doubt it will be arranged and if so our gratitude will be due to none other than Percy Wright!

WITH THE WALKERS.

A team represented the Club in Lewis's 25 Miles Outer Circle Walk at Birmingham, and after a keen struggle with Leicester Walking Club, finished second.

Our scoring men were: F. Rickards (6), J. C. Bidgood (7), C. F. Sutton (8) and A. Readman (17).

Colin Sutton is proving himself a dependable distance walker, and should strengthen our Senior team next season.

* * * * *

As described elsewhere, after three successive wins in the R.W.A. 50 kilos Championship, we have lost the title to Metropolitan W.C. Their win was an impressive one, and their scoring three—Harold Whitlock (1), Rex Whitlock (3) and Fred Redman (11)—well deserved their gold Championship medals, and the honour of winning the first Senior Championship for their club.

* * * * *

In a letter of thanks for our written congratulations, the Metropolitan W.C. Hon. Secretary writes: "We are, of course, most gratified that we have won a Senior Championship at long last, and we shall always remember with gratitude the help and encouragement we have constantly received from your club."

* * * * *

Congratulations to Percy Wright upon obtaining an A.A.A. standard, and so qualifying for his Club Honours badge.

* * * * *

In Open track handicap our walkers have been prominent, and among the prize-winners have been noticed: J. G. Coleman (a double first), L. J. Coleman, P. G. Wright (twice), E. C. Heather (twice), A. A. Harley and J. Cade.

* * * * *

Cyclist attendants are wanted to assist our walkers in the Brighton Walk on September 4th. If you are able to assist in this way, or have a friend willing to help our men, please get in touch with our Walking Hon. Secretary. Proper assistance and careful attention can do much for a walker in this test, and it is hoped that all our men will have adequate assistance in their efforts on the Club's behalf.

* * * * *

In the Polytechnic H. Invitation 2 Miles Team Walk held at the Kinnaird Meeting, we dead-heated for first place with Woodford Green A.C. Our scoring team was J. G. Coleman, P. G. Wright, J. E. Rutland and C. E. Churcher.

* * * * *

The Winter season will soon be on us and we hope to have a successful time. We should be glad to welcome some new talent, and the Novice race held early in the season should provide an opportunity for our members who have not yet won a prize for walking; and for friends of members willing "to have a go."

* * * * *

All entries and enquiries should be addressed to: A. A. HARLEY, 39, Roxborough Avenue, Isleworth Mx.

THE CLOSE SEASON.

Another track season will soon be over. (Thank Heaven! say the track captains, who are responsible for fielding teams in the various fixtures). Taken as a whole, however, I think we have done moderately well. New members have come in steadily and the club spirit is as well established as ever. As to results, after all the sport is the thing and although we are naturally pleased at any success we may gain, so long as our members enjoy their racing and the companionship of their club-mates, then we are satisfied that the club is proceeding along the right lines. As regards new members, especially the Junior ones, for Heaven's sake, if you feel that you are not making the progress you hoped, either athletically or as regards knowing your fellow-members, please get in touch with some of the club officials, who are always at Battersea Park. In a club of our size it is almost impossible to recognise every member, so I do ask you to make yourselves known, so that we can do our best on your behalf. One of the best ways of doing this is through the medium of club handicaps. By entering these you become known to the Handicapper (there are probably a few who wish they were not so well known to him!) and, moreover, you meet your fellow-members in competition and so become known to them. These remarks apply to track and cross-country alike and, with the Winter Season gradually approaching, I hope all our members will come along and sample the hospitality of "Belgrave Hall."

Before coming to the start of our Winter fixtures, I would just like to comment on our remaining track dates.

On August 18th we have arranged a Junior match with the Hercules A.C. (Streatham). This will be held on the Tooting Bec Track and I am sure the first meeting between us will be an interesting one.

On Saturday, August 21st, we have our usual fixture with the Metropolitan Police A.C. at Imber Court. This is a fixture always looked forward to by our lads. Everything is done for the comfort of the athlete, and there is even an open air swimming bath after the serious side is finished. Unfortunately, on the same afternoon we have the 1 Mile Medley relay in the S.A.A.L., which will be at the Herne Hill Track, in connection with the Fire Brigade sports at this venue.

We come then to our last track fixture, which is on August 28th, at Battersea Park. Our visitors in a series of match events will be the Cambridge Harriers and Highgate Harriers. It will be the first time we have had the pleasure of meeting Cambridge H., while Highgate are old and respected opponents. Also on the programme we have the following club handicaps: 100 yards, 1 Mile run, Discus Throw and High Jump. I trust we shall have good entries in this our last meeting of our Jubilee track season.

And so off with the old love and on with the new; or, maybe, in this case it should be the other way round. I am sure we have some members who simply long for the winter to come round again. And here again a word to new members: if you are at a loose end during the winter afternoons, come along to Wimbledon and have a run out over the surrounding commons and parks. We have packs to suit all paces, and there is even what we term a sprinters' pack. This is composed of those members who do not want to do any serious racing over the country, but simply wish to keep fit until the track season comes round again. There is a wealth of wisdom in this, and for youngsters, especially, nothing can build up body and stamina more than a good steady jog over the country.

We usually open at "Belgrave Hall," once known as St. John's Hall, but now re-decorated and the property of the club, about the last two weeks in September. On the first Saturday in October we hold the 3 miles running and 5 miles walking handicaps, open to all club members.

The following week we have two Novice races, these being a 3 miles run and 3 miles walk. Entry is free and the races are open to all Unattached novices, and also Belgrave novices. I might mention that a prize at running does not debar you from competing in the walking event; and, of course, vice-versa.

On October 23rd we have a match with the Tyrian club, which is composed of past and present members of the London University.

We come, then, to the annual triangular fixture between Ashcombe-St. George's A.C., Thames Valley H. and ourselves. It is the former club's turn to stage the race, which will be held on October 30th. This race is an all-to-score affair, so the more that turn up from each club, the more the merrier is the race.

On November 6th we are very pleased to welcome to Wimbledon the Cambridge University Cross Country club. With the South London Harriers, they will meet us in a triangular match over our course. Some of our lads have already had the pleasure of meeting the Cambridge athletes over their own course, as members of Mr. Holt's team in the annual match between these teams.

The second Saturday in November is always the date for the South of Thames "Junior" Team race, and I hope all our members who are eligible for this (if in doubt, I will soon advise you) will do their best to turn out. We used to enter five teams for this race, but of late years, partly due to so many men getting barred, we have not done so well.

By the time our next issue is before you, we shall have the Winter fixture cards printed; so I will comment on other forthcoming events at a later date.

All entries, and any enquiries, to

E. A. DUFFETT, Asst. Hon. Sec., 10, Barrow Road, Waddon, Surrey.
