Editorial · page 3

EDITORIAL.

The fourth war-time track season has seen Belgrave to the fore, and performances have, all things considered, maintained a very high level. Our mainstays have been first and foremost Club Matches, with Open and "Holidays at Home" Meetings providing handicap and scratch events. Many familiar names have disappeared or have appeared at long intervals in the results, but new names have taken their places, and we can be assured of a strong and keen nucleus for future teams.

Many of our absent friends have written to us, and their news, so eagerly awaited and read by their Clubmates, occupies a large portion of this issue. Add to this the results, read and re-read by members thousands of miles from Tooting and Wimbledon, and we get little room for those witty and saucy articles which members used to write. It is hoped, therefore, that it will not read too much like a catalogue or time table. If it does, the only suggestion is to take it in small doses, the paper rationing allows of no sugar for the pill!

Finally, a word of thanks to the chief supplier of "copy," Harry Parker, and to Ernie Duffett, Syd Roberts and Charlie Jones for the news from their respective sections.

THE HON. SECRETARY REPORTS.

Thanks must be given to all of those members who gave their time to officiate at our meetings at Tooting during the season. I'm not going to mention any names in case I miss anybody.

There are still many members serving in the Forces who have not written to the Club. The Hon. Secretary would welcome letters. News of others revives past pleasures and good times had with the Club, so just write a few odd lines. If you have not got the Hon. Secretary's address write to him at Belgrave Hall.

The Attendance Book has been placed in Belgrave Hall for your benefit. It helps the sectional Hon. Secretaries to know who are turning up for training and also gives us a guide to the numbers using the Hall. Our ration allowance is not too big, and if proof of numbers can be given, perhaps a bigger allowance could be obtained. So don't forget to sign the Book.

WANTED: Club vests. New ones are now unobtainable, so if you have a spare one don't let the moths eat it up, let the Hon. Secretary have it to pass on to one of our new members.

Belgrave Hall will be open on Wednesday evenings. It is most essential that white clothing should be worn during black-out.

We are most grateful to the Polytechnic Harriers for the help that their members have been to us during the Track Season, particularly Metcalfe and Maurice Wright. The services of Doug. Wilson were not always available owing to many calls on him by Jack Crump for representative matches. We hope to have the pleasure of the Poly C.C. runners and walkers at Belgrave Hall during the winter season.

We have to record our most sincere thanks and appreciation for the continued success of the Supporters' Club. This is almost entirely due to the hard labours of Les Cohen; assisted by Mrs. Cohen. Between them they are largely responsible for a considerable portion of the Club's income. So thank you both, keep it going!

A very interesting letter was recently received from R. A. Abel, who was with the Canadians when they visited us at Belgrave Hall in February, 1942. Abel was first man home for the Canadians, and was presented with a Club Spoon as a momento. In sending his regards to the Club, he wants a copy of the photograph taken that day. Can any member oblige? Members may also be interested to know that the Canadian Officer, Lt.-Col. La Blatt, who started the race, led the assault on the Casino in the Dieppe raid.

At the A.G.M. of the R.W.A. Southern Committee, held in September, Syd Roberts was re-elected to the Committee. Frank Thomson, Alf Harley and Jack Bidgood are past Chairmen, so we are well represented.

One of our walkers of a dozen or so years ago, Charles Briggs, has been sent to N. Africa on Police Administrative work with the rank of Colonel.

Column · page 5

NEWS OF MEMBERS.

Alf Harley, writing from Blackpool, says that he went for a five miles cross-country run with 15 others (out of 5,000). "The spirit was willing, but the flesh was very weak—not to say much too substantial; however, I enjoyed my plod."

Ken O'Kell is keeping fit with cycling and climbing the Derbyshire Peaks—another cross-country man gone west! He writes: "The Gazette is such fine reading after the shallow dribble of the daily press." Thanks for the compliment, Ken!

Frank Webb is now an L.A.C. and is "on tour" again. Having "done" Lincolnshire, he had a brief stay in Yorkshire, and is now in Worcestershire for re-grading. In the meantime he has turned gardener.

Reg Shepherd. We learn with regret that he was reported missing from air reconnaissance over Europe during May. Further news is awaited.

Dennis Rodaway, now a Lieutenant, R.A.N.S., and looking remarkably well, came along to the first meeting at Tooting in May.

Dick Norton, our very promising junior sprinter, is suffering from acute synovitis, sustained whilst playing football, and has been ordered not to run again for some months. Hard lines, Dick, and best wishes for a quick and complete disappearance of your trouble.

Charlie Warren, an old-time walker from Erith, sends his best wishes to all his Belgrave acquaintances.

Harold King, a former Club and Kent County Walking Champion, has had an operation on his eyes (cataract). He is now back at work and hopes to be a competitor in the next Brighton Walk.

Syd King, an old-timer, and when turning out salvage recently, passed his news-cuttings and records on to the Hon. Secretary, who found them very interesting and will in turn pass them on to the Club Historian. Others, please copy.

Fred Clay, apart from his sprinting abilities, has gained the Higher National Certificate in Mechanical Engineering. Congrats, Fred!

Fred Brunning, who is a cyclist as well as a walker, tried his hand at gymnastics recently. When cycling through Balham, he tried a hand-spring on the handlebars, but landed in the road on his neck—which he gave a crick! However, a week's rest in hospital, with the sister of Joe and Jack Flower to hold his hand, soon put him right again.

Les Bennett. Mrs. Bennett informs us that she has heard from Les at a Prisoner of War Camp in Japan. We rejoice with her on having heard from him after such a trying period. Communication is difficult, and further news is awaited.

Charlie Churcher. Still winning races out in the Middle East. In one race he missed the record by four seconds. By now he may have beaten it. Sends his regards to all his many Belgrave friends, and would eagerly swop a stretch of sand for some of the roads round Wimbledon Common.

Harry Churcher, now nearer to London, but unable to fit in any regular training. Turned out for the R.A.F. at the Epsom Meeting, but was disqualified. Hard luck, Harry! Hope we'll be seeing you on the road this winter.

Joe Coleman writes: "Keep the flag flying, and when we all get back home after this "do" we'll show our appreciation for the Bels at home who have kept dear old Belgrave alive." Thanks, Joe, we will! Joe himself has turned out in one or two track walks and given good performances.

Len Coleman, on the move again, last heard of at Sierra Leone. Spent a week-end at a French resort to which the Plymouth meeting seemed "like a baby show." To quote him further: "Vive L'Entente Cordiale!"

Ted Jackman, transferred to Portsmouth (N.F.S.). Has recently been up to town for an operation on his foot.

Percy Wright, in addition to the B.N.L., organises sports meetings and walks in Monmouth. When he can, he pops up to town for a race. Can still do with more Airgraphs for the "Overseas Cup." If you have any, he will be pleased to accept them at 14 Chapel Farm, Terrace, Cwmcarn, Newport, Mon. (Stop Press: Is now a Corporal. Congrats, Percy.)

Eddie Jury is helping to put walking on the map again down Portsmouth way. Nice work, Eddie. Keep it going!

Frank Thomson has now recovered from his illness and operation, and has been seen at meetings, complete with the inevitable scoring-board, and is very pleased to be with us again.

Bill Fish, recovering from a serious illness, and was at Tooting on June 12th for the match v. L.F.F. Was very pleased to see the "boys" again. We are all sorry to hear of his illness and we all wish him a speedy and complete recovery.

Charlie Cook. Still running! and has run in Veteran's sprint handicaps, and thereby been in the limelight in what few sporting columns we get in the press nowadays.

George Still, no doubt, will be showing some of the lads around the common again—and leaving some of them behind! (Stop Press: He has!)

Jimmy Flatt, although very busy, finds time for an occasional spin. At his local "Holidays at Home" sports he had to yield to a trio of Polish Commandos in the Mile.

Max Quackenbos, or just "Quack," ran sixth in the American Athletic Union 3,000 Metres Steeplechase Championship, won by Joe McCluskey. Regularly sends sporting news from the U.S.A.

Bert Footer, when we all thought he was many miles away, turns up at Imber Court for the match with the Police. Said something about "Commando Training" and "Fitness," and went out for a spin on the roads. In the excitement of seeing all the lads, he forgot to black his face, and to put a tree in his hair! He came back, the same old Bert!

Sid Ring, now working in Cambridgeshire, and has had the misfortune to injure his knee, which caused him to retire from the Poly Marathon. We all know his potentialities at this distance, and hope that it will disappear so that he can run to his next year's schedule and give us another first-class performance.

Reg Shotter, news has trickled through that he is now a Captain and is in India.

Jimmy Green, resting for a while owing to leg trouble, and was unable to run for us at Sandhurst in consequence.

Colin Sutton, with R.A.F. in India, has been laid up with stomach trouble, otherwise he reports very fit, and hoping to get some local competition.

Jack Field, now in the Army, has been doing some running, giving a good account of himself.

Norman Graves contacted Len Marchant at Hereford and in an Inter-Station race beat Len into eighth place. Norman won his R.A.F. Group Championship and had the honour of presenting a bouquet to the Air Commodore's wife. Good luck, Norman!

Bill Hewitt, in the R.A.M.C. and now with the B.N.A.F., writes that he is now married. (All the very best, Bill.) He says that he has a jog along the sea shore before sun-up just to keep in trim. Well Bill, we shall look for you at Wimbledon after this lot's over.

Albert Chetland still enjoys a spin over the Common, and writes of the "Espirit de Corps" silently running through the Club.

Len Marchant led his station to victory in an Inter-Allied meeting at Birmingham. He won the 440 and 880 and gave his team a good lead in the 880 yards leg of the relay.

William P. Roberts, the deaf and dumb sprint champion, was knocked down by a lorry and sustained a compound fracture of the left leg. He is getting along splendidly, and hopes to be able to resume next Track Season.

George Long, seeing the country with the Marines, hasn't been able to do any running, but eagerly follows the fortunes of the Bels in the press.

Frank King, caught a packet in the desert, but is making good progress.

David Laing was married recently. Congratulations, David. Met a crowd of the "boys" at Tooting when on leave. Is now up in the wilds of Scotland.

Leonard Smith has gained a commission and is now a 2nd Lieut., R.A.S.C. Was best man at David Laing's wedding. Tells of seeing "herds" of Belgravians on the road near Tooting.

Jimmy Kemp sends a short, chatty letter from Wrotham, Kent.

Len Spooner, now a Lieutenant, is at present in Palestine, and recently had an operation for hernia. We hope to hear that he will soon be racing again.

Wally Ives sends very lengthy letters from India. Has sent a scarf and a handkerchief with the Club badge embroidered to help the Club funds; they will be on show in the Hall. Has been promoted to Bombardier. Congrats, Wally.

Fred Rickards is keeping fit for post-war racing by a weekly cycle ride to Slough to visit his family.

Bill Sutton writes: "On landing in Pantellaria I espied on the beach a spherical object which to the best of my recollection looked like a shot. On closer inspection it was like the very thing. I've done a bit of putting and find I can do 33 to 34 feet." In a later letter he

says: "No wisecracks now, but I've been awarded the French "Croix de Guerre." Congratulations, Bill. We're all eagerly awaiting to hear the details.

Laurie Jackson (Jacko) writing from Bishop Auckland, says:—"I'm still keeping well and am looking forward to a return to activity with the Field Events Section."

Arthur Beaumont, now recovered from a bomb splinter in his elbow, got in the North African campaign. After a sojourn in hospital in Cairo, is now back with his unit.

Len Herbert was married on May 24th. Our best wishes to them both. Is still amongst the top-liners over 2 and 3 miles.

Bob MacMullen, jnr., also recently married. (All the best to you both.) Has been resting from competition for a few weeks, but is back on the road again.

The Patience Brothers. Dan has been wounded. (N. Africa) and is now recovering up in Ross-shire. Alec has also been wounded, but has returned to his unit. Capt. Rod, we believe, is still in Scotland.

Arthur Beales (Pilot Officer). We learn with regret that he is missing from air operations over Germany. It has since been learned that he is a prisoner-of-war and is uninjured.

Charlie Smart sends a suggestion for a war-time Club vest—a Claret and Gold dics after the style of the A.A.A. badge. He also suggests that we promote a Ten Miles Open Road Race, to become an annual affair. Gets a party of eight out training regularly at his station. Nice work, Charlie!

George Durrant, having convalesced at Halifax, is back with his unit at Catterick. He won the Inter-Squadron One and Three Miles. Says he is training regularly for the Championship, the results of which are awaited.

Tommy Clinton, one of our Irish Guards members, writes from hospital that he was wounded just before Christmas, whilst on a landing trip. He lost all his running kit. Can anyone help him out?

Tommy Scrimshaw is back in England after his long sojourn in Scotland. It was pleasing to see him "having a go" at Imber Court, but he obviously wasn't fit and lacked his usual finish.

Jack Edwards writes from Tripolitania and sends his thanks for the Xmas card which chased him from Wales to Scotland, thence on to Libya, to reach him in June. He says that it helped to recall some of the happy times he had with the Bels. The Censor stamped across part of his address. Can any member let me have it?

Cyril Pateman is still out in Ceylon, and is looking forward to the day when he can once again have a trot across the Common with the boys. "Remember me to all members, and I wish them every success."

Sid Jones, writing from the M.E.F., says that he has competed in an International Sports meeting with Britain, New Zealand and S. Africa, and was most surprised at not meeting any other members there. Competed in the Shot and Discus.

Ken Sheail, still with the B.N.A.F. and, having seen active service, is enjoying a well-earned rest. Hopes to be back in England shortly to renew acquaintance with "some of the grandest lads I've met."

"O.H.M.S. Ashcombe" Jones, who, despite his two score years, has been passed "A.1," and also his proficiency tests, is enjoying himself in Derbyshire, and goes out with Derby and County over the plough. And what plough! You want to hear what "W.C." says about it!

James Faulkner sends his regards to all Belgravians from B.N.A.F. He is a trooper with the R.A.C., and handles tanks.

Michael Broadbent, our Junior Champion Sprinter, is now in the R.N.A.S., and further news is awaited.

Teddy Read, we understand from Lilian, is in the Bengal jungle, amongst snakes and alligators, looking for Japs—apparently enjoying himself.

Norman Shrubb is still out in India, very busy but happy, and has no inclination for running. Has contacted Teddy Read.

Peter George is out in Canada doing his R.A.F. training. He says that he is enjoying the life and the country, and had a quick glance at New York on the way.

Allan Lomas, our chubby Rutlish member, is now with the R.A.F. in Canada. Had a good journey out, a very happy week in Massachussetts, where the Americans gave him a good time—iced beer which you can't knock back quickly; but New Brunswick, where he is stationed, is "dry"—one has to have a licence to get a drink! Has had no opportunity yet for any training, and has taken up photography to record his travels. Best wishes to all Belgravians.

John Quinlan, one of Jarlett's school, is now in the R.N. and stationed in Scotland. He wants to hear a few more items of sports news.

Jack Flower, now a Captain, writes from the M.E.F. that he has met Bill Snell whilst doing a spot of training on the sand. Sends his regards and best wishes to all his old team-mates.

Megaphone Man Cook has paid us several visits when on leave, but seems to have forgotten how to use the Meg!

Bob Boyden, writing from Redhill, wants us to know how "Submarine" Footer managed to get into the Marines. What'll he say when he knows "Our Bert" is a Commando? Bob says "our young champ" for 1963 is getting along fine—good for you, Bob!

Joe Daly, from Malta G.C., writes: "Many thanks for the 'Mag. It's great; it revives memories of fellows who slapped the back of the last man home just as hard as the winners, of Socials—a little rough sometimes, but never lacking in spirit." Sends hearty greetings to all Belgravians, and wants to know if there's any truth in the rumour about putting the Club Badge on the African Star?

Ben May, Audrey and the youngster, send greetings from Bournemouth. His secretary says he's so busy nowadays that he finds little time for anything else but work! Ho! Ho! You must be putting on weight, Ben!

Stan Botwright, despite having settled in billets in Stafford with his wife, made the journey to Sandhurst for the inter-club. He has represented both the R.A.F. and Jack Crump's team during the season, and picked up one or two pots for the bottom drawer for use when he returns to Bromley.

Bill Snell writes from the M.E.F.: "Thanks a million for the May Gazette which I have just received (Aug.). Also thanks for the Xmas Card which arrived in June. It's a source of great comfort to us out here to know that you chaps are doing your best to keep the Club's colours flying high."

Charlie Redgrave—Percy's Printer (B.N.L.)—despite so many calls upon his services finds just that little extra time to run off a few on the duplicator. Thanks a lot, Charlie! Has recently taken to hockey, and enjoys it.

Charlie Jones has put in a grand job of work in holding together the Field Events Section. He knows the capabilities of all his men, and what is more, where to find some spare ones when necessary. He is also responsible for "putting the Club across" in the local press. Thank you, Charlie.

Harry Shields: We often wonder how he gets through it all. You ring his office and are told he's away in Manchester or some other such place and won't be back for several days. But the teams are always there. How does he do it? Why, instead of complaining he's "too busy," he gets on with the job.

Bill Merryman, still in Staffs, brought a team to London for Joe Binks' Whit Monday meeting, and his C.O. was very pleased with their performance. Bill won his unit 3 Miles Championship, and was third in the 1 Mile, and in the winning Medley Relay Team. Wishes the boys all the best.

Bill Jones, still keeping our colours flying in Yorkshire, finished 23rd in the Bradford Whitsun Walk (15½ miles) in 2h. 37m. 35s.

Jack Rutland has recently been promoted to Bombardier. Congrats, Jack. Comes up from Wales whenever he can for a race with the lads. Always full of enthusiasm, and looks forward to his visits.

Tommy Green recently won the Hants County Road Walk Championship. Shall we have the pleasure of seeing him on the road again at Wimbledon?

Ron Bryan, another Hampshire member, together with a colleague, cornered the four County Cross Country Championships.

Will Rispin, M.E.F., wishes to be remembered to all his old associates at Belgrave Hall and is eagerly looking forward to the next "Mag."

Dick Towndrow, now stationed in one of England's front lines, has an O.C. who actually encourages sport. Now that is good news, as football is generally the utmost limit, and if you are a runner you are looked upon as a crock. Or, as our old friend Len Fletcher once said, if you talk of walking—well you are thought to be taking out Blondie in the A.T.S.

Len Camin, a football member, regrets he will not be able to do any C.C. running as his team is busy getting points in league and cup games.

TRACK RESULTS FOR 1943.

By Ernie Duffett.

Sat. May 22nd. v. Metropolitan Police A.C. at Tooting Bec. Track.

We scored a fairly comfortable win over our opponents, who had Frank Close, the English International running for them. Fred Clay ran well in the 100 Yards and also won the Long Jump for the Club, and we were pleased to see Tom Wiley out again. Les Cohen ran a good race in the 2 Miles, and Syd Jarlett and young Dave Main beat Moody, the Police Champion, in the 880 Yards.

Eddie Pack also made a welcome reappearance, and we hope he will soon regain the form which took him into an A.A.A. Final and a place in the Empire Games Team which visited Australia.

WITH THE WALKERS.

By Syd Roberts.

The Summer Season, just finished, has been one of great activity, both on the track and the road, and in both sections our walkers have given a good account of themselves, despite their hard work during the winter. The season's laurels, however, go to our old friend Tommy Green for his win in the Hants County 10 Miles Championship. Eddie Jury, another of our Hants walkers, chose to walk in the Highgate 20 on the same day, otherwise we should most probably have had two in the first three. In the Two Miles Championship, however, he took third place, with Tommy Green fourth, and Junior Green fifth—quite a good Club performance. Our other walkers to distinguish themselves during the season are J. Robins, who has been placed in no fewer than six races, and A. Readman, who took second place in the Police Championship.

Percy Wright, still going strong with his promotions in Wales, was no doubt very bucked to have Robins with him on two occasions. Percy still continues the good work with the B.N.L., and once again stresses the importance of passing it on as quickly as possible. News still comes in of the activity of our walkers abroad. They are certainly maintaining the high reputation of the Club, and we wish them every success.

FIELD EVENTS NEWS.

by Charlie Jones.

The season which has just closed has shown that the Field Events men made a fine contribution to the Club's successes in the summer matches.

We have been glad to welcome a few new members to the Section. Two of them, in "Jerry" Thompson, a heavyweight Shot Putter whose best effort of 39ft. 1in. when he won the A.A.A. Handicap at Tooting, was very good; and Guy Hendry, who shows promise of becoming a really good high jumper, have already shown their worth. Hendry is quite young. and by assiduously studying technique. looks as if he will master the "Western roll" very soon. Charlie Stirling and Roy Woodhead are also jumpers of promise.

Charlie Melchoir and George Powell have again shown up well in the Javelin, and lent valuable assistance in the Shot and Discus events. Charlie has again been honoured by the A.A.A. in representative matches. His best effort of the season was over 170 feet.

In the Long Jump we have had to rely upon Tom Wiley, who a number of times has reached 21 feet. He has been supported by Frank Simmons, Stan Blunden, and our Captain. Jack Quarton has also lent his assistance in this event in addition to the Shot and Discus.

We have been pleased to have the help of A. W. Lewis, the Welsh International, in the Shot and Discus events in a number of matches.

News has been received that Sid Jones, our Shot Putt Champion, who is a sapper in North Africa, was honoured by being selected to represent Great Britain in a match at Cairo against South Africa, New Zealand and Canada. He was fourth in the Shot with a putt of 36ft. 1½in. and third in the Discus with a throw of 110ft. 4in.

News of another well-known member of the Section also in the Middle East, in Bill Sutton, will be found on another page, and also an obituary notice of the late Pat Ridge, one of our Shot and Discus men.

MARATHON NOTES.

A few of our members have appeared in the long distance runs this year. In the "Kent 20", on May 8th, our team repeated their performance in the Finchley 20 by running S.L.H. a close second. Sid Ring was second to T. Richards (Mitcham A.C.). Mick Walker was 3rd, Dick Towndrow was 4th, and Bert Mitchell 20th.

The Red Cross "Ten" Race, held simultaneously with a 24-miles cycle race and 7-miles road walk, was a unique race, and a good crowd saw the three races start at the same moment. Ten 1-mile laps were covered, and over one stretch the same road and path used by the walkers were used. Charlie Smart was third in 58m. 49s, Sid Ring 4th in 59.35, and Mick Walker 6th in 60.11. Doubleday was 15th, and Bert Mitchell 22nd. Scoring 3 a side on time aggregate, we recorded 2h. 58m. 35s. to win by over 2 minutes from Reading A.C., who totalled 3.1.18. L.F.F. were third with 3.1.47. Charlie Smart very sportingly passed his team prize to the first non-scoring member—the Hon. Editor. Many thanks, Charlie!

In the Poly Marathon (26 miles 385 yds.), held on a four-lap course from Chiswick Stadium, Micky Walker, nursing a gammy leg, was 8th in 3h. 8m. Doubleday was 15th, just inside 3h. 18m. Sid Ring had to retire owing to trouble from a knee injury. One of our Police walkers, Jim Watson, made a plucky first effort, but retired at 22 miles.

Our sole competitor in the Reading "10" was Fred White, who ran well to finish 11th in 65m. 10s. In the S.L.H. "30", held at Coulsdon on Sept. 25th, over four laps of 7½ miles, Doubleday finished 16th in 3h. 59m. 4s., and gained a time standard medal by finishing within 45 minutes of the winner, G. Humphreys, S.L.H.

Mention must be made of the very good running of our Fire Force runner, George Hoath. He was first in the Red Cross Ten in 57.25, first in the Reading Ten, and, after leading at 20 miles, was third in the Poly Marathon. In addition, he has many good performances on the track to his credit. We all know what George can do over the country, and no doubt he will be the spearhead of the L.F.F. team this winter.

A FLIGHT OF FANCY INTO THE FUTURE.

A.A.H.

EXTRACT FROM "THE DAILY PRESS" DATED APRIL, 1960.

As I look down on the swirl of traffic which flows eastwards through Hyde Park towards the new Double Cross-over traffic bridge at Piccadilly Circus, my thoughts turn on this Spring morning through the years, and I ponder on the position the Belgrave Harriers have now attained when contrasted with their humble origins of nearly seventy-five years ago. I am sitting in one of the offices devoted to the production of "The Belgravian," lavishly illustrated these days and published weekly, and recognised as the leading authority on all matters concerning Athletics. These offices form part of the magnificent building in Knightsbridge, which houses the Club's Town Headquarters and preserves its associations with the City of Westminster, and from which training takes place most evenings over Rotten Row.

At Wimbledon the Belgrave Harriers still have their week-end headquarters now situated on Common Side; and every Saturday throughout the winter hundreds of athletes in the familiar claret and gold enjoy their favourite sport on the neighbouring roads and common land.

The Club still maintains its reputation for sportsmanship, athletic ability, and sociability and has consistently refused to allow wealth, or position, or influence to interfere with its principles. Some interesting customs continue; and the Club jealously safeguards its proud athletic reputation and does everything possible to ensure that Athletics is still its primary purpose. The Committee many years ago realised in its wisdom that the facilities the Club can offer, which are unrivalled anywhere in the world, might well attract a type of member who was merely interested in its restaurant, its billiard room, its luxurious lounges, with no interests in running, walking, or field events; and so all members are called upon during their first ten years of membership to make at least twenty appearances in athletic races or competions each year. The subscription remains at the humble few shillings which ruled at the commencement of the Second World War; and the frequent Social evenings are still a great feature of the Club life.

The Club's history is a momumental example of loyalty to ideals. As far back as the 1920's, the Club's Committee had thoughts on acquiring its own Headquarters. These ideals were pursued by an increasing number of enthusiasts, until in 1936 the Club's first leasehold was purchased. Still the ideals gathered momentum, and whilst the War of 1939—1945 caused a slight slowing down of efforts owing to the presence of so many members in the Armed Forces, the years following Peace enabled the ideals of many hundreds of members to be crystallised into reality, in the form of handsome premises in Westminster and Wimbledon.

It must be admitted that the Club hardly expected to be owners of such magnificent quarters in such a comparitively short time; but the new outlook towards Amateur sport which followed the winning of the War, plus a munificent grant from the Anglo-American Philantropic Association for the finest example of "self help" in the first half of the Twentieth Century, together with the funds accumulated by decades of enthusiastic Club members, made all things possible.

Many of the Club's members have gained positions of considerable importance in our present-day world. A notable example is bluff Sir Harry Shields, Bart., whose contributions to the solution of post-War traffic problems are well known; and whose baronetcy derives mainly from the original and brilliant idea of bringing all London's Underground train services to the surface, and of directing all surface routes underground. He recently unveiled a marble tablet on the Water Tower at Colley Hill, commemorating an incident of his youth.

Lord Parker, of New Malden, to whom much of the credit for the Belgrave Harriers' progress goes, was awarded his peerage in the Peace Honours List "for distinguished services to Amateur Athletics and the public." It is said that his constant and ever-increasing use of postcards represented all the difference between profit and loss in the Post Office accounts during the 1930—1940 decade. Tom Cotton, M.P. for the Battersea Park Division, is universally known for his fighting speeches in the House and his consummate command of language; and is ably supported from the back benches by A. W. Penny, whose rallying call of "Up, Surrey!" has often been heard in the House.

The erstwhile cross-country champion, Bert Footer, now leads a quiet life in the bosom of his family as "Mine Host" at "The Spiked Shoe," Putney Heath. It will be remembered that on leaving the Royal Marines, Footer secretly married Miss "Fluffy" Blonde from the "Windmill" Theatre; and later became Minister for Cross-Country Running in the 1948 Administration; a position he later resigned on account of a broken blood vessel brought about by constantly throwing his papers to the ground and retrieving them at a rapid rate, when discussing the selection of the 1949 International Team.

Gordon Doubleday is Managing Director of "Nutty Foods Limited"; and Tommy Green is Mayor of Eastleigh permanently. Charlie Churcher can be seen with his attractive wife at all the Club Social events, dancing with all his former grace to which is allied these days, an Egyptian mysticism. Percy Wright is leader of the Welsh Nationalist Party (how time brings changes!); Frank Webb remains a bachelor and pursues his sinuous course; and so on . . . One could continue for a long while following the careers of those who have carried the claret and gold vest with distinction on the road, cross-country, on the track, and in the field events; but space will not permit these interesting meanderings.

To all who have contributed to the amazing success of the Belgrave Harriers in the past; to those who help to guide and maintain its traditions in our time; a great debt of gratitude is due from the World, for these amateur sportsmen have shown that ideals worth pursuing and fighting for can come to realisation. There is a message in their achievements for all of us.

And so we wish the Belgrave Harriers continuing success. Success in the Athletic field and success in the wider field of sporting endeavour are certain to be theirs in increasing measure as the Club approaches its Centenary Year; and as the subsequent centuries succeed one another until the end of Time.

A.A.H.

THE ANNUAL GENERAL MEETING:

Belgrave Hall, 9th October.

In the absence of the President, Harry Evans, who was unavoidably absent, Alf Harley took the Chair. The meeting opened with the company standing in silence in homage and remembrance of those members who had passed away since the last A.G.M.

The Hon. Secretary, in his report, said that our runners and walkers had all given good accounts of themselves, not only at home, but in other parts of the country, and, indeed across the seas. One encouraging feature was the number of new members elected during the year—83. He praised the work done by Club officials, and thanked them for their support and co-operation.

The Hon. Treasurer then presented his report, and began by thanking the Hon. Secretary for much valuable help during the year. He explained the details of the Balance Sheet, paying tribute to the excellent work done by the Building Fund Committee and Les Cohen. All things considered, our financial position was good. Before the adoption of the accounts was moved, the Chairman interposed with a few words in praise of our Hon. Auditor (Jimmy Belchamber) who had held this important office for 31 years.

Tom Morrell, on behalf of the Committee, proposed that Les Cohen be made an Hon. Life Member. He spoke of the good work done by Les on behalf of the Building Fund, of his excellent performances over the country and on the track, and of his regular support of Club fixtures, and of his Club spirit. Percy Wright seconded, and Les was duly elected. Les modestly but thankfully accepted the honour the Club had bestowed on him.

Next came the election of Officers. Harry Parker, with George Powell seconding, proposed that Harry Evans be re-elected as President for a second year of office. This was carried. The existing Vice-Presidents, with the exception of G. Harwood (deceased) and J. Sawyer (deceased) were re-elected en bloc.

The other Officials were then elected (mostly re-elected!):— Hon. Secretary, H. Parker; Hon. Treasurer, W. G. Webb; Walking Hon. Secretary, S. C. Roberts; Running Hon. Secretary, E. A. Duffett; Field Eevents Hon. Secretary, T. C. Jones; Cross Country Captain, C. T. Carter; Track Captain, H. W. Shields; Walking Captain, A. Readman; Junior Captain, F. Clay; Starter, A. Fell; Timekeepers, C. Cook and J. Turner; Hon. Handicappers: Running, E. Duffett; Walking, S. C. Roberts; Hon. Auditor, J. B. Belchamber; Trustees, F. G. Thomson and E. Gordon.

The following were elected to the Committee:— F. W. Brunning, L. J. Cohen, G. S. Doubleday, S. Jarlett, T. Morrell, G. H. Powell, R. J. Savage, A. R. Shaw, C. H. Speechley, and A. E. Taylor.

Two proposals were then discussed and referred to the Committee. They were the possibility of organising Socials or Dances, and of promoting a Ten Miles Open Road Race.

Before concluding the meeting the Chairman expressed his gratification at coming to such an enthusiastic gathering. He drew attention to the fact that when the Cross-Country and Road Walking Championships were held again, our winning teams of 1939 would be five, six or even ten years older!; and was pleased to see that we were developing our young new members into teams worthy of defending our role of dual champions. He then closed the meeting and a vote of thanks was passed to him for the able manner in which he had conducted the meeting.

IN MEMORIAM.

It is with the deepest regret that we announce the following deaths.

W. H. Bowers (R.N.). His ship struck a mine and he went down with her. He was Club Junior Sprint Champion in 1939, and a regular member of our sprint relay teams, etc. A great trier and a great Club man, and will be greatly missed by all his clubmates.

R. V. Reynolds (East Yorks). He was caught in an ambush in Sicily, and to quote his O.C.: "He would have received the M.M., but it is not awarded posthumously." He had been with the Eighth Army for about two years after narrow escapes at Dunkirk. He was a promising young runner, and gained a Club Standard Medal at One Mile in 1939, and would no doubt have shown further improvement had he been spared.

"Pat" Ridge (Irish Guards). Died on active service in N. Africa. He had not been a member for long, but his broad smile had made him a firm friend of his colleagues in the Field Events Section, and, indeed, of all who came in contact with him. His performances in the Shot and Discus stamped him as a man of class. His efforts in the Discus were particularly good, and at one time he held the Army Shot and Discus Records. He had been honoured by the A.A.A. in representative matches.
