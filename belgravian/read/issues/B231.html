Young Belgravians on the world stage

European U23 Championships, Bydgoszcz, Poland, 17-20 July 2003. The 2003 season marked a big step forward for Chris Lambert. Troubled by hamstring injuries in recent seasons, Chris put his worries behind him and produced the goods for himself and coach John Powell, by racing to the gold medal in the European U23 Championships in Bydgoszcz, Poland. Facing the home-crowd favourite Marcin Jedrusinski, who was fifth in last year's European Championship in Munich, Chris ran a perfectly controlled bend to enter the straight on his rival's shoulder. A relaxed, powerful finish then brought him through to victory in the last few metres. The winning time of 20.34 was a personal record and also a full tenth and more inside the previous championship best.

Chris also shared in the golden glory achieved by the GB 4 x 100 metres squad. The qualifying round saw a British team that was three-quarters Belgrave scrape into the final after a near disaster when lead-off runner Grant ran into a slowly starting Lambert. But Belgrave team mates Darren Chin and Tim Abeyie (who also ran in the individual 200m) pulled the quartet into the last qualifying position. Tim was replaced in the final by Edgar, to leave the team a mere 50% Belgrave, and this time all went well as they headed the French side by 7/100ths to win in 39.54.

Tim Benjamin set his best ever time of 45.71 to win his 400 metres heat but lining up for the final was feeling less than 100% as he prepared to do battle with the French European Junior Champion Leslie Djhone. In fact the Frenchman ran a scintillating race and Tim had his work cut out to pursue, and finally overtake, the Pole Wieruszewski and claim the silver medal. The Belgrave man later admitted that Djhone would surely still have been the winner, even if he had felt better on the day, but he may have run a shade faster.

Goldie Sayers placed 11th in the Javelin, not so far behind her season's best, but Scot Thompson was out of sorts as he was only able to manage 48.89 in the discus, some 7 metres down on his best for the year. Dominic Girdler had an injury problem and was unable to compete in the sprint hurdles.

European U20 Championships, Tampere, Finland, 24-27 July 2003 Amy Spencer had been suffering from knee problems leading into the games but nevertheless was able to equal her outdoor 200m best of 23.45 as she won her first round race - the fastest of all the qualifiers. In the final she was battling for a medal as she entered the straight but faded a little and had to be content with fourth. No doubt a silver medal in the relay went some way towards relieving her disappointment.

Unfortunately, the previous winner of the women's 200 metre title, our own Vernicha James, was unable to take her place in the line-up for the 2003 championship, having been unable to rid herself of the hamstring injury that has been dogging her since the indoor season.

In the men's 200 metres James Ellington placed 2nd in his heat behind the 100m champion Helmke of Germany, his time of 21.36 being his second fastest ever for the distance. In a highly competitive final James went faster still and placed 6th, just 2/100ths outside his lifetime best, with 4th to 6th places all within 1/100th of a second.

The mighty Laurence Oboh has had his winter conditioning plagued by injury but it's now looking as if he has put those problems behind him. His powerful back-straight run in the sprint relay put the British team right into contention and with two 100 metre finalists to follow the team powered to a win over Germany to claim the gold medals.

Editorial · page 3

It feels that this issue is hot on the heels of the last one, but there is certainly no shortage of material to fill it - it has been such a busy summer. Naturally track & field has the lion's share of the coverage, but latterly there has been great success on the roads as well to report, particularly the string of important wins in the relays. Although we have included general results right up to the end of November, we have been unable to squeeze in the cross country relays and Surrey CC League events - these will follow in the next issue, hopefully due out in May 2004, when the winter and spring season is complete. In that issue we also plan to include the 2003 club ranking lists and the start of a series which will record some of the history of our club.

This year saw the re-emergence of the junior men in the National Junior League and they also contributed to a record seventh win for the seniors in the British Athletics League (BAL). The senior women, as well as again triumphing with the men in winning the BAL Golden Jubilee Cup, also capped their season with qualification for the UK Womens League (UKWL) and this, together with major developments in our track & field programme, means that next summer's fixtures will have a new look.

In the light of these new developments we have decided not to continue with the Thameside League for our Under 13 and Under 15 athletes, although rest assured they will be catered for in our more structured programme. We would like to take this opportunity, therefore, to thank all those who have played their part in organising the Thameside over the last 21 seasons - in particular Derek Crookes who has been in the midst of its administration since the league's inception in 1983.

The new developments to which we have referred have been triggered by the prospect of our women competing in the UK Women's League. This competition is for seniors only. We could have continued to compete in the Women's Southern League as well, which additionally includes U15 and limited U17 competition, but with our top senior athletes unlikely to devote as much time to the WSL as they would like, we would be faced with difficulties in sustaining two teams where much travel is involved.

Additional complications were provided by the National Junior Athletics League planning to go mixed in 2005 and the fact that the National Young Athletes League had already become a competition for both boys and girls in 2003.

We believe that our new plan is just the thing to offer meaningful competition to all our T&F athletes whatever their age.

We will be pulling out of the Women's Southern League but have become founder members of a new competition - the London Inter-Club Challenge (LICC). At the time of writing this is planned for women only - but discussions are ongoing regarding an equivalent men's event. This competition will start by offering four matches per season, all of which will take place inside the M25. Up to ten athletes per club will be allowed to compete in each event with the best two scoring in the match. There will be no relegation or promotion but a challenge trophy will be awarded to the overall winning team at the conclusion of the fourth match. There will be no barring clauses. Athletes who compete in the three UKWL matches will be able to benefit from the extra competition if they wish while, with up to ten per event, athletes of a more modest standard will also get the competition they require. There will be limited competition in these matches for U17 and U15 athletes.

Our successful National Junior League Men will be joined in the Brent Division by our first ever Under 20 Women's team. Although the men's and women's points will not be amalgamated to provide the division winner in 2004 - they will be in 2005. And to form the 2005 Southern Premier Division the best four combined men's and women's teams in that division in 2004 will remain in it but that leaves four places open for the best combined men's and women's teams from all the other divisions - including ours. Under 20s will also be permitted to compete in senior competition in the LICC, or indeed the UKWL or BAL, should they be selected.

We have reapplied to join the National Young Athletes League - five matches where boys and girls will be able to enjoy the full range of competition in their own age-groups - U17, U15 and U13. Those in the U17 age groups will also be permitted to take part in the National Junior League.

But that's for the future - read on to to see how your Club has fared during the long hot summer of 2003.

And please write in with your comments, or submit articles for publication. We don't have a 'Letters' page because no-one writes to us!

Derek Crookes

From “The Belgravian”

75 Years ago

A letter to the Editor in the first issue of the year referred to the possible origin of the Club badge. The writer, who signed himself "Cocoanut", gave proof that the badge was derived from the arms of the City of Westminster and that the cross keys possibly denoted that St Peter was the patron saint of Belgrave! One suspects that the writer ought to have signed himself "Chestnut" as the matter is still a topic of conversation.

Our runners won the Surrey Cross Country Championships for the third time in four years, the team being led home by Arthur Penny. A month later and we triumphed in the South of the Thames at Gatton Park, Reigate, plus taking the trophy for the first club with twelve men home. A very fitting win as Harry Hare the Club President held the same office for the South of the Thames.

Just to add to the letter from "Cocoanut" another correspondent "Full Circle" made the observation that the Club motto meant "Ever Ready" because our ancestors foresaw that the Club would eventually produce leading lights in the athletic world. You can say that again!

The National Cross Country Championship at Beaconsfield was run in a blizzard which caused many to drop out including half our team but we bounced back in the Brighton relay where with four men setting Club records we managed seventh place gaining medals for the most improved team.

The only National Road Walk Championship in those days was over 20 miles and the critics felt we had little chance so it was all the sweeter to take first place with Tommy Green leading the way and one the early editors of the magazine, Ben Knifton, as fourth scoring man.

Early track fixtures included a match against London AC at Battersea in which Lord Burghley appeared for the host Club and the subsequent Inter Club Relay Meeting gave us an easy win. A Club 880 yards handicap received 61 entries - a Club and possibly National record for turn out and the final was won in just over 2 minutes.

50 Years Ago

The July issue started with the tragic news that the Editor and Club President, Frank Elson, had died. The consequential debate as to the format and finances for the magazine led to the proposal to charge for the Belgravian. Almost a year had passed since the previous issue and so space was at a premium. We finished third in the Surrey County Road Relay and it is interesting to note that the team included Bill Lucas, Arthur Bruce, Charlie Walker and Len Bishop all still seen at the Hall from time to time! Add to these Bob Taylor who saw us home in second place the Mitcham to Brighton Relay and you have the feeling that not much changes in 50 years. In the National Relay from Westminster to Brighton we finished second to SLH just over 2 minutes behind and there was a plea, from Bill Lucas, that we needed younger blood to replace the older members of the team - 5 of the team were over 35.

The National Cross Country Championships saw us field teams in all the races then held. We were 9th in the Senior, 16th in the Junior with G Biscoe in the team, and 8th in the Youths with George Flanagan in the scoring four.

On the track the Leyton Floodlit Meeting on a cold blustery evening saw Dennis Merrett, Ted Gaworski, John Bromley and Bob Taylor among a good Club turn out. County Championship results recorded 7 wins, 7 seconds and 7 thirds - all very regular and names included Basil Walden in addition to some noted above. Bob Taylor equalled the Middlesex record held by the great Arthur Wint in the 880 yards, and Ivan Mayers beat Ron Holtum in the sprints while Jack Brown won the Kent one mile.

The walkers had their triumphs, none more so than Norman Guilmant over the longer distances who after his second place in the Brighton won international colours in Italy where he, unfortunately, collapsed but no lasting harm was done. Team building was the message and steady progress was made in all the Open walks which boded well for the future. The longer distance men did their stuff and we won the National 20 in which George Beecham won the first of many winning team medals.

25 Years Ago

The Editor bemoaned the lack of pack training and saw as one problem the fact that our membership was drawn from a very wide area - more true than ever today. One of the incentives mentioned in "Grapevine" was that Haig the distillers who sponsored our Road Relay had upped the team prize from a gallon of the "hard stuff" to a bottle for each member of the team!

Very good turn outs were to be found in the Boys, Youths and Colts events with John Gladwin and Mark Sinclair featuring in teams with the latter finishing 30th in the Surrey Cross Country Youths Championships. Our Seniors were not having too good a time and were finding life difficult in the Surrey League and most of the photographs show snowbound fields which may have accounted for some of the problems.

"With the Ladies" included a health warning to the effect that we lacked coaches and ought to pay particular attention to this. The lady walkers had success in various races including third place in the National. With Wendy Dunsford as team Field Events Captain, we were well set. Club President, Ray Middleton, was turning out along with Richard Dorman, Paul Blagg and Carl Lawton to keep the walking flag flying although it was the first occasion on which we failed to have a team in the Brighton. Doug Fotheringham was, then as now, still striding round and although we won the several titles we missed our own Open walk by just one point.

The back cover of the magazine featured a trio of runners who completed the Harlow Unigate Marathon the centre one being John Davies, another still seen around the Hall on occasion.

Hon. Secretary's Report to A.G.M. 2003

Roger Alsop, General Secretary, September 2003

Success breeds success and, following on from what Alan Mead described in his report to the AGM in 2002 as the "most successful year in our 115 year history", then the last twelve months have certainly not been a disappointment.

Track & Field In our track and field teams, the men's British League team dominated yet again, winning not only all four matches, but also the British League title for the 7th consecutive year. They have now won this title more than any other club, though we still must win next year if we are to equal the marvellous eight consecutive victories of Wolverhampton & Bilston. Our women, although finishing in the bottom half of the table in Southern Division 1, were offered the opportunity to compete in the UK Women's League qualifier match earlier this month. This was as a result of their performances in the Jubilee Cup. Despite only seven days' notice, and missing many of their leading internationals, they convincingly won the match, thanks to the great effort and commitment shown by all those taking part, and have now gained that all important promotion to the UK Women's League.

In Lisbon, the men once again represented the UK in the European Champion Clubs Cup and finished 6th of eight teams, and in conjunction with the ladies team, we retained the Jubilee Cup, now in its second year.

Success, however, does come at a price. With many of our men either taking part in international competitions or competing at national level, our men's Southern League team, after relegation to Division 2 last year, finished in the bottom part of the table.

Our Junior men now seem to be coming into some good form under the leadership of Gerald Sprigings. They won their final junior league match and with many of the juniors already experiencing senior and international competitions so we have a bright future ahead of us. Last winter the Youths and Boys team won the Surrey Cross Country League and they are looking to repeat that success in the coming winter.

Race Walking Our race walking section has seen no material changes over the previous year. Unlike in our other senior sections and also our masters where we have younger competitors coming through, our race walking section struggles to attract new blood, a problem faced by the sport as a whole in this country. However, our walkers continue to enjoy and are very enthusiastic about their sport and surely it must be time to put a little more emphasis into this section, which boasts Belgrave's only Olympic gold medallist.

Road Running In road running, the men have again continued their reign of success by retaining both their National 12 Stage and National 6 Stage titles, in addition, on the way to those achievements, also retaining the Southern 12 stage title and winning the Southern 6 stage title for the first time ever. The men were also second in both the AAAs 10 km and Half Marathon Championships, as well as placing 9th in the European Champion Clubs Cup Half Marathon in Almada, Portugal - the first time Belgrave had been asked to represent the UK in a road competition.

The ladies team is also continuing to improve. Although the ladies were not medallists at Southern or National level last year, it is now only a matter of time before they are enjoying success similar to the men's team and they are looking forward to the new year ahead with great enthusiasm and team spirit.

Cross Country In the cross country section, we struggled in the men's Surrey League, finishing 3rd, although this does follow the pattern of previous years - 1st, 3rd, 1st, 3rd, so perhaps this year we can look forward to regaining that title! In the major Southern and National Championships, we finished well below our potential, and this is the one area where we need to encourage everyone to turn out and represent the Club, so that we can aim to be crowned National Champions over the country as well as on the road. Although no significant victories for the ladies cross country team, they are now firmly established in Surrey League Division 1 and have high hopes for the forthcoming season.

We should take this opportunity to thank all the team managers for their invaluable contribution and for all the time and effort they put into running these teams. Of course, we should also thank our sponsors, adidas, for their continued support of the Club and our supporters and helpers who follow the teams across Europe.

Individual Performances Now turning to individual performances, in the major worldwide championships, seven Belgravians qualified for the World Athletic Championships; Sean Baldock, Tim Benjamin, Dwain Chambers, (who achieved a silver medal in the 4 x 100 m), Julie Hollman, Rachel King, Shelley Newman and Amy Spencer. In the World Indoor Championships, we were represented by Tim Benjamin, (a bronze medallist in the 4 x 400 m), Sarah Claxton, Rachel King, Chris Moss and Amy Spencer. Vernicha James was also selected but had to withdraw due to injury.

In the World Student Games, we were represented by Chris Lambert (won gold in the 100 m), Matt Douglas (won silver in the 400m hurdles and bronze in the 4x400 m relay), Goldie Sayers and Michelle Carey. In the European U23 Championships, we were represented by Tim Abeyie (gold 4x100 m), Tim Benjamin (silver 400 m), Darren Chin (gold 4 x 100 m), Dominic Girdler, Chris Lambert (gold 200 m and 4 x 100 m), Samson Oni, Goldie Sayers and Scot Thompson. In the European U20 Championships, we were represented by James Ellington and Amy Spencer. In the European Youth Olympic Festival, we were represented by Wade Bennett-Jackson, who took gold in both the 100 m and 200 m, Amala Onuora and Chris Gearing.

In the World Cross Country Championships, we were represented in the long course by Spencer Barden and Mark Miles. In the European Cross Country Championships, we were represented by Spencer Barden and Allen Graffin in the long course, as well as Jonathan Blackledge in the junior race.

Of course, many more of our members, across the age groups and events, have produced fine performances, winning county, area and national championships. All these details can be found on our website and in the Belgravian magazine. Thanks should be given to Alan Mead and all those who contribute to these important publications.

General Matters I now turn to some more general matters relating to the Club. Following an EGM in February this year, Belgrave Harriers have now gained Community Amateur Sports Club status. What this basically means is that we now have charitable status and for every £1 donated to the Club from taxed income, we are able to claim a further 28p from the government.

In April this year, we set up and incorporated Belgrave Sports Promotions Limited - known simply as BsportsP. This is a trading company, the profits from which are utilised wholly for the benefit of Belgrave Harriers. The Company is run on a voluntary basis by members of the Committee and all the shares in the Company are held by Belgrave's Trustee's on behalf of Belgrave Harriers.

So what about the immediate future? We have already made a good start a few weeks ago, with the men's team winning the Surrey Road Relays for the first time since 1997 and our ladies winning bronze, with Angela Walker setting a new course record on the way. This weekend, we will be at Aldershot where the men will be defending their Southern 6 stage title and the women will be aiming to win their first medals in the 4 stage competition. If we can keep the momentum going we'll be heading to Sutton Park as favourites to win the Men's National 6 stage for the 3rd consecutive year, with a highest ever placing on the cards for the ladies.

With all this success, we should not forget that there are many other people who give their time voluntarily to the Club and to the sport as a whole, often taking on small roles which are invaluable to the running of the Club and I would like to take this opportunity to thank those individuals, particularly to the team of cleaners, who came to the rescue when the Hall was without a regular cleaner last year. The success of the Club as a whole relies on all the individuals who devote their spare time. We are always seeking more volunteers and if you think you can assist in any way, however small, then please do come forward. It is due to the time and effort given by all the volunteers and the athletes who are competing for us, that we are now enjoying such a sustained period of success, of which we can all be very proud. Long may it continue.

Roger Alsop, General Secretary, September 2003

Helsinki 1983 versus Paris 2003

Rex Bale

Rex Bale recalls his visit to the first IAAF World Championships in Helsinki twenty years ago and compares some of the performances with those in Paris.

The first World Championships were dominated by the USA for the Men and the Eastern Bloc countries but with enough British successes to keep the UK contingent happy. Daley Thompson, Steve Cram, Kathy Cook and Fatima Whitbread were prominent. In the heptathlon Judy Livermore (Simpson) had her moments too, notably jumping 6 cms higher than her three closest rivals who included Jackie Joyner who went on to do great things. Judy's 1.92 metres would have placed her fifth in the individual event. However on Day 2, I saw her out shopping for souvenirs and signing autographs in her 'lunch break' and wondered if this was the best preparation for her final afternoon's efforts to grab a medal - perhaps not, as she had three no-throws in the javelin and decided to skip the final event, the 800 metres.

I cannot recall a single controversy in Helsinki. Certainly the only athletes seen lying on the track were not protesting but writhing in agony. We were prevented from seeing a head-to-head in the womens 100m between Evelyn Ashford (USA) and Marlies Gohr (GDR) since poor Evelyn suffered a hamstring tear 30 metres down the straight in the final. Alberto Jauantorena of Cuba, winner of the 400/800 Olympic double in 1976 and now a member of the IAAF Council, was another victim. He tore two ankle ligaments when he stepped onto the kerbing after just managing to qualify for the semi finals as a fastest loser.

Comparing the standards with the Paris there were some remarkable similarities and some drastic differences. Women's hurdling appears to have stood still since in both Championships the first five ran under 12.8 seconds for the 100m and the first six under 55 seconds for the 400m. Furthermore the winner in Paris was the slowest ever (12.53). The men's long jump finals were also similar with seven men over eight metres in both Championhips, although Carl Lewis' spectacular opening leap of 8.55 metres in Helsinki stood apart from the rest. It was a different story in the women's event where the first four jumped longer than Eunice Barber in Paris. These four included 18 years old Heike Daute (Dreschler) who won the gold, and Carol Lewis, sister of Carl, now embarking on a career as a B movie actor.

Both the Helsinki 1500m and 5000m were slow tactical races to such an extent that the last man in Paris would have beaten gold medallist Steve Cram by three seconds, and in the 5000m thirteen runners bettered Eamon Coughlan's winning time. The marathon brought out a CBP from Gharib of Morocco (2:08.31) this time around but in depth Helsinki was a good match with nine under 2:11.15, including our own Hugh Jones, and fourteen breaking that time in Paris.

The times in three of the four relays were closely matched. In the Men's 4x100 the USA may have sneaked a win at the expense of the GB quartet in Paris but they did not quite better the world record time of 37.86 set by the USA team in Helsinki. No wonder since this team included the three 100m medallists - Carl Lewis, Calvin Smith and Emmit King - and the 110mH bronze medallist, Willie Gault. The Women's 4x400m was the exception since the USA team's time in Paris was the slowest in the history of the Championhips and would not have gained them a medal in Helsinki where the Eastern Bloc countries of East Germany, Czechoslovakia and Russia filled the top three slots.

Apart from Aouita and Joyner other stars in the ascendency in Helsinki were Sergei Bubka making his breakthrough in the pole vault at 19 years old. Merlene Ottey (4th in the 100m), the late Florence Griffiths-Joyner ('Flo-Jo' - 4th in the 200m), and Silke Gladisch (Muller) - sprint relay gold medallist.

The Worlds return to Helsinki in 2005 and anyone thinking of going should beware of drastic weather changes. In August 1983 a heatwave in the first half of Championhips week was followed by sharply dropping tempertures in the second half and by the end of the week the night temperature dropped below freezing. Rain too lashed down to such an extent that the pole vault qualifying competition was abandoned. I for one was wearing all the clothes I took on the last two days in the magnificent but almost roofless stadium. Fortunately a roof is promised for 2005 !

The Backroom Boy

Alan Mead

Don Anderson, President of Belgrave Harriers 2003-3004 by Alan Mead

The Backroom Boy

It's 10 o'clock and the night is foul. A rising wind rattles the letterbox and hurls rain against the windows with a staccato patter. "What a night!" But thoughts of pity for anyone out in such conditions are interrupted by the chiming of the doorbell. "For goodness sake! Who can that be!" The struggle to open the door, apparently gripped on the other side by some fiend of the night, is finally won and standing, dripping, in the porch, waterproofs and crash helmet glistening in the streetlights, is ... Don Anderson.

He's come to collect the flags so that he can get an early start tomorrow in marking the course for the our home Surrey Cross Country League race. And one instantly knows full well that he will mark the course before noon and then remark it when vandals move the flags in an attempt to direct the youngsters race across the golf course fairway; he will turn out to run in the men's race - and then, when everyone else is heading for home, he will go round again to collect the flags up.

It's a similar story of dedication in the heat of the summer. Soon after midday, having taken a half day off work, he'll be up at Battersea rigging the computers ... and cabling ... and camera ... and lighting ... to set everything up for the evening's adidas SPEED Grand Prix. After testing and re-testing, everything at last seems to be working. At 7pm the first wave of sprinters tears down the 100 metre straight. Yes, the clock's running, the wind gauge is registering and in a few tenths beyond 10 seconds the photo electric beam is broken and the electric clock flashes up the winner's time to the 1/100th. The meeting moves on. The middle-distance men come on to do their stuff, dusk falls, the lights over the finish line come on and the sprinters come back for the 200s. Eventually the track is deserted - except for two or three stalwarts, one of them of course being Don. The cable runs have been coiled, scaffolding shifted and barrow-loads of equipment stored away in the club-room. Eight or more hours after arriving at the track he at last gets to leave the park.

Don is the backroom boy to beat all backroom boys. If there's a job to be done then he'll do it. What would we do without such people?

Born in Croydon in 1955, Don moved to New Malden as a youngster and went to Beverley School. Frankly, he wasn't keen on school at all and was far more at home making some spare cash by working at the local greengrocer's. Before long he was rising early to start work as a barrow boy in London's fruit and vegetable market, hauling half a ton of spuds at a time. This hard graft gave him a considerable strength advantage over other boys in his year at school - but it also gave him a few headaches as he struggled into class, late and yawning, with a day's study ahead of him. As he now regretfully admits, his school work suffered, but when it came to activity on the sports field his fitness told and he found himself in Beverley's rugby, cross country and track & field teams. In 1969 he won the Kingston and Surrey Schools 3,000 metres championship.

The sports master at Beverley was one David Philp who encouraged Don to join the local athletic club - Surrey AC - who where in Division One of the newly formed British Athletics League. In the next few years he found himself training for 800s and 1500s with coach Charles Elliott at Crystal Palace. But when it came to competition, Surrey AC were short of 5000 metre and steeplechase men and, as ever, Don slotted in where he was most needed.

The distance runners of Surrey AC, with no cross country base of their own, tended to drift up to Lauriston Cottage - Arthur Whitehead's place on the edge of Wimbledon Common - and at one time Don actually lived at the cottage with a group of other runners.

Upon leaving school Don had stayed at the greengrocers but now, influenced by Arthur, was encouraged to join the Army and in 1975 signed up with the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers where he trained as an electronics engineer. It didn't take the Army long to find out that Don was a runner and could handle some tough going so he was dispatched to Lympstone for a commando course. Don likes things tough - but not necessarily that tough! But it did his running the world of good.

In 1976 he was posted to Arborfield in Berkshire, home to the modern pentathlon squad who won gold in the Montreal Olympics that year. Commanding Officer Major John Bunyard was a member of the squad and when he saw Don outrun one of his Olympic team members, Danny Nightingale, it was no time at all before our Don could say goodbye to spud peeling and guard duty as he took up training with the pentathlon group - a good move. In 1978 Don won the REME Corp South East and Southern Zone steeplechase championships in 9:48 and turned in a sub-2 minute 800 metres in the same meeting. He was only the second junior rank in his corps to receive colours in athletics - the previous "squaddie" to receive such an accolade being Olympic modern pentathlete and gold medallist Jim Fox.

In 1981 it was time to put away his uniform and get back onto "civvy street". The Army had been good for Don. Ok, he had not been one for studying while at school but while serving with the REME he had gained GCE 'A' level passes in Maths and Physics and an HNC in Electronic Engineering. The electronics group Racal was now to profit from Don's talents as an engineer and later he worked for Siemens and IBM as a computer engineer.

Don had always kept in touch with Surrey AC, running for them whenever he could, and now he fell in with Pete Gilbey, Ollie Foote and Grenville Tuck. Training was a twice daily affair and they often clocked up 100 miles per week. But things were not going well for Surrey AC. They were so short of club officers that virtually every administrative task fell to Club Secretary Geoff Davis. Geoff had already approached Belgrave around 1981 to see if amalgamation could be discussed - and sure enough, in September 1982, Don and David Mote and Bob Bridges all supported Geoff and the amalgamation went ahead. The fact that the union took place just a few days ahead of Belgrave's qualifying match for the British League upset a few people (who have not forgotten it to this day!). Belgrave won that match, with Don scoring in the team, and the following year the Bels won British League Division Five. The climb to the top had begun in earnest and twenty years later, as we look back, we can see that Don played his part in all that we did - athlete, Southern League 'C' team manager, Road Running Team Manager, Road & Cross Country Team Manager, Veterans' Team Manager, minibus driver, timekeeper, field event judge and in the last few years Electric Timing Officer. He was even called upon at the Manchester Commonwealth Games to assist with the electric timing for the Triathlon.

Don met Val when she was manning a drinks station on the Belgrave '20'. Val herself supports us hugely as a field events judge and does a great deal of work for Stragglers RC - for whom Don is a second-claim member. Hercules Wimbledon AC also benefit from Don's membership as do, of course, Lauriston Runners.

Could we have a President more steeped in athletics? We don't think so! Have a great year Don - and can anyone tell us when the Club's President last ran in the National CC Champs?

Both her individual efforts were tight races, pipping Bournemouth's Jenny Molloy, and later eclipsing a Harrow runner in the same fashion in the 200m. This left Hannah Stares and Maureen Knight to take charge of the hurdles races. Hannah was well clear in both 100 (14.6) and 400 (61.7) and Maureen easily won the short event (15.4). She finished with a huge personal best in the triple jump (11.47) and left the stadium floating on air.

Nineteen year old Rosalie Powell made an impressive league debut with third place in the 1500m (4:49.5) with steady running and a very strong finish. Helen Alsop was also well pleased with her sub 11 minutes time in the 3,000 metres which was won emphatically by Harrow's Andrea Whitcombe, past national cross country champion and currently a successful triathlete.

The consistent duo of Caroline Wilson and Louise Collier, the one-lap wonders, were once again dominant. In the pole vault it was a one-two for Lucy Webber and Jenny Searle and in the shot Eva Massey's massive throw of 15.77 metres was a Club Record (outdoors). Diana Holden in the hammer (49.21) had done her job before the officials had got stuck into their sandwiches, and another impressive double was in the long jump where Caroline Stead and Michelle Nestor had the two longest jumps of the competition (5.45 and 5.27 respectively). Michelle ran a fast third leg in the short relay where the team won in a nippy 50.2 seconds, the fastest of the season, but she ran out of steam by the time the 4x400 relay arrived. Her 5-minute mile pace in the first leg left the others playing catch-up but it did result in a very exciting race with Bob Bridges' watch showing 60.8 for Caroline, 56.7 for Hannah and a tremendous 54.9 for Jennifer who finished a metre or so behind the Harrow winner.

In the under 15 girls competition Shana Kemp ran the 100m flat, and sprinter Holly Gates turned to the throws. Shana's 100 metres (3rd 13.7) and Kirsty Burns' 1500m (4th with 5:24.2), were the pick of the results.

Match 5, Ashford, 9 August 2003. Not one of our better results, writes Catherine Eastham, with a number of key senior athletes missing and the limited number of juniors still affecting the points. The extremely hot weather had an impact on athletes across all teams. Despite the conditions there were some positive results from our intrepid heptathletes, Julie Hollman and Maureen Knight, doubling up to win both 100m hurdles and shot putts. The best results came from the field events, with Michelle Nestor, Caroline Stead and Diana Holden contributing to the number of first places achieved. On the track, there was a personal best for Tilly Heaton in the 3000m 'B' string, and new member Apeike Umolu (Under 17) could be one to watch in the sprint hurdles.

Match 6, Sutton 6 September 2003. Although strengthened slightly by a larger contingent of girls, the team struggled in the shadow of the new Sutton indoor athletics centre and ended the afternoon in last place and feeling vulnerable in the lower regions of the Division 1 table. However they were cheered by the news that they had requested, and been granted, a place in the UK League qualifying match at Abingdon on 13 September - an end-of-season bonus they did not expect and which caused headaches for team managers, Cynthia James and Catherine Eastham!

Jennifer Culley was hoping for a less strenuous afternoon than normal having had 'flu a couple weeks back, but ended up doing both hurdles races, the flat 100m and both relays! She was untroubled in her first race which she won by 25 metres easing up, and then won the 100m B in 12.3 seconds, just one tenth second slower than 100m specialist Amala Onuora. Amala entered new territory by running a leg of the 400m and acquited herself well, not being tempted to go off too fast. Julie Mitchell was second in the 800 and also in the 1500 metres where she was involved in a burn-up down the finishing straight with the Windsor winner. In this race, Debbie Hearn was delighted with her time, only two seconds short of her best, and is looking forward to the cross country season. Fellow runners over the country, Tilly Heaton and Louise Cooper also warmed up for the winter, running well in the cooler conditions and gaining valuable points with their third and second places respectively; Tilly gained another pb, (10:54.8) and Louise was agonisingly close to ducking under 11 minutes again. Caroline Wilson ended the season with two more quality 400m races, individual and relay, and has competed in all six matches this season.

In the field Diana Holden and Gail Porter not only did their usual events, hammer and discus, but contested the other two throws as well, covering for Maureen Knight who was competing for England in a representative match. The highlights of their efforts were in the hammer where both achieved their best throws of the season, 51.80 and 28.25 metres respectively.

In the Girls competition, Holly and Isabel Gates covered the sprints and Holly also tackled the 800 metres. Lily Beadle and Sophie Woodward-Fisher made their club debut in the 75m Hurdles and another newcomer, Chloe Sanderson, took on the throws.

Early promise but it's relegation for 2004

Reports by Rex Bale

Men's Southern League Division Two

Early promise but it's relegation for 2004

Match 1 Battersea Park 3 May 2003. The men opened their account with a host of good performances with the youngsters in the team gaining a clutch of personal bests and gaining valuable points in the process to help the team to a total of 113 points for third place just behind Bracknell with 115. Hosts Serpentine won the match with 123.5 points.

Sixteen year old Roy Collier won the pole vault with 2.60 metres, and was fourth in the high jump clearing the same height (1.65, pb) as the second and third jumpers but having more failures. He has become a force on the track too with a pb of 56.5 in the 400m (3rd B string). Jamie MacRae, also 16, ran a pb in the 800 (2:07.9), tackled the 1500 and also leapt 5.03 in the long jump to place 2nd. Chris Catto jumped a metre further to come 2nd in the A competition. A third under-17 competitor Gary Mabbutt had a super 400/800 double with 56.4/2:13.3. Kwadjo Adjepong won the 200 in 23.7 and the 100m B race in 11.6. In the 5,000 metres Malachi Byansi set the pace and the others were content to sit behind him until they took off with four laps to go. Warren Lynch with a good winter of road and cross country behind him, also overtook Mal, finishing 4th in the A race, with Mal 2nd in the B.

Paul Beaumont made it a hurdles double triumph winning the sprint in 20.1 and ducking under the minute for the lap event.

There were Belgrave double wins in both javelin and triple jump. Moritz Wendt was getting too much height with the spear at first but flighted his last effort perfectly for his best effort of 43.09. Chris Privett gave good support in this event (35.18) and in the shot with a 2nd B placing (10.41). Greg Beard with good rotational technique heaved the 7.26kg weight beyond 15 metres, a mark which he was to better on numerous occasions during the season.

It was a close call in the 4 x 100 relay. Anchor man Kwadjo's dip at the line was not quite good enough to get the verdict as Don Anderson's photo finish picture proved. In the 4 x 400 the Bels came second again with 3:42.8.

This fixture was hosted by Serpentine but we had an officials team which was probably a record for an 'away' match, seven for the track and four for the field - that was in addition to team manager Bob Bridges, who did his customary hammer and pole vault events, and assistant Paul Beaumont.

Match 2 Perivale 17 May 2003. Our sprinters were out in force at the second match. So much so that the Bels monopolised a non-scoring 100m race. Lawrence Oboh had a tremendous win in the 100m with 10.5, half a second faster than his win in the Middlesex Championships the week before which was no doubt partly attributable to wind direction, having to face a strong headwind in the County event but enjoying a tailwind in this one. Peter Walsh gave good support, coming second in the 100m B and winning the 200m B. Michael Parper showed he is back with a vengeance winning the 400m.

Team manager on the day, Paul Beaumont, led by example winning the first track race, the 400mH in 59.8 and was later second in the sprint hurdles in 17.7.

In the 1500 Alaster Stewart led the chasing pack back to the runaway leader of Ealing & Southall but a Tonbridge athlete blasted down the home straight to take the front runner on the line. In the high jump, James Tuson had a tough assignment under very blustery conditions in his first appearance of the season, clearing 1.85 along with two other competitors but coming third on number of failures. Roy Collier was second in the B competition with a pb of 1.70.

In the triple jump, David Wellstead, who snatched victory in the Surrey Championships in the very last jump of the competition, was third here with 13.59 metres. Wesley Smith had a busy afternoon with the throws, flitting from one event to the next, and excelled in a high quality javelin competition where he won with 54.50 metres. Simon Marwood who came sixth in the British Universities Championships in the 3,000m steeplechase ran 14 seconds faster here to win in 9:13.5.

Match 3 Battersea Park 31 May 2003. The result of this match hinged on the last event, the 4 x 400m relay, where Milton Keynes AC finished well clear of the home team to win the match by a 6.5 points margin. The 'Spanish Plume', the area of extremely hot weather, had moved northwards to settle over southern Britain, and triple jump winner David Wellstead remarked that without a cooling breeze, Battersea felt hotter than Valencia the previous weekend where he competed for the Club in the European Clubs Cup. David's event was one of two field events where we scored double firsts, the other being the shot where Greg Beard and Grant Sprigings did the business. In the javelin Wesley Smith's last two almost identical throws (54.64 and 54.70) ensured that he won by a comfortable margin. Reports by Rex Bale

National Junior Athletics League

Juniors stage a revival and win their final match

Match 1, Mountbatten Centre, Portsmouth 27 April 2003. The Belgrave team was a little depleted for Gerry Sprigings debut as team manager but it was a case of 'everyone's a winner' - by large margins in most cases. James Ellington's 10.7 in the 100m was half a second and 5 metres ahead of the second placer, and Wade Bennett-Jackson did even better in the B race with his 10.6, nearly 10 metres clear at the finish line. The duo were equally dominant in the 200, with James 22.4 and Wade's 21.9 clear wins against a 1.8 head wind.

In the one-lap sprint Set Osho swept past the rest of the field along the back straight into a strong wind, and although feeling the effects in the home straight still clocked 51.2.

Sprint hurdler Ryan Dinham hit 3 hurdles hard and was very unbalanced for the rest, but battled through to win in a pb of 15.4. His badly grazed knee needed medical attention and talk of an anti-tetanus injection made him feel worse! However he made the start line for the 4x100 relay, running the first leg from a standing start. In spite of this, and three 'safe' changeovers the team clocked a superb 43.7, only to be disqualified for a lane infringement at the third changeover, and loss of badly needed points.

The throwers were not to be outdone by the sprinters however, and Kyle Stevens and Chris Gearing achieved a pb apiece, Kyle in the hammer (51.05m) and Chris in the shot (16.47m). The two also managed to come out on top in the discus.

It was a hard decision with Belgrave having the top three athletes, James Ellington, Wade Bennett-Jackson, and Chris Gearing, but after a long wait U20 Captain Chris Gearing was made Man of the Match for his huge throw in the discus.

Match 2 Thames Valley Centre, Eton 22 June 2003. With the SEAA Championships being held the same weekend and the AAAs taking place the following weekend, our Club suffered - as did the others judging by the many unfilled team places. Nevertheless there were some fine moments. James Ellington won by a healthy margin in the 100m with 10.7 seconds and gained the Athlete of the Match award for this performance. Our two heavyweights did us proud again with the 'Chris and Kyle' double act producing maximum results. Chris Gearing heaved the shot to 16.84 metres, a pb which puts him at the top of the UK Rankings. He also won the discus with his best effort of 46.19 metres coming in the final round. Chris also ran the third leg of the 4x100 relay showing a burst of speed which didn't deserve the rebuke that he should have been disqualified for taking up two lanes! Kyle Stevens was pressurised in the hammer but came out the winner by throwing 48.80 metres, just 31 cms further than the WSEH runner-up. He also won the B shot (14.85).

Middle distance runner Jamie MacRae demonstrated that 800m is his better event with another fine run, clocking 2:05.5 to gain second B spot. Roy Collier took one vault (2.20) to place third, and then concentrated on the high jump competition in which he gained equal second.

Match 3 Guildford 27 July 2003. Nine competitors turned out for the penultimate match of the season and mustered their highest points so far, showing what can be achieved when more of the events are covered. On this occasion it was sprinters and hurdlers who were in short supply which affected individual events and relays alike. Anthony Adebo was the sole representative in the short sprints, clocking 11.9 seconds in fourth. In the 400m Set Osho, drawn on the inside lane, passed four of his rivals in the first 100m and was unofficially timed at 23.2 for 200m. It was hard work from then on and Michael Williams of Havant just failed in his bold bid to catch him, clocking the same time (50.4) but Michael was rewarded by gaining the Match 2 'Man of the Match award' for this performance.

In the 800m B race Jamie Macrae tried to catch a tiring Windsor runner who had taken off at the bell, but failed by a couple of seconds. In the combined A and B 1500m race Jamie and Mark Humphries, making his league debut, decided to race each other and Mark's finishing burst in the final straight almost did it, just one-fifth behind but with second B race position to his credit. In the field Roy Collier surprised everyone with an impressive pb in the javelin of 36.44 metres which gave him second place, just one metre or so behind the winner. A surprise in this event was also in store for Kyle Stevens who, throwing the spear competitively for the first time, bettered 30 metres to give him third spot in the B competiton. Kyle had taken a fortnight's break from training but was still 6 metres clear of the runner-up in the hammer, won the B shot, and was second in the B discus as well. Chris Gearing, who placed eighth in the shot final at the World Youth Championships in Canada recently, threw his first, and best, effort 16.62 metres, and also saw off the opposition in the discus.

Paul Cowling contributed valuable points with his efforts in the 800m, long jump and relay, and Charles Davis, making his debut, competed in the 400m and 4 x 400m relay.

Match 4 Battersea Park, 17 August 2003. Team manager Gerry Sprigings capped his first season in charge of the Junior Men's team with strong performances in most departments which culminated in a fine win to end the season. Kyle Stevens set the standard in the first event, the hammer,with a pb of 51.79 metres, but it was his shot put effort of 14.59 metes which earned him the Athlete of the Match award. He just edged out Onen Eyong who leapt a staggering 7.26 metres in the long jump, an event in which Gary Wilson won the B with an almost equally impressive 6.83 metres. In the 100 metres James Ellington and Lawrence Oboh notched up sub 11 second times running into a headwind and James teamed up with a rapidly improving Anthony Adebo to score maximum points in the 200 metres. Ryan Dinham showed good recovery from injury clocking 15.39 in the 110m hurdles; his anchor leg in the 4 x 400m relay was also a bit special, making up a large deficit to take the Aldershot runner on the line. The other members of the quartet, Roy Collier, Anthony Adebo and Jon Regis ran themselves to exhaustion to give him his chance of glory, spurred on by the loudest support of the day from a good turnout of home supporters. A little earlier in the afternoon, the 4 x 100m relay was won in 43.84 just outside the 43.7 seconds (hand timed) clocked at Eton in the second match with an almost completely different squad - which speaks volumes for the depths of sprint talent in the junior ranks at present. Jonathan Blackledge was in good spirits, having just achieved three 'A's in his A levels. His sub-4 minutes 1500 metres was a race against the clock, with the second runner half a lap behind when Jon crossed the line.

Paul Evans bows out

Surely there can have been only a very few runners in Belgrave's 116 year history that have served the club as well as Paul Evans. Child Protection

The importance to the Club of a workable Child Protection Policy (CCP) was endorsed at the 2003 AGM when the Club Child Protection Officer (CCPO) became officially recognised as a committee post. John Powell has done considerable work already in the past three years or so establishing procedures for our Club to follow and he becomes the first elected CCPO. The following is a summary of the role which might form the basis of a detailed job description. Note that a 'coach' includes a team manager or assistant, or anyone advising or instructing a club member.

1. Maintain a Child Protection Policy (CPO) and update it as necessary. 2. Ensure that all Club coaches who are working with athletes under the age of 18 (U18s) obtain Police clearance by completing an Applications for Disclosure form, using the published guidelines. 3. Ensure that Data Protection legislation is applied to Club coaches. 4. Ensure that the register of cleared Club coaches is published in our Club fixture lists and 'The Belgravian' magazine. 5. Ensure that a copy of the CPP and a copy of the updated register of coaches is given to the countersigning adult on each new U18 club member's application form. 6. Ensure that a Code of Practice for dealing with U18s is provided for team managers. 7. Address any complaints regarding the conduct of a coach using the formal complaints procedure. 8. Ensure that youngsters' complaints of coaches conduct are dealt with correctly by club officers using published best practice 'do's' and 'don'ts'. 9. Liaise with the Criminal Records Bureau as required. 10. Report to the Belgrave Harriers Committee.
