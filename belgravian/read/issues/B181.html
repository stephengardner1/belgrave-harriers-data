COMMENT

COMMENT

The International Track Association's professional athletic circus seems to have got off the ground in the United States in spite of one or two set backs but the organisers still appear to be searching for some really outstanding performances to set things alight. There is no doubt that although things are slowly changing in amateur athletics in Britain, we could still do with a lot more showmanship and presentation to attract and keep the public's interest. However, when one hears stories of the athletes in the ITA not breaking their professional records by too much each time out, because they will find it that much harder to earn their bonus next time, then one feels sure that the touring athletic circus is not the "be all and end all" of professional athletics.

What is unfair is that men and women in the higher echelons of our sport spend hours every day, winter and summer, to become the best, and yet are not allowed to defray their losses by endorsing products or accepting reasonable expenses.

Athletics at Wembley before the F.A. Cup Final was a great idea and was appreciated by the crowd and TV viewers alike. Now, shouldn't those who took part receive some material benefit for their efforts, like their footballing brothers who took the field afterwards.

* * *

Youngsters are becoming interested in sport at an earlier and earlier age and we welcome with open arms those lads that have shown an interest in athletics and have joined Belgrave Harriers. Please remember though that you have years ahead of you so take things steadily and enjoy your new activities. It is inevitable that you will meet in competition boys some years your senior and you will be beaten. Don't be disheartened. Your time will come and your natural growth and increasing strength will see you through.

grapevine

grapevine

Whenever professional soccer managers hit the headlines there are many Belgravians who grimace and hope that a career in the limelight will not eventually attract our Manager cum Cross Country and Road Running Secretary GORDON BISCOE. Fortunately Gordon's soccer interests at the moment only extend to the occasional appearance in his firm's colours. They recently took on a team of girls and were thrashed 2-0 !

Many will remember our Club Record holder GERRY LeROY, one of our outstanding squad of quarter-milers in the late 50's/early 60's. He is still running 400 metre races - and good ones. A win in the Kent County Championship in 50.7 earned him a run in the Inter-Counties meeting.

Time and again it is proved that advancing years are no excuse for a falling off in ability. Our evergreen walker DAVE McMULLEN was recently selected to represent Surrey at the age of 53.

Congratulations and best wishes to DOUG. JARDINE and his new wife CAROL on their recent marriage. The sneaky so-and-so didn't tell anyone until it was all over but we won't hold it against him.

Next Sunday when driving home from your training session, why not tune in to Radio London on 206 metres. CHARLIE GILLETT our Track Vice-Captain presents his weekly rock 'n' roll programme from noon until 1pm. Charlie is not our only representative on this station by the way. You may be lucky (?) enough to hear 'news-hound' TOM POLLOCK giving a round up of athletic results during the sportsnews, or even DAVE McMILLAN on the traffic reports.

MEL FORD is another to have an interesting job. He is a member of the Special Air Service and spends a good deal of his time skiing, free fall parachuting, etc. No wonder he keeps so fit.

Our President and past Hon.Editor CLIVE SHIPPEN couldn't keep his literary talents suppressed for long. He has become the Editor of a new quarterly publication 'Veteris', the magazine of the Association of Veteran Athletes. Athletics for veterans seems to be becoming more and more popular and 'Veteris' is sure to give this branch of our sport an even greater boost.

The globe-trotting CHRIS STEER is off again to the United States shortly and hopes to be in Los Angeles in time to see Laurie O'Hara run in the U.S.Masters Meeting. As we bade him farewell in the pouring rain after the Club 10,000 metre Championship one could only feel envy as he called out to those who will be going to the meeting, "See you in California".

When it comes to buying your shoes, bags etc. don't forget to call in at the friendly sports shop down in Kingston Road, South Wimbledon. W. & G. Hales, whose advertisement appears elsewhere in this magazine are sure to be able to supply just what you want - and there's discount too if you are a member.

PRESIDENT'S PARS

Clive Shippen

PRESIDENT'S PARS

Saturday, May 12th, was a particularly successful day for Belgrave Harriers with wins being recorded at all points of the compass. I was at Victoria Park in East London where, on a beautifully sunny day, the claret and gold shone forth to produce a display of team packing which was truly invincible. To see five Belgravians finish between 11th and 18th in a National Championship was indeed meritorious and underlines our continuing strength in this field of our activities.

Meanwhile, well to the West of London our track and field team were engaged in their opening Southern League fixture at Oxford. An excellent turn-out and some good performances saw us run away to the highest score of the day. Not to be outdone, our road runners were far to the South busily annexing the Chichester to Portsmouth '16', with Bill Weller, Pat O'Connor and Trevor Hart carrying the colours at this venue. Despite all that success, we still had Laurie O'Hara spare to skate away with the Veteran's 10 mile championship in Richmond Park. May we have many more such days in store.

* * *

When that great Belgrave walker Harry Churcher died on 24th June 1972 he left behind a unique collection of medals - a collection which spanned 20 years from 1932 to 1952 and included complete sets of A.A.A. track championship medals at 2 and 7 miles besides numerous National and International awards.

Mrs. Churcher has most generously handed these to the Club and it was your Committee's wish that these should be held in trust for her.

At the last meeting of your Committee it was agreed that the medal collection of this great Belgravian would be mounted in a wall case and displayed in the clubroom so that, in the words of Mrs. Churcher, "they will perhaps help to inspire the younger members of the Club and popularise the art of Race Walking still further".

* * *

What a joy it was to be at Wimbledon Park Track all day on Sunday, May 13th. Yes, all day! I was not training you appreciate, simply present at an occasion which brought together over 60 unattached youngsters between the ages of 8 and 14 years. In the morning they were all introduced, in groups, to each of the principal field events. That alone required 5 coaches and 5 helpers! But there were plenty more as well - no less than 6 timekeepers and 6 judges were on parade for the afternoon's running while the field event competitions were in full swing.

There is a strong spirit pervading through the Club at this time and if there are any doubters they would have done well to witness that day's programme. Our thanks go to Bill Couzens, whose brain-child it was, and to that large number of hard working helpers who saw it through. With each youngster receiving a certificate recording his performances at three chosen events we can feel satisfied that we were truly implementing Rule 4 of our Constitution - " The object of the Club shall be the encouragement of amateur athletics".

* * *

The picture shown on the left hand page was taken no less than 51 years ago at Earlsfield. Price's candle factory at Battersea(now in the process of partial demolition) had a strong sports club and Price's A.C. had more than its fair share of Belgravians, though not all worked for the firm. The picture was taken on a training day and features several Belgravians, five of whom became Club Presidents. They were Teddy GORDON, Harry HARE, Jock McKAY, Harry EVANS and Micky PYER. If anyone can fill in the missing names I would be pleased to hear from them.

We are saddened to hear that the Percy half of the stalwart Wilson brothers is seriously ill in Bolingbroke Hospital. Percy and Joe featured in our walking teams for many many years and I know that Joe will do all he can to keep the partnership going by conveying to Percy our best wishes for his recovery.

* * *

I was very pleased to hear the many praises that were sung as a result of Belgrave's 5th place in the National Road Relay at Derby, particularly those that came from outside the Club. All will agree that we had a very sound and confident team. Indeed, our weakest link (if you could find one[illegible] was the strongest of any team on view, and each of the four teams that beat us had spells well to our rear. For over 4 hours of running we were always between 5th and 9th positions as the eventual medallists yo-yo'd around us. Birchfield needed four fastest stages and Coventry two to beat us, while our local rivals Hercules Wimbledon who had the most solid 'big six' on view could only squeeze ahead of us by 6 seconds to become first Southern Club. Bouquets to those who beat us, but if ever a team deserved a special medal this day it was ours.

* * *

The Surrey County Championships at Motspur Park produced two sets of husband and wife champions. John and Judy Vernon took the men's long jump/ women's hurdles double, while our own Carl and Barbara Lawton took the men's walk/women's high jump double. Barbara is now a regular supporter of our walkers. Perhaps our walkers will reciprocate when it comes to the Women's A.A.A. Championships!

Clive Shippen

FROM THE BELGRAVIAN 25 years ago... and 40 years ago...

FROM THE BELGRAVIAN 25 years ago...

THE LONDON OLYMPIC GAMES 1948 ................. we had the satisfaction of seeing three of our club members compete in the Olympic Games as members of the Great Britain team. Actually another member, E.Gailly, was a member of the Belgium team and put up a magnificent performance in finishing 3rd in the Marathon race. Will Nankeville also ran the "race of his life" by finishing 6th in the 1,500 metres final and when one looked through the list of those qualifying for the final, this was indeed a sterling performance.

Harry Churcher and Bill Lucas also performed well without actually "hitting the high-spots" and cannot be said to have let the team down, rather was/it a question of our men being outclassed by the wealth of opposition they were up against.

and 40 years ago...

THE SOUTHERN AMATEUR ATHLETIC LEAGUE........... As Champions of Division I of this league, we have every hope of again distinguishing ourselves this season.

The first event of the season was held at Finsbury Park Track on May 6th, a team race at 3 miles, and we ran out easy winners, scoring 23 points to the 38 points of South London H., and 42 points of Herne Hill H. Our successful team consisted of A.Penny (2), C.T.Carter (6), H.E.Footer (7), A.Allum (8).

Unfortunately, this excellent start was not maintained, for in the 4 x 440 yards race held at Deptford Park on May 20th, we finished last of the six clubs. If we are to finish at the head of the league this season, we shall have to make amends in the remaining four events to compensate for this lapse.

At the moment we are placed third with South London Harriers and Herne Hill Harriers ahead of us. The next fixture is the 4 x 220 yards relay on June 10th at the White City Track.

TRACK & FIELD

Alan Mead & Bill Couzens

TRACK & FIELD

We've made a pretty good start to the season and the morale of our team is second to none. Of course most clubs have to get their competitors to 'double-up' to cover all events in league type matches but the willingness of our lads to do this is really pleasing. In fact the whole team seems to be constantly aware of how we are doing pointswise during a meeting and are prepared to do anything to grab those few extra marks.

A little disappointing was our turn-out in the Surrey Championships. There is a trophy for the club with the most points scored throughout the whole championships including the relay meeting at Wimbledon Park and somehow we managed to get 3rd place. When you consider those of our number who didn't enter and the fact that the pole vault for instance had only one competitor (not from Belgrave) and was won in 9 feet odd, you will realise that we could swamp this meeting and carry away the 'goodies'.

Welcome to Dave Moulder, ex-marine Javelin thrower who was introduced to Belgrave by Cliff Brooks. Dave was Navy Javelin Champion in 1970 and is capable of throws over 200 feet. He has already competed for us in the Southern League Meeting at Oxford and threw 176 feet without training for almost two years. He is keen to get fit again and will add the much needed strength to this event.

Watch out Southern League ! Eric Barker is back and has already recorded good performances in the Hammer, Shot and Discus. Eric who really needs eight days in a week to fit athletics in with his working commitments will add to our field event strength by turning out as often as possible

Alan Mead & Bill Couzens Track Captain Field Events Hon. Secretary

K.O'd. IN THE 1st ROUND

K.O'd. IN THE 1st ROUND

MAY 6th 1973 - WEST LONDON STADIUM - BRITISH ATHLETICS CUP.

One hundred clubs at sixteen venues throughout the British Isles were engaged in this first ever round of the British Athletics Cup with the first two clubs at each match to go on to the semi-finals, hoping to challenge for a place in the final at Crystal Palace in September.

It was thought that it would be touch and go whether we would survive and as it happened our rivals Hillingdon and Queens Park H. were far better suited to the one per event rule than ourselves, and even Blackheath got home ahead of us. Nevertheless it was a great opportunity to get our main track and field men together for the first time of the season and blow some of those cobwebs away.

Eric Barker produced three very promising efforts in the Hammer, Discus and Shot events and one looks forward to seeing him in action regularly throughout the summer. Talent with the power to exploit it is a rare combination. Another to score heavily was Snowy Brooks, although for him this was a rather quiet day as he only took part in six events including the relays.

Our only winner was John Bicourt whose 8:57.4 in his speciality, the steeplechase, was a particularly fine run for so early in the season. A BELGRAVIAN NORTH OF THE WALL

Carl Lawton

Little mention has been made of the work being done by our 'North of the Border' paid up member Gerry Cameron. Our roving correspondent, however, whilst on a round trip, discovered Gerry hiding in the small village of Carnock, in the shadow of Dunfermline.

Despite rumours that he is spending his time cultivating children as some people cultivate potatoes, this is in fact untrue, as his total to date is only two girls and one boy (the latter also being a paid up member of Belgrave). Our correspondent was given excellent hospitality as well as sporting activity in the form of football and wrestling in the spacious garden.

Gerry is of course a Grade One Walking Coach (what's that ?) the only one that we have in Belgrave. He is working hard to help the continuation of walking in Scotland but as usual comes up against the 'hard nuts' of athletics who don't want to know anything about walking. He welcomes any other Belgrave members to visit him and stay, and always has plenty of food in, although not much space to eat it in as so much room is taken up by the freezer.

Our junior members, Dave McMullen, Ray Middleton, etc., will wonder just who Cameron is or has been. Well, 'has been' sums it up. Gerry joined Belgrave 12 years ag when working in London. His work has since taken him to Market Harborough, Swindon and Scotland (north of Watford for the uninitiated). His walking has tended to suffer due to this moving around - at least that's his story. He has, however, many successes to his name including consecutive wins in the Belgrave Junior 5 mile championship and the Belgrave Horwood Cup races. He also reached the dizzy heights of third in the Southern Area Garnet 10 miles, and will casually mention that he gained a national first team medal in the National 20 kilo's at Coventry in May '67. .... John Moullin was fading badly and Belgrave looked in a bad way when the battle sound of the bagpipes and swirling haggis was heard as Cameron stormed through to win the day......

Finally, the high point of Gerry's walking career was a glorious performance in the Dawlish '5' a few years ago, a win in fact that he is too modest to talk about to many people.

know the rules — LONG JUMP

know the rules —

LONG JUMP

(a) The competition may be decided in either of the following two ways - the conditions should, if possible, be printed on the programme and must be explained to the competitors before the competition begins: (i) Each competitor being allowed from three to six trials; or (ii) Each competitor being allowed three trials and the three to six best competitors being allowed three more trials.

(b) Each competitor shall be credited with the best of all his trials.

(c) The take-off shall be from a board the edge of which nearer to the landing area shall be called the "scratch line". If a competitor takes off before reaching the board, it shall not for that reason be counted a failure.

(d) The distance of the run is unlimited. No marks shall be placed on the runway, but a competitor may place marks alongside the runway. No competitor may place, or cause to be placed, any mark beyond the Scratch Line.

(e) If any competitor touches the ground beyond the scratch line or scratch line extended with any part of his body, whether running up without jumping or in the act of jumping, it shall be counted as a failure. Note: "Ground" includes any form of "No jump" indicator.

(f) (i) The measurement of the jumps shall be made at right angles from the nearest break in the ground in the landing area made by any part of the body of the competitor to the "scratch line". If in the course of landing, a competitor touches the ground outside the landing area nearer to the scratch line than the break in the ground to which the measurement of the jump would have been made, such jump shall not be measured but shall count as a failure. (ii) The distance shall be measured to the nearest ¼ inch (1 centimetre) below the distance covered, i.e. fractions less than ¼ inch (1 cm.) must be ignored. If calibrated measuring equipment is used its accuracy must be checked with a steel or fibre-glass tape; otherwise a steel or fibre-glass tape should be used and the part of the tape showing the distance jumped must be held at the scratch line.

(g) The employment of weights or grips of any kind is forbidden.

A 'SALUTE' TO OUR CATERING TEAM

Harry Shields

A 'SALUTE' TO OUR CATERING TEAM

After the Southern Road Relay on April 7th my admiration for the catering committee of the Club was without bounds.

Following a five hours competition it was reasonable that those responsible for dispensing would be jaded, out of stock, impatient to get home. Not a bit of it, there were the ladies, charming, colourful and dispensing the needs of the inner man with tea, lemonade, rolls of various fillings, cakes, etc., all tastefully laid out, with a competence and pleasantness that would put many elite establishments to shame.

Again the following Saturday, on the occasion of the Club 20 Mile Walking Championship, although a smaller fixture, there was the same treatment of mouth watering ham and cheese rolls, cakes, tea, etc. It was so unexpected, when experience over many years, partly due to the lateness that a 20 mile finishes, was to anticipate a cup of tea and even then only if made by yourself.

How pleasant it was to sit, happy in the communal gathering, to reminisce on the afternoons happenings and to reflect that another afternoons sport had been successfully completed.

On the way home, my thoughts led to the object of an athletic club. There have been many definitions but none contain those facets of friendship, hardships, fun, achievements, disappointments which mould the character of a club, from which is produced the tempered steel leading to the finished sportsman.

George Borrows had the right idea, when he wrote early in the 19th century accounts of his many journeys. Before athletic clubs were thought of he wrote, "Wild Wales" and immortalised much of its beauty, describing paths traced by many club parties in their holiday wanderings. " There's night and day brother, both sweet things; Sun, moon and stars brother, all sweet things; There's likewise a wind on the heath, Life is very sweet brother; who would wish to die. " He was a great walker, teller of stories and companion, and if about today must surely have joined the "Belgrave Harriers". How he would have enjoyed a spin over the heath and common, and participated in his great love of food and talk after a hard days slog.

The diaries of Samuel Pepys, 150 years earlier, show the same urge for refreshment and love of companionship after a strenuous day in the dock-yards on the nation's business.

Our worthy catering section follow these traditions and fully understand the necessity of meeting these basic needs. They do much to revive the gastronomic feats of the 1930's, the Social Teas at the Hall, at Youth Hostels where we stayed and out-shone the best, our proven ability at inter-club meetings to out-play and out-eat whenever put to the test, the stories of cheese and onions eaten on the walls of Hampton Court Palace. These must be replaced by new memories, revived by splendid catering and social pow-wows afterwards. Who knows where it will lead and prowess it will awaken in all sorts of pursuits.

Even if you were late that Saturday for work, thank you Brian, and all your helpers for their dedication and splendid eats.

Harry Shields

SOUTHERN ROAD RELAY

SOUTHERN ROAD RELAY

A good TEAM effort by the Bels.

Twelve noon. With whitewash arrows daubed on the road that very morning, pointsmen in their positions and the take-over area roped off, Belgrave Promotions Inc. had made their preparations well and the eighth Southern Counties Road Relay Race held at Wimbledon was ready to roll. Each year the traffic becomes that little bit worse so this time it was decided to slightly alter the course, thus avoiding a couple of the trickier junctions. The alternate long and short stages now measured 5 miles 334 yards and 3 miles 553 yards.

STAGE 1: Peter Carton 24:37 (fastest).

Once again Pete showed what a tough competitor he is. With the leaders constantly jockeying for the front position, he was always ready to cover any break-away and as the hills took their toll it developed into a battle between MacNamara (TVH), Faircloth (Croydon) and our man. As they approached the end of the leg Peter opened up and there was no one else in it. His time proved to be the fastest of the day by any of our long stage runners.

STAGE 2: John Stow 16:04 (3rd fastest).

The first of the short stages saw John setting off with Davies (TVH) haring after him. The "Valley" man swept past and went on to clock the fastest short stage time of the day but John was not put off in the slightest and brought us to the next take-over 23 seconds ahead of Hillingdon, now 3rd, and 32 seconds down on Thames Valley. No other Belgravian was to beat his time over the short stage.

STAGE 3: Bill Weller 25:25 (8th fastest).

Already it seemed the field was stringing right out but at the end of stage 3 Bill had cut half a minute off of TVH's lead only to see Hillingdon A.C. go even better, breaking through to the front with 13 seconds in hand. Cambridge H. and Cambridge & Coleridge A.C. both crept up and now 26 seconds covered the first five teams.

STAGE 4: Trevor Hart 16:14 (2nd fastest).

Carrying on the good work, Trevor got past Stevens (TVH) and made ground on Wainer (Hillingdon), all three of them increasing their lead over the next few clubs. Aldershot moved up to 6th after having made a bad start of 17th on the first leg but they were still over a minute down.

STAGE 5: Lionel Mann 25:58 (12th fastest).

Certainly no "cut and dried" race this, as TVH, thanks to Mike Davies, again hit the front whilst Hillingdon slipped back to 8th, never again to be in the hunt. Lionel kept Belgrave hopes high, closing on Davies again during the last mile of his stint. Local rivals Hercules Wimbledon came up to 5th with a fiery run from Briscoe.

STAGE 6: Colin Pearson 16:41 (7th fastest).

A grim looking Colin was faced with the prospect of holding on to our 2nd position and as he sped away from the take-over area Cambridge & Coleridge, Cambridge H., Hercules Wimbledon and Medway were hot on his heels less than half a minute behind. TVH fractionally stretched their lead but Colin fought magnificently and as he handed over to Gerry North with half the race now gone, he had pulled away from those behind.

STAGE 7: Gerry North 25:15 (7th fastest).

Traditionally the "names" begin to appear about here in the race and this day was no exception. Bernie Ford steamed through the field setting up the day's best long leg time and taking the dreaded Aldershot team up to 2nd whilst Malcolm Thomas, 1972 'National' Champion, took TVH even further away. Still, contrary to pre-race opinion, Belgrave were having a good day and although Maurice Benn (Cambridge H) also passed our Gerry, Beevor (Hercules Wimbledon) and Newble (Medway) couldn't do likewise.

STAGE 8: Pat O'Connor 16:22 (2nd fastest).

Pat scooted past Cambridge H. but Aldershot were also going well and TVH's 36 second lead began to rapidly dwindle. Soon the red and green colours of Aldershot headed the field for the first time as they got 26 seconds clear of the "Valley" with ourselves a further 22 seconds down.

STAGE 9: John Phelan 24:54 (=4th fastest).

John Phelan ran a "blinder" and pointsmen were to be seen leaping up and down with excitement as he closed up to within 4 seconds of TVH and maintained our 3rd position as Aldershot sank back to 4th and Hercules Wimbledon rocketed from 6th to 1st with a 24:24 clocking from Bob Holt. After 40 miles of racing, a mere 8 seconds covered the leading four teams.

STAGE 10: Tony Fairclough 16:20 (2nd fastest).

It took just 16 minutes and 20 seconds for Tony to prove that he is back into our 'A' teams with a vengeance, putting us back into 2nd spot. Hercules Wimbledon crashed so TVH again held the lead but with two stages to go we were beginning to sniff the medals.

STAGE 11: Laurie O'Hara 25:11 (=4th fastest).

Another change round in the positions and the red-headed Adams took Aldershot back to the front. Laurie gave his all, around the roads he knows so well from past '5¾'victories, fighting for every second that he could give to our anchor man Bob Smith.

STAGE 12: Bob Smith 16:28 (5th fastest).

The last leg. Bob looked as nervous as a kitten as he took over 9 seconds down on Fred Bell (TVH) and 27 seconds down on Fletcher (Aldershot). Bell attacked immediately and gained ground all the way round, with Bob desperately plugging away behind him. With a mile to go it seemed Bell would catch the leader and go on to win but he had made his effort and whilst Bob closed on him again towards the end, Aldershot went away to take yet another title.

It was an excellent result from our point of view with every one of our team running well and confounding our critics.

The race was over but our organisers were still in top gear. Result sheets had been posted after every leg and soon the official result was available to all. Now came Belgrave Promotions Inc.'s master stroke. The glorious sunny weather specially arranged for the event was now switched off and before you could say "Gordon Biscoe", cleansing rain had swept the last vestiges of whitewash from the roads.

Copies of the official programme, autographed by the King of the 6th Stage - Colin Pearson, were later seen changing hands at 5p a time.

5th in NATIONAL ROAD RELAY

Colin Pearson

5th in NATIONAL ROAD RELAY

APRIL 28th 1973 - DERBY.

Leaving London at 7.30 am we drove up the M1 and after early morning cloud, the sun came out to stay for the rest of the day. Ideal for spectators, if not for the runners.

The noon start saw 24 runners toe the line for the first long stage, a circuit of 5¾ miles. The course was a tough one. After 2 miles came an extremely severe mile long hill. Picture if you can the 'kiss of death' on our own 5¾ course laid end to end three times and you'll get an idea of what the lads were up against.

Starting the ball rolling for us was Peter Carton. With his fine Southern Relay effort behind him we were hopeful of Peter coming in with the leaders. However, that was not to be, for Peter, although not sparing himself, was slightly below form and came in 9th, 40 seconds down on the leaders, Birchfield Harriers.

Next off, on the first short stage was Trevor Hart. The short stage also took in the long hill and this apparently suited Trevor well, for he pulled up three places to bring us home in 6th spot having passed the Stoke, Hillingdon and Morpeth runners.

John Bicourt took care of stage three, the second of the long legs. Not having raced recently, though, John was forced to concede two places so that we were now laying 8th. John seemed to find the hill a bit tough and had to give best to Stoke's Roy Fowler and Hillingdon's Tony Moore.

Stage four was taken by Bob Smith, who despite giving his best, lost ground to Whitty of T.V.H. although he managed to overtake Beard of Hercules Wimbledon. Ahead an enthralling battle was taking place for race leadership between Stoke and Hillingdon. Whilst we were involved in an equally thrilling tussle with Aldershot, T.V.H. and Hercules.

Bill Weller on stage five closed right up on Birchfield's Smedley to take us by our great pre-war rivals. On Bill's shoulder was Bob Holt who managed to get by after the long hill had been climbed. Bill was also overtaken by Derby's Mike Tagg but still kept us in place by picking off the Hillingdon and Sale runners.

John Stow, recovering from tonsilitis, hardly treated stage six like a convalescent's outing for he took us up two places overtaking Bartlett of Birchfield and Collins of Hercules Wimbledon in the process.

Next away, suffering from what seems is the Club's current affliction, was tonsil troubled Gerry North. Gerry did not let his discomfort worry him and running his usual gritty race he maintained our position and held off Dave Holt, who had taken over a mere 8 seconds behind him.

Pat O'Connor took the eighth stage and was really on form bringing us home 5th and taking us to within two minutes of the leaders, who in race order were Tipton, Stoke, Birchfield and Aldershot. Early contenders Hillingdon had by now faded to 15th.

John Phelan was on stage nine and although dropping places to Coventry and Aldershot, John left us still in contention for a place as only a minute covered 3rd to 6th spots, although Tipton and Birchfield were two minutes clear of the field.

On stage ten Tony Fairclough took us past Smallheath and into 6th place once more, having to put in a determined effort to hold off Kirby of Cardiff. Meanwhile the leading position had changed again, the order now being Birchfield, Tipton and Stoke, the latter being two minutes down.

Laurie O'Hara running stage eleven clocked fourth fastest of our long legs and went past the Stoke runner. Laurie was caught at the top of the hill by Hercules Wimbledon's Steve Badgery. Some thought that he would be able to hold on to Steve and pip him at the end with his famous finish but this was not to be for Steve drew away over the last mile. Laurie did well to keep the gap down to a mere 6 seconds.

Lionel Mann took the twelfth and final stage which turned out to be the most exciting of the day. Lionel tore after Hercules Wimbledon's Tim Beagent and was on his shoulder after a mile. Beagent, however, after a more leisurely start managed to pull away to bring his team in 4th. Lionel managed to outsprint Goodfellow of Stoke to bring us in a praiseworthy 5th. It was slightly disappointing not to have been first Southern club home but there's still next year to come.

Colin Pearson

the barn dance

Wyoming comes to Belgrave Hall complete with check shirts, neck ties, bibs, braces and gingham dresses. What a night we all had dosey-do-ing and happy-hoe-downing to the music of the Triumph Country Dance Band which was melodius enough to get the room swinging and yet still be pleasant enough to talk.

Old social nights were once again revived by the "Swinging Jock Strappers" led by Bill Couzens on the guitar. He was also the vocalist. Bill was strongly backed by "Fingers" Penny - the cigar puffing pianist from the deep south who had his glass of water rocking on the top of the piano in traditional style. A second Bill - friend of Gordon's (nothing like that) did sterling work on the banjo and he helped to keep Charlie Manning (Bass) in tune. Charlie is tone deaf like John Martin on his sally ack. symbols. Stewart Barrett's harmonica played the sweetest sounds, always coming in at the right time, unlike "Scrubber" Gordon on his wash-board who came in all the time on the assumption that he would be right some of the time. But as a compere he knitted the whole shambles together to make a very successful group and the grapevine now informs me that their next performance is being kept secret but I understand they are having talks with Lew Grade.

It was so nice to see everyone so happy for the whole evening, with many familiar and unfamiliar faces having such a good time together even if they didn't know what they were doing in the way of dancing. Undeterred by their lack of ability the floor was always crowded and the bar relatively empty. This may have been because of the efficiency of our barmen in dispensing the drinks which were quickly whipped downstairs.

Thanks Bill ( Couzens) for a lovely evening. When is the next one ?

Eve Stimpson

The Joys Of Cross-Country

Sem E. Serkel

The Joys Of Cross-Country

The flag is up, all eyes thereon, The flag drops, off they go Like a broad coloured ribbon, Some too fast, some too slow.

A lengthened pattern now takes form As bustling rush calms down; Of some who to the front did storm, To stay have no renown.

Past meadow and herd of cattle, Fields of green, plough of brown; In the lead the stalwarts battle, Plod the rise to its crown.

The ribbon has become a thread A line of toiling men, The tail furthering from the head Now far from starting pen.

The leader kept free of trouble, A ditch! One stride he took, Over stile, cross field of st[illegible]ble, Splashes through shallow brook.

Others follow, plodding along Some back, their effort spent Some gather strength, running strong All with forward intent.

By a path through a wood they run, 'twixt gaunt trees bare of leaves Flickers the light of Winter's sun; Scent of mould on the breeze.

Out to open country they come, Across meadow they go, Down by orchard of slumb'ring plum And flight a startled crow.

There ahead on distant hills brow Forward supporters stand, "Here they come, they're coming now" Says a voice of this band.

On and nearer comes the leader, Plodding hard on the rise, Past bushes and tree of cedar, Heeding not the crowd's cries.

To the top comes the leader strong, Not far distant he sees The finish and the waiting throng, In meadow clear of trees.

Steady he runs with no one near Through funnel to the end Sounds of ovation to his ear, His title he did defend.

Running hard comes the second man, Another on his heels Putting in everything he can Fronts as the other reels.

So they finish, some fast some slow, Muddied from head to foot, Moistened brow and exertion's glow, All tried, none can dispute.

Sem E. Serkel
