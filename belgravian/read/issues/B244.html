It’s all to play for in the BAL Premiership Division

Track & Field

At the halfway stage of the 2006 season and going into match three at Birmingham on July 1st, Woodford Green wEL and Belgrave are virtually neck and neck - both on 15 league points but the Essex club having a 25.5 match point advantage.

At the second match at Manchester a weakened Belgrave squad wound up third with the result that the Essex based team had an advantage of one league point overall. But rumblings among team managers resulted in Birchfield’s team being scrutinised a little more closely – and it became obvious that due to a misunderstanding with the rules, they had fielded too many foreign and higher-claim athletes. The match was subsequently rescored with a few of their guys having been removed from the results and as a result, in match two, Birchfield slipped to 6th and Belgrave moved up to 2nd. Woodford Green wEL and Belgrave now have one win and one second place apiece.

We live to fight again

Bill Laws

Premiership Division, Match Two, Manchester Sport City, 6 May 2006.

We live to fight again

Last year our match at Manchester proved to be our nemesis, writes Bill Laws. This year’s visit had been viewed with trepidation, especially as England’s footballers were playing Jamaica at Old Trafford just eight miles away. Travel was bound to be difficult and could we get the team there in one piece?

Fourteen athletes had to be replaced

At the beginning of the week the turnout looked good enough to continue our winning ways but by the end of the week overseas duties and injuries meant that half our winning match one team were missing. Fourteen athletes had to be replaced. Some places could not be filled but nonetheless we were rewarded with some splendid performances.

Joe didn’t disappoint

Another early start – to fit in the double fixture with the UKWAL Div 1 fixture – saw our single vaulter, junior Joe Ive, determined to make up for his match one hiccup. And he didn’t disappoint. In swirling conditions he equalled his recent 5.00m pb and narrowly failed the Beijing qualifying height of 5.15m to gain a fine second place. Meanwhile there was controversy in the discus as the out of stadium field was declared to be too sloping to qualify for legal marks. The circle, too, had been cast just two days earlier and had a ridge in it. All this made Woodford’s Emeka Udechuku’s winning distance even more impressive. Leith Marar improved on his match one distance but in a close battle he finished below target while Mark Allen, competing for the first time for Belgrave in the discus, was also a bit below par.

But the track started with a bang, as in the A 400 hurdles Ryan Dinham ran his first sub 51sec clocking from an outside lane to just miss out on first spot and the B race saw the first appearance in this event for Belgrave of another young athlete Chris Minn who too was rewarded with a pb.

Graeme jumps the field

This was quickly followed by some exciting tactical 800m races. In the first, Graeme Oudney had only one race plan – to go from 300m. And he did! A slow first lap of 60sec saw the field gather ready for a sprint finish but they were all surprised as Graeme jumped at 500m and quickly opened a 25m gap which even the fastest sprinters could not pull back. Negative splits are not unusual but to run the second lap in 52 secs which was a huge 7 seconds faster than the first, is pretty rare. The B 800 was a mirror image of the first but on this occasion Jonathan Blackledge got boxed down the back straight and by the time he had extricated himself in the home straight it was too late to catch the leaders. But another negative split.

Welcome back Wade

And the sprints that followed continued the momentum with Wade Bennett Jackson making a welcome season’s debut after injury, claiming a fine 3rd in the A 100m followed by a second placing in the B race for Darren Chin. It was unfortunate that for both races the electric timing failed and the track judges had to decide the positions. Wade was clearly second in the unbiased eyes of the Belgrave supporters. Later Darren Chin was to win the B 200m from lane 1 in a big season’s best after Jason Hussain had disappointed in the A race, running nearly a second slower than his previous week’s Inter-Counties time.

We were sacrificing big points

All we can say about the steeplechase is that with only one entry we were sacrificing big points. Nevertheless veteran Paul Beaumont’s 4 points were better than none. The flat 400 was another event where our missing specialists made point scoring difficult. Ryan Dinham was feeling the effects of his earlier event but gave 110%, as too did Ian Deeth who has returned to the team after a five year absence. But there were not enough points here for us to advance. And in the 1500m Richard Ward was both our only middle distance man from match one and our only entry in this event. By now too many points had been uncontested and when the scores were announced late afternoon, Belgrave had drifted back from 1st to third spot with Birchfield just ahead of WGwEL. Our hope now was that Birchfield would push WGwEL into second spot so we could end the match with equal league points. But it was not to be.

Non specialists William Sharman and James Everard performed well in the high jump but the competition was so classy that they made little impression on the points. A similar scenario unfolded in the javelin where both James Everard and Wes Smith threw further than in match one but were rewarded with fewer points. James also got a mark in the triple jump where Farel Mepandy bettered 15m to gain a fine second spot in the A event. Meanwhile Mark Allen and Chris Gearing redressed the balance with good points in the shot when a drop to fourth team spot appeared a possibility.

Hurdlers come up trumps again

A glimmer of hope was provided when William Sharman squeezed a sprint hurdles victory into a huge headwind followed by a clear second victory from Dominic Girdler in the B event. William was to be rewarded with an invitation to the following Sunday’s Gateshead Grand Prix. Simon Jones was a welcome debutant in the 5000 and was to see one of Birchfield’s many Kenyan athletes run a non-scoring 13:48 time trial. Backed by the ubiquitous Will Cockerell we were just holding on to third spot. Will has been compiling statistics on the Bels’ 5000 BAL representatives and appears to have proved he has competed twice as many times as his nearest rival. No doubt a bit more of that in “Skippers Corner” before long!

So to the relays

Well aware that a dropped baton could change the placings of most of the teams, the Belgrave squad played it safe and were only headed by the WGwEL quartet who won by virtue of some very slick baton changing. With the long relay to go we were still aware that a baton infringement could change the final result. And then, with 15 minutes to the start, Jason Hussain was unwell and to his great credit Darren Chin stepped in to fill the breach. How he managed to sprint down the home straight only he knows but he gave Graeme Oudney a sniff of gaining a place, Graeme responded with the fastest split of the day but again our lack of specialist one lappers was to prove our undoing. But we had held on to third match place and we live to fight again in Birmingham in four weeks time.

It was a long drive home through throngs of football supporters, celebrating a 6-0 win over Jamaica. The journey gave plenty of time to ponder a fascinating roller coaster competition.

The win streak goes on

UK Women’s Athletics League

Division Three, Match One, Bedford, 3 June 2006.

The win streak goes on

Yep! Unbeaten in 2005 and winners of the opening UKWAL match in 2006, the quest for promotion goes on, but this was no easy match. Marshall Milton Keynes AC made a great fight of it and Blackheath & Bromley Harriers, promoted with the Belles at the end of 2005, will be sure to have a say in the matter before this season is over.

Points were hard to come by but five individual winners tipped the balance our way with the pole vault our top event thanks to Janine Whitlock and Jenny Searle. Janine needed three tries at 4.15m but her eventual clearance was a new Division Three record and only 5cm shy of the overall league record held by ... Janine herself, set seven years ago when she competed for Trafford AC.

Hannah Stares was a double winner – taking both hurdle races and prepared to tackle whatever the team managers threw her way. Our other win came from none other than Michelle Nestor – 11.06m in the triple jump – and as we’ve said before, goodness knows what she’d do if she ever trained.

Sarah Wellstead scored well in the A strings of the horizontal jumps – two second places to her – and it was good to see Diana Holden in the team and scoring equally well in the A hammer and B discus where U20 Jade Lally maintained her consistent 2006 series of throws well over 40 metres to place second in the A event.

Great start then - and we look forward to more of the same at Coventry on July 2nd.

Roads still paved with gold

Road Running

Horwich Carnival 5km inc. AAA Championship

Roads still paved with gold

The AAA 5km Championship is only in its second year and on each occasion it has been held as part of the delightful Horwich Carnival Races – a meeting that also takes in the BMAF 5km Championship, the conclusion of the Countryside Tour of Horwich & Rivington, various cycle races including the British National Elite Men’s Circuit Championship and the Carnival Parade complete with beauty queen and the famous Horwich RMI Clog Dancers – all on the same circuit (though not, thankfully, at the same time).

Local man Paul Freary was in action early, figuring that we had enough fire power in the senior men’s race to do without him and electing to run as a Master. He tucked into the slipstream of Blackheath & Bromley’s Dave Taylor who set a ferocious pace and the two were clear of the field after one lap. But there were two more circuits to go and our man was unable to hang on to Taylor who went on to set an astounding veteran time of 14:38. Paul won the M35 category but didn’t want to make too much of that as two, albeit outstanding, M40s were ahead of him by the time the line was reached. Charlie Dickinson came through as the race progressed - eventually just missing out on M50 medals - while the team race went to our local rivals Herne Hill, headed by Roger Alsop.

In the AAA Championship race Birchfield’s men tore away from the start with only Chris Davies (Telford) and Peter Riley (Leigh) getting anywhere near them. But Mark Miles worked hard throughout the race and his final placing of 5th (3rd in the AAA Championship) is a pleasure to record. He was a little disappointed with the time – but what a way to come back in his first race after a long lay-off. To say that we are thrilled to have him back in the team is an understatement.

Simon Jones had reported a minor calf problem but his solid run as tenth Briton was key to our team placing while it was great to see “Wardy” mixing road success with speedy track runs. Do you know that as a result of his glandular fever and two years of below par results – Richard Ward is now operating at 3:48 1500m level on 30 miles per week?

In 2005 we missed out on the AAA team title by the narrowest of margins, having equalled Salford’s score but got our last scoring man home behind theirs; even that situation had only been reached after some overseas competitors were removed from the scoring (without which we would have won) while others (Birchfield’s African stars) were not. The intervening year had seen us propose that AAA team places should be calculated on accumulated times, therefore obviating the need to decide who should be removed from the scoring and who shouldn’t – and this has been accepted as the rule for 2006 onwards.

Another complicating feature of these events is the fact that for an overseas athlete to win a AAA individual medal he or she must have been resident in the UK for nine months - thus ruling out the Birchfield guys - while to score for a UK club an overseas athlete only has to be resident for two months.

Even getting our first three home onto the scoring sheet had not been without problems as Richard Ward had been left off the result and our Team Manager, ensconced in the warmth of the Cafe Marina and doing justice to a post-race minted lamb pie with brown sauce, was dragged off to Race HQ to clear things up after a phone call from Richard outlined the potential setback.

Suffice it to say that at the conclusion of the race we were resigned to the fact that we had placed second team on the day to the awesome onslaught of Birchfield’s men, bolstering ourselves with the thought that we were, at least, first “British” team. We went along to the presentation to receive our second set of silver medals, only to fine that Birchfield, though winning the Open Race, were not included in the Championship event. The golden booty was ours.

Greg Billington - a double win in Europe

May 19-20. In Wiesbaden, Germany, Greg Billington – not quite 17 but competing in under 19 competition – won a DODDS European Championships (Department of Defense American Schools) double at 1500m and 3000m in poor conditions and with tactical flair.

Greg and coach Mick Graham have been working on “change of pace” and in the 1500m the Belgrave man was able to turn on a 43 second last 300 for the win in 4:01. In an all-out run he should go well under 4 minutes.

For the 3000m the weather conditions were horrendous with the track being closed due to lightning as the runners were ready to start. When the runners were finally allowed to begin the race, they were soaked and cold and the track was covered with lakes.

It was another cat and mouse race and this time Greg hit the accelerator with 500m to go, covering the final lap in 61.0 seconds. His winning time was 8:58. He was 15:25 last year and has come on considerably since then – thanks to Mick and his own hard work.

From The Belgravian – 75 Years Ago – 1931

June 24th – The chief event of the evening was the 3 Miles Championship for “The Griffin Cup.” Unfortunately Arthur Allum, the holder, was unable to compete due to ankle trouble, sustained at the end of the Winter season.

Wally Bayliss led the field in the early stages of the race, but was displaced after a while by Arthur Penny, closely followed by Jack Flower. It was obvious that the championship was going to resolve itself into a struggle between the two last named and this proved to be the case. Over the last lap Arthur drew away, and although Jack seemed to gain a little in the last fifty yards, Penny won by a few yards in the Club record time of 15min. 6sec. C.T. Carter ran well to finish third and would have been much nearer the leaders but for an attack of “stitch” over the last two laps. He should show considerable improvement over the country this coming season. Fourth came H.E. Footer and fifth N. Dewberry.

The 2006 edition of this club championship race – for the very same “Griffin Cup” – will take place at Battersea Park on Wednesday 19th July.

July 3rd and 4th. – A.A.A. Championships. T.H. Scrimshaw distinguished himself by running second in his heat and fourth in the final, with T. Hampson, S. Townend and Dr. Peltzer in front of him. Truly remarkable running for a lad not yet twenty-one.

With the walkers. We are very glad to see Tommy Green out again, and although he is not yet his old self, he has already been responsible for some very fine performances. Best of these, perhaps, was to beat T. Lloyd Johnson, the National 20 miles and 50 kilometres champion, in a 25 miles race at Manchester.

Tommy was recovering from the misfortune of losing a thumb in the course of his work at the railway works at Eastleigh. He went on to win the Olympic 50 kilometres Walk at Los Angeles in 1932.

Triathlon

Tim Weeks

Just in case you didn’t know, with the number of triathletes we already have as members, we thought it would be a good idea to enable Belgrave Harriers to compete together in these events as a team.

Well, all the hard work is now coming together and we are ready for lift off!

Triathlon is currently a bit of a boom sport, especially in SW London, and we felt it was a great idea to provide an additional section to the club. Triathlon is fantastic cross training for running, or if you are bored with just running, it offers the opportunity to try something new!

The club is now affiliated with British Triathlon (the triathlon equivalent of UK Athletics). So what now?

Hedgeweek Triathlon League

We need a Triathlon Team to compete in three out of the remaining five London League matches. It’s a similar set up to the Surrey XC league – as many people can enter as they like – and the scoring team is the first five athletes across the line but must include a veteran (40 plus) and a female – although the veteran and female can be the same person. Got that? More details are on the website.

We plan to take part in the Hillingdon Triathlon – 16 July, the Tritons Aquathlon – 19 August, and the Serpentine Duathlon in October.

Training

Swimming training will take place on Thursday evenings from 9pm to 10pm at Kings College School, Wimbledon. All standards will be welcome. We plan to have one or two swimming coaches on hand. The cost for the session will be £4 to start with, although this may reduce over time. Anyone – whether interested in triathlon or not – will be welcome at these sessions. For example we may have an injured athlete who want to “run” in water or someone who simply wants a swim. The pool will be sectioned into an area for those training for triathlon – and the rest. All will have to pay the weekly fee.

Group running is planned for Tuesday evenings from Belgrave Hall, and a group bike rides on Sunday mornings.

Further information about the triathlon section

It should be noted that being a member of Belgrave Harriers does not give British Triathlon Association insurance cover for racing and training as an individual. Therefore, triathlon members, if they so wish, should insure themselves as individuals, thus covering training and competition, or they should take out BTA day membership for competition (contact Tim for more info).

Belgrave subscriptions for those intending to take part in Triathlon will be the same as for ordinary membership – £35. Those who are already Belgrave members will not have to pay any membership fee in addition to their normal one.

We will probably limit the number of new members joining for triathlon to a certain extent. This can be revised as we see how it goes.

So that’s all folks! Let’s get this show on the road. Belgrave has the potential to be a dominating force in triathlon, just like athletics. If people want to speak with me, the easiest way is my personal mobile.

Remember, we need a team of five for the 16 July!

Rising star – Joe Ive

Pole vaulter Joe Ive completed a remarkable double at the South of England Athletic Championships at Crystal Palace. The rapidly rising 19-year-old Belgrave Harrier took the Under 20s title on the Saturday and then captured the senior crown on the Sunday.

Joe’s weekend to remember had started with a hollow triumph in the junior event following a second attempt clearance at his opening height of 4.70 metres before two failures at 4.90m. He was unable to take the permitted third attempt following an equipment failure which disrupted the competition severely, making for a controversial conclusion to the competition.

This was in stark contrast to Sunday’s success with Joe vaulting superbly with clearances at 4.70m, 4.90m, 5.00m third time, to equal his personal best, and finally 5.10m first time to set new personal figures. Then followed three failures at the 5.15m World Junior Championships qualifying height for Beijing in August.

Before Joe got to work, the Belgrave U20 Club Record stood to Mark Shippen – 4.20m – dating back to 1977 and equalled by Ian Mackenzie in 2000 and Mark Allen in 2004.
