Editorial · page 3

Many members have said that this winter's results have been a disaster, with demotion from Division One of the Surrey Cross Country League such a contrast to the track and field successes of last summer. Certainly our Cross Country Secretary Colin Pearson has been pretty fed up with the lack of support from team members over the ``country''. This, together with our Treasurer's warnings about the state of finances within the club and lack of any of the sponsorship deals enjoyed by other clubs have made it seem like a very dismal winter.

But is this really the case? One of the benefits of editing the club magazine is the gathering of news from all areas of the club and there is no doubt that, on the whole, there is far more good news to report in this edition than bad.

Our Ladies, with their wonderful team spirit, have done well to gain promotion to Division One of the Surrey League in such a positive manner, and Belgrave's young stars in the Youths and Boys team convincingly won their section of the Surrey League with the Colts also doing well to finish third. We haven't had successes like that from our youngsters since the days of Gladwin and Co. Even in the Men's Cross Country we have a new star in Kaesse Tadesse who warmed the hearts of many on a freezing day at Parliament Hill with his second place in the Junior race.

There has been a feeling on the committee for a while that some aspects of the management of the Club might need to be reviewed. Athletics has changed so much over the last ten or twenty years with the introduction of open Athletics and sponsorship and it is important that Belgrave should reflect those changes. It is also important for the club to review areas which are not doing so well and become pro-active in helping those areas.

At an extra committee meeting held specially to discuss this topic on March 1st it was decided to set up a number of sub-committees whose areas of responsibility are to cut right across the traditional sections of the Club. For instance, it is intended that the Track and Field group should cover both genders, all age groups and include track walking; the Road Running, Cross Country and Road Walking sub-committee would have a similar structure. Sub-committees are also being created for Finance, Social Activities, Sponsorship and Public Relations, Membership Services, Belgrave Hall, Relationships with external bodies and, finally, Planning and Development. The idea is that each of these groups' activities will still need to be ratified by the General Committee which, it has been suggested, should be restructured along slimmer lines than at present.

The Planning and Development sub-committee would be a sort of ``think tank'' for the club. It has already played a great role in developing and presenting the ideas of Club members who felt that it was time for our administration and organisation to be looked at critically. Some of the ideas that have come up have, to say the very least, been radical. Some, even if accepted by the current General Committee, would require changes to be made at the Annual General Meeting.

There have already been those who have said that we tried to form sub-committees before and it didn't work, while some older members have commented that the ideas are nothing new, the club used to be run this way in the past. Well, no good ideas are truly original, so hopefully Belgrave can learn from the past, make changes where they will benefit our Club and look forward to continued progress into the next century.

From "THE BELGRAVIAN"

50 YEARS AGO ..... May 1943

News from members in the services:-

Lieutenant F.W. 'Billy' Pritchard of the anti-tank regiment has been killed in action in North Africa. He will be remembered as a sprinter of merit.

Captain Tommy Green recently met with a bombing accident and suffered three slight wounds.

Wally Ives, out in India, writes ``Always I am waiting for the call which means HOME and WIMBLEDON''. His letter took eleven weeks to arrive.

Arthur Beaumont says he is now in Tripoli for a rest after the big advance. Tells unusual tales about the Arabs and tent life.

Percy Wright, in South Wales, acknowledging 'The Belgravian', says ``It's equal to a flash of cockney in the heart of this place and believe me that's saying something. When the mag arrives it carries me back to stale sinks and an overcrowded bath tub''.

Alf Orton says that after 8 1/2 months in the desert he can now get in to Cairo. Hasn't felt like training; though a 2:06 time for 800 metres has nearly made him change his mind.

Flying Officer Bill Lucas found time to look in at Belgrave Hall when on leave from instructional flying duties.

Joe Flower, writing from West Africa, says of 'The Belgravian' ``What pleasure that little journal brings''.

Dan Patience, after having been stationed in the wilds of Ayr, bemoaning his fate because he couldn't get a run, now writes from North Africa! Says that all he does is marching, bathing and eating oranges.

Len Fletcher says that if he asks for leave for walking ``The beaks of this Fred Karno outfit think you want to take a WAAF out''.

Flight Sergeant Colin Sutton has hopped from the Middle East to India. He has started training again and met the Indian Champion, Simba, over 1500 metres but was beaten. There will be a return match.

Reg Shepherd, back from Canada with his ``wings'' is now in Scotland. (News has just been received that Reg has been posted missing after a reconaissance over Europe).

Knut Moe our Norwegian member, according to rumour from a neutral source, has escaped to England.

Les Bennett is still amongst the missing in Malaya. His wife Olive writes ``It is over a year since I last heard from my husband. I have not given up hope and have great confidence that he is safe and well''.

Report · page 5

Kaessa breaks the drought

Charlie Dickinson and Alan Mead

ENGLISH CC CHAMPS., PARLIAMENT HILL, 27 FEB

A magnificent second place by Kaesse Tadesse in the Junior Men's race warmed the hearts of Belgrave supporters in the Arctic conditions of Parliament Hill fields. We have had a long, barren stretch as far as cross country goes. There have been relatively successful Belgrave Junior teams, and our Juniors and Seniors were near the front in the early to mid-sixties but how long is it since a Belgravian last took home a ``National'' cross country medal of any variety?

Charlie Dickinson and Alan Mead report

Returning to the famous course for the first time since Julian Goater won by nearly two minutes in 1981, and although the strength of the fields was perhaps weakened by the revamped Inter-Counties/ International Trial three weeks before, the National nevertheless produced three very exciting races. The course had been slightly altered because of gas maintenance work around Kenwood and therefore had even more twists, turns and undulations than in previous years. This together with numerous boggy patches left by the winter rain, the terrible uphill start, and a freezing wind bringing squalls of snow from the north, certainly produced a test of the ``traditional'' kind.

Youth 7k

The youngest age group was the one that promised most for Belgrave in the way of team positions. Our lads could mostly be classed as belonging to the shorter middle-distance group, perhaps not best suited to 7,000 metres over Parliament Hill. However, it was felt that a team place inside the top ten might just be on the cards.

Imagine the excitement then, when after the charge up the starting hill, Ben Hamill was placed handily on the shoulder of joint favourite Scott West, leading the field as they went over the top. As if that wasn't enough the cheeky blighter had the audacity to wink to the Belgrave supporters cheering him on. Unfortunately even bold Ben couldn't sustain this sort of action for the full distance. The loss of a shoe together with his extravagant start had him spinning back through the field to a position well below his potential.

Adam Jones was another who would have expected to place a lot higher, so in spite of a brilliant 34th from Matt Hillier, who was really flying over the last section of the race, and an equally impressive 54th from Gavin Mason, the team had to settle for 17th. Nevertheless, we were still 4th Southern team home.

Junior 10k

In the Junior race, Kaesse was well to the front of a large leading group at the start, then began to force the pace in the long downhill sweep coming out of the South Meadow on the first lap. This broke the field, apart from Spencer Barden who had won so convincingly in the South of Thames a month before, Tim Dickinson, running a 'blinder' for Blackheath, and Stefan White of Coventry Godiva, runner up at Luton in 1991, and running for the third time as a Junior.

These four continued to battle it out as the race entered the second lap with Barden, the favourite, now leading and Kaesse clinging to third place. But this effort visibly tired the strong looking GEC Avionics man, giving Stefan White his chance. With Kaesse hanging on like grim death, White made his move half way through the final lap and the pair quickly opened up a huge gap over Barden.

As they swept around Kite hill to the finish, Kaesse made one final spirited effort to close a five yard gap, but White's strength and experience prevailed and he pulled away round the fields to the finish.

For a man who dislikes the cold, dislikes the mud, and dislikes hills, Kaesse had run so very well.

Danny Winson was our only other Junior. For a man who competes over 400 and 800 metres in our track teams, Danny has been showing good form in his last few cross country races but he too suffered from a shoe lost to the glutinous Hampstead mud.

Interestingly, only four of the twenty-six teams that finished had a full complement of six runners.

Senior 15k

The Seniors were led home by Jim Estall who ran what appeared to be a comfortable race. Owain Lewes was next scorer and ran probably his best race of the season. He ran in bare feet which seemed ideal for the conditions but strange for someone who sells running shoes. Perhaps he knows more than we do! Mike Lake ran another steady race.

Letter · page 6

Membership moans

Carl Lawton

I am sure all club members will be pleased to be aware of the Club's financial problems and the efforts some of their elected officials go to, to ensure the careful management of our limited resources. We are not a sponsored Club and have to rely on subscription and generous donations from members. Unfortunately we are blighted, as no doubt other organisations are, by a proportion of ``members'' who decline to pay these subs. As at March 23rd over 80 ``names'' are outstanding. Regretably some of these are well known and, in cases, from their gains from the many major athletic meetings at which they compete, it is unfortunate that they cannot see their way to providing the Club with the small amount that is requested.

For those of you that would criticise that some of these are poor and live a hand to mouth existence, well, this may be the case, but we can only help if we are informed. Non-reply to letters tells us nothing.

One simple way out of this annual predicament is to make subscriptions optional: only send out one reminder per year and let the Club stew from lack of finance. This will make my job nice and easy and then I won't have an axe to grind about non-payers.

Carl Lawton

Column · page 7

Sidetracks

Tony Whatley, an old friend of Tony Verdie's from some way back, turned out for the Belgrave Half-Marathon, hoping to renew their acquaintanceship. He sends his regards and hopes to catch up with him soon at some other race.

Colin Pearson bumped into Alf Horton a few months back. Alf is well and asks that his best wishes be passed on to all that remember him up at the Hall.

We read in the Veterans Athletic Club Newsletter that Cecil Gittins has been elected President of that Club for 1992-93. The newsletter goes on to praise him as one of the hardest working officials in our sport, something that we in Belgrave can only endorse. Congratulations, Cecil.

Good old Marcello! Together with his willing band of helpers, his hard work in organising another Sunday afternoon Italian ``binge'' at the Hall netted the Club nigh on £200.

Tom Carter has been corresponding with one of his old colleagues Dave Main, who is now living in Tournon in France where he is converting a barn to a small house. Previously he had lived in Karpathos, Greece for seven years. Dave recalls the lads of '46. ``Oh! What happy days they were. I can still re-live our training runs around the Common and sometimes into Richmond Park, you pushing as all to the limit (except for the odd day when you were not feeling so good) and then Len Herbert or myself would try and push you - great fun it was. It does not seem to have done the members any harm either, they all seem to be steaming past the eighties and into the nineties - long may they go on.'' Dave keeps fit by hitting the odd golf ball. One of his sons has run the Boston and London Marathons and he has a nephew who has also completed the ``London''.

Hearing about the prospective trip by a Belgrave party to Stuttgart for the World Championships, Charlie Jones recalls the trip he made with fellow Belgravians to the Berlin Olympics in 1936. He had to save for two and a half years to rake together the £35 they each needed for the jaunt. Charlie remembers the rail and boat fare figure to this day - five pounds, nine shillings and seven pence halfpenny! They combined the athletics with a walking holiday along the Rhine, staying at Youth Hostels. Charlie had a fall just a year ago and doesn't get out as regularly as he used to but both he and Betty are well.

Win Shepherd too recalls a Belgrave trip on the Continent - to the Rome Olympics of 1960. They had trouble from the start when they couldn't get into their hotel room due to its occupancy by a group of Polish swimmers who were refusing to leave as one of their number had got to the finals. They managed to get put up elsewhere and Harry Shields ended up in the hotel Manager's flat - immaculate decorations including a marble floor - and the first thing he did was set up a primus stove on the floor to brew up a cup of tea.

We understand that Sheena Lucas underwent a hip replacement operation a few months ago but has been making a good recovery. Our best wishes go to her and we look forward to seeing her at one or other of our summer competitions.

A fractured limb has been the lot of walker Paul King this winter. Motorcyclist Paul now shares a little more in common with Barry Sheene, having had an assortment of steel pins inserted in his leg to hold it together after a collision with a car. Actually Paul was lucky he didn't get his other leg broken as well, for when Messrs. Warburton and Lawton arranged to go and see him in hospital, they only found out by chance that he had discharged himself without telling anyone. Believe it or not it wasn't too long before 'Kingy' was managing to get up to the Hall again on training nights - and even if he couldn't get out for a spin, he took advantage of the Club showers. But amazingly, as we go to print, we find that not only is Paul out training again but is planning to enter one of the Veterans Walks at Battersea in a few days!

Unfortunately a little more news about those on the injured list. Poor Gary Staines is, if nothing else, learning how to be patient. Having completed the long haul back to fitness after his achilles tendon problems and operation and with the track season all but upon him, he has now suffered troubles with a calf muscle and at best is likely to have to put his season back a bit.

But for 400 metre star Lewis Samuel the season is already over due to a ruptured achilles tendon, perhaps the worst of athletic injuries and one which hit him, we believe, while playing basketball in Lanzarote. To Lewis and all others on the injured list we send our hopes for a speedy recovery and return to the team.

An interesting document to come the way of ``The Belgravian'' Editorial Offices is a copy of a completed application for a US National Collegiate Championships record. Mike Edwards, representing his Texas outfit Abiline Christian University, vaulted 5.25m to help his team win the competition at the Dakotadome, Vermilion, on March 13th.

A dozen or so members of our track and field team gathered in the Mayor of Merton's Parlour on the evening of Monday 5th April. The Mayor had been thrilled to find out that Great Britain was being represented in the European Clubs' Championship by a team from his own borough and wanted to be introduced to the Bels. He presented a Merton Borough flag for us to fly in Budapest and the Council made a most generous donation towards the costs of transporting the team to Hungary.

Haringey's Dalton Grant is a fine ambassador for our sport and we have him to thank - although he probably doesn't know it - for the re-launching of the athletic career of Julia Cockram. Julia's 1.59m High Jump, set as an Under-13 in May 1980, has stood the test of time and still ranks as the equal 9th best performance ever by a British Minor Girl. Third on that list is current international Debora Marti's 1.65m, also from 1980. OBITUARY F.A. "Mickey" Walker (1908-1992)

Clive Shippen

Mickey Walker was a contemporary of Tom Carter's. Both were born in 1908; they joined Belgrave within nine months of each other and later in their lives went into business together.

He was not one of the stars who featured in our formidable teams of the '30s and '40s, but he was a sound supporting runner who frequently featured in 12-to-score teams and on one occasion was just pipped for sixth scorer by Alf Taylor when the ``Southern'' was held at Taplow.

In the late 1930s he concentrated on distance running with some success. He gained a 1st Class standard in the Polytechnic Marathon of 1938 and the following year won the Surrey County '20' title ahead of the legendary Bert Footer who was making his debut at the distance.

His success in this race led to an over-confident start in the Polytechnic Marathon where ``he blazed through the first five miles in sub-28 minutes'' (Belgravian) - quite a pace in those days. He faded to a creditable 12th. Seeing Footer finish 3rd made him wonder what might have been.

Unlike Carter, Walker did not regard Belgrave as a major part of his life and when he moved to Cornwall he allowed his membership to lapse. He re-joined the Club in 1963, at Tom's instigation, and visited Belgrave Hall very occasionally. He died at his Cornwall home in 1992 at the age of 84 years. Our commiserations are extended to his family and friends.

WALKING TO SUCCESS

Paul Warburton

A chance to try something different -

From small acorns grow unexpected opportunities of athletic stardom. This summer if you're in the vicinity of Battersea Park on certain Tuesday evenings listed at the end, why not give racewalking a try?

Now just before you turn the page because you wouldn't be seen dead bum wiggling, at least give me the rest of the paragraph. What are your current chances of county, international or major championship honours?

I would reckon that if you are athletically fit and open-minded the British walker has six times greater chances of doing one of the above than the British runner. Six times? A top road running field is 300 - a top walking field is 50. Every current Belgravian walker (aged between 30 and 60) has won a representative honour of some sort. Candidly, the British walking standard is not that strong. Some international vests in recent years have been won, especially by females, with remarkable ease.

I was an average runner in 1982: 25:50 was my best for 5 miles. Giving walking a go was a means of recovering from injury. I decided to stick with it. By 1986 I had won National team medals, Southern area championships, county championships, and a sackfull of open race medals and prizes.

Kerry Saxby was part of the excellent 'Little League' system in Australia where young children have a dabble at all athletic events, and discovered her event when she had a go at walking after an average showing at running, throwing, etc. At the time of writing she has just won another major championships medal indoors at Toronto to go with the 28 world records or bests already posted by the Queensland girl.

Of course, anybody that knows anything, knows that walking is not a soft option. It's not easier than running. For a start it's the only event that combines technique and aerobic effort. And so it has cardio-vascular benefits the equal of running without the threat of accompanying injury.

I'm not saying walking is for you. But it's like eating something new. If you've never tried it, how can you say you don't like it? Why not give walking one try before rejecting it? You might discover a new athletic career ...

Paul Warburton

SURREY COUNTY VETERANS CC CHAMPS., RICHMOND PARK, 17 OCT

BVAF NATIONAL CC CHAMPS., BURNLEY, 7 MAR

It was an early Sunday morning start for the combined Belgrave/VAC party who made the long trip north to Burnley for this year's Vets National. A successful trip too, with Bob Treadwell winning the M40 race, silver medals for the Belgrave M60 team, and Bob Belmore a rival member of the winning Barnet squad.

On arrival, Townley Park fell somewhat short of the vision some had of beautiful north country moors. The start was a flat scrubby playing field alongside a dirty stream, then came a long, long, muddy climb of at least three quarters of a mile up to the edge of a council estate at the top of the surrounding hills. Down again, past a huge ugly factory, and then came the wade through a smelly stream to the finish. All this three times. However, the day was very well organised, the competition intense, and one wise old runner kindly reminded us that when the race was last held in the South the venue was Wormwood Scrubs!

With Laurie O'Hara missing from the Belgrave team it was Charlie Walker, in inspired form, who led the Belgrave M60's in the first race. Charlie finished 7th in his age category, an improvement from last year and even ahead of a tired looking Tony Stone. Strong runs from Bert Stroud and Pat Newell made up the scoring three with Arthur Bruce and Fred Paget freely offering advice from the side. Barnet had their new star, Ron Higgs, and did just enough to take gold, which of course was much commented upon afterwards by Bob Belmore.

Bob Treadwell had rested from the National at Parliament Hill the previous week in anticipation of the strong field and the demanding course. He found the long, slow climbs to his liking, reminiscent of several Continental hill races that had given him success in the past and suited to long striding runners with long hamstrings who are able to find the right breathing rhythm. (All age winners were built thus.) Bob got away on the first climb, and although always wary of the in form Brian Kirkwood from Scotland who finished ahead of him in the Inter-Counties, held on well for a great victory.

Charlie Dickinson hasn't got long hamstrings and was rather disappointed with 26th place after finishing 11th the previous year. The long drive as chauffeur may also have taken its toll.

With Maggie Statham, coached by Bob Belmore, and Steve Charlton also winning their races, it was a successful day for VAC and the South. The long drive home gave us a mixture of snoozing, stories of races from the past and more friendly Belgrave/Barnet banter.

"Don't worry, we'll have you lot in the relay".

"Why don't you rejoin us instead of running for that North London lot ? On second thoughts it would be a waste of time, we don't run a 'B' team !"

Well, perhaps not quite so friendly !

Another successful season

Leo Coy

Another successful cross-country season has ended with good all round performances by all age group teams and some very good individual results.

The season got under way in October with a win for the Youths in the Surrey CC Relay. The event was held on the old Surrey AC relay course in Richmond Park.

Our HHH League performance was probably our best ever. We managed to get team or individual prizes in all groups as well as winning third overall for combined age groups. Simon Vella, Gavin Jones, John Wilson and Adam Jones all performed very well in each match.

In the Surrey League our combined Youths/Boys team had no opposition all year. Matthew Hillier won two races with Adam Jones and Gavin Mason winning the other two. This, with good back-up from the Boys, ensured that we won with ease. Tom Evans and Simon Alexander had great runs at Lloyd Park - they surprised some of the Youths and pushed them out of the scoring five! Unfortunately we will be in Division Two with the Seniors next year.

The County Championships saw mixed performances with the best result coming from Simon Vella, who came 2nd in the Middlesex Colts' race.

At the Southern, we finished our full quota of eight in the Boys' age group and the team were 20th. Simon Alexander had a good run to lead the team home in 51st place. The Youths team finished 7th overall, without Gavin or Stuart Booth. Matthew and Adam had excellent runs.

This year for the first time we participated in the Indoor Sportshall League for the U13 age group. The league was held over three matches and took place in large sports halls. The events consist of sprints, relays, paarlauf, plus throws and jumps. Each team is made up of six competitors and each can only do one individual event. Everybody takes part in the final relay.

The competition was fierce, with an excellent atmosphere at each venue. It is an exciting event which will continue to grow in popularity and will be an alternative to an easy winter for our young throwers, sprinters and jumpers.

We performed well throughout and won the final match at Cheam. Es Hassan, Andrew Hill, Duncan Thompson, Steve Taylor and Dwaine Patterson were selected to represent Surrey in the National Event.

Thanks to Chris. Knight, Billy Bull and Martin Milner for their help throughout the season and also thanks to the parents who supported us so well. I must not forget Steve Whorlow, for his role as acting team manager when I was delayed - I did have a long way to travel. Captains Toby, Marlon and Adam played their part all season during both training and competition.

Next season sees the start of the new age bands which will mean that most of our Youths will miss their second winter season in the age group and will become Juniors. Let us hope that they continue to improve together and become our Senior team of the future.

Step by step guide to Athletic Meetings

Sue Porter (Club Starter & General Secretary)

Everyone competes at athletic meetings, local leagues or national leagues and probably only notice the field and track officials in passing. They're those men and women in the navy jackets!

How do these officials get involved in the first place? Often it's as a parent helping out, holding tapes, collecting shot, even timekeeping. Then before they know it they're hooked and, having taken a straightforward test, become a Graded Official, the first step on the ladder to becoming Chief Track Judge, Referee or Timekeeper.

So now we have a band of officials, how do we proceed?

We first have to agree on a date to hold the meeting. This is a lot more difficult than you would expect or believe. With so many meetings held in the summer, tracks are booked up very early. And of course it's best not to clash with national or county events, or any of the established leagues.

Once the date has been sorted out then a timetable and list of events can be drawn up. Adverts for entries are placed in the magazines and circularised to other clubs in the area and then it's time to sit back and wait for the entries to come pouring in.

On the day itself, the most important concern is the weather. As long as the rain isn't too heavy, or the snow can be swept off the track and field areas, we are all systems go. Also, whether or not all officials and helpers manage to park their cars close enough to the track to haul their bags of wet gear and other equipment; and whether the refreshments are available throughout the meeting. Officials work best on sweet tea and ginger biscuits.

After a successful meeting you'd think it would be an easy matter to lock up and return all the equipment to the rightful places. But there's always some items of kit to return to owners - if you can find them. One starter, who shall remain nameless, was without his red jacket - heaven forbid - for some months. Don't ask how he managed to be an official starter without it.

So then it's time to retire, battered and shattered and mentally bereft, to go through the results and send out the medals, etc., not collected. Oh well! Leave that 'til tomorrow ... Tomorrow's another day - and next week's another meeting ...

Easy isn't it? You too can help, you really can. You're only a 'phone call away from a fun-filled summer officiating for Belgrave Harriers.

Sue Porter (Club Starter & General Secretary)

A Team Manager's DREAM

Harry Porter

Harry Porter writes about Jacinta Moore née Coppinger, one of our Ladies' Captains, whom he describes as

A Team Manager's DREAM

I first met Jacinta in November 1985, just after the cross-country season had started. It was my second season as Ladies Team Manager, having succeeded Derek Jones after his five year stint. Since that time Jacinta has competed on the Road, Country and Track, making over 150 appearances in the Belgrave team despite a break of some months due to muscle injury and extensive physiotherapy.

Jacinta's first appearance for Belgrave was at the Surrey Women's Cross Country League Meeting at Ewell Court, just before Christmas 1985. She had only recently come to England from her native Dublin and had joined us soon after her arrival. That first season started with a good first run, 23rd, and she went on to finish the winter in 13th. Not bad at all for a first attempt.

She had originally wanted to run longer distances and even now enjoys an occasional half-marathon. In fact she came second to Leslie Watson in the Welwyn 20 mile road race in February 1985 and in 1990 ran alongside the likes of Greta Weitz and Rosa Mota in the Women's Great London Run over 10k where she finished a creditable 21st in 38:34. That 10k was a pretty good performance when you consider that the day before she had run in the Hertford County Road Relays where her second-claim team had placed 2nd.

Representing Hertfordshire on numerous occasions over both Country and Track, Jacinta's best result was a fourth position in the County Cross Country Championships of 1988 and she has run for that county in the Southern and National Inter-Counties events. On the track she was silver medallist in the Herts. County Championships 3,000m for two consecutive years from 1988, running 10:40 both times.

Having lived in Surrey for the last two years, Jacinta now has Surrey qualifications and in 1992 ran the in the County 10k Road Championships when the Belgrave Ladies were second team. The team went on to win the first ever Women's Surrey Road League in the summer of 1992.

As well as track and cross-country, Jacinta also enjoys the longer relays, and in particular the now defunct 25 x 1 mile relay held at the end of the track season. She competed in the race every year until the last in 1989. However, since 1988 the Ladies have also taken part in the Ostende Relays. The distances in this event vary from 100m to 600m and Jacinta has run a 600m leg ever since the team first ventured onto the Continent. She was also a member of the Southern Women's 3 x 800m gold medal winning team in 1987, along with Jacqui Smiter and Jackie Stone.

Jacinta has been Track Captain since 1986 when she saw the team finish 7th in their first season in the Southern Women's League Division 2, narrowly missing promotion direct to Division 1. And in 1987 she led Belgrave into the semi-final of the GRE Cup, the only Division 2 team to have achieved that status since 1984.

Jacinta considers that her most memorable day was the Melbourne Trophy International match at Crystal Palace in September 1987, where she came 3rd in the mile in 5:16.86. However, I would have to disagree, remembering her 4th place in the Surrey Women's Cross Country League match at Ewell Court, November 1990. But then again, from the spectators point of view, her 3,000m in that GRE Cup semi-final qualifier at Kingston in June 1987 takes some beating.

She had gone out hard in front, leading the race for the first three laps. The rest of the pack, after recovering from the shock, gradually managed to pull her back and the Belgravian dropped to 4th position, to all intents and purposes, spent. But not so, because 400 metres from the finish she changed gear and went for the front again, making up over 30 metres in the last lap. To roaring and screaming from Belgrave teammates she surged through at the line to finish 1st. The whole field crossed the line within 5 metres. It was a thrilling race and she had gained six valuable points which took the team into the semi-finals for 1987. Jacinta had knocked a massive 1 minute off her personal best at the beginning of that season and a further 13 seconds had come off just two weeks previously. What a tremendous performance and what a memorable day for the whole team.

Apart from having competed for Belgrave and Hertfordshire, Jacinta also enters many road races, both here and in Eire. In 1991 she won the Limerick 5 miles road title, and she also took the Billericay 9 miles with 57:20.

However, life changed drastically and irrevocably for Jacinta in that year. She met her future husband Michael after an unfortunate accident with wet paint - you will have to ask Jacinta for the full story - my lips are sealed. But the incident did prove very fortunate for the couple who married towards the end of 1991, when Sue and myself were privileged to attend on behalf of the Club. The wedding was held on a beautiful sunny day at a quiet country church beside the shimmering waters of a lough.

At a photocall afterwards, the neat group of Michael, his parents and two brothers were swamped when the photographer innocently called for the bride and her family. Jacinta's parents, her eleven brothers and sisters and their wives and children surged into view - the photographer needed a wide angle lens to cope with this sitting!

"It was a quiet year," says Jacinta.

In June 1992 Jacinta spread the glad tidings that she was pregnant, and her daughter Cliona was born on 1st February this year. Jacinta had raced in the Wimbledon 5k in early December, just eight weeks before the baby's arrival, and was back in action in the very last cross-country race of the season, which included the Belgrave Championships, on March 13th. Almost a Liz McColgan!

Many team managers would dream of having a Jacinta in their team. She is wholly committed to her club where, for her, events takes precedence over any other competition. She is one of those rare people who are able to get that little bit extra from people when that little bit extra can make all the difference. A good motivator without a doubt, the Belgrave Women's Section is so much the better for her presence.

For everything you have achieved for us, Jacinta, we all thank you. Well done - and here's to the future too.

Harry Porter

Feature · page 28

The Record Breaker

Alan Mead

I had set out hours ago, having walked to the bus-stop at the Malden Roundabout and then ridden the number 72 route all the way to Shepherd's Bush, dispatching a "hay stack" of corned beef and tomato sandwiches long before crossing the river at Hammersmith. There was a great confluence of athletics fans in Wood Lane. Thousands, like me, were trekking up from the "Bush", and hordes poured out of the tube station. All had come to the White City to be at the 1960 AAA Championships and watch the likes of Peter Radford, Milkha Singh and Gordon Pirie go through their paces in preparation for the forthcoming Rome Olympics.

Belgrave had some pretty good sprinters and middle-distance men in those far off days and I was keen to see them in action but in 1960 the Belgrave Walking Section was supreme. Walking Secretary Jack Goswell was able to deploy some half a dozen internationals in team races and they had swept all before them - the Leicester '20', RWA '20', RWA '50' - all had featured 'BELGRAVE H' at the top of the result sheet. This day was going to give me the chance to see one of those heroes, Stan Vickers, in combat with his Midland rival Ken Matthews of Royal Sutton Coldfield. Both were to duel in the walker's sprint - the 2 miles. Both were in superb form and the likelihood was that the UK record of 13:11.4, set 56 years earlier on 13th July 1904 by George E. Larner, was going to go.

High up on the concrete terracing of that draughty stadium, now long gone, I had a bird's-eye view of the arena. The dull red clay track contrasted with the surrounding grass oval of the greyhound-racing circuit that separated the fans from the competitors. No digital results display-screen in 1960 of course, but a huge white results board and a large clock with a sweep second hand by which one could judge the athletes' progress versus the record book.

The three weeks before the event had already seen some astounding 2 mile walk times. At Motspur Park in the Southern Champs. Stan had won in his best ever time of 13:31.8, beating his own championship best by 17 seconds. On the same day Matthews had taken the Midland title, also in a championship best, with 13:36. Just a few days later in a club match against the Stock Exchange at Battersea the Belgravian had gone faster still with 13:26.4 and then, the very next Saturday, on Peterborough's grass track, Ken and Stan clashed in an invitation race where the latter confidently sprinted to victory and actually equalled Larner's 1904 record. Again at Battersea, on the Wednesday night preceding the AAA, the record withstood another assault as Vickers scorched to a time just one fifth of a second slower. Surely that time would crumble today.

Eighteen men faced the starter and the four East Germans, Lindner, Hildebrand, Adolph and Pathus, together with the Sikhs Zora and Adjit Singh, made it a truly international field. Stan was not the only Belgravian in the line-up as Eric Hall was also hoping to impress.

The start was unbelievably fast. At the gun Zora Singh tore into the lead and after a single circuit the timekeepers logged lap one at 1:34.3 - that's 12:34 pace! Matthews and Vickers were in tow and then came Colin Williams, Adjit Singh and Lindner. But the inevitable happened, Zora had thrown caution to the winds with his effort and after half a mile the judges pulled him out, a fate that soon befell his fellow countryman and two of the Germans. Now it was the powerful Matthews blasting along at the front with Vickers so close behind him that they were later described as "siamese twins".

The mile came up - 6:30.2 - had such a time ever been recorded before? Williams was now in trouble some 15 seconds behind and was soon to be swept up by Lindner and Hall. It was obvious that the burly Sutton Coldfield man was out to avenge that defeat of the previous week but how on earth could he keep such a pace going. One of these two must crack soon.

Further laps of 99 to 100 seconds unreeled - one and a half miles in 9:51.4. I can't remember a walk before or since causing such an uproar from the crowd. Still only one yard covered the two protagonists as the strident clangour of the last lap bell cut through the cheers. Ken Matthews needed just one more lap in 1:40.5 to take the record but something in the angular frame of the man in his wake foretold of more drama to come.

Stan suddenly veered out and round the Midlander in an all-out heel and toe sprint. For an instant Matthews seemed stunned before he too released every remaining spark of energy left in his body. Vickers had taken twenty yards by the back straight and as the final turn was approached, spectators were torn between the vision of Ken Matthews desperately trying to get back into contention, Stan Vickers racing towards the finish line, and that giant second-hand on the result board, sweeping inexorably on towards the top of the clock and the 13 minute mark. Tumultuous cheers reached an even greater volume as the Belgrave Harrier crossed the line. He had clearly smashed the record and for an instant it seemed that he might have even gone under 13 minutes. Within a short while it was announced that Larner's time had been beaten by 9 seconds and that Matthews too had gone inside the old mark.

1, S.E.Vickers (Belgrave) 13:02.4 (UK and AAA National Record); 2, K.J.Matthews (Royal Sutton Coldfield) 13:09.6; 3, D.Lindner (E.Germany); 4, E.W.Hall (Belgrave) 13:51.8.

It's a sobering thought that Stan's time was worth around 12:09 for the modern-day equivalent of the race - 3,000 metres.

That day at the White City was, of course, just a landmark on the road to greater glory for several of the competitors. In the sweltering heat of the Rome Olympics where just about every British distance athlete performed well below par, Stan became the 20 kilometre Walk bronze medallist in 1 hour 34 minutes and 57 seconds, 49 seconds behind the great Soviet Champion Golubnichiy. At the half-way point Stan had passed a badly suffering Ken Matthews who commanded a 19 second lead at one stage, only to falter in the conditions and later require hospital treatment. Eric Hall also posted an excellent performance in that Rome race, coming home 10th. Ken's day of Olympic triumph came four years later at the Tokyo Games when it was his turn to win gold with Lindner 2nd.

But back on that July Saturday evening in 1960 it just remained for a 16 year old Belgravian to walk back to Shepherd's Bush and wait for the bus, head a-buzzing with the excitement of seeing a fellow Club member setting a UK record.

Six mile road race.

Feature · page 32

1948

Arthur Bruce

1948 by Arthur Bruce

1948! A year that conjures up pictures of titanic struggles climaxing in the first Post War Olympic Games in austerity ridden Britain with eager athletes deprived of Olympic competition since 1936, anxious to prove themselves. Arthur Bruce continues his tales of athletics from the post-war years.

After two post war seasons a pattern of World Athletics had solidified. And when the Games were ushered in on Friday 30th July 1948 at the newly laid track at Wembley Stadium, armed with our daily shilling (5p) programme, we were geared for a feast of athletics.

Wooderson's "National"

The British winter season that had preceded the Olympics had produced a number of post war stars to which had been added Sydney Wooderson, who after a magnificent track season of 1946 had professed his intention to finish his career by attempting to win the National Cross Country title.

Starting with an exploratory season in 1947, he entered 1948 with his running programme geared to the National in March at Graves Park, Sheffield. He achieved his objective after a tremendous struggle with our own Vic Blowfield, only taking the lead in the finishing funnel. Belgrave won the team race with 154 points beating their conquerors of 1947, Sutton Harriers by 20 points. The Bels scoring six were home in 57 places. Wooderson's subsequent performance in the International at Reading was disappointing, and coupled with the English team being comfortably beaten by the Belgians who were led by surprise winner, Jean Doms. For Wooderson it was the end of a brilliant career dating back to his public school mile win in the early 'thirties.

The year progressed to a short road relay season climaxing in April with the classic London to Brighton Relay which produced another great club struggle, with Bill Lucas taking Belgrave into the lead over Birchfield on the last stage into Brighton.

The track season

The track season was of course organised along simpler lines than today, with only the British Games and Inter-Counties at Whitsun weekend to interrupt the progression of County and District leading to the AAA Championships and selection for the Olympic Games held in the first week of July.

The principal talking points in early season were the hopes of McDonald Bailey, the Trinidadian born sprinter who had elected to run for Britain and who had shown such good form since 1946. This year, however, he was plagued with injuries and confronted by a new star. Alastair McCorquadale arrived suddenly on the sprinting scene, beating Bailey in the Kinnaird Trophy meeting. The other major talking point concerned Roger Bannister, the new Oxford discovery, who had been a surprise inter-varsity mile winner in 1947. Bannister wisely had turned down any thought of running in the 1948 Games, preferring to work to a long term plan for 1952 and Helsinki.

The AAA Championships proved to be as exciting as predicted. The 100yds provided a win for John Treloar the Australian sprint hope who just edged out McCorquadale and Bailey. McCorquadale returned to win the 220yds. Another Australian prodigy, Morris Curotta, won the 440yds from the best Britain could offer. The 880yds, probably the finest race of the Championships, seemed to be a struggle between Arthur Wint and the visiting New Zealand Champion Doug Harris until a few yards from the tape when both were overwhelmed by John Parlett's late surge. Bill Nankeville (Belgrave) gained the first of his four 1 mile titles with Roger Bannister in his Championship debut, in 5th place.

Other titles to go abroad were the 3 miles won by Wooderson's old rival Willie Slykhuis, the Dutch star, and the High and Long jumps to visiting Australian Olympians. The most promising performance from Britain's point of view was the win by the 18 year old schoolboy J.Burrell in the 120yds hurdles, beating several established stars. One could say that the British Olympic team possessed a number of possible winners while the opposition was difficult to gauge.

The "Games"

The Olympic Games were four weeks later, with 53 Nations providing 815 athletes. They were without Germany, Japan, Russia and most of the African Continent - a compact affair but of high quality nevertheless.

The opening day revealed the strength of the American team, especially in the sprints, 800 metres and 400 metre hurdles, with Britain's sprinters and middle distance runners also qualifying. The climax of the day was the revelation of the Games, Lt. Emil Zatopek of the Czech Army who demolished the field in the 10,000 metres, winning by over three-quarters of a minute and breaking the legend of the Finnish runners in this event, a distance they had dominated for over twenty years.

Day two, Saturday July 31st, threw up a surprise winner in the 100 metres. Gold medallist was the American third string Harrison Dillard, in reality a World class hurdler. Our two hopefuls McCorquadale and McDonald Bailey made the final and were 5th and 6th respectively. John Parlett excelled himself with a time of 1:50 to qualify for the 800m final while the US produced yet another world class hurdler in Ray Cochran to take the 400m hurdle title in 51.1, a new record.

Monday brought Fanny Blankers-Koen the first of her four wins as she took the 100m for Holland. Mal Whitfield retained the US grip on the 800m by holding off Arthur Wint in a close finish and the day ended with Emil Zatopek just failing to catch Gaston Reiff of Belgium in monsoon-like conditions in the 5,000m, after appearing well beaten.

On Wednesday the stream of excellent performances continued with Blankers-Koen beating our own Maureen Gardner narrowly in the 80m hurdles for her second gold medal. Finland had their expected win in the men's Javelin and the USA made a clean sweep in the 110m hurdles, Bill Porter winning in 13.9.

The strength of Swedish steeplechasing was shown on the Thursday, with the first three home led by Sjoestrand in 9.04. This was followed by a memorable 400m final in which Herb McKenley was overtaken in the straight by his fellow Jamaican Arthur Wint, the 800m silver medallist.

Friday, and after another downpour yet another favourite, the World 1,500m record holder Lennart Strand was held off by his fellow Swede Henry Eriksson; our own Bill Nankeville finished a meritorious 6th in 3.52. The other final that day saw Blankers-Koen win her third gold in the 200m from Britain's Audrey Williamson.

The last day provided the expected relay wins in the men's events by the United States, the longer relay somewhat luckily after Arthur Wint pulled a muscle on the third stage while making inroads into the American lead. The women's sprint relay went to Holland, giving Fanny Blankers-Koen gold medal number four, and the Decathlon showed the world the new American discovery Bob Mathias, who at the tender age of 17 upstaged his more experienced rivals. Both walks went to Sweden but only after much dispute amongst the judges which cast a cloud over future track walks. The grand finale, the Marathon, provided its usual high drama with Cabrera of Argentina catching Belgium's Etienne Gailly four hundred metres from the tape closely, followed by Britain's Tommy Richards, who had run a very sensible race.

Belgrave Olympians

Belgrave's involvement in these stirring events were a fine 5th place in the 10,000m track walk by Harry Churcher, Bill Nankeville's equally good 6th place in the 1,500m final and Bill Lucas' run in the 5,000m heats where he was unfortunate to draw Zatopek's heat. A word should also be said about the marathon runner Etienne Gailly, for while in England in the Army, he had run for Belgrave. The runners were not Belgrave's only contribution, as a scrutiny of the programmes exhibited a number of Belgrave officials among the judges, announcers and officials who had made the Games such a success.

Team performances by the Bels during the year were first rate. Major titles over the Country included the Surrey, Southern and National titles, coupled with the South of Thames Senior and the principal road relay of the year, the London to Brighton. The walkers maintained their usual high standard by winning the National 10 mile Championship and also took the Surrey title. In the remainder of the walking Championships they were only thwarted by the excellent Sheffield and Surrey teams.

On the track, excluding those already mentioned in the Games, a number of excellent performances were produced. Alan Sexton ran a classic race to win the AAA Junior 220yd title and evergreen Charlie Smart, home on leave from Germany, finished 5th in the AAA 6 mile Championships, only a few yards away from Olympic selection. On studying the results of the closed Belgrave events and Championships one is stuck by the depth in all sections of the club and the support given to club handicaps and Championships, and the keen competition for places in Belgrave teams.

Arthur Bruce

Len Herbert and Bill Lucas were often involved in battle royal. Len, who gained his international vest in 1946, invariably used to sit in on the taller, longer striding Bill, waiting to turn on his powerful sprint in the final stretch. But on this occasion, believed to be at Wimbledon Stadium in 1951, Bill held the sprinter at bay. W.E. "Bill "Lucas, DFC, made his international debut in the heats of the 1948 Olympic 5,000m.

THE YACHT HANDICAP, WIMBLEDON, 3 OCT

ARM

A steady drizzle did little to dampen the spirits of young Stuart Booth who must have thought it was his birthday when he found the handicapper ushering him towards the front of the indian file making up the start of the 1992 'Yacht' Handicap. Dennis Jones was next away and then came a short wait until a gaggle around the six-minute mark were sent off, Stuart among them and grinning like the Cheshire Cat as he rounded the turn at the end of Lauriston Road. Bels raise their game for Southern Road Relay

SOUTH OF ENGLAND 12-STAGE ROAD RELAY, THURROCK, 4 APR

Perhaps 11th position may not seem much after the glories and past triumphs in the old London to Brighton and then the Belgrave 12-Stage, but this was a magnificent performance by a team decimated by illness, injuries and withdrawals.

It is a tribute to team manager Colin Pearson that he managed not only to put together a team, but one which finished high enough to qualify for the National Road Relay and in doing so beat all our rivals from the Surrey League except Boxhill, who won, and Hounslow, who only came by us after Marco Mazzotta went off course.

Throughout the afternoon, Colin's cheerful confidence spread throughout the team, half of whom were unfit, or in the case of Marcello Bizio, actually ill. He had got out of his sick bed to run. We were without Evans, Staines, Foote and Paton but it didn't matter. There was John Gladwin running in the same team as his old friend Mark Sinclair for the first time in years, the pair of them enjoying themselves and the occasion. And several others were given the chance to represent Belgrave and they ran above themselves for their club.

Of course the occasion was not like Wimbledon. The relay has been moved from our home patch because of ever increasing problems with the traffic: here the changeover was just in front of the main stand of a small running track set in the heart of a bleak part of Essex.

After the gun it was half a lap of the track, then out through a housing estate and past a handful of spectators, down a narrow, straight, flat country lane and off into the distance. Five minutes later the crocodile of runners could just be seen as they ran up the incline of the bridge over the busy Southend road. Yes, it was Giovanni Rizzo ahead, silhouetted against the electric pylons and A13 traffic a mile away. Then, not a sight of them until they returned to negotiate a nasty cross road threequarters of a mile from the finish, one which later in the day had short and long stage runners coming and going in three directions, tempers fraying, motorists honking, marshals becoming frantic - a scene all so familiar. Thurrock were learning what putting on a road relay was all about.

Boxhill led at the end of the first stage, a position they were never to lose, with Belgrave's Roger Alsop, running so well at the moment, just on 25 minutes in 24th place. Owain Lewes, who is aiming for a season of shorter track races, showed the benefit of recent speed work with one of his best runs for the club on the next short stage.

On leg three, Jim Estall produced his usual reliable run in spite of high mileage marathon training: "Can't I run two legs ?" Then a half fit Frank Ward got round on memory to hand over to Steve Halliday, down from Halifax for the race, as cheery as ever and having a blinding run with 4th fastest long stage of the day to move Belgrave up from 17th to 10th.

Progression continued with John Gladwin, again only half fit, on the next short stage, and handing on to Kaessa Tadesse who had the difficult task of setting out just behind a bunch containing Andy Bristow and Dominic Bannister, the Southern champion. Bristow ran a time second only to Eammon Martin's fastest of the day but Kaessa also ran an excellent leg and broke Bannister 1.5 miles from the finish.

Mark Sinclair ran even faster than John Gladwin, passing on to Mike Webb, rather happier on the country but as reliable as ever. Then a buoyant Darrell Maynard was a second faster than Mark. Poor Marco Mazzotta, not really fit enough for a long stage, but willing to fill in for Marcello who had been ill all week with a tooth abscess and 'flu. Marco took a wrong turning, lost at least a minute, and then ran himself to exhaustion trying to make up for it.

This left Marcello on the last leg, could he maintain 11th position and qualify the team for Sutton Park later in the month. Of course the answer was yes. When ever did Marcello let the team down in a situation like this. He ran with fire in his eyes and produced the team's fastest short stage, just ducking under 14 minutes.

In the end there was just over a minute between the leading three teams but the chasers never really got close to Boxhill who seemed to be about the only team at full strength. No, it didn't have the same atmosphere as Wimbledon, let alone the London to Brighton, but it was an enjoyable and successful day for Belgrave nevertheless.

THE LONDON MARATHON, 18 APR

After Paul Evans' exciting 1992 season which included, of course, his fine 5th place in last year's 'London', he was most people's favourite to sweep up the honours in this year's edition of the classic. He had raced sparingly in the run-up to the event yet destroyed the field when he had ventured out in his racing kit. But we know so well that there is no such thing as a certainty in athletics where a very fine line divides the super-fit from those suffering injury or illness. Unknown to us spectators, Paul had started suffering from a sore throat on the Saturday afternoon but come the morning of the race, he was still intent on competing.

At the 'House on the Bridge', a bar on the southern approach to Tower Bridge nearing the 13 mile point, a television visible from the pavement had thoughtfully been left switched on overnight; no doubt the licensee was hoping to attract the marathon crowd when he opened later in the day. At 9.30am, like moths gathered around a candle flame, athletics fans were shoulder to shoulder on the footpath outside, noses pressed against the bar window to watch the initial stages of the race. At the early mile markers Paul could be seen in the leading pack but with the women's race due along soon it was time to desert the TV and take up a place at the barriers.

Nothing matches actually being present at a big sporting occasion. We had been warmed up by the wheelchair competitors, the Liz and Lisa show had moved on past, and now, with the energy of a tide in full flood, the leaders in the men's field, a group some thirty strong, burst around the corner from Abbey Street into Tower Bridge Road with the accompanying cheering and flag waving from the watchers being pushed along ahead of them like so much flotsam.

Where was Paul? There - no that's the tall blonde Polish runner. What could have happened. Jim Estall came by and cheerfully waved back on hearing his name called. Some hundreds of runners streamed through before Evans was sighted, a mere shadow of his normal self and, to be honest, looking quite unwell. He came out somewhere over the bridge and must have been bitterly disappointed to leave the race in the hands of those he had previously been confident of beating.

Nevertheless, he has already been selected for the World Champs. in Stuttgart and if he can pop out that 2:08 effort then, without the prior warning of 'London', it's going to cause an even greater shock.

NATIONAL 12-STAGE ROAD RELAY, SUTTON PARK, 24 APR

It was in 1974 that the National Road Relay was held for the first time at Sutton Park and there have been some impressive runs by individuals and teams throughout the intervening years. This year the winning time was well outside the top ten but for excitement it was second to none. The Midland clubs, who hold their area championship on the same course, were strongly tipped to provide the medallists but when it came to the 'cards down' it was the South that held the aces. The Shaftesbury Barnet team manager judged things just right, for John Sherban, on the penultimate leg, set the fastest long stage time of the day in turning a 30 second deficit into a 55 second lead. And then the thrills continued with Boxhill Racers, through Tom Bignall, moving from outside the frame into 2nd spot on the very last lap while Swansea's tail-end super-vets held the chasing hordes at bay for 3rd.

The Belgrave outfit was again hit by illness and injury with young Marlon Anderson getting pretty close to the action as next on the reserve list.

Stage 1 was taken by Steve Halliday who was most certainly on for one of the fastest times of the day - except that while holding a 70 yard lead over the field he went off course, lost about a minute, and then trailed in a disconsolate 18th!

Our fastest short stage came from Roger Alsop on leg 2. He covered the 3m 8y in 14:40 to retrieve three places and, bearing in mind the gradients, set a time which must equate to about 15 minutes for 5,000m, a target he surely must achieve before too long.

Kaessa Tadesse was the pick of the rest of our long stage runners, benefiting from Steve's misfortune to become our fastest over the 5m 706y stretch with 26:06, and Johnny G took yet another step along the road to fitness by running a 14:50 effort that makes an appearance over 800m in our team for Budapest begin to look a real possibility.
