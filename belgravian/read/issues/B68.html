Editorial · page 3

ACHIEVEMENT ! English Cross-Country Champions, 1935 : Belgrave Harriers.

A.A.H.

To have succeeded where we have so often failed—to have achieved an ambition which once seemed almost beyond hopes of realisation—to have tasted the sweets of victory when we have known the dregs of bitterness and disappointment. To have advanced gradually from the darkness into the dazzling light of publicity that is directed upon a champion club—to have striven and to have toiled; and now to know the feeling of exhilaration which accompanies the fulfilment of aspirations. That is our reward. Hard-won honours are ours.

The satisfaction which comes from a win such as ours is all the more real and heart-felt because of the difficulties with which we have had to contend in the past. Every impediment to progress and advancement known to the smallest and poorest club now, has been our lot in the past. We have had to fight for our very existence in the past; but we have won through and in the 48th year of our history assumed the title of English Cross-country Champions.

Everyone of us feels that he has helped, in varying degree, towards the success which has attended our efforts, and this is the quintessence of Club-spirit. It is given to few to bring honour and distinction to the Club name, but we are all part of the spirit which has inspired the effort. One of our younger walkers tells the story, against himself, of how he rushed into the street after hearing the news over the radio of our Beaconsfield win and, meeting a friend, shouted excitedly, “We've won the English Cross-country Championship.” To which his astonished friend replied, “Where were you placed?” Upon being told that our young friend was many miles away at the time of the race, and never ran (opportunity for joke! gambit declined) he walked away marvelling at the inexplicability of human nature! Well, at least, we can understand!

Congratulations have rolled in from all directions, for which we are very grateful. Our win was certainly a very popular one and it is good to know we stand high in the estimation of our fellow-sportsmen.

Well, we are English champions, and next season will find us defending, not challenging. We shall do our utmost to hold the title and our runners will find, as our walkers have found in the past, that they are expected to win, win, win, whatever the opposition. The second or third placing which seemed out of reach a few years ago, and which, only yesteryear, was so greatly prized and gave our Club-members and supporters such intense pleasure, will to-morrow be regarded as failure. A new standard of comparison has arisen, and that our team will do their very best to live up to it is beyond question.

We can congratulate ourselves upon being the only club in the history of Athletics to place its name upon three National team trophies. At the moment we hold both the Cross-country 50 kilos. walking team championships. The 20 miles walking title has been ours in 1924, 1925, 1928 and 1929, and we have great hopes of annexing it again in a month's time and so complete a glorious treble. It would be a wonderful finale to the Winter season.

And so let us close by heartily, sincerely and gratefully congratulating our Cross-country captain, H. E. Footer, and his team upon the new honours brought to the Club, not forgetting all the hard workers who have laid foundations in the past, and the helpers, reserves and supporters of the present. As to the future, the inspiration has been provided. Our young men will see that it has not been provided in vain! In winning the Southern Counties “Junior” title for the first time, our younger runners have shown they have real talent, and in the years to come we have but little doubt that they will bring more and more credit to the Club to which they belong.

A.A.H.

A.A.H.H. E. Footer

THE ENGLISH CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP, BEACONSFIELD, MARCH 9th, 1935.

A.A.H.

Having generalised about our successful tilt at the highest cross-country honours, the time now comes for something more detailed. But how to treat the subject? Is our win to be the occasion for a welter of superlatives, a panegyric of praise, a laudatory outpouring partaking of all that flies fastest to the head in the English language? Or is it to be regarded rather as the inevitable reward of planned progress and persistence, allied to splendid team spirit?

Undoubtedly the latter is the truest approach. Knowing the determination to succeed which has permeated our team for some time past, one would have been surprised, upon looking back at some future time, if the English title had not been won. Nevertheless, it must be admitted that there was something of a dramatic suddenness about the manner in which our hopes were realised. A fortnight previously we had surrendered our Southern title to Surrey A.C., and whilst we had plenty of confidence that this result would be reversed, and that Salford would have to yield to us this time, we were not so optimistic as to think that Birchfield, holders for the previous seven years, with eight Internationals entered in the team, would be humbled and forced into fourth position! However, we anticipate. Let's start at the beginning.

Two hundred and ninety-five runners thundered from the starting pens, and in the first 50 yards Shaw and Shields had nasty falls over two prostrate Ealing men. They picked themselves up and had all the field before them. The first lap of one mile was soon covered, and then out into the country on the first lap of three miles of fast, undulating park land, with our boys fairly well placed.

At four miles we had another view of the runners. Bailey (Salford) led with Close (Reading A.C.) on his shoulder. Jim Ginty was fourth at this point, and our other men all seemed to be weighing up their opponents and running strongly. An attempt to get an idea of the scoring was doomed to failure owing to the speed with which the men passed. We could see, however, that Surrey AC.'s sixth man was behind our eighth. Salford had three men prominent, but where were the others? Birchfield seemed well beaten even here. Currie, the Midland Champion, had retired at 1½ miles, and they had two men in the first bunch, the rest nowhere! However, we have been deceived before by the wearing of white vests and so were not unduly jubilant.

At seven miles it was obvious that, bar accidents, victory was ours. Ginty had fallen back a little and was running with Bert Footer, Dan Patience being a few yards in the rear—all in the first 25 places. A few seconds later came a very heartening sight, Alf Taylor, Harry Shields and Tom Carter all running together. Bert Shaw, Arthur Allum and Jack Flower were all running strongly and we rejoiced to find our ninth man in front of Birchfield's sixth.

Over the last lap many places were gained, our Taylor-Shields-Carter trio, in particular, pegging back many opponents. Dan Patience was another man to run strongly and looked as if he might even win if the race continued for an extra mile or so!

In the last mile our boys gave all they had, and we had the remarkable scoring sequence of 11th, 14th, 15th, 18th, 19th, 20th places. Better packing can never have been seen before, and the scoring epitomises the team spirit existent in our ranks. It was a wonderful sight, shortly after Close had won from Flockhart and Bailey, to see six men in claret and gold passing down the roped “funnel” to hand in their numbered discs to your humble servant It is no easy task acting as recorder, when such splendid team packing exists, I can assure you!

Well, we had won! Our remaining three runners were soon in, and we had finished our complete team of nine in 57 scoring positions.

The congratulations poured in. Our boys regained their breath and composure, completed their toilets and dressing, had their photograph taken, and adjourned to Burnham Hall for the presentation of awards by the Hon. Mrs. Lawson.

Cheers greeted Bert Footer as he accepted the “Frank Wynne” Cup on behalf of the Club. Cheers again as he took the “Finch Wayte” Cup for the first Southern Club. Cheers yet again as Bert was called upon to receive the replica awarded to the first man of the first Southern club to finish. A speech was demanded and Bert obliged in his best film-star manner and called for three cheers for the previous title holders—Birchfield Harriers.

Carried away by his enthusiasm, he led the cheering by waving the miniature cup above his head, and the lid dropped the four feet odd to the ground and bounced into the footlights. More cheers!

It would be invidious to discriminate when our whole team did so well. We have only to refer you to the result for the superlative merit of the performance to be evident. To use Biblical language, “they were stronger than lions, they were swifter than eagles.”

It must have been a proud moment when Bert Footer realised that one of his greatest ambitions had come to pass, and that he had led home a Champion team—not by a narrow, fluky margin—but with 56 points to spare.

Same again in 1936, please!

A.A.H.

Column · page 7

FROM OUR PRESIDENT.

J. R. McKAY

At last we have achieved our greatest ambition and captured premier honours in cross-country running, namely the English Cross-country Championship, and our congratulations are extended to the members who comprised our team. After our amazing set-back in the Southern Championship it came rather as a surprise to find all our team in such fine fettle on March 11th. Perhaps the defeat at Hassocks did our men good; but in any case, the dreadfully muddy condition of the course, which did not suit our team at all, made the result no criterion for a fortnight later. However, that race lost us the backing and support of the Press, and mention of the Belgrave was conspicuous by its absence in the papers.

As we have noticed previously, lack of publicity is usually a good omen, and it certainly was in the case of the “National.”

Nevertheless, this oversight was quickly remedied after the race, and one paper kindly informed the world of our Building Fund efforts.

To revert to the great race, not only was our victory a surprise, but the easy manner in which it was accomplished was astonishing. To “close in” our 6 before the third man of any other club finished was a rare feat. In addition, this happened with A. Penny, our stalwart International, leaning on crutches and looking on as a spectator; and A. Allum, International and one of the mainstays of our team, failing to finish close enough to count in our scoring six.

Apart from these six, congratulations are due to the other three men who ran so extremely well that any one could have taken the place of our first man home and still left us with a winning margin! We realize their disappointment in not attaining their championship medal. Allum, Shaw and Flower have all scored for the Club in past National races, but we know their natural disappointment soon turned to joy at the Club's win.

However, we hope to emulate the example of the Birchfield Harriers, and make a habit of winning this event and so give futher opportunities to our members of earning these coveted medals.

J. Ginty and D. Patience are to be congratulated in winning their “Honours Badge” as a result of the running in this “National.”

After the success of our Juniors in winning the Southern Counties Junior Championship, every confidence can be placed in our future chances in all cross-country races. We have chased the “bogey” away and our fear of the Midland and Northern opposition has faded into thin air. The progress of our juniors must be closely watched as our future successes will depend on their efforts; but with R Savage looking after them we have little cause for anxiety.

Our success in the Ilford Relay augurs well for our prospects in the London—Brighton Relay, and once again we have shown the Athletic World our strength in team races.

Not only did our first team break the record for the course, but only four other clubs could beat our second team.

We are all looking forward eagerly to May 11th, for the R.W.A. National Championship at Chingford, as we hope to pull off the “treble” National Championships. That our men have the ability to win there is no doubt, but unfortunately in walking there is more danger of set-backs owing to “lifting.” Our team suffered in that respect in the “Junior” Championship at Birmingham on March 2nd, but this is not likely to happen at Chingford. If we do win this race we shall have set up another record in Athletics. It is to be hoped that all members will support this event and come along to cheer our men along the course. Encouragement in a long race of this kind goes a long way in helping to keep up the spirits of the competitors.

As the track season is fast approaching members should be taking advantage of the mild weather and commencing their training rather earlier than usual.

The track men have been set an excellent example of the standard expected of the Belgrave nowadays, and the results of the Summer season are being looked forward to with great confidence.

J. R. McKAY.

Column · page 9

JUST JOTTINGS.

Should this issue of our Gazette reach you before Saturday, March 30th, don’t forget the Smoking Concert being held on that evening at “The Rising Sun,” Ebury Bridge Road, S.W.1, on behalf of the Belgrave Building Fund.

* * * * *

The illustration of our winning team in the English Cross-country Championship, appearing on another page, is published by kind permission of the Editor of “Sport and Play” and by the courtesy of the proprietors of this well-known Midlands sporting weekly, Sport and Play (1930) Ltd., Lionel Street, Birmingham.

* * * * *

Heartiest congratulations to Bert Footer upon his inclusion in the English team for the third year in succession. Bert was not called upon to travel to France, but will receive his colours.

* * * * *

We now have the satisfaction of knowing that our name appears on the three Team Championship trophies awarded in connection with the National Championships. The “Frank Wynne” Cup (Cross-country) and the “Horlick” Cup (50 kilos. Walk) are at present on the Club sideboard, and we have held the Senior R.W.A. 20 miles Championship Shield on four occasions.

Our Club is the only one to have held all three trophies.

In addition, in the “limited” championships—which may sound like a contradiction in terms—we have succeeded in winning the R.W.A. “Junior” Walk Championship (1920, 1922, 1930, 1933); the Southern Counties Cross-country title (1934); the “Junior” Southern (1935) : the South of the Thames Championship (1929); the Surrey cross-country title on many occasions; and the Relay in 1931 and 1932.

So Belgrave must figure on nearly every championship trophy of any account. Additional to these are Brighton Relay (1934); “Nijmegen” Shield (1933); “Knott Memorial” Shield (Brighton Walk) on many occasions; “Sir J. Colman” Cup (1929); “Odling Smee” Cup several times; and many other trophies awarded for events of lesser importance. We have also headed the First Division of the Southern Amateur Athletic League, for which no award exists, in 1932 and again last track season.

* * * * *

Jim Ginty recently won the London Divisional Army Cross-country Championship from another of our members, Dan Patience. They were placed third and fifth, respectively, in the Army Championship proper, and are certain to represent the Army in the Inter-Services race. Congratulations!

* * * * *

It has always been our Cross-country Captain’s ideal to have twelve men running within a minute of one another over ten miles. The “National” result, with 52 seconds covering our scoring six, shows that his ideal is fast becoming a reality!

* * * * *

Congratulations to our popular Asst. Hon. Secretary and Hon. Handicapper, who has recently been added to the A.A.A. Board of Handicappers.

* * * * *

In view of this year’s very special success, it is hoped that as many as possible will attend the Club Dinner at Carr’s Restaurant, Victoria, on Saturday, May 4th. Tickets are 5/- each and you are asked to get in touch with Bill Rice as quickly as possible and so allow him to make the necessary arrangements. Ladies are specially invited.

Let’s make the Club Dinner a great social success, worthy of the Athletic successes which have come our way.

* * * * *

The Brighton Relay will soon be upon us and if you have not yet booked your seat on one of the Club’s buses, it is advisable to do so at once, and avoid the risk of disappointment. A limited number of seats only are available.

Arrangements will be much the same as in previous years. Our buses will leave Westminster at 10-30 a.m. and proceed by easy stages to Brighton.

The Brighton Corporation rules compel us to leave promptly at 6 p.m., and passengers for the return journey will be picked up at a point about a quarter of a mile east of the Palace Pier, on the Lower Marine Drive.

This day’s outing is always a memorable and happy one, and eagerly-awaited. So if you propose to come along, write at once (fare 5/-) to the addresses given elsewhere.

Brighton Relay Orders : Club ties to be worn. Our quartermaster, W. G. Webb, will be pleased to send you one upon receiving your remittance. His address appears on page 1.

* * * * *

Arthur Penny’s damaged foot continues to make very slow progress, but we hope that the Spring will find him out and about again. The season has been most disappointing to Arthur, and we have all shared his regret that such a minor fracture should take such a long time to knit together. We hope that future seasons will make up to him, in some measure, for what he has lost this year.

* * * * *

Our President elsewhere asks all Club supporters to be present at Chingford on Saturday, May 11th, when the R.W.A. 20 miles Championship is to be held from “The Jubilee Retreat.” It would certainly be very pleasing for our representatives in the Walk to have the active support and help of their club-mates. Our probable team is a very strong one and has a wonderfully fine chance of gaining another “National” trophy for the Club.

Last year we lost by a bare two points, but on this occasion we expect to have the help of both Tommy Green and Joe King, who were prevented from travelling to Sheffield last year.

10 MILES CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP FOR THE MACHER SHIELD.

A. A. DEACON

A record field of 27 lined up for the start in terrible weather, there being a very strong wind blowing, which the runners had to face for a considerable part of their journey. A good start was made, and the hardy group of “he-men” were off on their long trail or trial. A snowstorm commenced which could not have improved their hopes. The runners were soon out of sight and the hardy group of spectators also did a spot of running to the nearest shelter, where the return of the runners on their first lap of 5 miles was keenly awaited.

A small group is seen, which as it draws nearer is recognizable as C. T. Carter, J. Ginty and H. E. Footer running in that order. They sweep past to continue the second lap, having shown 29 mins. 27 secs. for the first 5 miles. A. Allum, D. Patience, Jack Flower and H. Shields are well up with the leaders, and a good finish is already assured. W. Rice, A. F. Mitchell, A. E. Taylor, A. Patience, A. Shaw are all running well and evidently going “to have a go.” We, the spectators, are now poor, cold morsels, and some genius sends out for a jug of tea, which was very welcome.

Out in the cold, cold snow once more we go to await the finish. A little white dot is seen—someone suggests it is a snowflake—but no! it is our little “H.E.”, running like the champion he is to record a very popular win in the time of 59 mins., which, considering the conditions, was very good going. C. T. Carter is next (“Well run, Carter”), followed by A. Allum and D. Patience, all running well.

SHORTS FROM THE RACE.

1.—25 finished the course.

2.—H. E. Footer has won this race five times, the first being in 1925.

3.—Mr. H. Parker and Mr. W. L. Bayliss were responsible for scoring and timing—an unthankful job in such weather.

4.—A match run in conjunction with the race between Belgrave H. and Cadogan A.C. resulted in a win for Cadogan by 58 pts to 78 pts.

5.—Eight finished within three minutes of the winner.

6.—Alec Patience and Dan Patience were rewarded for their good running with first and second prizes in the sealed handicap.

7.—A. F. Mitchell, third in the handicap, was another to do well and D. Martin is deserving of a word of praise on his first attempt at ten miles.

A. A. DEACON

THE SURREY CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIPS.

The Senior Championship held from Coulsdon provided us with a fairly easy win, and we had the pleasure of supplying the first two home in Footer and Carter.

There was one crowning misfortune, however, for it was in this race that Penny fractured the bone in his foot which has robbed us of his services since. He completed the course and scored in our team, but has not run since. Mitcham A.C. did well to finish second and their improvement has been one of the features of the season.

In the “Junior” race held at Imber Court a week later, the South London Harriers proved the strongest team and we took third place. Chantler and Martin ran well, with Jordan not far away, but we dropped points in our search for a fourth man.

THE MIDDLESEX CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIPS.

Contested at Northolt as usual, Junior and Senior races were held on the same day. In the Junior event, E. S. Stimpson, who has been commended for his promise in our columns before, ran exceedingly well to finish in third place. With another year’s strength and experience he should win this event.

For the greater part of the Senior event our boys (who ran on behalf of their second-claim club, Cadogan A.C.) were fighting it out with the Polytechnic Harriers. In the last couple of miles, however, they drew away from the opposition and eventually ran out easy winners. Arthur Allum lost the individual title to Humphris, but led his team home to another very fine win.

THE SOUTH OF THE THAMES “SENIOR.”

T. C. Jones

The “Senior” at Epsom Downs on the 9th February was contested by a large field and our boys, although badly drawn, got away fairly well.

When seen at the end of about a mile and a half the field was led by Close (Reading) followed by a bunch including Cohen (Ashcombe) and Pell (Herne Hill). Our first man was Bill Rice 21st, then Patience 26th, followed by Walker, Duffett, Ring, Webb, F. R., Mitchell, A. F., Chantler, Lindsey, Doubleday, and then smiling Syd Warboys.

We then indulged in a race of our own to the next vantage point near Tattenham Corner, when we saw that Patience had moved up to 15th Bill Rice 25th, Ring 27th, Martin 32nd, Webb 45th, and then our handicapper 48th (looking as though he was thinking that it might be easier work with a whistle). At this point we seemed to have a good chance of a place.

When we saw the field at the end of about five miles, Close was still leading, closely followed by Pell. Patience was still our first man, but had dropped four paces. Bill Rice had gained two, and the others were in more or less the same positions, and our hopes of a place were still justified.

We then went to the finishing straight and on counting the positions it seemed that we had at least secured third place, and this proved correct.

Once again we must congratulate Mitcham, who scored 127 points, and also secured the “Sir Jeremiah Colman” Cup for the first club to finish 12 men. Reading were second with 168, and ourselves third with 192.

We last won the South of Thames Senior in 1929, and it seems that several years must elapse before we are again successful. The reason for this is that our successive placings in the “Southern” and “National” each year rule out our improving men according to the rules of the South of the Thames Association. In the circumstances, and losing, as we do, two or three men’s eligibility in this race each year, a “placed” team must always be considered a very successful result from our point of view.

Just this as a side note : On returning to the dressing room we had no option but to congratulate a member of the “Belgrave Sisters” on her choice of negligée—she certainly looked “in the pink.”

THE SOUTHERN COUNTIES CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIPS.

Syd Ring

When it was first heard that the “Southern” Championships were to be held at Hassocks, Sussex, it was surmised that the runners would have to tackle the South Downs, perhaps even Ditchling Beacon. When the more enterprising and enthusiastic team members and others arrived early and looked over the course, it was found to be flat (compared with the Downs) but very heavy and rain-sodden meadowland. It was ideal for the spectators, the runners being in sight nearly the whole way.

The Juniors were first away, and we must heartily congratulate our team upon its win and some good packing. At the end of 2½ miles Chantler was 13th, Martin 15th, Stimpson 17th, and Chantler 23rd. The two first named put in splendid finishes, and Stimpson found an extra bit to split the Aldershot pack, which incidentally enabled us to win by one point. Jordan backed up in noble fashion, and we were winners of the “Finch Wayte” Cup for the first time.

Our team were well away in the “Senior” race and at the end of a mile we had our twelve in the first fifty places. Footer, Ginty and Carter were especially prominent of ours. Returning from the first lap our good start had gone, but we were well placed with Surrey A.C. looking very dangerous.

At the end of the second lap (this sounds like a track race!) Close (Reading), Humphris (S.L.H.) and Edwards (Navy) were leading. Ginty, our new acquisition, was fourth, closely followed by the Nichols Bros., of Surrey. Footer (7th) was evidently feeling the effects of a recent change of occupation. Next came Carter 11th, closely followed by four Surrey runners and our rivals had their six in sixteen places. Our next men were D. Patience (21st), Allum (26th), Taylor (37th).

Returning to the finishing point, we met our victorious Juniors, now changed, and waited for the finish. After a short while Close finished in fine style beating Humphris by about 50 yards. Edwards, of the Navy, was third, and was followed by Ginty and Footer, of ours. I think the crowd cheered our pair as much as the leaders. Bert must have fought the whole of the last lap, easing only when he caught Jim Ginty near the finish. Then came the others and although we had picked up points, Surrey had held on well and finished distressed but victorious. They had defeated us by 9 points, and deserve our congratulations for a splendid show.

Summing up, I think our boys had an off day, and with the exception of Ginty and D. Patience, all should have been up further.

[Hon. Editor's Note.—Our contributor is, of course, fully entitled to his opinion expressed in the last sentence above, but I feel this judgment may be a little harsh. Many of us, bearing in mind Penny's absence, and Taylor's recent indisposition, had not expected our team to get near our score of the previous year. That they scored 85 points against last year's 81, and finished six in 26 as against six in 27 last year, seems excellent in itself. That Surrey were able to score under last year's totals reflects the greatest credit on them and, well as our boys ran, we met a better team on the day. In the light of more recent happenings our defeat may well seem surprising, but then the charm of uncertainty is present in Athletics as in most sports. We had the satisfaction of regaining the “Odling-Smee” Cup for the first Surrey Club to finish twelve men].

A REVIEW OF THE OLD AND NEW SEASONS.

The Winter Season is now rapidly nearing a close, and our Summer programme is fast taking shape, and we hope to have the fixture cards out very shortly.

On 30th March will be held the Inter-Counties Cross Country race at Luton. We should have quite a number of men competing for various counties, Footer and Carter for Surrey, Allum, Shields Dan Patience and Taylor for Middlesex, and Parker for Sussex. I still think, however, that this race comes too late in the season to be interesting, but perhaps the Counties will make a move a bring about a change in the date for future years.

Then comes the big day, April 13th, the London to Brighton Relay, of which we are the holders, and on the form so far displayed it would be a bold man who would prophesy our defeat. Birchfield H., if they run, are always to be respected, but after their “National” defeat I cannot possibly see them turning the tables on us. No, sir! if anyone may do it, then I think it will be our Southern conquerors, Surrey A.C. On their running in the Ilford race the time put up by the actual first team men was excellent. They hope to field a much stronger ten for the Brighton race, but if there are any doubts, then I think it hinges on the reserve question. There is no possible doubt that so far as bringing men in at the last minute is concerned, we are in an unassailable position. Our “B” team's running in the Ilford Relay proves this, and it is not going too far to say that this team would beat many that will run to Brighton on April 13th.

We shall, as mentioned elsewhere, have the usual two vehicles going down with our team and supporters, and if you want a seat on them, then make sure to write straight away and so avoid being disappointed.

With this race our Winter season comes to an end, and on looking back I am sure everyone must be more than satisfied with our men's performances. In the races in which the Club has competed, our record is: South of Thames “Junior,” 2nd team; Surrey County Senior, 1st team; Southern Counties Senior, 2nd team; Southern Counties Junior, 1st team; English “National” Championship, 1st team.

Quite a good record, don't you think, but one which could be bettered, I agree. However, I think we have now tasted the sweets of victory in every race that is held, that is, as regards the country, so now we had better turn the spotlight on our Track runners and their prospects.

As most members are aware, we are dependent upon the L.C.C. for permits for the use of Battersea Park Track, and although we are without dispute the Club with the largest membership using that track, we seem to come off very badly in the allocation of permits.

To begin with, we have lost our date on the first Saturday in May, when it will be remembered we usually have a match with the L.A.C.; and in its place we have been given Saturday, May 18th. This date was not suitable for the L.A.C., but I am pleased to say we have fixed May 1st for a match with them at the White City Track.

On May 11th we have our, now, annual fixture at Drayton Green Track, with the local club, Ealing Harriers, also our old friends, Ashcombe A.C. and Slough A.C.

To come back to May 18th, we have now arranged for a double-barrelled match, with such opponents as Surrey A.C. and Leigh-on-Sea H., and I hope to fix up with two other clubs in order to make it a three-cornered affair for both of our teams. It has been suggested that we make one a Captain's team and the other the Vice-Captain's, so I trust our members will support these matches and so make the arranging of them a worth-while job.

In connection with this meeting we shall also hold some Club Handicaps, and here again I hope our members will show better support than last year. After all, your Committee spend a great deal of time in deciding which events would be most suitable for the members, and as you can see from our Balance Sheet our Prize bill is a considerable item, so it is up to the members to justify the continuance of these events by turning out in large numbers whenever they can.

There is one other point about our mid-week meetings. That is, this season they will mostly be held on Monday evenings. Here, again, we had to accept what the L.C.C. offered us and I hope the alteration from the usual Wednesday to Monday will not affect our entries in any way.

Our Track Championships will be held on the usual Saturday, which is August 3rd, and this year we are departing from the usual custom, and are having Championship events only, with the exception of two Field event handicaps. The reason for this is that on July 29th, which is the previous Monday, we are holding a meeting at Battersea Park and shall devote this entirely to Club handicaps.

I expect by this time you will have noticed that I have not mentioned a date for our 3 Miles Championship, and in the absence of a suitable date in June, we have decided to have this race at our meeting on September 14th. This, I know, is rather late in the season, but by this time our men will already be training up for our opening Road Race at Wimbledon, so it should suit them all right. Last year we had an Open Team race at our September meeting, but the support accorded it was rather poor, and so we are staging an equally attractive race in our own 3 Miles Club Championship.

Well, I hope this rather brief outline of our Summer programme has given you some idea of what events we are going to have, and I trust our members will be as successful on the track as they have been over the country.

All entries and enquiries can be sent to our Asst. Hon. Sec., E. A. DUFFETT, 10, Barrow Road, Waddon. Or to our Gen. Hon. Sec., W. L. BAYLISS, 71, Ravensbury Road, S.W.18.

THE 1935 “JUNIOR.” “Aht! Git on Aht!”

A.A.H.

Such were the words (so he alleges—not too seriously, we suspect) that cut short Percy Wright's progress in his first “Junior,” and similar commands, expressed, we hope, with greater courtesy and in more refined language, caused many, many walkers to lose personal interest in this year's championship. If all those disqualified had immediately turned about and retraced their steps to the starting point, one might have been forgiven for enquiring if a last-minute reversal of course direction had been decided upon.

An examination of the result shows that only one club succeeded in finishing a complete team of eight men. Two teams finished seven men; four clubs six walkers each. Seven teams had five men still walking at the finish; three clubs only had their bare scoring four; and two teams did not close in at all. Of course, casualties accounted for some of the retirements, and it is not certain that every team started a complete eight, but something of the havoc wrought can be gained from these details. Of 157 starters, 107 finished the comparatively easy course.

We do not suggest that the competitors were butchered to make a Judges' holiday; but the numerous disqualifications remain as the outstanding impression gained by the onlooker.

Our two teams had met at Euston, and despite the regrettable indisposition of John Rutland, who had promised to do so well in this race, but at the last minute was unable to travel, we made the journey optimistically, knowing that our first choice would give a good account of themselves at Fort Dunlop later in the day.

And so it proved. Despite early setbacks in the shape of the disqualification of Charlie Churcher and Charlie Speechley, who had both been expected to be with the leaders, and later that of Wright, who was shown to be in the wrong, as previously mentioned, our remaining “A” team men stuck gamely to their task and had the reward for their perseverance in the shape of the third set of medals.

At about five and a half miles, a hurried check of the positions showed Surrey Walking Club to be winners bar accidents, with Dunlop W.C. second and ourselves third, with the London Vidarians W.C. and Royal Sutton Coldfield W.C. close on our heels. Clearly, our boys could not afford to take things easily and it is to their credit that they never spared themselves in their effort to do the utmost for he Club.

LEN STOREY, in finishing 12th, gave us his best performance yet on the road. Throughout he walked with scrupulous fairness and intense determination. We shall certainly expect to find him in the first six next year.

EDDIE HEATHER was another to show up well. His position was a hard-earned one; and we look to him to do even better next year. He has great possibilities and should be the proud possessor of a “Junior gold” before long.

LEO JONES finished in 23rd position, and again showed that tenacity of purpose which we always associate with with him. His quick, short stride never seems to tire, and I am confident that we have not yet seen him at his best, well though he walked on this occasion. I will also prophesy that one day he will walk a first-class twenty miles; but that can come later.

REG ELSON nobly did his job as our fourth man, making every place he could. This cannot rank as one of his best performances—last year he was placed 13th—but that he has all the potentialities to be in the first flight we well know. Next year should see Reg challenging the best of them. We are apt to look upon him as one of “the old brigade,” forgetting that he started walking at sixteen years of aage, and has now only just reached the age of 22.

FRANK THORN can still give Charlie Speechley 15 years or so, but it will be remembered that he was placed third as long ago as the 1926 “Junior.” In this year's race for eight miles he was our fourth man, placed in the thirties, but from this point he dropped back. Lack of training consequent upon heavy business duties was telling its tale!

BODDINGTON had the distinction of leading the “B” team home. Room will have to be found in the “A” team next year for him!

SNELL, who scored in our placed team last year, but had been unable to undergo a full preparation this year, was his steady self.

EAST pursued the quest for “nine-minute” miles, and our fourth scoring man in the “B” team was FRANK JARVIS, followed five places later by DON HEATH.

To summarise, all things considered, we may be well satisfied with out third place. This was not gained easily—a glance at the result shows we had only three points to spare—and the result reflects credit upon our men.

We cannot conclude without heartily congratulating our Surrey W.C. friends upon their very fine win! To win the “Junior” two years in succession is an exceptional feat, and one which we should be very proud to emulate. Incidentally, this win of Surrey's brings their total tally of wins to four—the same as ours. We must certainly draw away again next year!

As a last word, our sympathy to Churcher, Speechley and Wright whose conscientious training had such a barren reward. Also to Rutland, who must wait another year before tasting the “Junior,” and to Doug Asher, a very keen Chelmsford member, who was compelled to retire from the race owing to internal troubles.

May 1936 and future performances compensate the five abovementioned for present disappointment.

A.A.H.

WITH THE WALKERS.

The “Junior” is now a thing of the past and our good record in this race, set down in our last issue, was not besmirched. A description of this event—a marvel of organiation and a credit to the Local Committee—appear on another page. The moral for the young walker seems to be: cultivate a style which is not only scrupulously fair but also looks above suspicion. Whether the fault at Birmingham was over-zealous competitors or over-zealous judges is not for us to discuss, but be certain you do not offend to the disappointment of yourself and your team.

In the London Transport S. & A.A Open Walk held at Bellingham, little seemed to go right for us. Tommy Green had entered but at the last moment an important business appointment prevented him from coming to London. Treacher, competing for us as a team man for the first time, suffered disqualification, and Don Brown was forced to retire owing to a chilled stomach. Little wonder then that we did not win the scratch team event which carried with it the “Berman” Cup on this occasion. In fact, it is rather surprising that we could still take second place, and this is due to some very fine walking by Joe King, sound work by the Bros. Churcher, and good backing by the youthful Rutland.

RESULT.

A fortnight later came the Ashcombe A.C. event over ten miles, mainly in Richmond Park. Our entry reached big proportions, and the claret and gold was well in evidence throughout the field. Our “A” team, consisting of Messrs. Wright, C. Churcher, Rutland and Brown, took the second set of team prizes.

RESULT.

It is a curious fact that our Club, which has secured at one time or another practically every walking honour of any consequence, has yet to win the Surrey County Championship. After the decision of this year’s race over a stiff course from our quarters at Wimbledon, we are still in that same position. However, we gave Surrey W.C., the holders, a fright on this occasion, and were leading at one time. The last mile enabled our friends to gain a slight advantage and they won with three points to spare. Our boys all did well, and even when our four scoring men were in, we also finished the next two. Six in twelve!

RESULT.

On the same day, the Middlesex Championship was held at Southall, when several of our men were interested. Bill Treacher did well to finish second.

Speechley was 10th and Bidgood 18th (both walking in Polytechnic colours); whilst Rutland and Wright finished together in 20th position. The last-named two and Treacher walked as members of Cadogan A.C.

Following upon the County Championships, several of our walkers received invitations to compete in the Inter-Counties Walk at Enfield on March 23rd. Among those so honoured are D. E. Brown, H. G. Churcher, C. E. Churcher (Surrey); W. A. Treacher (Middlesex); and J. F. King (Sussex). Congratulations!

Report · page 23

TEN MILES WALKING CHAMPIONSHIP.

This event was held in bitterly cold weather, violent, icy blasts handicapping the walkers throughout the race.

Brown and Treacher were together all the way until within a quarter of a mile from the finish, when Don managed to get away.

Brown's walking was very jerky on occasions, and he appeared to be suffering from shin soreness. This race was not one of his best performances, but the intense cold must be partly to blame. Bill Treacher always seemed comfortable but was unable to respond to Brown's finishing burst.

Harry Churcher walked well for eight miles in third position, when the cold cramped his legs and he fell right away, yielding places to Charlie Speechley, his brother Charlie, and Jack Rutland. The last-named again showed that, at the age of seventeen, he has both speed and style, and a splendid future.

Don Brown in addition to the “Murphy” Shield, also took the the sealed handicap, with Bill Treacher again in second place.

Third prize went to another of our promising young walkers, Eddie Heather.

THE BUILDING FUND.

FRANK H. ELSON.

“The presence of..................................of requested at the Opening of the New Headquarters of the Belgrave Harriers.”

To see these words suitably inscribed on an invitation card has been the hope of some, the ambition and intention of others.

That we are nearer to our object to-day than we were two years ago is obvious. Two years ago all we had were a few “halfpenny boxes” among the pioneers of the scheme. To-day we have to the credit of the Building Fund over £250. This is, without a doubt, a definite step towards our objective.

The thanks of the Club are due to all those who have helped to make this possible, but the honours most certainly go to the pioneers who started it. The Committee had long been aware of the difficulties which cross-country and, to a lesser degree, walking clubs had experienced in the encroachment of building operations, and our good fortune in being established near Wimbledon Common and Richmond Park was greatly appreciated. But although our courses may be there for ever, they are of little use to us if we are unable to obtain convenient quarters, and we cannot rely upon St. John’s Hall in perpetuity. Hence the matter came to be discussed on Committee once again and it was decided, on the proposition of H. L. Rollins, I believe, warmly supported by the rest of the Committee, that definite steps be taken to institute some sort of scheme. W. H. Hare, to whom we owe a great debt of gratitude for his many club activities, evolved the “halfpenny” scheme.

To supplement this, V. J. Peacock devised a very complicated idea to which he devoted much time and thought. Admirable though the intentions were, we could not decide to embark upon this scheme, which would have had far-reaching effects, and it was decided not to proceed with Mr. Peacock’s plans. All conversant with the idea greatly appreciated Mr. Peacock’s labours on the Club’s behalf which were put forward solely with the idea of providing our Club with Headquarters.

Mr. Gordon was also a prime mover in the early stages and is now. He is responsible for our Building Bricks, and I would ask you to show your appreciation by filling yours and asking for more.

There are many little schemes on hand at the moment for the increase of our capital, and I feel sure that now we have achieved something definite we can arouse some enthusiasm among those who have not yet helped.

In saying this, I regret there are only a few doing this work for the benefit of many. I mention Messrs. Wright, Bayliss, Cotton, Jordan, Harley, Chantler and Walker in particular for their regular weeky contributions to the Football Scheme, and all those others who are assisting in some way or other.

But I would like to see more of the younger members helping, for it is they who will derive the greatest benefit and help from our new headquarters.

The older members will possibly be too old to run or walk (by that I do not mean the far, far future), so the only benefit they will get will be the possible weekly ablutions amidst the luxurious surroundings of our well appointed baths. But, nay, they also will derive great benefit from the perpetual reminder of Belgrave’s greatness, for I can assure you that it will be a building worthy of our great name and record. It will attract the right class of young man to our club; it will make training a pleasure, thereby getting even better results and greater honours, thus ensuring the maintenance of our great name in athletics.

Yes, it is a great venture, and it behoves every Belgrave Harrier to do his utmost to make this thing possible.

Remember, it is no longer a dream, it is now a probability. Why not make it a certainty?

We shall be happy to send all those who have helped a card inscribed with the words with which I commenced this little attempt to bring home to you all the necessity for your own building, and to show a little appreciation to all those who have made the scheme what it is now. I hope you will all now endeavour to do your bit.

FRANK H. ELSON.
