Editorial · page 3

Heartiest congratulations to H. Parker, who was recently elected President of the South of the Thames Cross-country Association. We trust he will have the pleasure of handing the "Dewar" Shield to our team, as did W. H. Hare in 1929, when President of the Association.

* * * * *

Congratulations also to Alec Traquair and Pat. Parker, upon the occasion of their recent weddings. We wish them many years of happiness.

* * * * *

Our very heartiest congratulations and good wishes go to Eddie Pack upon his selection to accompany the team to visit Australia for the British Empire Games. The honour is well-deserved, and we are sure Eddie will fully justify his selection. Belgrave's sincerest good wishes will go with him!

* * * * *

A proposal that a trophy in memory of the late W. A. Rice should be purchased by his friends and presented to the Club in perpetuity, probably for the Junior 880 yards Championship, has received the Committee's endorsement. No subscription list will be circulated and no member will be asked to subscribe, but anyone among Bill's friends who would care to associate himself with the proposal is asked to get in touch with A. A. Harley, who is acting as Hon. Treasurer of this special fund.

* * * * *

The current year's subscriptions are now due and our Hon. Treasurer would be glad to receive all outstanding amounts. The early part of the Club year calls for heavy expenditure, and immediate payment of obligations to your Club would be very helpful.

Probably the outstanding item of interest in the early part of our Winter programme is the visit of L'Union Sportive Metropolitaine to Wimbledon on Saturday, December 11th. This fixture is still subject to the approval of the governing bodies in both countries, but we are looking forward to interesting running and walking matches versus our Parisian friends.

We have never yet entertained Continental visitors, but this may well be the forerunner of similar fixtures in the future. Teams from across the Channel have visited this country before for track and cross-country matches, but it must be a long while since a team from abroad contested a walking match.

We write well in advance of the fixture and trust that nothing will intervene to prevent two of the most interesting team matches of recent time taking place. It is hoped that as many club members as possible will come along to assist, to give our friends a cordial welcome, and to support our teams.

* * * * *

Another date to remember is November 27th, when our Club Open 7 Miles Walk takes place from Wimbledon. We expect our usual fine tntry, and all those who come along may expect to see the pick of Southern walkers, and some fine walking and keen competition. The Road Walking Association have allocated the "Berman" Cup to our race, to be held for a year by the team winning the scratch team race.

* * * * *

It may be of interest to Club members to know the names of those representing them on the outside Committees and Associations. They are as follows :—

Amateur Athletic Association (Southern Committee) : F. G. Thomson.

Road Walking Association (Southern Committee) : F. G. Thomson (Past President, R.W.A.), and A. A. Harley. J. C. Bidgood is also a member of this Committee, nominated by the Metropolitan Police W.C.

Southern Counties C.C. Association : No representation.

South of Thames C.C. Association : H. Parker (President), and E. A. Duffett.

Surrey County A.A.A. : F. G. Thomson and H. Parker.

Middlesex County A.A.A. : A. A. Harley (Vice-President), and F. R. Webb (as members of Cadogan A.C.).

Report · page 4

The End of the Track Season.

On July 22nd we had an enjoyable evening meeting at Richmond; our opponents being the Junior sides of Thames Valley H. and the Polytechnic H. Our Juniors were not so well represented as hoped, but nevertheless those that were there showed up very well.

In the 880 yards N. C. Shrubb finished third, the winner being Bavage, of T.V.H., a fine young runner. The 440 yds. was won by V. D. Wynyard, of the "Bels," who ran away from his rivals in quite fast time. We also gained third place in the 220 yds. with W. H. Bowers.

The final points were: Poly H. 30, T.V.H. 29, Belgrave 24; and I am sure all our boys enjoyed their evening very much.

On the 28th July was held the final Inter-Club Relay Shield meeting at Mitcham. We had a good turn-out and won all four events, our boys being at the top of their form. In the 4 x 300 yds. we had Eddie Pack doing the last leg and although the shape of the track was not suitable to his huge stride, he soon put the issue beyond doubt. We had previously won both the 4 x 100 yards. and 4 x 880 yds. In the latter, Alby Bird had a comfortable run over the last stage, Dee, the Welsh crack, preferring to take things easy when he saw Alby go off about 30 yds. in front of him. The 1 Mile team race provided the large crowd with a big thrill; Les Cohen and Thorogood, the A.A.A. Indoor 2 Mile Champion, having a ding-dong battle over the last 200 yds. Cohen stayed home the better and so paved the way for a win in the team race, Arthur Penny also running extremely well.

Incidentally, our score of 60 points is the best scored in the series of contests for the Shield since its inception in 1925.

On Saturday, July 31st, we had as visitors to Battersea Park old friends and rivals, the London Athletic Club. They seemed to be still smarting under the partial reverse we inflicted on them at Birmingham and so turned out quite a strong side. G. R. Dowling accomplished a very fine performance in winning the 100 yds., also the 440 yds., and being placed 2nd in the 220 yds. With J. V. Powell winning the 880 yds. and 1 Mile, these two runners were mainly responsible for the L.A.C. winning the match by 26 pts. to 22. We did, however, manage to have the first two in the Shot Putt, and S. Jones with 37ft. 1in. and Bill Sutton with 36ft. 9½in. are to be complimented on the steady improvement they are showing at this branch of sport.

Also at this meeting we had some Club Handicaps, one being the newly innovated 880 yds. Junior race. L. Marchant proved a ready winner and looks like being a real champion in a few years' time. A very small field turned out for the Club 880 yds. handicap, and here W. J. Ashford won, with none other than Percy Wright second, and S. Garland third. Percy showed himself to be no mean runner, so now we know why he has met with such success in the walking world! In the 220 yds. handicap A. S. Blunden ran finely to win, with H. B. Taylor second, and F. W. Pritchard third. Blunden and Pritchard have also been well to the fore in our sprint relay teams and it certainly looks that at last we have some sprinters worthy of the name. In the 2 Miles run, J. C. Edwards made good use of his 350 yds. start with H. G. Verney showing some of his old-time form by securing second place. R. W. Tobin also showed continued improvement by gaining third place, and should prove an asset to our cross-country teams.

On Wednesday, August 11th, we had our usual evening meeting and in the 100 yards handicap J. C. Bright showed promising form to win. S. C. Botwright, off 2 yds., was second, with Dicky Best, off 19 yds., third. The 1 Mile handicap was won by a newcomer, J. O. Goodman, and the manner in which he won made quite a few people open their eyes! He is as yet very inexperienced; but he is definitely one of the most promising track runners we have had for some time. Second place was taken by J. T. Beer, who is only just out of the junior stage, but who shows steady improvement; while F. H. C. White ran extremely well off 60 yds. to gain third place. This was Fred's first appearance in "miles," but he was more than satisfied at the result. The 440 yds. was very poorly supported, and S. C. Botwright made good use of his liberal mark to win, with Eddie Pack doing inside 50 seconds to gain second place. R. G. Michael was third, but the main feature of this race was Pack's fine running from the scratch mark.

In the 2 Miles Walk Handicap the long-markers predominated, and J. A. Bellchambers was the winner, with Reg. Elson second, and E. G. Cornish third.

We also had two field event handicaps and in the Shot Putt the winner was, yes! Frank Webb. He made good use of his 16ft. start to win with 37ft. 6in., with Bill Sutton 37ft. 1½in., and George Powell 36ft. 10½in. In the Long Jump, F. W. Pritchard was first with 21ft. 2in, Tom Wiley 20ft. 9in., and Micky Walker third. You will notice how our non-field event members keep popping up in the placings, so come on, you field event wallahs and do your stuff!

On the 18th August we had a team running for the "Nevill Challenge Cup" and they did very well to gain second place, being beaten by a strong Achilles team. Most of the other London clubs were competing and our runners are to be complimented on their fine showing against such opposition.

The final event in the Southern Amateur Athletic League—the 1 Mile medley relay—was held at Herne Hill on August 21st. Owing to holidays we were devoid of any of our recognised half-milers, and so Bill Lucas was pressed into service. With Pell running for Herne Hill, and Eeles for Southgate, also Williams of Poly., our chances of beating these clubs were slight. To make matters worse, at the last moment Eddie Pack was forced to cry off, and so Fred White had to be asked to do the 440. Actually Fred was there to run in the 1 Mile open, so that to ask him to do a quarter in such company was—well, you ought to have heard what he said! Nevertheless he ran, and although handing over last we must thank him for helping us out of a hole. Our sprinters gained slightly on the other clubs, and Bill Lucas went off just behind Eeles, but well behind the main bunch.

Eeles set a scorching pace, but Bill followed him through and our hopes began to rise. At the bell Pell went in front and soon had the race in hand, but Lucas was now feeling that fast first lap, and although overtaking Poole of Blackheath, he was unable to gain any further places. With Herne Hill and Mitcham, who were our nearest rivals in the league table, gaining first and third places, this left us "holding the baby," and so next season will see us in the Second Division. They say the second division is not so bad (I mean, of course, in "jail"), so let us hope we shall find it the same in the league, and I am sure our boys will do their best to make their stay as short as possible.

On the same day we had a fixture at Imber Court with the Metropolitan Police and although our forces were very much depleted, those who did go enjoyed the afternoon's sport. Thanks to our walkers and field events section, we put up quite a good showing, but hope that next season this fixture will not clash, as apart from whether we do well or badly, we do at least like to show our hosts the common courtesy of turning out a full side.

We had our final club meeting on August 28th, at Battersea Park, our visitors being Cambridge Harriers, and the Highgate Harriers. Some good running was seen in the match events, the 440 yards having quite an exciting finish when L. W. Herbert came with a late burst to win on the tape. W. E. Lucas won the match 880 yds. and also gave us the lead over the same stage in the relay. Alby Bird came over from Tooting, where he had run in the Inter-Counties match to do the 440 yds. in the relay, and we won comfortably. Herbert had also been running for his County at Tooting, and we admire the club spirit which prompted these members to undertake such a strenuous afternoon's work.

Our Juniors also had the same clubs in opposition in three junior events. We won both the 3 x 100 yds. and 3 x 200 yds. races; while the 880 yds. team was won by Cambridge H., with the "Bels" second.

We would like to thank our opponents for providing us with a very interesting meeting, and would particularly like to thank the Cambridge Harriers' officials who came along and helped. At this period of the year, not only is it difficult to field teams, but even the officials must take their holidays some time.

At this meeting we held a few club handicaps and in the 100 yds. G. K. Talbot, one of our junior members, proved the winner. H. B. Taylor was second, with W. Timberlake third. The 1 Mile was won by J. T. Beer, who improved upon his second place at the last meeting. Jack Flower showed a welcome return to some of his old form by gaining second place, with R. W. Tobin third. The way Jack came down the finishing straight was reminiscent of some of his best performances, and we hope he will also find his form over the country.

The High Jump handicap was won by L. Jackson, who is our new Field events Vice-Captain, with C. A. Melchior second, and Frank Webb third. Frank certainly supports these field events very well and I am sure no one grudges him his successes. In the Discus Throw, G. R. Crawley made good use of his allowance to win, and his success is not out of turn. S. Jones was second, with L. Jackson bagging another by being third.

We seem to be getting the right fellows in our field events. With Bill Sutton as Captain and Jackson to assist him—not forgetting George Powell, who although handicapped by his hours of work, still contrives to come along and give our young members the benefit of his great experience—we should eventually build up a section second to none.

So ends another track season and, although on first thoughts we might appear to have had a bad season, actually, on performances it is one of our best to date. True, we lost our place in the S.A.A.L. 1st division, but against this we won the Inter-Club Relay Shield with the highest total of points ever scored. We also gained our first share in the "Waddilove" Trophy; and what is more important, our first place in an A.A.A. Relay Championship.

We also had more Junior matches than ever previously, and the results prove that we have as fine a lot of junior runners as any club in the land. It is the junior of to-day who is the senior of to-morrow and the motto of any club should be "look to the juniors." We have been particularly well blessed in having H. M. Quackenbos as Junior captain, and we all regret the fact that owing to exams., etc., he will not be able to devote so much time during next summer as previously. But the seed has been well sown and we hope that next year will see our juniors more to the fore than ever, and so make us realise our ambition of seeing Belgrave as strong a club on the track as they are over the country, and in the walking world.

The Annual General Meeting.

This year's A.G.M. was held on September 23rd, at our own Hall at Wimbledon. A fair number of members attended, and the reports of Hon. Secretary and Hon. Treasurer were well received. The Balance Sheet revealed a satisfying state of affairs, and gave evidence of the Club's progress in the matter of the acquisition of its own quarters.

The election of Officers, headed by that of W. L. Bayliss, who will again serve the Club as President and Hon. Secretary, proceeded, and a full list of those responsible for the government of the Club during this season will be found in the Club Handbook and Winter Fixture List. Incidentally, if you have not yet received your copy, a line should be sent to the Hon. Secretary.

The Meeting unanimously elected G. H. Powell and F. Rickards to Life Membership, upon the recommendation of the Committee.

Several matters were raised under "Other Business" and referred to the new Committee for their attention, and a Vote of Thanks to the Chairman closed the Meeting.

* * * * *

At the first Committee meeting, J. R. McKay was re-elected as an Hon. Auditor, and the Hon. Editor and Assistant Hon. Editors were re-elected.

Report · page 9

Our Jubilee Year.

For those unable to be present at the Club's Annual General Meeting, we give a summary of the happenings in our Jubilee Year. We have set ourselves a high standard in recent years and of necessity must expect an occasional disappointment. Our greatest disappointment came in the shape of relegation to Division II of the Southern Amateur Athletic League. Some perfectly sound reasons could be given for this failure, but we will content ourselves with saying that we hope to regain our status next season.

Our Jubilee Year was notable for several excellent performances. Harry Churcher walked seven miles in an A.A.A. Championship, faster than any Englishman had ever done previously; and Jim Ginty is the first Belgrave man to be placed in the English Cross-country Championship. Eddie Pack recorded 49 1-5 seconds in finishing fourth in the A.A.A. 440 yards Championship; and for the first time on record, a Club team was placed third in an A.A.A. Relay Championship.

Over the country our team recorded its "lowest ever" on points in the English Championships, and failed to win by four points only. For the first time, a Belgrave team went abroad, at the invitation of L'Union Sportive Metropolitaine, and scored a very fine win in a well-contested race. The Surrey Junior and Senior Championships were won by our teams, and the Brighton Relay lost by a bare yard.

Our walkers maintained their usual prominence and team positions in the most important races were : R.W.A. Junior Championship, 3rd; R.W.A. Senior Championship, 3rd; R.W.A. 50 Kilos Championship, 2nd; and Brighton Walk, 1st—a very fine record.

In many respects, we enjoyed our best track season yet. Our members were very prominent in the A.A.A. Championships and other races, and we were gratified at sharing the "Waddilove Bowl" with the L.A.C. on our first visit to the Birchfield Harriers' annual meeting. The "Inter-club Relay Shield" was won by a decisive margin, and a special word of thanks is due to H. W. Shieds on his efforts in arranging teams. Our Field Events men were well up to their usual standard, and we were pleased to witness J. Dodd's win in the A.A.A. Pole Vault Championship. Skelley and Finneron were also prominent during the season.

In track walking, H. G. Churcher was prominent, as already mentioned, and D. E. Brown and P. G. Wright were third and fourth respectively in the A.A.A. 2 Miles Championship.

So much for performances. The Club carried through its Winter and Summer season most successfully, although the absence of reasonable dressing accommodation at Battersea Park was a severe handicap. However, the newly-equipped Pavilion is now in use, and will be fully appreciated next season.

Innovations in our Jubilee Year were the introduction of Junior Championships (under 19) at 100 yards, 220 yards and 880 yards, and the award of standard medals in connection with our Field Events Championships. We would express our gratitude to Mr. Daily for the presentation of trophies for our Long Jump and Javelin Championships. The Club promoted the usual 7 miles Open Walk in November, and Inter-Club scratch walks in June.

A most successful Jubilee Dinner and Dance was held; and numerous Socials, Dances and a Summer Outing contributed much towards the social life of the Club, for which our thanks are due to our Social Hon. Secretary.

Probably the most important event of all during the year, however, was the fact that the leasehold deeds of Belgrave Hall were handed over to the Club. Thus an ideal, present in the minds of many members for years past, became a reality, and now we seek to consolidate our position and plan further improvements.

To conclude, we can look back upon our Jubilee Year as one in which much was accomplished, and the Club's sincerest thanks are due to those who have served it in any way.

Column · page 10

Ten Years Back.

A glance ten years back to our "Gazette" of October, 1927, is of interest. This was the first issue to appear in the now familiar claret and gold cover, and was edited by B. J. T. Knifton. In this issue we read of the Club's sorrow at the passing of its President, R. W. Ricketts; of an A.G.M. at which W. H. Hare was elected President, and W. L. Bayliss took over the Hon. Secretaryship, in succession to F. G. Thomson.

T. W. Green had just made his first attempt in the Brighton Walk and had finished second to the Italian, Giani. The Belgrave team (Green, Rathbone, Manning) was successful in the team race by one point, from Surrey Walking Club.

An interesting debûtant in our Three Miles Road Run at Wimbledon was T. H. Scrimshaw. With 1 min. 50 sec. allowance, he finished next to last! W. G. Webb ran in his first Club race on that day, finishing 8th, and beating the Hon. Editor by one second. Scratch man was E. A. Duffett, who finished fifth.

F. H. Elson's name appears for the first time. The November issue contains the present Hon. Editor's first contribution to our Gazette—an alleged humorous article entitled "The New Terror," dealing, if you please, with the menace of an Hon. Editor who stalks the land demanding articles for the Club journal!

Belgrave easily won the triangular fixture v. Thames Valley H. and Ashcombe A.C., E. A. Duffett leading the field home. The same issue shows T. W. Green winning the Club Open Walk, with a Belgrave team (Green, Fletcher, H. F. King and Scamell) winning the scratch team race.

These were the happenings of ten years ago!

Report · page 11

The Brighton Walk.

Eleven Belgrave walkers entered this year's race to Brighton, and all eleven were present at the start, at 7 a.m. exactly, on September 3rd. Due to the presence of an unknown quantity in the race, W. Cummings, champion of British Guiana, the pace in the early stages was very fast. Our visitor began to fall back at seven miles, however, leaving Harold Whitlock in the lead, which he retained to the end. At Redhill, Whitlock was two and a half minutes in front of record, and gained yet another minute before Horley was reached. From this point he slowly lost ground and at Handcross was behind his 1935 figures. Eventually he finished the course in 8h. 2m. 38s. and scored his fourth successive win—a really splendid record worthy of the heartiest congratulations. J. F. L. Henderson, winner of the London—Brighton and Back Walk earlier in the season, walked steadily throughout to finish second. Third came our Fred Rickards. With six miles to go, Fred was in second position, but a bad time in the last three miles enabled Henderson to take second place. Fred's time was, however, within a few seconds of his previous best, and his wonderfully fine performance at 39 years of age should inspire many of our younger members.

Our next to finish, in eleventh position, was Colin Sutton. He completed the course in 8h. 49m. 40s.—a wonderfully fine first attempt. Throughout Colin walked well within himself, and at an even pace, as though he were a veteran in experience on the Brighton road. No less meritorious was the walking of W. Urry, who followed Sutton. He, also, was making his first attempt in this race and showed fine judgment. It is most unusual for two men to finish in the first dozen at their first attempts, and we heartily congratulate Sutton and Urry. Our team was completed by R. Martin, who finished in 17th position. Bob has served us well in Brighton Walks on many occasions, and again he contributed his part towards another Belgrave win. Our score of 43 points gave us a reasonably easy win over Woodford Green A.C., and we regained our hold on the "Edward Knott Memorial Shield." Third were Leicester W.C., and the promoters, Surrey Walking Club, fourth.

E. L. Reeve, remembered by many as a prominent Club walker in years past, was our next to finish. He was 42nd, and followed by D. Willis. A comparative newcomer, Harry Thaxter, did well to finish 46th, and beat ten hours at his first attemptt. S. Bicknell, a member we see but rarely, and G. S. Doubleday, taking a holiday from marathon running, both pluckily finished the course despite badly blistered feet.

A. A. Harley and B. H. Clark retired from the race at Crawley, when recording useful times, and both will, no doubt, find this year's experience of use in future races. Among those finishing the course were two second-claim members, W. E. Ives, whose time was 10h. 24m. 3s., and A. H. G. Pope, who finished 5th in 8h. 34m. 37s.

The Club Long Distance Walking Championship was decided in conjunction, and the "Belgrave Cup" and championship medal goes, of course, to Fred Rickards.

C. F. Sutton takes second medal, and W. Urry third. Incidentally Urry also won the prize for the first unplaced "Service" athlete to finish.

A pleasing feature of the race was the fact that we entered more walkers than any other club. We were very glad to regain the Team Shield, particularly as our chances were not considered too rosy by outsiders, in the absence of old stalwarts in J. C. Bidgood and F. E. Bentley. It did prove conclusively that we have young walkers capable of keeping up the high standard our walkers have set in past years.

Once again, our very sincere thanks are due to all who helped our walkers as attendants. Without the sympathetic co-operation of this gallant band of workers, the walkers would find it very difficult indeed. When thinking of our team win and the excellent performances of our walkers, we are all reminded of those who helped to make it possible—the attendants.

A happy party made the journey homewards in the Club bus. Supporters, walkers, attendants, and Frank Thomson only knows how many cycles, were allocated positions. We hear that one or two decided that inside, perhaps, after all, was best! And so homewards, with another Brighton team win to our credit.

Feature · page 14

Strolling or Sundays Not-at-Home.

G. S. DOUBLEDAY

With the coming of the Winter season the Strollers are now herding together for their all-day Sunday jaunts—not that they only stroll in the Winter, the "hundred per centers" turn out 52 Sundays a year (even after walking home from Socials!). However, those who have been out before will be turning out occasionally, if not regularly, and this article is intended for those who have not tasted the joys of it.

We usually knock up 15—20 miles, which, if done over rough, hilly country, is a fair day's walk. This is done to accompaniment of talking, joking and (after dark!) singing; and one can learn quite a lot about Club life and personalities; and pick up valuable athletic information when one of the veterans of the party gets going. We do not, of course, always talk "shop."

We try to get about as much as possible, and sometimes grace the Southern Railway Conducted Rambles with our presence. Sometimes we sleep out on a Saturday, staying at Youth Hostels. This enables us to go farther afield and to make an earlier start. By this means we have covered the South Downs, Chiltern Hills and Isle of Wight (twice) on Bank Holiday week-ends.

The personnel of the strollers is a motley crew, especially when we don capes and caps when it rains! Our Hon. Editor, Alf. Harley, can be justly called the Patron Saint. Bert Footer is the map-reader and pub-diviner. Micky Walker sets a standard of eating which few can hope to equal. Bert Lindsey is the court jester (perhaps he'll be "caught" one day). Charlie Jones is often missing, only to be found in the rear admiring the scenery through an exposure meter. Charlie Churcher likes to get up to the front and set the pace—almost inviting a "tear-up." Woe to him who takes up the challenge! Arthur Beaumont, Fred White, Dick Michael, Ted Stimpson, Percy Wright, Alby Bird, Joe Daly and George Murray are also frequent strollers, and now the prodigal Alf Taylor has returned to the fold.

We usually take our own "rations" for mid-day, and find an ad lib tea place. A tone of cheerfulness always prevails—even the rain doesn't damp our spirits—it only "whets" our appetites.

So if you want a carefree Sunday in the open, just ask one of us for particulars. You will enjoy yourself and come again! Perhaps we'll be seeing you. Good strolling!

G. S. DOUBLEDAY.

Social Matters.

The Golden Jubilee Year is over and from the A.G.M. the Club commences its 51st year of history. Much has been written of the athletic performances and the team spirit which makes these performances possible. It is in the promotion of team or Club spirit that our social functions play a prominent part. Our Socials and Dances provide a magnificent opportunity for the discussion of Club topics; for the newcomer to become acquainted with his Club-mates; for old members who wish to see how the old Club is progressing. In fact, for all members to get together and so maintain that good fellowship for which our Club is so famous.

Already this season we have held one Social, and other Socials have been arranged on the following dates:— Nov. 6th, 1937. Jan. 29th, 1938. Dec. 11th, 1937. April 9th, 1938.

In addition to these, we are holding four Dances, particulars of which appear elsewhere in this issue. The Annual Dinner-Dance has been arranged to take place on May 7th, at Gatti's Adelaide Gallery Restaurant, Strand, W.C.2.

The Social Hon. Secy. would like to take this opportunity of thanking those people who have assisted him in the past and he looks forward to their co-operation in the future. If anyone has any suggestions that may improve our Socials, etc., or if anyone is willing to organise a "turn" (we should have some talent somewhere among the six hundred!) the Social Sec. is waiting to hear from them.

In conclusion, we extend a cordial invitation to all those who are not regular patrons of our Social functions to come along and bring your friends with you. We can assure you of a good evening.

All particulars from A. E. TAYLOR, 11, Fitzneal Street, W.12.

Column · page 15

Field Events.

Our Field Events men are to be congratulated upon again winning Division I of the Southern Amateur Athletic League. The Club's best thanks are due to all who contributed throughout the season to this success.

During the Winter it is hoped that all members will continue with light training, and an occasional jog over the country will do no harm.

A three-event field event match versus Metropolitan Police is proposed for Boxing Day, in conjunction with our usual handicap meeting at Battersea Park. The suggested events are Discus, Javelin and Weight; either on individual or team basis. Trials will be held about three weeks before the match, and any members wishing to be considered should get in touch with the Field Events Captain, W. H. Sutton. It is advisable to train steadily from now on, for sudden entry into competition without the very necessary gradual preliminary training may result in strained muscles.

It is understood that the A.A.A. are considering appointing a Finnish Field Events coach in the New Year. If this much-needed scheme comes to fruition, the Coach will probably visit the Public Schools and Universities during the week, and devote week-ends to instructional courses in London. In this event, it is almost certain that Batteresa Park, and possibly Belgrave Hall, will receive several visits; and it is up to every member to take advantage of the exceptional opportunity to acquire knowledge. We have clamoured for years for really expert advice, and if the chance now presents itself, we are sure all our members will attend and show appreciation, even if it involves some personal inconvenience or re-arrangement of private engagements. We all await further news with great interest.

Field Events enquiries should be addressed to:— W. H. SUTTON, 33 Tintern Road, S.W.4.

With the Walkers.

Another Winter season is here, and we have made a good start. With a Brighton team walk win, recently recorded, an easy win in our first inter-club match of the season, and a whole heap of success in the first "Open" of the year, we can feel satisfied. We have had some splendid scratch teams at seven and ten miles in the past, but this year's team personnel seems to be well up to previous standards. A continuance of the excellent form shown so early in the season should ensure many more successes in the months before us.

* * * * *

We are glad to welcome several new members to our ranks. Several have already shown up well in racing, and there are others likely to develop into first-class walkers in the near future.

* * * * *

Our "Junior" team for the Championship next March will quite likely be the strongest we have ever fielded—and that is indeed saying something. The Club's low record of 26 points in 1933, when H. G. Churcher, D. E. Brown, A. H. Field and A. A. Harley formed our team, stands a good chance of being beaten, so strong does our team appear.

The Championship is still several months away but carefully balanced training and racing during the intervening period should give us a very fit team. Of the constitution of the team it would be, perhaps, a little too early to write now, for there are many with claims.

All being well, another issue of the "Belgravian" should appear next February, and we may be able to indulge in a little prophecy then.

* * * * *

We are indebted to Mr. F. G. Thomson for a summary of the principal walking results from abroad. Unfortunately, pressure on our columns does not permit us to reproduce these results in full; but it is very evident that road-walking is progressing at a great rate on the Continent. Details are given of racing in France, Germany, Norway, Denmark, Holland, Latvia, Italy, Sweden and even Abyssinia. Familiar names to appear include Laisné (France), Schwab (Germany), Dalinsch (Latvia) and Mikaelson (Sweden).

The standard appears to be very high, and we look forward to the time when international team walks are an accepted part of our programme.

The difficulties, of course, are many; but difficulties were made to be overcome, and we trust the R.W.A. and A.A.A. will be able to make some progress in this direction.

* * * * *

The Club Open 7 Miles Walk takes place on Saturday, November 27th, at Wimbledon. As usual, all our active Club walkers are expected to support our promotion, and it would be very helpful if entry forms were sent in well before the official closing date.

We expect the usual excellent support from other Clubs; and an extra attraction this year will be the stern competition for the "Berman" Cup, allocated by the R.W.A. to our race, to be held by the winners of the scratch team race.

We trust all those not actively participating will come along to Wimbledon on November 27th as spectators.

* * * * *

December 11th is the day when we expect foreign opposition at Wimbledon. To give all our walkers a chance to turn out against our French friends, all our members will be eligible to compete and a sealed handicap will be held in conjunction, embracing all Club members. Additionally, the Club Junior Championship for the "Horwood Cup" will be held in conjunction with the match. All first-claim members having a "Junior" qualification according to R.W.A. rules can compete for the "Horwood" Cup, which is held at present by C. Speechley.

The Five Miles Walk Handicap.

An entry of 25 for our first handicap of the Winter season on October 2nd was very gratifying, and seems to indicate a good season ahead. In this race the man with the greatest allowance starts away first, and this was the privilege of G. S. Doubleday. He has been known as a runner in the the past, but it is certain that in a few months' time we shall look upon him as one of our future "Junior" team men. He was closely followed by two other newcomers of undoubted promise in A. E. Elliott and J. Ogilvie.

In a short time all the walkers were in action, and the results of our Hon. Handicapper's deliberations were awaited with interest. It was soon obvious that Tony Bellchambers and Reg Elson, who started off together, would set a very hot pace. So hot was it that nobody else could catch them, and Elson, with his extra years and experience, managed to draw away from his younger rival in the closing stages. Both walked very well, as did also Bob Martin, who took third prize.

Among the entrants were R. Stevens, A. H. Bridger, and H. Thaxter, in addition to those already mentioned, racing for the first time at Wimbledon in a club race; and all showed they will be valuable in their assistance to our teams.

The race must be considered one of the most successful yet held, and the form displayed gives us hope of a record season. Our usual team men seem to be shaping up well, and among the newcomers there is much talent only requiring the necessary training and perseverance to enable them to become first team men in their turn. The future certainly looks very bright.

Highgate Harriers' Open Walk.

"Belgrave Well off the Mark." "Clean Sweep in Highgate Open." These headings taken from "The Sporting Life" indicate that we had little to complain of at the first open walk of the season. Eighteen of our walkers entered, comprising two teams—the largest entry from any club. All started, and claret and gold vests were soon prominent at the head of the field.

Harry Churcher eventually had an easy win from F. J. Redman, and V. W. Stone.

Our supporting men were always well placed, and at the finish we had our fourth man in actual 10th position. We had six in 14 positions; and seven "A" team in the first 18 places. Naturally such splendid walking gained an easy team win for the Club, and our "A" team were also successful in the handicap team event. In this the "B" team were placed third.

Thus nine "firsts" went to the Club and four "thirds," and Len Coleman took the special prize for the member of the Club with largest entry, not gaining any other award. So Belgrave had 14 prizes out of 23!

The individual handicap winner, H. G. Clayton (6m. 30s.) actually finished fifth in the scratch race, and held a special interest for us as he has been advised and encouraged by Tommy Green. He is now a Belgrave Harrier. Our men's positions, etc., are given below:—

At Imber Court.

On November 16th, our walkers visited Imber Court for a match v. Metropolitan Police, and, as usual, found among our rivals many who are usually our team-mates. Thirteen aside were the numbers decided upon for scoring purposes, and away went 40 walkers over the usual flat seven miles course.

Before a mile had been covered, our men were well placed, and as the race progressed so did our lead over our Police rivals. We had the satisfaction of finishing the first seven men, and ten in the first thirteen placings. This gave us our easiest win for many years. In fact, in recent years we have often been narrowly beaten, the strength of the Police in the middle section of the race usually being the deciding factor.

It is worthy of note that 15 of the first 19 to finish are Belgrave Harriers. It is always pleasant to visit the very sporting Police team at their Imber Court quarters, and we may be well sure that they will be all out to reverse their defeat when they visit us at Wimbledon in January.

Club 3 Miles Road Race.

This was held at Wimbledon on Saturday, October 2nd, in fine weather and a good size field turned out. Actually there would have been an even larger field, but quite a good number went out for a training spin, including among them most of the club cracks. Although it is as yet early in the season for racing; in the old days it was always considered good fun to take part in this race, whatever the result, but with the march of progress, perhaps, different tactics are now required.

As to the race itself: George Tyrer went off with his usual long start, with George Still going off next, and George managed to keep in front until nearing home. He was then passed by R. Stride, Jnr., a very promising young runner with a fine stride (excuse the pun!) and he made good use of his start to run home an easy winner.

Freddy White showed that marathons had not taken all his pace and ran well to gain second place; with a newcomer in R. W. Johnson just beating Bert Smith for third honours.

Ted Stimpson put up the fastest actual time. He was the last to start and worked his way through the field in fine style. Another to show good form was J. A. W. Kemp, while K. N. Wood made a welcome re-appearance to do third fastest time. These three were the only ones to get inside 15 minutes, and when one considers the times of the good old days it will be seen what tremendous improvement our runners have made.

Race v. Tyrian Club at Coulsdon.

On October 23rd we visited the S.L.H. quarters for a race with the Tyrian Club, which consists of past and present members of the London University. Southgate Harriers were also present to contest a match over 5 miles.

Although raining all Friday night and best part of Saturday morning, we arrived at Coulsdon in sunshine; but our joy was short-lived, as on our way to the starting point the rain came down in sheets, and with a driving wind both officials and competitors were soon soaked to the skin.

Jimmy Kemp, evidently feeling robust, was soon up in the front, together with Les Cohen, and they kept in the lead most of the way. Nearing home Penny and Footer came along and with Cohen going slightly off the trail, these two and Kemp eventually came home together.

Scoring 7 aside, varsity system, the points resulted: Belgrave H. 43, Tyrian Club 88, Southgate H. 100.

We have to thank our hosts for a very jolly Tea which we all enjoyed after the run.

Over Plough and Country.

E. A. DUFFETT, Running Hon. Sec.

The Cross-county Season is now well into its stride and the attendances at Belgrave Hall, Wimbledon, are well up to previous seasons. The mid-week runs at the "Rising Sun" are also being well supported and I hope all our members will appreciate how helpful these mid-week runs can be. We use a course which is ideally suited to cross-country training, and, moreover you have the opportunity of training with several Internationals, who are always pleased to give you the benefit of their long experience.

On November 13th, at Dartford, will be held the South of Thames District Team race. Owing to the various barring clauses, we have to call on more or less our junior runners, but this season we seem to have a team that should improve upon our second place of last season, With our own Harry Parker as President of this Association, I am sure none would be more pleased than he at a Belgravian success.

The following week, November 20th, we have the usual Inter-Club run with Mitcham A.C., Croydon H. and Epsom H. This year we are only holding the one meeting which will, in turn, go to each club's quarters. Actually Mitcham were going to act as hosts for this race, but owing to their restricted dressing accommodation they have asked us to hold the run from Belgrave Hall. I wonder how many clubs have such quarters as ourselves? Lucky is he who joins the Belgrave, and with the gradual advance of the builders upon open country it will soon become a question of suitable country to run on. Here, again, we are extremely fortunate in having the fine open Wimbledon Common, with the lovely stretches of Richmond Park for our members to run over.

On November 27th we hold our annual Open Walk from Belgrave Hall and, owing to the large entry, we always arrange an away fixture for our runners. It is taboo for any runners to go to Wimbledon, at least for a run. If they wish to give a hand with the walk, I am sure our Walking Hon. Sec., Alf. Harley, will be pleased to have their names. We have, accordingly, fixed an away fixture with Highgate Harriers, who are also entertaining the R.A.F. Uxbridge, and this will take place from their quarters at De Bohun Farm, Southgate. It is some time since we last visited Highgate, but we are always pleased to meet a club with such a fine record over the country.

On December 4th we are holding the Surrey County Youth's Cross Country race over a 3 miles course at Wimbledon. The last time we held the race from our quarters we also provided the winning team; so I trust our junior members (this race is open to those under 18) will turn out and duplicate this performance. If those who are qualified will kindly send me their names, I can send them full particulars.

Then comes what is going to be a red-letter day in the Club's history. On December 11th we entertain a foreign club to a race over the country—our walkers are also meeting their walkers—and I think this is the first time that such a fixture has ever been arranged in this country. Our opponents are none other than the very good hosts of our Paris trip last season, L'Union Sportive Metropolitaine. We hope to give them as good a time as our lads had over there, and also give them a run over typical English country. On this day we hold our 7 miles race for the "Blackstaffe" Shield and the race with our visitors will be held in conjunction. There will be the usual Sealed Handicap open to our own club members, so be sure to enter and have a run against some foreign opposition.

On December 18th, we are holding the Club Junior Cross-country Championip for the "Savage" Shield. This is over our 5 miles course and is open to all under 21 years of age. On the same afternoon we entertain Ealing Harriers and the Metropolitan Police A.C. to a run, and this will give a chance for some of our newer members to show their paces.

The following Saturday is Christmas Day, and we take this early opportunity of wishing all our members a Very Happy Christmas!

On Boxing Day morning we hold the usual meeting at Battersea Park track at 11 a.m. The events are all handicap races—120 yds., 2 miles Run, 3 miles Walk. To those sprinters who have been doing some winter training at Wimbledon, this should prove a good opportunity of proving whether it has helped them or not. The cross-country men always turn out in large number for the two miles run; and the "snaky" ones, who have dined wisely over the holiday, usually reap their reward. Entries should be sent to me at least a week before for handicapping purposes. Entry fee for each race is 1/-.

The first Saturday in the New Year is also the first day of January, and we welcome to Wimbledon the Dartford Harriers, a very well-known Kent club. Also the Guards Depot, Caterham, are coming along and we hope they will enjoy our country, also the amenities of Belgrave Hall.

January 8th is County Championship day, and the Surrey race is being held at Coulsdon from the S.L.H. quarters. I notice that Penny and Footer are already being tipped as probable winners and with such a fine send-off we should have no difficulty in securing team honours once again. We have won this Championship for the past five consecutive years, and eight times out of the last nine years. Also on this day is the Middlesex race, in which we have several members competing as members of the Cadogan A.C., and we wish them every success.

A week later, the 15th, is held the Surrey Junior C.C. race; this time at Shirley, which is just outside Croydon. The age limit for this race is 21, and as holders of this title, we hope that this year's team will do their best to retain the honours.

The Inter-Counties Cross Country race is due to take place on January 22nd, at Gatton Park, Merstham. We usually provide the bulk of the Surrey team, while our members are also seen in the vests of Middlesex and Kent, so that on the whole there is quite a good Belgrave flavour about this race.

On January 29th we hold our 10 miles C.C. Championship for the "Macher" Shield. Last year, Jim Ginty provided a surprise by winning, but whether Jim will defend his title is doubtful, owing to the fact that now he is out of the Army, his job has taken him some distance away from us. We all hope he will be able to turn out for us once again; and lead our team as he did in last year's National race; and we can be sure that he will make every effort to do so. For those who are on the fringe of our senior team I would remind them of the sealed handicap which is held in conjunction with this race.

At this time of the season out teams will more or less have taken shape, and we hope that this time there will be no breakdowns or other unforseen contingencies which will prevent us from regaining the title of English Cross-country Champions.

That we have the men to do this no one will deny, and although beaten by a mere 4 points last year (and that with our sixth man scoring 41), I feel sure that given reasonable good luck and an even share of the breaks of fortune which in the past has not smiled too freely on us, we will bring the title back to the South once again.

All entries and enquiries to

E. A. DUFFETT, Running Hon. Sec., 76, Greenside Road, W. Croydon.
