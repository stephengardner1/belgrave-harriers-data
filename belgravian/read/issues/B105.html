Editorial · page 2

From the President

W. G. WEBB

I take this opportunity of expressing my appreciation and thanks for the honour you have conferred on me by electing me President of the Club. There is no honour of which I could be more proud. I am deeply aware of the responsibility of this office and will do my best to carry on in the tradition of my predecessors. In this endeavour I shall be guided by the light they have left burning and sustained by the confidence that I shall have the co-operation of all members.

Nothing is more important to the Club than this spirit of co-operation. Our continued success both in administration and in the field of sport must, in the end, depend on the strength of each member's personal sense of responsibility for the affairs of the Club. We must never allow ourselves to leave everything to a small group of enthusiastic members who can be relied upon not only to attend committees but also to shoulder the enormous amount of work entailed by their decisions.

" Teamwork " has always been our watchword—more difficult, perhaps, in a big team than a small one, but even more essential. I feel sure that the grand old Belgrave team spirit will carry us forward to even greater success.

On a more personal note, my wife and I wish to express our thanks for the very handsome clock presented to me on my resignation from the Treasurership. I shall value it always, not only for itself, but as a reminder of the many happy years I have spent with the Club.

W. G. WEBB.

W. G. Webb

The Hon. Secretary—Alf. Harley writes:

ALF. HARLEY

We managed in recent weeks a unique performance in Athletics—that of losing two Senior National team titles in eight days! However, we can console ourselves with the thought that no other athletic club was ever in the position of holding two such titles at the same time. There is profit to be found in defeat and virtue in challenging for a title as well as defending it. They'll come back, giving us redoubled satisfaction; and our younger generation of runners and walkers the thrills and zest in attaining the highest things which wearers of Belgrave colours are entitled to expect.

* * *

We can still win, of course! Our runners provided winning teams in both Surrey and Middlesex Cross-country Championships. The South of Thames Championship was won, despite barring clauses, for the third time in four years, with Derrick Burfitt leading the field home. The Southern Championship, too, was held, although it must be admitted not so convincingly as on some former occasions; and our walkers have always been prominent in all their racing, and once again retained the Surrey County titles with ease.

The London-Brighton Relay gave us a really first class win despite determined opposition from 24 other clubs. We accomplished our second hat-trick of wins in this series of races, and have won this event more times than any other club in the history of nineteen races over the familiar course. This year's race was undoubtedly the most exciting ever, with several clubs taking the lead at various points and more teams than ever before with a possibility of winning, with only ten miles to go. However, Belgrave prevailed as so often before; with Birchfield, Tipton and Reading striving to the end—gallant and sporting opponents as ever.

* * *

To our already long list of Club Championships has been added yet another—a Junior (under 19) One Mile Championship which will be contested at our Championships Meeting on Saturday, August 6th, for the first time.

Happily, a Life Member, Vice President, and former Cross-country Captain, Ernie Musselbrook, has come forward and generously presented a handsome challenge cup for this new championship. He has expressed the hope that this trophy will eventually carry the names of many who will go forward to the Senior ranks and assist the Club to team honours—for Ernie was ever a team man. We gratefully acknowledge his generosity.

* * *

The only Club Championship without a trophy is the Hop, Step and Jump, and no doubt this will be remedied in due course. It is whispered that a Junior 440 yards Championship may be the next to be considered by the Committee.

* * *

From the Summer fixture list it will be seen that a very representative range of fixtures has been tabled. We are very grateful to the London Olympiads A.C., for permission to include several events in their programme on June 22nd.

It is to be hoped that all members will make the fullest use of the facilities provided and events arranged during the Track Season now upon us. Given the right approach, we can be as effective a force in Track and Field athletics as past years have shown us to be in the realms of Cross-country and Walking.

Our Chief Coach and all his assistants, together with the Hon. Secretaries and Captains are anxious to develop and weld our undoubted talent into effective teams; and we have our thoughts and our eyes on the various Relay Championships and on performing creditably in all our Inter-Club fixtures.

With the co-operation of every member—determined to do his best for the Club—we can look forward with confidence to the Summer Season.

* * *

Our good wishes go with Colin Sutton and his wife and Junior Member Robert, on their emigration to Adelaide in far away Australia. We are sorry, indeed, to lose such a fine sporting family and wish them every success in their new environment. Colin has won many Club Championships and assisted the Club to many team wins; and his walking has ranged from 2 to 100 miles. In 1938, he was a member of our winning "Junior", 20 miles; and 50 kilometres R.W.A. Championship teams—a 100 per cent. team member in a 100 per cent. R.W.A. Championship effort, and a unique performance in the world of walking. We shall certainly miss Colin, not only for his abilities but also for his grand sportsmanship and pleasing personality and his great loyalty to the Club. When serving with the R.A.F. in India, he still found time to walk, and finished second in the All-India 50 kilometres Championship—and he still had time to look out for talent for the Club. It is to Colin that we owe the introduction of another grand sportsman and performer—Vern Blowfield—"discovered" by Colin at Service athletic meetings in India and "ear-marked" for Belgrave when still thousands of miles away!

All the best to you, Colin and your family; and let's hear from you.

Good wishes to Basil Walden, on B.O.A.C. duties in Tripoli; John Briggs and Jack Shields, now studying at Oxford University; to Reg. Ruddick, now qualified for our teams and eager to show his undoubted ability on our behalf; to our Chief Coach, George Clay, recently returned from hospital after an operation; to Harry Thaxter, "Mine Host" at The Sun Inn, Diss, Norfolk, who recently sent greetings to all friends; to E. Thomas Gilbert, who is now serving in H.M.S. Zest; to Don. Arnold, now a corporal in the R.E.M.E.; to our Hon. Secretary, on being elected to the Southern Committee A.A.A., on our nomination, eighteenth of 48 nominations made; to Charlie Smart on getting a Civil Service appointment in this country and so giving us the benefit of his running rather than his Control Commission colleagues and German spectators; to Michael Porter, formerly of Sale Harriers, but now first claim to us on recent Road Race successes. Undoubtedly he will help our teams.

We are grateful to Len Marchant who has undertaken the duties of Hon. Press Secretary and does his utmost to ensure that our notes appear in at least ten local newspapers every week.

* * *

Our profound sympathy goes to Past President Teddy Gordon on the loss of his wife—so well remembered by so many of us. We shall miss Mrs. Gordon at our meetings and functions; and recall with gratitude her many services to the Club in the past. Particularly, one remembers breakfast served on the roadside in the small hours on our Annual All-night Strolls to Brighton; and those many helpful gestures in the early days of the Building Fund.

Hon. Treasurer's Notes

The following donations to general club funds have been received during the past six months from the following members, and I wish to express the thanks of the committee for their generosity:—Messrs. G. A. Atkinson, O. Horwood, £2 2s.; J. G. Coleman, T. H. Cotton, T. C. Jones, £1; F. S. Bradnum, 11s.; L. J. Cohen, A. A. Harley, H. E. Footer, A. E. Taylor, F. R. Webb, 10s.; E. Musselbrook, 5s.

These are additional to donations given by various members to the Open Walk Prize Fund, and the Children's Party Fund.

* * *

Many members seem unaware that Club Subscriptions become due in September of each year, and cover the period up to the following September. A member who joins at any time after September pays a full year's subscription, but his next subscription becomes due the following September. It will assist me considerably if subscriptions are paid promptly, without waiting for a reminder.

* * *

It may not be generally known that the Committee have ruled that members serving in H.M. Forces under the National Service Act may be excused payment of their subscriptions, but it is essential that such members should advise the Hon. Treasurer that they wish to take advantage of this concession. If no communication is received we have no means of knowing the member is entitled to be excused payment.

* * *

One other small but important matter, do please keep the Hon. Secretary or Hon. Treasurer informed of all changes of address, and so save the bother of having communications returned "Not known."

Club "Handicaps"

Are you a handicap or an asset to the Club? Excluding Life members and members serving with Services, we have a membership of about 650. Of this number nearly 300 have paid their subscriptions for the current year, whilst of the remainder, 215 have paid for last year, leaving nearly 150 who have not paid anything since the Club year 1946-7 or earlier. If you are in arrears with your subscription please send a remittance straight away to the Hon. Treasurer, and so save him the time, and the Club the expense, of sending a reminder to you. Remember that the cost of the Club Magazine, the Handbook, Prizes, Medals, and many other items have to be paid for somehow, so do not be a handicap by leaving it to others, but pull your weight.

The "Overseas" Trophy—Percy Wright says:

PERCY WRIGHT

Remember it? We planned it for after the war with the money that we made on those "Airgraphs" you chaps sent me. Unfortunately, the man that bought the "Airgraphs" died and this source of income died with him. We had, however, succeeded in getting quite a tidy sum—£6 19s. 6d., to be exact. I had hoped to plan something else to raise the money to a sufficient sum to purchase a suitable trophy, but my present distance from the Club quarters is not convenient. It has been

suggested to me that I make an appeal to all those who served overseas and would like to see the idea of the "Overseas" Cup materialise to send along a subscription. Well, lads! what about it? Send your cheque, postal order or stamp, either to me or to the Hon. Treasurer, not forgetting to mention what it is for. We should also be pleased to have any suggestions on how the trophy shall be awarded.

This appeal is addressed to all Club members who served overseas and not only the Walkers.

Walking Notes

SYD ROBERTS

WALKING NOTES By SYD ROBERTS

The winter season opened with our usual 5 miles Handicap, with a match against the Police in conjunction. In all, 40 Walkers started, including 25 of our members, a promising start for the new season. In the absence of Harry Churcher, Bob McMullen led the field, followed by Bob Readman and Smith of the Police. F. Tasker, rapidly improving, won the Handicap, with Brian Shepherd second and Don Heath third.

In the first Open of the Season, Highgate Harriers 7, at Parliament Hill Fields, we entered three teams, but neither Harry Churcher or Joe Coleman were amongst the competitors and with Jack Rutland nowhere near his last year's form, we had to be content with third scratch team.

A fortnight later came the Enfield Open and although it was the same day as the Club Dinner, this did not deter the majority of our Walkers from making the long journey to Enfield for the race and back to Clapham for the dinner. This race was the beginning of a winning sequence, practically all our men walking up to form. Harry Churcher soon set up a good lead and remained unchallenged. In addition, we took scratch team honours with the "B" and "A" teams second and third respectively in the Handicap and George Oliver second in the Individual Handicap.

Next came our own Open with, as usual, the biggest entry of the season. Although a little below last year's total of 248, there was an almost total absence of provincial entries due, no doubt, to the ever increasing cost of travelling. The weather, however, was not very kind to us and as a result of a very dense fog a number of competitors were unable to reach Wimbledon. Despite the conditions, 182 Walkers faced the starter, and once again Harry Churcher led throughout. Backed up by McMullen, Readman and East, we succeeded in retaining the "Berman" Trophy. Our "B" team once again finished second in the Team Handicap and Len Fletcher, proving he can still show some of them the way round, finished fourteenth and was awarded the prize for the first unplaced member.

Our thanks are again due to those loyal members who contributed to the prize fund and also to the large number of officials who gave such valuable assistance under very trying conditions.

A fortnight later we held the Horwood Cup Race. A field of 22 juniors lined up for the start and once again Bert East led them home, with George Bentley second and Pat Sapcote third. Charlie Speechley, still having a go, won the Handicap, with George Atkinson second and Joe Scammell third. On the same day a Novices 3 miles race resulted in a win for E. Evans, one of our new Walkers.

The following week our Walkers travelled to Bexley Heath for the Cambridge Harriers Open and continued our winning sequence in the scratch race with Harry Churcher leading the team home. Once again the "B" team were second in the Team Handicap.

With the new year, came the Met. Police Open at Imber Court. In a field of over 200 starters, Harry Churcher soon took the lead on this very fast course and the scratch team added yet one more to their list of Open

wins, this time Charlie Churcher getting his first place in the winning team. With the continued run of Handicap placings our teams were unable to get in the first three.

The following Saturday, we held our 10 miles Club Championship, which, needless to say, was once again won by the invincible Harry, with Bob McMullen second and evergreen Bob Readman third. Alan Robins, the first "junior" to finish, showed a marked improvement and was placed fifth. A match in conjunction resulted in the Police being beaten by 55 points.

And so we come to the Junior Championship at Enfield on February 5th. Although we had a reasonably good team it was generally thought that competition would be keen between Cambridge Harriers, Highgate Harriers and ourselves, and not much was known of the strength of Midland and Northern Clubs. Sheffield, however, proved to be very strong and their scoring four finished in the first dozen. Coleman of Highgate and Long of Cambridge, having been ruled out of their respective teams by the referee, we were given second place with 48 points. Unfortunately for us this was later reversed by the A.A.A. Southern Committee, the revised placings being Highgate second, 52 points, Cambridge third, 53 points, Belgrave fourth, 56 points. Alan Robins again walked exceptionally well to lead the team home with Bert East, George Bentley and Pat Sapcote supporting him. George Oliver, who had been off form recently, was relegated to the "B" Team, but was fifth club man to finish.

In the Surrey Championship we once again secured double honours, Harry Churcher leading the team in his usual style, followed by Charlie, Mac and Bert East. Charlie was again selected as Team Captain for the County, the other three also being members of the team with Syd James as reserve, for the Inter Counties Championship at Leicester.

Our Cadogan members in the Middlesex Championship did not fare so well, with the exception of Bob Readman who finished second to Jim Morris, the team being fourth. Bob was selected to represent Middlesex and Alan Robins, finishing fourth in the Kent Championship the same day was selected to represent that county.

And so to Leicester for the National 10. Keen competition was once again expected from Highgate and Cambridge, with little known of Sheffield apart from their "Junior" win. Harry Churcher and Allen of Sheffield set the pace from the start, but unfortunately at about 3½ miles Harry failed to please one of the Northern judges and was disqualified. This left Sheffield in a very strong position and at 6½ miles they had three men in the first seven with a total score of 31 points. Our first four were well placed at 6, 8, 9 and 19, 11 points behind Sheffield, with Charlie Churcher rapidly moving up. Jack Rutland, however, was having some trouble and slowed up slightly. Highgate occupying fourth, fifth, sixteenth and twenty-fourth positions were a few points behind us. Towards the last mile, Mac showed signs of tiring and whilst Charlie moved up still further, eventually overtaking Bob Readman, Mac slipped back to tenth position. Jack Rutland clung on desperately, maintaining his position to the finish, but with one or two in the first twenty dropping back, Highgate improved their third and fourth positions gaining seven points against our loss of one. Sheffield also lost a few points over the last stages, but this did not prevent them maintaining a clear lead, and with L. Allen nearly two minutes in front of Jim Morris, they took double honours, with Highgate in second place, beating us by one point. Congratulations to Bob Readman in getting seventh place and thus qualifying for Club Honours Badge. Bob was also a member of the winning team in the Inter Counties Championship which was won by Middlesex.

A Review of the Cross Country Season

By Ernie Duffett

Although naturally hoping to do as well as last season, when we cleared the board so far as the major championships went I think that apart from our dismal showing in the "National" race, that our ruuners can be said to have done well throughout the season.

We started by failing to secure a place in the South of Thames "Junior," but this was due more to the selection of our teams than to the failure of our runners. Eric Herbert, who was our second man home in 15th position, had been put in the "B" team and we had to go back to 57th for our fourth scoring "A" team runner. Even so we only lost third place by 1 point, but with Eric Herbert in our "A" team we should have been an easy second to Surrey A.C., who won the race with the low score of 30 points.

We won both the Surrey County and Middlesex County events, the latter by reason of our members who ran for their second-claim club, Cadogan A.C. Frank Abberton was the individual winner of the Surrey race and Derrick Burfitt only failed by a couple of yards to repeat this performance in the Middlesex event.

In the South of Thames 7 miles Championship at Horsham, we repeated our win of last season and have now won this race three times in the last four years. As the winning six scorers are barred from competing again, it will be seen the considerable strength which we have, and it is this that makes us all the more optimistic about the future.

In addition to again winning the "Dewar" Shield we also won the "Colman" Cup awarded to the club which has the lowest score for its complete team of 12 runners, and indeed our score for 12 men was lower than some clubs had for their team of six counters.

The Southern race was held at Camberley and with a field of over 350 runners, team scoring was naturally high. Even so, our winning total of 183 points was the highest ever in the history of this event, so I think the best that can be said was that we were the best of a poor lot!

Here again we won the "Oddling-Smee" Cup for finishing our complete team before any other Surrey club, and we actually had 11 men in the first 96 scoring places, which in such a large field is excellent running.

So to the "National" at Birmingham and although we were not too optimistic we certainly expected at least to get a place, and so continue our unbroken list of placings in this event since our initial third place in 1933. There was a huge field of 449 runners and it does seem as if some qualifying rule should apply to clubs entering this event as some of the competitors were hopelessly outclassed and a large number were lapped.

We had some consolation in winning the "Charles Otway" Cup, awarded to the first club, outside the placed teams, to finish its team of 9 men, but hope that next year we shall once again be up to the front and fighting it out with the Sutton lads, who will be hard to displace from their positions as champions.

Our "A" and "B" teams had been having a battle royal in 2nd and 3rd positions, but Alec Olney on the last "leg," put up a really smashing performance to clock 23.4 for the journey of about 4½ miles, and took his club into second place.

Our "B" team finished third with our "A" team fourth, and as there were four sets of prizes, our lads were well satisfied with the result of their efforts.

The Track Season for 1949

Ernie Duffett

Ernie Duffett writes:—

Your Committee have arranged a full list of fixtures for both Senior and Junior members, and there are also a number of "B" team matches for the lesser lights.

We open our track season on May 7th with a club meeting at Tooting, and we then have matches arranged for each Saturday in May.

The County Championships are on June 4th, with Surrey at Motspur Park and the Middlesex at Southall.

On the Whit Monday, Surrey County are holding their Junior 4 x 110 yds. relay and the Senior Mile Medley relay at the "Charlotteville" meeting at Guildford, which is a very good grass track.

June 18th is the "Kinnaid" meeting at Chiswick with Poly Marathon race from Windsor in conjunction, and we have decided to again hold our Club Marathon Championship for the "A. F. Mitchell" Shield on the result of this race.

On June 22nd, at Tooting, we are holding our 3 miles track championship, by kind permission of the London Olympiades A.C., who have the track booked for this evening.

June 25th is Southern Championship Day and this year they are being held at the Hove Stadium track.

On July 9th there are the Southern Junior Championships at Cambridge and although this is rather far from town we hope as many of our Juniors as possible will enter.

The A.A.A. Championships are on July 15th and 16th at the White City, and they are also holding the Junior Relay Championship in conjunction with the Senior events.

On July 30th the A.A.A. Junior Championships are being held at Birmingham on the Birchfield Harriers track at Perry Barr.

Club Championship Dap is August 6th at Tooting, and a new addition to our list of championships at a 1 mile for Juniors under 19, for the "Musselbrook" Cup. Entries for all events should be sent in at least a week beforehand and only members entered on the programme will be allowed to compete.

The Surrey County Youths Championships are at Tooting on September 10th, and the following Saturday at the same track we hold our final track meeting of the 1949 season.

There are handicap events to suit all ages, also a 1 mile race for Unattached Novices, so if any of your friends fancy themselves as a runner, don't forget to bring them along on that day.

All entries for running events should be sent to me at the following address, a card will do, as you can pay the entry fee on the day.

Column · page 26

Field Events Gossip

Terry Bateman

Terry Bateman writes:—

This coming season, it is hoped, the Club will improve so that we shall at least attain if not exceed our pre-war standard in Field Events. We have a number of new members to support this hope. "Steve" Gibson, in the High Jump, has already achieved quite good performances with "the Scissors" style. All the winter he has been gradually evolving the "Western Roll" technique and before the end of the season should show improved performances. With Dick Negus back in training, and, we hope, back to his form of two years ago, we should be quite strong in this event. We hope also to have the assistance of John Boxall now that his examinations are nearly at an end. Unfortunately, our chance of having Wilf Anderson, our Club Champion, out are practically nil as he is on a tour of duty in Germany with the R.A.F. "Steve" Gibson, Les Christmas and Stan Botwright will be our main hopes in the Long Jump, but it would be good to see Geoff Wood taking part in this event more often as it is felt he has good possibilities. Geoff Wood and Les Christmas, together with Bert Machell, will be our Hop, Step and Jump men. Geoff Wood should get near A.A.A. Standard, and Les Christmas with more training getting over 40 feet. In the heavy events we do not seem to have any new blood. Jock Mackenzie in the Discus should again show improvement and will be backed up by Sid Jones and Bill Sutton. Sid and Bill will be our Shot Putters, but Jock Mackenzie and Les Christmas have possibilities if they only get down to training. There is a need for more men in both these events and some of those heavyweights who find running a bit too arduous might think seriously of taking up either or both events. In the Javelin we hope to have the assistance of the two old stalwarts. Charlie Melchior and George Powell. However, it is to Alan Dunn, Geoff Jarvis, Arthur Allen and Terry Bateman we look for improved performances and from at least one, distances approaching, even if not beating those achieved by the old stalwarts in previous years. Geoff Jarvis, who is in the R.A.F., is also quite a good hurdler both over the High and 440 yds. distances. We hope he will be able to get leave for the more important matches. John Briggs, now at Oxford, is also a hurdler of possibilities, but we cannot expect him to be able to turn out until the end of the term. As far as the Juniors are concerned our strength is rather unknown although we have a large number who are interested in Field Events. Doug Whitehead, who was very promising last year in the Javelin and Shot, is now in the Forces and will probably have difficulty in getting along for matches. John Morrison should show improvement in the Shot. Terry Batemen, our new Field Events Hon. Secretary, needs all your support in his first season to make it a really successful one. So you are expected to rally round him not only to improve your own performances but that of the Club in general. Your coaches for the Field Events have felt that some have not made as much use of their services as they would have liked but it is still not too late. Therefore, if you are really keen on improving your performances, contact them and you will find they are willing to give the benefit of their advice. The biggest day for Field Events is the Polytechnic "Sward Trophy" meeting at Chiswick on May 14th. Every event is included and we have to provide two men for each so it is expected you will get down to training immediately. This is followed by the County Championships on June 4th. We should like to see these Championships really well supported. Even if you cannot hope to get a place there is always a standard medal to be aimed at.

The Kinnaird Trophy and the Waddilove Trophy now include most Field Events and we must have strong teams to support the runners.

The Club Championships are fixed for August 6th at Tooting. All the way through the season there will be inter-club matches in which Field Events will be included as well as handicaps at all events for which we expect your full support.

A number of Open Meetings are including Field Events handicaps and our support of these is needed. Not only will it give you added opportunity for competition but only by this means can the country discover new talent. Details of these meetings can be obtained during the season from the Hon. Secretary or Captain.

Frank Simmons Writes

Frank Simmons

Dear Juniors,

This is a reminder to you all that the summer season is fast approaching, which means quite a lot of you will be thinking of returning to the track. Some of you have already met me, some have yet to meet me, but to all I will be pleased to see you. I may as well tell you now that I am your junior sprint coach and that I work in close co-operation with George Clay, who is our chief coach. For Field Events Juniors, Charlie Jones and Bill Sutton are your coaches. Incidently, you did not make enough use of them last summer, so perhaps you did not do as well as you thought. This can be remedied by you alone, by turning out for coaching regularly. Micky Pyer is middle distance coach and if you pay attention to him you will do well, as I am sure if you have any running in you Micky will help you to put ti to the best use. To the sprinters, George Clay and I have ambitions but can only achieve them if you pull your weight. We have had a crack at the 4 x 110 yds. A.A.A. Junior Relay and only just failed. We want it this year and with your help we will get it. Also I want more to endeavour to enter into County and other individual championships. In short, we want to make our Club feared at shorter distances as the Cross Country boys are and our walkers are over their distances. I must warn you that the preliminary training may be boring but stick it as it all leads to bigger things. Who knows, you may be Belgrave's first Olympic Sprint Champion. I know I am aiming high but it can be done and the coach's job is to help you. Tuesday evening P.T. class under George Clay is going well at Belgrave Hall, but again I would like to see more of you there. It is designed to help you. To all the juniors, all-day Sunday rambles are taking place fortnightly and again I would urge you to come along. Charlie Jones leads and I can assure you of a very good time in good company.

Wishing you all a very good track season and assuring you of the help of all your coaches.

YOUR JUNIOR CAPTAIN.
