Editorial

YOUR EDITORS.

Dear Fellow Members,

A letter was recently received from V. W. F. Law, who joined us in 1931 and is now the Hon. General Secretary of Bournemouth Athletic Club. In one of his paragraphs he writes: "I am always quoting Belgrave and planning things as I remembered them when I was proud to call myself a 'Belgravian.'" May we, Mr. Law, with all diffidence, point out the irregularity of your statement? Once a man has become a true and tried Belgravian he remains so, and thus the axiom should be "I am a Belgravian" and not "I was a Belgravian."

Bournemouth A.C. are indeed fortunate in having as their Secretary one who has learnt the true meaning of club spirit and we are happy in the knowledge that although Mr. Law is now Secretary of another club and without doubt utterly loyal to that club, he is still a "Belgravian."

This can be said of others who have done so much to further the interests of the Club in so many diverse ways. Men like Tommy Green, who served the club so loyally whilst enjoying international honours. The Harry Parkers, Frank Thompsons, Harry Hares, Dick Murphys and many more grand sportsmen, who although not perhaps first-class athletes, also served so well. Then there are those who do no particular job of work, those who we seldom see except on such occasions as Championships and Boxing Day, and lastly those who for various and similar reasons like Mr. Law's have left the club. All are proud to be associated with our club and wherever they are will always remain "Belgravians."

To the younger members who, perhaps, have not yet experienced the sentimental association of our Club we say, become a good Belgravian and enjoy all that "I am a Belgravian" implies.

YOUR EDITORS.

N.B.—Mr. Law also writes that should any member of the Bels be spending their holidays at Bournemouth and wish to continue training, they have only to get in touch with him and he will do the rest. Address: V. W. F. Law, 4, Cotlands Road, Bournemouth, Hants.

Obituary · page 2

Harry Parker

It is with profound regret that we have to record the death of Harry Parker, on Sunday, April 30th, and our heartfelt sympathy and sincere condolences are extended to Mrs. Parker and Harry's two daughters.

The passing of Harry in his year of office as President of the English Cross Country Union and Southern Counties Cross Country Association is tragic indeed, and leaves us with a tremendous sense of loss—one which we shall not fully realise for some while to come. Athletics and the Belgrave Harriers are the poorer for his passing; the richer for the forty years of enthusiasm, ability and service he rendered so willingly, so cheerfully, and so effectively.

Harry's long connection with our sport began in 1910 when he joined his local club, the Westminster Harriers and ran with and against many who, like himself, were destined eventually to bring honour to the Belgrave Harriers and in turn be honoured by the Club. Thus, Oscar Horwood, a schoolmate, Harry Goodfellow, Ernie Musselbrook (then in Kennington Harriers), Dick Best and others were his contemporaries; and Harry soon showed himself a useful performer and displayed his tremendous sense of team spirit and solidarity, combined with a desire to be of help in the administrative sphere which characterised him all his life. A dispute with the General Committee of the Westminster Club (of which the Harriers were only a section) led to the formation of Chelsea Harriers, and Harry was second in the Club 8 miles Championship and prominent in all its races.

In 1912 it would seem that the Chelsea Harriers ceased to exist and many of its members became Belgrave Harriers. From this time on Harry knew he had found his true love and for 38 years gave something of himself to the Belgrave Harriers. He had the satisfaction of seeing the Club progress, slowly at times, 'tis true, from junior status to the highest honours that athletics can bestow. For some while he was a regular team member, known, as one would expect, more for his pertinacity and grand team backing than for individual brilliance; and all the while he worked for the Club and for the success, the honours, the prestige that later years were to bring.

It is hard indeed to state just how much one man's efforts have meant to an amateur organisation such as ours where so many have contributed so much over the years, but it is safe to say that Harry Parker's efforts have not been surpassed. At one time or another he served the Club in almost every office that ever existed. His little black notebooks were always in and out of the breast pocket; the thousands of reminder postcards he sent off were the subject of joking but it was always Club work at its best—the essential, unassuming work that must go on and on if the Club is to go on. Harry longed to do good by stealth and over and over again we had cause to be grateful for his benefactions at our various quarters, and let us not forget the "Pyramid" Cup which he presented to the Club.

It was during the war of 1939-45, however, that Harry's greatest work for the Club was done and these efforts must be rated all the higher for the difficult conditions under which they were carried out. Almost single-handed, Harry kept in touch with members in all parts of the world, and if I may intrude on a personal note for a moment, they were eagerly awaited, informative, cheery epistles, full of news of the Club at home—and how we enjoyed them! And so despite all the trials of those six years the structure of our Club was kept together; which meant (let us remind ourselves) that athletics generally could continue, for Belgrave and its headquarters, was to a large extent, the centre of athletics in the Metropolitan area in those difficult days. After the war, Harry became President of the Club, an honour well deserved and crowning the efforts so worthily made in such a variety of offices in previous years.

In addition to his great club work, Harry had served for years on the various Cross Country bodies and had been President of the South of the Thames C.C. Association and as previously mentioned, was this year in the highest official position in Cross Country Running, President of the English Cross Country Union. He had served as an A.A.A. Handicapper, an official at the 1948 Olympic Games, and was usually to be found with an important job at all the principal and cross country track fixtures.

At Harry's cremation, numerous friends and Club and Association representatives gathered to pay their last respects to one who had served our sport so well, and had won our hearts in the years that had passed. Appropriately enough, the ceremony took place within a stone's throw of our cross country course.

We mourn the passing of one we have been honoured to know, and privileged to work with, and he will not be forgotten whilst the Belgrave Harriers live on . . . R.I.P.

Harry Parker Memorial Trophy

As you will have read elsewhere in this magazine, the Club has suffered a great loss by the death of Harry Parker, a man who gave many long years of his spare time and energy to the services of our Club and to athletics in general.

Your Committee feel that we should not let the memory of his name and service fade, but that we should do something to permanently remind the present members and bring to the notice of new members this example of long and devoted service to the Club. It was decided that the best way of achieving this object would be to put up for annual competition a trophy, suitably named and inscribed, probably in connection with a cross-country event, a branch of sport in which Harry was particularly interested.

It was felt that members who knew Harry and appreciated his work for the Club would like to contribute towards the cost of such a memorial trophy, and I feel sure we shall get a response from you that will enable us to purchase a trophy in keeping with the great respect and affection with which Harry was regarded by us all.

This appeal is primarily to our own members, and it is not the intention of the Committee to approach the many good friends of Harry who are not members of the Club, although we do not intend to refuse any of them who particularly feel that they would like to be associated with us in the provision of this memorial to our mutual friend.

Furthermore, it was decided that members will not be canvassed for their contributions towards this fund. We are bringing it to your notice by means of this note and it will be up to you now to show your appreciation of Harry by sending or handing your donation to the Hon. Treasurer, 141 Bramblewood Close, Carshalton, Surrey.

Do it now before it slips your memory!

It is perhaps necessary to point out that this appeal is entirely separate from the collection made just before Harry's death, when Bert Footer, on behalf of the Committee, collected over £20 with the object of making a presentation to Harry to commemorate his many years of office in the Club and other athletic organisations, culminating in his election to the Presidency of the English Cross Country Union. Unfortunately he died before a decision had been reached as to the precise nature of the presentation, and in the circumstances your Committee decided that the money should be handed to Mrs. Parker to use as she thought fit.

Harry ParkerBert FooterMrs. Parker

5 Miles Road Race

Our usual 5 miles road race with sealed handicap was held at Wimbledon on March 18th and although only a week after the National Cross Country Championships, a large field was seen out. This race is used as a pointer to our Brighton Relay team and the fight for the leading places was as keen as ever. The times recorded compared favourably with last year's result and it was interesting to see how Ray Tooby and Mike Stearman fared in competition with our senior runners.

The individual race was won by Vern Blowfield, who ran in quite his old style to win by half-a-minute from Bill Lucas, our 1948 Olympic Games representative.

Ches Walker, who is just 21 years of age, ran very well to gain third place and this lad should develop into a real champion in a few seasons' time.

Les Stokell ran his usual determined kind of race while Jack Brown seemed content to take matters easy in front of Archie Bedford, Steve Charlton and Jack Bragginton.

Ray Tooby came next and to finish so well up was indeed a fine performance for a lad only just turned 18 years of age.

Ted Stimpson and George Lucas found that they could not match the pace of the younger members of the Club but will no doubt come again, especially if they train really hard as one has to do if they hope to stand any chance of making our teams these days.

Mick Porter and Mike Stearman followed by Len Adams also did well but Derrick Burfitt was suffering from a heavy cold and never seemed to get really going.

Arthur Bruce and Len Bishop are two others now out of the junior stage who could be of use to our senior teams if they really give their minds to it and it is to the younger members of the Club that we must look for support if we are to regain our lost honours next season.

The handicap was won by Len Bishop, with A. W. Saville, an office colleague of Bill Lucas, in second place and with Ray Tooby and Jack Bragginton dead-heating for third position.

T.V.H. Road Relay

Held at Cranford on March 25th, and there was an entry of 34 teams. We ourselves had five teams out and these comprised four men who each had to do a lap of just over four miles.

At the first change-over T.V.H. "A" were in the lead, followed by the Milocarian A.C., with our "A" team in third position. Our "B" team were 7th, our "E" side 13th, our "C" team 20th and our "F" team in 24th place.

T.V.H. were still leading at the next change, but our "A" team were now 2nd and our "B" team had come through to 4th, mainly due to a fine run by Ted Stimpson.

Birchfield Harriers, the holders of the trophy, were well down the field, and although they eventually finished 8th, this was quite a change for such a famous club.

At the end of the third stage our "A" and "B" teams were now 2nd and 3rd and these positions were maintained over the final lap.

From a team performance this was a very good result and with such clubs as Surrey A.C., Coventry Godiva H., Birchfield H., Oxford City A.C., and Reading A.C. with teams in the field, our lads can be said to have run very well indeed.

Metropolitan W.C. 15 Miles. Regent's Park, April 1st

A very large field turned out for this race, which is now one of the most popular of the season. Although the Sheffield boys were busy with their own race in Sheffield, J. Proctor made the journey to London to walk as an individual, and right well did he upset all the sages by walking extremely fast in the early stages and maintaining practically the same speed throughout, eventually returning the fastest time ever for this event. All our lads started fairly steady and at three miles Charlie Churcher and Bob McMullen were in 5th and 6th positions, whilst Bob Readman and George Oliver were together at 15th. At seven miles Proctor was well away in the lead, Charlie and Mac still 5th and 6th, Readman 12th, Oliver 18th, Sapcote 20th, Bidgood 24th, Bentley 26th, Wright 28th, with Clayton taking it easy owing to a strained leg. At this stage the team race showed Surrey W.C. leading wih 34 points, Belgrave 2nd with 45 and Woodford Green 3rd with 58 points and we were fairly confident. At 10 miles the team position had completely reversed itself with us still in the middle. Woodford were now leading with 32 points, we were 2nd with 34 points, and Surrey A.C. 3rd with 36, and our chances looked rosier as the fourth Surrey man was cracking. He was now behind Woodford's third man but in front of our third, which meant we woud pick up a few points. At 13½ miles Woodford were out in front with 22 points, whilst a battle was going on between us and Surrey A.C. for second place with Surrey slightly in the lead. At this stage Charlie and Mac were still going strongly. Jack Bidgood was instructed to get Pope and Kent of Woodford, and like the good worker and team man that he is, he duly carried out his instructions. Although he succeeded in making up a few places over the last two miles, he was unable to make any impression on the Woodford scoring, who brought off a very popular and well-deserved victory. However, Jack's great work over the last two miles resulted in him pegging back Surrey A.C. and we eventually beat them into third place by one point.

The Brighton Relay

Held on April 1st, this year's race was confined to Southern clubs only and we missed the excitement of battling against our old rivals from the Midlands and North.

Conditions were ideal and four new lap records were set up and although the winner's time was faster than the last four races it did not approach the 1938 record time.

We congratulate Thames Valley Harriers on their first win in this race and all their lads ran very well, but it was mainly due to the long lead that Alec Olney and A. R. Miles set up over the first two stages that they were able to lead all the way without any other team getting near enough to worry them.

As regards Alec Olney's record breaking run on the first leg, this was simply superlative running, and when you think back on some of the well-known runners who have done this stage and yet not come within a minute of Olney's time, well all we can say is that Vern Blowfield was not disgraced in handing over in 9th position and his time was actually faster than Derrick Burfitt did over the same leg last year.

Ted Stimpson went off next and he ran a "blinder" and took us up into 6th place, and here again his time was faster than last year, although not approaching Pat Parker's 26.18 in 1937.

Steve Charlton took over for the uphill stage through Redhill and on his first run in this race he did well in taking us up another two places and his time was the third fastest recorded on this leg.

Jack Brown went off next and he mantained our fourth place although losing ground to the Blackheath runner. This was one of the stages where the existing record was broken and Jack's' time was also inside the old record of 27.25.

Derrick Burfitt was our next runner and he gained a place passing the Reading man and his time was the third fastest on the day and was six seconds faster than Len Herbert did last year.

Archie Bedford now set about the field and running like one possessed, he caught the Blackheath runner and we were now 2nd to Thames Valley and Archie's time was a new record although slower than the 10.25 which Willie Nankeville did running for Walton A.C.

Les Stokell was our next man over the long and crucial stage from Hanacross to Hickstead and our best time for this leg was Tom Carter's 30.26 in 1937, while Vern Blowfield did 30.40 in 1948. Les, however, could never seem to get really going and he was soon passed by the Blackheath runner and then A. A. Robertson of Reading caught him, and nearing the change-over the Aylesford man also got by and so we were back in 5th place with the leaders further from us than ever.

Ches Walker, our youngest runner, went next and running in nice style he caught and passed the Aylesford runner and also gained on the other teams and his time was second fastest for this stage.

Len Herbert then went off on the climb up Dale Hill and although he could not pull back any places he gained on the teams in front and actually recorded fastest time on the day for this stage, although not so fast as Eddie Short did last year.

Bill Lucas took the last leg into Brighton and as he has had the honour of being first into the finishing straight by the Aquarium for the last three years I expect Bill found it a bit unusual running in 4th place. He did second fastest time, being a second faster than Ellis of Thames Valley, the actual winner, but for the first time since 1931 we found ourselves beaten out of a place.

However, we had some consolation in the fact that the first four teams home qualified to take part in the "National" Road Relay being held in November and we hope that our team will be stronger by then and that once again we shall lead the field into Brighton.

Bognor 10 Miles Road Race

Held on Easter Monday, there was a very strong wind blowing, but conditions seemed to favour the runners and Alec Olney set up a new course record with a time of 50 mins. 52 secs.

The next two men home were Miles and Manley, also of Thames Valley, and so they scored a hollow victory in the team race. We were pleased to hear that we had been awarded second place after a tie with Portsmouth A.C., our third scoring man being placed nearer than theirs.

Steve Charlton ran very well to finish 6th.

Tom Carter and Stan Davies gave Steve good support and Bert Footer also qualified for a prize as the fourth member of our "A" team. It was quite like old times to see Tom and Bert going up for their "pots."

First Track Meeting

Our first Club track meeting at Tooting Common was held on April 15th and we had senior and junior matches versus Victoria Park Harriers.

The weather was not too good and just after the start of the meeting we had a hailstorm but managed to carry on and get the meeting completed, for which we thank all those officials who braved the elements on our behalf.

In the senior sprint events we were poorly represented and the visitors had a dual winner in R. D. Fergusson, who has on occasions run for the A.A.A.

We won the 880 yards when Ches Walker beat L. N. Lait, with Archie Bedford in third place, but the visitors had first two in the 440 with Jim Beaumont gaining a point for us.

In the 1 mile, Derrick Burfitt and Jack Brown strode round in the lead and never looked like being troubled and they ran in together.

We scored major points in three of the five field events with our Latvian member, A. Bergvalds, winning both the Shot and Discus, and young A. J. Sinton won the High Jump.

When we totalled up the points we found that V.P.H. had scored 47 to our 43 and we congratulate them on their win.

In the Junior match Jimmy Rae was second in the 100 yards, while Dave Marshall occupied the same position in the 220 yards.

Alan Picton won the 440 yards, and this seems to be his best distance, but our team lost the 4 x 110 yards relay.

We lost points on the two junior field events and the final score was: V.P.H. 31 points, Belgrave 17 pts.

It is yet early in the season to attempt to judge our prospects for the season, but it still seems as if the lack of first-class sprinters will be our biggest headache.

Club 20 Miles Walking Championship—April 22nd

A really good field of 24 walkers faced the starter. Right from the outset, Bert Clayton forged to the front and at 1 mile had a clear lead of 100 yards from the rest of the field, which came along in two batches, the first consisting of probables and possibles, and the second batch of hopefuls and, we hope, future probables and possibles. Bert Clayton continued to walk in grand style and at 8 miles was leading by the same 100 yards from Charlie Churcher and Pat Sapcote, with Bob McMullen, walking his first 20, some 40 yards further back, followed by Messrs. Wright, Bidgood, Bentley and Oliver. It is interesting to note that Jack Rutland, who finished 4th, was at this stage 12th. At 11 miles, Clayton had increased his lead to 400 yards from Charlie, and McMullen and Pat Sapcote had dropped to 4th, with Wright and Bidgood making up ground. Oliver was now 8th and Rutland 9th, Bob Readman, seemingly taking it easy, 10th, and Tony Bellchamber, also walking his first 20 very strongly, 11th. Further back, Harry Shields, another “ first timer,” was pegging away. At 16 miles Bert Clayton appeared to be falling back slightly, or was it Jack Bidgood coming up? For at this stage, Bert was leading by about 300 yards, with Charlie 3rd, about 100 yards further back, with Mac in close attendance and Pat Sapcote coming up strongly. Jack Rutland was now 7th. At 17 miles Bert had come into his former strong style again and at this stage there was no doubt who would be the winner. His lead was now 600 yards and he was walking splendidly. Jack Bidgood looked good for a second and there were signs of a real fight for third place between Mac, Pat Sapcote and Jack Rutland. Charlie was tiring and retired. Further back, George Oliver, Tony Bellchamber and George Bentley were still walking strongly, especially Tony, who was walking like an old hand. Over the last two miles Mac tired badly and dropped right out of the picture, but we compliment him on putting up such a grand show for the greater part of his first 20.

The result gave us great hopes for the coming National 20 and a headache for the Selection Committee as we had no less than ten men inside 3 hours 15 minutes over this strenuous course.

“Brockman” Trophy Match

The second annual meeting for the “ Brockman ” Trophy was held at Tooting Track on May 6th, and eight clubs had been invited to compete.

With points scoring 6 for first place down to 1 for sixth position in each event it is obvious that you have to do well in all the events to stand any chance of winning the trophy.

In the circumstances, and seeing it is yet early in the season, I think we did quite well in being placed 4th of the competing clubs.

The 100 yards saw us with two competitors in the final, which was quite a good start, and Gordon Lowers finished third with R. D. Holtum sixth, and the latter is a useful addition to our sprinting ranks.

In the 880 yards both Alan Bishop and Archie Bedford failed to qualify for the final of this event

The 220 yards saw Alan Sexton qualifying for the final, but he was obviously short of training and did not push it unduly when finishing in fifth place.

We scored our only win of the meeting in the 1 Mile with Derrick Burfitt beating Morley of Southgate in quite good time, and as the latter had beaten Derrick the previous Wednesday when running for the A.A.A. against London University, this win gave our runner the chance to turn the tables on his opponent.

In the 440 yards both Cope and McKenzie failed to make an appearance and it is a pity that when chaps are sent a card that they do not reply if they cannot turn out. We had Alan Bishop and T. Underwood running for us but they failed to qualify for the final, and we could certainly do with another George Dunckley.

The 3 miles was another event which found us short of runners, a sad state of affairs for a club which prides itself on our distance strength, but Bill Lucas, although still suffering from the after-effects of a touch of 'flu, ran quite well to finish in sixth place and so score a point for us.

In the field events we fared rather better than usual, and in both the Long Jump and the Hop, Step and Jump we took third and fourth places, through P. Lowe and J. Mendes.

A. Bergvalds, our Latvian member, scored points for us in the Weight Put and Discus Throw, but we missed the help of Charlie Melchior in the Javelin Throw.

The meeting, although rather long drawn out, was carried through on time and provided the spectators with some good performances; two meeting records were equalled and five were broken, including the 3 miles, which V. Lillikas, the A.A.A. 6 miles champion, won in the fast time of 14 mins. 38.5 secs.

Final placings of clubs resulted as under:— 1, Herne Hill H., 106; 2, Poly. H., 59; 3, Southgate H., 44; 4, Belgrave H., 35; 5, Ilford A.C., 17; 6, Cambridge H., 10; 7, Reading A.C., 6; 8, Woodford Green A.C., 4.

“Gordon Stewart” Trophy Match

Held at Drayton Green Track on May 13th, this meeting took the form of a four-sided match between the local club, Ealing Harriers, Walton A.C., Maidenhead A.C. and ourselves.

In the 100 yards heats both our runners qualified for the final and in this “ Ginger ” Holtum was unlucky to be adjudged the loser in a “ blanket ” finish. George Lowers was close up in 3rd place but as Walton had the winner and the 4th man they scored 6 points to our 5 for this event.

The 880 yards saw Archie Bedford and Jim Kennedy running for us but up against a strong Walton trio, which included Willie Nankeville, the A.A.A. mile champion, we had to be content with 4th place through Bedford, whereas Walton scored a “ maximum ” of 8 points for this race.

In the 1 mile it was our turn to score a “ maximum ” and we actually had the first three men home, with Jack Brown leading Vern Blowfield and Ches Walker in the good time of 4 min. 31.4 secs.

We only had one runner, R. A. Bartlett, qualify for the 220 final, and he eventually finished 4th, with Walton and Ealing sharing the other points.

The 2 miles team race saw Bill Lucas lead the field home in 9 mins. 57 secs., and with good support from Reg Bolam (4th) and Steve Charlton (6th) we won the team race from Walton A.C.

Thus with one event to be decided we were still two points behind and as in the Medley Relay, Willie Nankeville was running the 880 stage for Walton, our chances of regaining the trophy seemed slight.

Such proved to be the case, as Nankeville gave his team about 25 yards lead from Walker and Underwood running the quarter with Holtum and Bartlett doing the 220's, found themselves unable to reduce the gap and so Walton retained the trophy which they won last year, with a total of 33 pts., with ourselves scoring 28, Ealing Harriers 13 and Maidenhead 3.

R.W.A. National 20 Miles Championship Bellingham, May 13th

Although we were beaten into second place, our boys put up a grand show and walked splendidly throughout—but stay, before we sing our own praises, let us first congratulate a very gallant team, Woodford Green, in their triumph which they achieved after fighting hard for so many years; the splendid walking of the Sheffield boys Allen and Proctor, and finally, with greatest respect, the performance of T. Lloyd Johnson on once again getting a place in his 50th year. We feel that this was the greatest performance of all and we share with all walkers our sincere compliments to this grand athlete.

At the start, Allen got away very fast with Bert Clayton close on his heels and the rest of our boys well up filling various positions from George Oliver 15 to Tony Bellchamber 60. The feature of the event was the exciting team race, the lead changing hands many times. At 5 miles Belgrave were leading Sheffield. 9 miles showed us still leading with 56 points, Surrey W.C. 61, Sheffield 72 and Woodford creeping up with 75. At 10½ miles Surrey went into the lead with 47 points to our 50 and Woodford 3rd with 62. At this stage Sheffield's 3rd and 4th men had dropped back and almost out of team hopes. Another check at 13½ miles showed us leading Woodford by 58 points to 62, but it looked as if Woodford men were piling in from everywhere and so the club whips got busy. Bert Clayton was going well in 7th position and looked like catching Woodford's first man, which he eventually did. Charlie Churcher was taking a bashing but quite enjoying it and there were hopes he might catch Goodall, Woodford's 2nd man. Pat Sapcote was determinedly chasing three Woodford men and the odds seemed that he would catch them, in fact he assured us that it was in the bag, and he certainly did it. Then came Percy Wright, walking perhaps faster than anybody, and our hopes were right back again. This state of affairs caused much blood pressure, but Woodford managed to hold on to their slender lead. Meanwhile another keen duel was in progress between our lads and Woodford for the Nijmegen Shield. At 15 miles Tony Bellchamber was our 8th man and tearing along as if he had only just started and quite bucked because he had left Woodford's 8th man behind. (Mention should here be made of an incident before the race when Syd Roberts met with a street accident just before meeting Jack Bidgood and Co. at Chelsea. George Bentley stayed behind to accompany Syd to hospital, but the incident undoubtedly upset Jack and completely put him off his form. We are pleased to report that Syd is now fully recovered and would like to thank all those who kept his wife busy at the phone enquiring as to his welfare.) However, Jack fought desperately to keep in front of the 8th Woodford man, whilst Jack Rutland and Tony Bellchamber, like good team men, stayed with their vice-captain as long as they dared to keep him going. After a desperate battle, Jack reaped his reward, beating the 8th Woodford man by 20 yards to win his 6th Nijmegen medal (given by the club to members scoring in this competition), and we have

good reason to believe that Jack is the only person in the country who has scored in the Nijmegen Team on six occasions. Thank you Jack for a very brave performance. Another item of interest and one which certainly deserves mention, is that all our team men, both “A” and “B” finished the very strenuous course.

Did you know that—

H. E. Footer (otherwise known as Our Bert) joined the Club in 1922 and is therefore one of the oldest in membership still in our active ranks.

Although lacking in inches Bert makes up for this by possessing plenty of “ guts,” a good club spirit, and tons of determination, which are essential factors if you wish to rise to the top in the cross-country world.

Bert gained International honours by representing England over the country in 1933 and 1934, and in both these races he finished in 4th position and so worthily upheld the prestige of the Belgrave Harriers.

He was a member of our winning team in the South of Thames “ Junior ” race in 1925 when our placings were: H. E. Footer 2; A. D. Pyer 6; H. W. Bruce 7; K. B. O'Kell 9.

The 1933 Southern Counties Senior C.C. race was won by H. E. Footer, while he also won the South of Thames Championship in 1930, and he won the Surrey County Senior individual title in 1930, 1935 and 1937.

Bert was also a member of our winning team in the Southern Counties Senior race in 1934 and of our winning “ National ” teams in 1935 and 1939.

He has represented Surrey County on numerous occasions and was once a power in Territorial running as a member of the London Irish.

During the War he served in the Royal Marine Commandos and the sight of a Footer with his blood up is enough to scare even the most formidable opposition.

Bert has also won both the “ Blackstaffe ” Shield and the “ Macher ” Shield and at one time his stock of medals was one of the eighth wonders of the world.

He also used to run the crucial 9th stage in the London to Brighton Relay and was a member of our winning teams in 1934, 1935 and 1936.

He has run for the Club in every conceivable kind of race and also did quite well over the Marathon course, being the present holder of the Club Championship at that distance.

Bert was cross-country captain for many years and led the Club to their first “ National ” success.

He is now one of our coaches and we strongly advise all those under his tuition to mark well his advice which he is always ready to give.

Entries for Club Events

The Committee view with grave concern the haphazard manner of entering for Club events. Notice is hereby given that entries must now be sent to Sectional Secretaries at least one week before the event, preferably with entrance fee. We ask you to consider this right and proper decision reasonably and to co-operate by readily complying. There are many good reasons for this decision, ask your handicapper for one! We are sure he will give you a good one and Bill will be “ True ” in his veracity on this subject. Make a point of studying your fixture card, it is given to you for YOUR information and remember—you have been warned.

Notice · page 23

Officials for Club Events—Calling for Volunteers

It is our intention to set up a panel of Track and Field Officials, with proper Club status, for our own Club meetings, but this can only be done with your assistance. Members are required as Track Judges, Field Judges (high jump, long jump, discus, hammer, etc.) and marksmen. The work is very interesting and affords an excellent opportunity of maintaining an active interest in the Club apart from a competitive one. If you have any experience or qualifications, all the better, but if not, we shall be pleased to give you the proper training under the guidance of our existing officials both on training nights and at our regular meetings. In due time you may be given the opportunity to pass the A.A.A. examinations and thus become fully qualified judges. If you would like to take an interest in this side of Club work, send your name in to the Secretary of your Section and he will pass it on to the General Committee.

We earnestly ask for your co-operation in this matter and repeat once again—ANY VOLUNTEERS.

News in Brief

G. Warr won the London Universities 1 mile track walk in record time.

D. J. Merrett has been selected to run for the A.A.A. and also won the 100 yards for London Universities in their match against an A.A.A. team recently.

Tony Bellchamber finished 2nd in the Inter-Banks 7 Miles Road Walking Championship.

Len Herbert ran very well to finish 2nd in the Inter-Counties 3 miles run when he clocked 14 mins. 37.8 secs.

Ron Kemp won the Hampshire Constabulary 2 Miles Open Walk at Eastleigh off 250 yards.

Four of our walkers, Harry Churcher, Bob McMullen, Ron Kemp and G. Gregory were invited to walk in the 10,000 metres race at the White City on July 5th, mainly a trial for the European Games.

The Club has met with successes in many open meetings and our team of Walker, Sexton, Beaumont and Underwood won the Mile Medley Relay at the Charlotteville meeting on Whit-Monday. Geoff Osmint won the open 100 yards at the same meeting and “ Ginger ” Holtum was 3rd after winning the Open 100 at the Watford meeting on Whit-Saturday.

It is interesting to note that the three Police Walking Championships, 2 miles, 10 miles and 34 miles have been won by “A” Division whose teams on each occasion have consisted of all Belgrave Harriers. The individual titles have also been won by our walkers, viz., Readman, Readman and Bidgood respectively.

Mick Porter finished 5th in the Doncaster to Sheffield Marathon Race on Easter Monday, which was run in very bad weather conditions.

Bert Clayton was 3rd in the Inter-Counties 7 Miles Track Championship. Harry Churcher won the 2 miles.

Thanks are due to Bert Mitchell for kindly presenting two miniature shields for this year's Marathon Championship.

Doug Whitehead writes from Abusultan, M.E.A.F., that he has gained his R.A.F. Egypt “ blue ” for javelin and recently won seven events in the station sports. Also he shows the right spirit by joining two of his comrades to the Club. Sends best wishes to all and will be with us soon again.

Pte. Joe Tranter (Splinter) writes from Malaya and by all accounts is doing quite well. Has recently won four cups and a medal and says he is running like a carthorse—some carthorse. Also sends good wishes to all Belgravians.

We are happy to report that Frank Thompson is now quite well again and is, in fact, attending athletic meetings.

Harry Churcher once again won the Business Houses 2 Miles Track Championship, with Bob McMullen 2nd and at the same meeting young Dan Godfrey won the 100 and 200 Youths' events.

Vern Blowfield won the 1 mile title when beating Stan Cox, the Olympic Games runner.

J. W. Brown, V. S. Blowfield, C. W. Walker and L. A. Adams gained 3rd place in the 4 x 1 mile “ Victory ” Relay at the Inter-Club Relay held at White City on June 10th.

Alan Sexton has won the R.A.F. 220 Yards Championship.

Congratulations to Dr. Woodard and Mrs. Woodard on the birth of their second son, Nicholas Andrew. Born June 20th. In order to create competition within the family we learn he is a member of H.H.H. We are happy to report that his elder brother, Christopher, who is a Belgrave Harrier, is now quite well after his recent serious illness.
