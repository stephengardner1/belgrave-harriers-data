EARLY SEASON SUCCESSES.

Our ladies got the Track and Field season off to an early and successful start when on April 16th they travelled to Withdean Stadium Brighton for their first Southern League Div.3 Match and came away with a sufficiently clear win to end the day top of the Div. ahead of Herne Hill. Everyone was a star but pride of place must go to Jackie Harman who gained the sprint double by a huge margin. This was Jackie's first League appearance for us and she could be a big factor in our promation chances.

Our men were quick to follow the example of the ladies when on the 7th May our top team journeyed up the M1,M6 and M5 to Warley to take on the two teams promoted with us to Div.5 of the British League plus established British League competition. There were a few nerve racking moments as the coach rounded up members from various parts of London but eventually all arrived safely to post a series of excellent performances which left the opposition way behind. One of the intrigueing features of the British League is that we shall be meeting the same opposition 3 more times and if we finish in the first two places then we move up to Div. 4. No doubt the opposition will be working out ways of pulling back points but we still have a few cards up our sleeves - so please whatever you do,put the 3 remaining British League Matches well up your list of priorities. And the result of the first match?

1.BELGRAVE 301; 2.Windsor S&E 291; 3.Cannock Chase 248; 4.Bristol 247; 5.Hillingdon AC 219; 6.Met.Police 172; 7.Stretford 168;

BUT MEN'S SOUTHERN LEAGUE TEAM DISAPPOINTS.

Meanwhile on the 23rd April the men's Southern League team travelled also to Withdean Stadium at Brighton and it soon became clear that a shortage of Field events men would hamper our chances of a good result. In fact if it had not been for Reg Hopkins Jnr. and John Aska the final points total would have been even worse. There were some bright spots however. John Gladwin (eligible having missed our British League qualifying match last September) ran a lone 800m to record a fine early season 1;50.1,while first year Junior John Warren took 6 secs off his 1500m p.b. to clock 4:03. Despite such redoubtable efforts we finished equal last in a fairly close scoring match and there will need to be greater support if we are to achieve our objective of avoiding relagation.

BELGRAVE MOVES UP THE RANKINGS.

The Annual NUTS Club Track and Field Rankings for 1982 have been published recently and they reflect the improvements which resulted in last year's promotion to the British League. Catch 22

Catch 22

The introduction of the wider page 'Belgravian' was made with the hope of reducing pages and hence printing costs. By some strange coincidence the change in format occurred at a time when our whole range of activities expanded and try as I might I cannot provide an interesting and factual account of your activities in less than 44 pages. There was a time when your Hon. Editor could look forward to some kind of respite in the winter but what with the increasing interest of members in indoor competition as well as the despatch of teams onto the Continent no longer can the winter season be regarded as our quiet period. So what ? Well there is no doubt that the whole scope of our Club's work has increased and what with the new all weather Track at Battersea as well the planned improvements at Belgrave Hall things are likely to get even more demanding than hitherto.

The time is not far away when we will have to review the way we manage our affairs. Anyone who has attended recent Committee meetings at which representatives of all Sections, Sub Committees , Working Parties and so on discuss and control the affairs of the Club will realise that decission making is becoming more and more drawn out as the numbers attending increase. Add to this the fact that as our membership increases so our turnover moves into the small business bracket and you will see that some thought should be given to these matters in the not too distant future. The problem is - who's got the time to sit back and produce a master plan for the future ? CATCH 22!!

grapevine

Belated congratulations to Richard and Sonia Dorman on their marriage last June. Richard, who now lives in Nottingham, has made a welcome return to racing and is already well on the way to regaining his top form.

Oliver Foote went into hospital at the beginning of March to have his wisdom teeth removed. Four weeks later he was our fastest long leg runner in the Haig-Belgrave relay and by a big margin! We wonder if this technique for shedding weight will catch on.

While on the subject of improving racing performances it has not gone unnoticed that John Bicourt has started shaving his legs! We can report that this new development has something to do with John's new career in films and TV. Intrigued? Watch this space!

Among ex-Surrey AC members receiving the last copy of the 'Belgravian' was P.W.(Jimmy) Green. No stranger to us for he competed in Belgrave colours during World War 2. Jimmy of course needs no introduction as the man who started Athletics Weekly' after the war. Jimmy is now in his 70's but still trains local athletes.

Walter Batson, competitor in the first Barking to Southend Police walk, another former Surrey AC man and now a member of Belgrave is back on the road again after illness.

George Williams, a wonderful stylist, former Belgrave walker and International was recently seen in Bournemouth and sent his regards to all his 60's compratriats; Messrs Middleton, Vickers, Hall, etc..,

To reach the very top in todays athletics requires not only talent but complete dedication. Both John Gladwin and Stuart Paton have shown their commitment by changing occupations to give themselves more time for training. Mark Sinclair has gone one step further and, following a house move to Dorking is taking a 6 month sabatical to concentrate on his running. We wish all our dedicated young men the very best of good fortune in their endeavours.

Another member reaching for the top but in a somewhat different context is Don Anderson. Don has taken 6 weeks leave from his teaching post to undertake a training holiday in Kenya - At an altitude of 12000 Ft. We knew Don was determined to make our Southern League team!

Many of our regulars at Belgrave Hall have been asking after Bill Downey. Sadly soon after last years 25 by 1 mile relay Bill contracted a hepatic condition and has only recently resumed training. We wish him a speedy recovery.

In Memorium — MRS E R SAVAGE

Gordon Doubleday

In Memorium

MRS E R SAVAGE

We were saddened late in November to learn from Win Shepherd that her mother had died recently in hospital. Mrs Savage was in her 80's and had not enjoyed good health over the last few years. Nevertheless her interest in the Club never flagged and on a number of occasions during the last few years we were able to welcome her to our meetings. Married to Past President the late Dick Savage she was a great worker for the Club, travelling with and supporting our teams. She was one of a gallant band of ladies who, half a century ago, cut piles of sandwiches and brewed gallons of tea for Belgrave socials which followed Club Championships. A supporter of womens athletics she was a member of Selsonia AC now disbanded and was a Past President of Surrey County Womens AAA. Until the formation of our Ladies Section we were a mens Club but that did not prevent us from honouring her by making her a Vice President for years of service to Belgrave. A number of old friends from Belgrave attended the funeral to say 'Goodbye' to a fine lady. Our sympathies are extended to our other lady Vice President -her daughter Win- and the family.

Gordon Doubleday.

The Lupton Shuffle

C.S.

The Lupton Shuffle

At the Los Angeles Olympic Games of 1932 Tommy Green of Great Britain and Belgrave Harriers won the gold medal in the 50km walk. In the closing stages of a hard-fought race he beat the Latvian J. Dalinsch in a time of 4hrs 50mins 10 secs, after being nearly a minute down in third place at the three-quarter distance.

Tommy was one of Britain's two gold medallists at that Games (Tommy Hampson was the other at 800metres) and he returned to a hero's welcome. The press hung on his every word.

Prior to the 1933 London-Brighton Walk Tommy let it be known that he was out to be the first man to break the 8 hours barrier on the Brighton Road, and the Press got hold of the story.

The publicity clearly upset James M.K. Lupton of London Athletic Club who could not resist writing to the Editor of the West London Observer with his views on race walking. He put himself out on a most precarious limb by describing such walking as "professional shuffles" and ridiculing the supposition that anyone could legitimately walk from London to Brighton in under eight hours.

Well, Tommy won the race for the fourth time but failed by just 79 seconds to fulfill his target and prove Mr. Lupton wrong. And no one who saw Tommy Green walk competitively would question his mode of action.

Two years later Mr. Lupton was made to eat his words by the impeccable stylist Harold Whitlock who clocked 7:53:50 and went on to repeat Tommy Green's achievement by taking the 50km Olympic Gold Medal the following year in Berlin - again one of only two gold medals for Britain.

In 1957 the course was lengthened by ¾ mile to 53 miles 129 yards and another Olympic Gold Medallist-to-be, Don "Little Mouse" Thompson, set the sensational record of 7:35:12.

If James M.K. Lupton was famous for anything, it was setting himself up to be shot down by three of this country's greatest walkers.

Not to be outdone (and to make this story start and end on a Belgrave note) Past-President Ray Hall got in his own shot at Mr. Lupton in 1962 when he set a Stock Exchange record over the "Thompson" course of 7:58:38.

SPORTS CORRESPONDENCE

RACE WALKING, OR THE "PROFESSIONAL SHUFFLE."

To the Editor, "W.L.O."

Sir,—I notice in the Press that Mr. T. W. Green has expressed his determination to walk from the Clock Tower, Westminster, to Brighton Aquarium, a distance of 51 miles, 1607 yards, in eight hours in the Surrey Walking Club's race on September 9th.

It makes anyone that knows what really legitimate walking is roar with laughter to think that any human being could cover the London to Brighton distance in eight hours.

Fancy anyone walking a mile in six minutes, 22 seconds, the present so-called world's record, and eight miles, 438 yards, in the hour, both impossible feats.

This race walking has been going on for a great number of years. It is not walking, but what used to be known in the eighties as the "Professional Shuffle." I have seen most of the so-called champions for the last half century, and I can say that I have never seen any honest walking amongst them. It is not walking, but a mix up between a walk, a run, a trot and a shuffle.

It took A. F. H. Newton 5 hours, 53 minutes, 43 seconds on November 13th, 1924, to run the London to Brighton course, which at once makes any one walking the distance in eight hours an absurdity.

All the walking records standing on the books to-day are nothing but "Professional Shuffles," and were never accomplished by fair and legitimate walking.

It is a very great pity that the International Amateur Federation and the Amateur Athletic Association recognises this mode of progression as legitimate walking, but to those that know anything about walking, it is nothing but a burlesque.—Yours, etc.,

JAMES M. K. LUPTON. London Athletic Club,

WITH THE LADIES

Derek Jones

After the Euphoria of the summer season Cross Country Training got off to a very slow start, with only 4 runners turning up on occasions. However, events seem to have snowballed and whatever the weather, normally 12 girls (on one occasion 18) have turned up at Belgrave Hall on Saturday afternoons and Sunday mornings for a run over Wimbledon Common. Some of them positively dislike Cross Country Training but have persisted and have found that the training is keeping them fit. Circuit Training at Heathbrook Centre, due mainly to a late start also began slowly but more girls are coming along for training. We have had a recruitment drive going with some of the local schools and a few useful links have been forged. Some of the schools seem to think that it is a good thing that their children are benefiting from our training. Of the newcomers to Belgrave real promise has been shown in competition and training by Jackie Stone, Anita Davison, Kim Marsh, Julie Godfrey, Trudy Attrell, Sarah Wotherspoon, Sarah King, Sian Davies, Tania Morrison and Andrea Coore. Determination in training too has been shown by Nikki Marsh, Lisa Davison, Rachael Parrott, Christina Gauge, Amanda Grant and Mo O'Connell (until she was injured). Steady progress has been shown by sprinter Jessica Palmer who is one of those who dislikes Cross Country Training but looks better for the training and also competition and probably surprised herself by finishing 7th in the Club Cross Country Championships. Suzanne Jordan and Tracey Johnson have been fairly regular in their training but more determination is required in both traing and competition.

Unfortunately, the Senior Squad had been conspicuous by its absence but will be strengthened by the addition of Rosemary Honeychurch and Linda Sparks (regulars at Wimbledon and Heathbrook) Jackie Barclay has been a regular at Heathbrook but since moving house has found the trip to Wimbledon a bit far. Just to prove what a good all round athlete she is Jackie ran in the Surrey Road Relay and was second in the Club Cross Country Championships. Wendy Dunsford has not minded making the long trip from Harrow to train and compete on a number of occasions.

Although not a member of Belgrave for reasons which will become clear, 11 year old Julia McGrath has shown really exceptional promise, coming 4th in the Wandsworth Cross Country Championships, 11th in London Inter-Schools Cross Country and 7th in the London Inter-Boroughs. Julia has competed for us but will be moving to Birmingham shortly and we wish her well.

Pride of place must go to Gail Porter. Gail is now a triple champion and has shown determination and dedication in training. Her three wins were in the Wandsworth Schools Cross Country Championships (1st in 1981 and 3rd in 1980), the London Inter-Schools Cross Country Championships (7th in 1982) and the Junior Club Cross Country Champion (1st in 1982). All the races were won from the front and in the Wandsworth Schools at Morden Park she smashed the course record and finished well over a minute in front of the second placer.

From the beginning of 1983, we have had good attendances at cross country meetings and what with a number of parents showing interest and being prepared to come along in cold weather as well as finding that they have enjoyed each others company, the Belgrave contingent has grown. For instance, at the Mitcham AC/Prestige Meeting at Morden Park at the end of January, the Belgrave party totalled over 40 (compared with 9 in 1982). Also, the junior field in our own Cross Country Championships was the largest we have ever had. Some of the girls who have joined us have been shy and reserved but just by training and competing have gained in confidence and have surprised themselves with their own ability.

We now look forward to the summer season with some excitement and believe that with the additions to the squad we had last year, we are quite capable of gaining promotion to Division 2.

Just saying "thank you" seems a bit inadequate, but my thanks go to Harry Porter for his efforts in Cross Country Training and also in Circuit Training. Harry leads the Cross Country Pack Runs and is always urging the girls to do better and most have responded. My thanks also to Frank Simmons who although not always enjoying the best of health has been to most Wednesday and Sunday sessions and whose advice has been invaluable.

We still need help, however. So instead of sitting at home watching T.V. Saturday afternoons and Sunday mornings why not come along and give a hand?. We need people to help especially at home meetings.

NOTES ON TEAM SELECTION

NOTES ON TEAM SELECTION

It is sometimes believed that participation in any one of the British League matches will preclude selection from Southern League competition for the rest of the season. This is not the case. If an athlete does compete in a British League match he is only precluded from the next Southern League meeting. Therefore all team members who competed in last September's qualifying match will not be eligable for the first Southern League Match whereas all those who did not compete will be eligible. It is, however, possible for Southern League athletes to compete in a subsequent British League Match without penalty.

BELGRAVE FORM NEW COLTS AND BOYS LEAGUE.

BELGRAVE FORM NEW COLTS AND BOYS LEAGUE.

When the Surrey/Belgrave merger took place last year we had hoped to use the Surrey AC slot in the Ebbisham League to provide plenty of competition for our growing band of colts and boys. Mysteriously the Ebbisham League management decided that the Surrey slot should be relinquished. Not to worry we thought,Belgrave had been waiting to join for several years and so the vacancy would come to us anyway. Not on your nelly! We were passed over and so what to do? Well, to cut a short story even shorter, Derek Crookes came up with the idea of forming a new League based on the new all-weather track at Battersea. A quick advert in A.W. and we had a good enough response to get things going. The new League is to be known as the Thameside League- at least for this year. There are even more ambitious plans ahead. Several Clubs have indicated their interest in participating next year particularly if we include girls as well. Now that has the makings of a really good idea. Such a combined competition would create greater team spirit and would eliminate the expense of running separate teams to different venues. The GLC has been approached to see if they will support such a League and their initial response has been very encouraging We ask therefore that all colts and boys take full advantage of the chances offered by this new League and turn out in force for every match! YOUNG ATHLETES

This is the second season of the new management, mars bars and all and the following results show an improvement on overall and individual results far beyond our expectations. Before detailed analysis, I would like to thank all the young athletes for their efforts during the winter, running through often very tough conditions. I would like to thank Duncan, Reg, Ted, Rosemary and Claire for sharing the load, also the increasing band of parents for solving the transport logistics of ferrying indistinguishable boys and mud. I never thought I would attend a race where the support for the Bel-boys outnumbers even the 'host' clubs but it has happened regularly this winter. I am grateful for this and the friendly atmosphere that exits within which the boys have maintained often dramatic improvement while sustaining an obvious enjoyment of the sport.

Reports by Ian Horton.

COLTS

We know if we want to eventually boast strong teams in each age group we must build from the base and therefore perhaps my greatest satisfaction at the completion of this season is to record we have 25 members in the colts section, the majority of whom have been active throughout the winter often fielding 3 teams. We have enjoyed 'many firsts'. We have fielded the first micro colts team and ran the first Belgrave mini colts championship, won by Adrian Ross and Second Russel Isles, both of whom (at the age of 10) have already made significant impact in the U.13 races. Teddy Davies is already an experienced campaigner and two years their junior, even so the age competition must be awarded to Stewart Booth, who at 7 ran a seasoned race at Canvey Island.

The colts were the most prominent in striking firsts for the Bels, pride of place going to taking the Surrey League 1st division title, winning convincingly against distinguished clubs, including Herne Hill, Croydon, Ranelagh. The most satisfying aspect being that eight boys contributed to the scoring team, G. Allison, B. Leigh, J. Caines, D. Steward, G. Webb, D. Cunningham, A. Ross and P. Whiston.

The first division individual title was won by Ben Leigh. Gareth Allison won the club championships, gained a creditable 8th in the Surrey C.C. Championship and represented Surrey County to finish 41st in the Inter-Counties 'B' race.

From this base I am optimistic we can develop strong colts and boys teams next season.

BELGRAVE CROSSES INTO EUROPE

David.J.Mote.

BELGRAVE CROSSES INTO EUROPE

MARCH 27th 1983 - OSTENDE - WELLINGTON MEDLEY RELAYS.

HERMES CLUB OOSTENDE have been organising the Wellington Medley Relays for 27 years and for the last 3 years a team from Surrey AC has crossed the Channel to compete. Hearing of our merger (how news travels) a Belgrave team was invited to compete along with 15 International Clubs to make up a total field of 50 teams. The race comprises of 12 legs ranging from 300 to 1200m. Due to the large number of competitors (600 in our race alone) the venue is a racecourse on the outskirts of Ostende and the race is held in front of the main grandstand to provide good viewing for a maximum capacity crowd and for the TV audience which follows these races with avid interest.

The UK teams created much interest and apart from the Bels there were strong contingents from Blackheath and Aldershot.

Our race started at 4pm with John Barry contesting the first 800m leg and he brought us in contention in 8th place. Everyone excelled themselves and the excitement rose as John Gladwin moved us into 2nd place on the 6th leg. The remaining runners tried desperately to take us into the lead as the race hotted up. For the spectators it was unbearable as the lead changed hands and after some 20 minutes only 10 seconds seperated the first 8 teams. The final surge saw Blackheath take victory in 20.08.6 with Belgrave 6th in 20.15.4 just one place ahead of Aldershot.

It was great to see the team spirit as our top track speedmen lined with our middle distance specialists. We even had a small band of vocal supporters and thanks to the Couzens and Pritchards for their backing. And finally special thanks to Leo Coy and Bob Bridges who respectively managed the team and organised the travel arrangements.

This year was an integration year where the team got it's feet under the table. I believe with the potential we have we can go back next year and turn the table over. Why not come and join us??

David.J.Mote.

APRIL 9th 1983 - WIMBLEDON - BELGRAVE HAIG ROAD RELAY.

Up to seven days before the race we had the makings of a good team, one that would surely get us back into the first 9 places & hence qualify for the National 12 Stage Relay again. However once again it proved to be wishful thinking and during the ensueing week no less than 6 team changes and multiple variations to the running order had to be made. Injuries and illness accounted for most of the drop outs but not all, as even a race of this standard does not seem to have the attraction it once did in the days when people would give their back teeth to be in the team! However credit must go to those who still ran although one degree under.

The race itself was blessed with fine weather and a record entry and with fine opening runs on long & short legs by Olly Foote and Mark Sinclair it looked as though we would still make it. However it was not to be and we gradually dropped down the field to finish 23rd out of 41 teams some 6 places lower than in 1982 but some 2½ minutes faster! I do not propose to analyse the team performance in great detail but the highlight of the race for us was seeing John Gladwin well and truly back in action on the 6th stage in recording 15m31s, the second fastest on that leg and equal fourth fastest of the day.

Finally I would like to record my thanks to all those who helped to literally get the show on the road, from Bill Couzens who let me bully him into organising all the course markers-- once again a loyal band without whom the race just could not continue. To Clive Shippen and his equally able team who hardly ever see the race but who this year excelled themselves with a truly magnificent result sheet. Hope to see you all again next year!!!

Gordon Biscoe.
