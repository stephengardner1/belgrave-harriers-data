Column · page 3

Heartiest congratulations to J. F. King upon his recent marriage. Joe has been winning Sussex walking championships for many years past and has represented the Club with distinction on many occasions. We wish Mr. and Mrs. King the best of luck in their new venture.

* * * * *

We are glad to see Arthur Allum about again, apparently none the worse for his recent operation for mastoid. It came as a severe blow to us to learn that our Cross-country International had been rushed away to hospital to undergo a critical operation. Happily his strong constitution and bodily fitness enabled him to pull through quickly; and now he is keenness itself, and anxious to be up and doing. He has great hopes of getting fit in time for the "Southern"; and it goes without saying that we all trust he will be successful in his efforts; and that his enforced stay in hospital will not prove an insuperable handicap.

* * * * *

Owing to the resignation from the Committee of K. B. O'Kell, who is now living at Dagenham and finds it impossible to keep in close touch with Club affairs, a vacancy occurred on the Committee. It was also decided to fill the vacancy caused by the election of H. Parker to the position of Asst. Hon. Secretary, thus becoming an ex-officio member of Committee.

To fill these two positions, as empowered by the Club rules, H. E. Footer and H. W. Shields were appointed members of Committee.

In resigning his position, Ken O'Kell, ex Club captain and champion, makes it quite clear that there is no diminution in his interest and enthusiasm for the Club, but he feels that with so many keen men anxious to serve the Club, and able to do so, and with himself uncertain of being a regular attendant at Committee meetings, it is only fair that he should withdraw in favour of someone else not handicapped in the way he is.

No doubt we shall have an occasional scheme from him as in the past just to show that his thoughts are still with us, and we can be certain that he will show up when the Championships come along!

* * * * *

J. E. Flower, H. E. Footer and A. W. Penny were recently honoured by being invited to run as members of Mr. E. J. Holt's team versus Cambridge University. The race took place over 7½ miles of very heavy country and was won by the visiting team—largely due to brilliant running by two of ours, Footer and Penny, who finished equal first. Flower also scored for the winning team, and so our triumvirate showed the Cambridge men that our colours were worthily worn; and our men's performances in keeping with the reputation our cross-country men have been building up for some years past.

* * * * *

Congratulations to Messrs. D. Martin, G. R. Chantler, J. E. Barnett and E. Pointer, who formed our team which won the Surrey County Youths' Race at Wimbledon on December 3rd.

* * * * *

We have pleasure in welcoming to membership F. J. Bankhead, A. Beavan, G. E. Chaffin, C. F. Clark, R. B. Gilson, J. Hewitt, E. W. Hayward, W. H. Piggott, A. R. Wright, and extend to them our usual good wishes and hopes for future successes.

* * * * *

You'll be along to the Boxing Day Meeting, at Battersea Park Track? We are always glad to see members whose active days, perhaps, are over; or who are unable to find the time for training, and this meeting of ours gives a splendid opportunity for re-union.

All active men are urged to send along their entries at once to the Hon. Secretaries concerned.

* * * * *

The Hon. Treasurer has Club vests, ties, badges, etc., for sale as per notice in the Club handbook.

* * * * *

The Christmas season would appear to be a very fine time to make your contribution to the "Belgrave Building Fund." The number of halfpennies is gradually growing, but out Hon. Treasurer won't be happy till he receives your little bit!

* * * * *

We congratulate F. A. Arrowsmith upon his recent appointment as Hon. Secretary of the University of London Athletic Union. Arrowsmith has represented us creditably on several occasions in past seasons, and it hoped that his secretarial work will not prevent him from turning out for us during the next track season, although the demands on his services by the University are bound to be heavy. He was entered in the Sussex County Team at the last Inter-Counties Meeting, and still has plenty of time in which to gain further honours.

WITH THE RUNNERS.

Micky Walker

"Micky Walker" commences this month's comments on racing exploits with a description of

THE ROUND-ILFORD RELAY.

Once again the Belgrave Harriers have proven their capabilities in producing yet another winning team in this year's Ilford Road Relay. Despite adverse weather conditions, the Belgrave boys tore up the Ilford course from start to finish in one long burst of brilliant running.

A stiffish wind was making itself felt as Carter took off on the first relay, but nothing deterred, he went forward to present his team companions with the comfortable margin of a 200 yards lead, and establishing for himself the very pleasing time of 19 min. 25 sec. The challenge was then taken up by F. Webb who, let it be noticed, accomplished the not unfair time of 20 mins., under the handicap of making his own pace. A surprise was created in the third relay in the shape of F. Shields, a junior of no small merit who pulled out a remarkable run in 19 min. 51 sec. The name of this promising lad will not easily be forgotten in the inevitable after-the-race "inquest."

C. Mains ran well with 19 min. 40 sec. to his credit, as also did J. E. Flower, Shaw and A. Taylor, their times being 20 min. 26 sec., 20 min. 38 sec., 20 min. 23 sec. respectively. W. Rice produced some of his old form which, however, was not too aged to enable him to secure the useful time of 19 min. 40 sec. Intense interest was caused in the race by H. E. Footer and A. Penny, two of the club's most prominent men, whose keen but friendly rivalry has always spurred each to greater things. Bounding with his customary zest, Footer made ground with every stride, and it was not surprising to learn that his last year's record was scattered to the four winds, with the vastly improved time of 18 min. 58 sec. to replace it. Last relay, and Penny keyed up to beat his rival at all costs. Running like one possessed, he reached the last quarter mile to the accompaniment of enthusiastic cries from numerous supporters, doing much to urge him on to even greater efforts. What a run! He finished faster than he started, showing the exceptional and almost incredible time of 18 min. 46 sec., thereby smashing the standing record by 4 sec.

Altogether a most satisfying win, marked by many very meritorious individual performances, and a collective effort which gained further prestige for the Club, and an addition to the Club sideboard in the shape of the newly-awarded Cup, of which we become first holders.

BELGRAVE HARRIERS v. EALING HARRIERS.

The Hon. Editor fills the breach and writes on:—

BELGRAVE HARRIERS v. EALING HARRIERS.

While our first choice were away at Ilford winning the Relay promoted by the local Club, our remaining strength entertained the Ealing Harriers to a race over five miles at Wimbledon.

Len Rollins had been detailed to write an account of this sporting match and had agreed to do so—nay, had volunteered—but next day, as we wandered through the highways and byways of Surrey (and whenever Footer led, the roadways!) he resigned his commission. It may be that the caustic enquiries asking how it was possible for man finishing 35th to describe the progress of a race had something to do with his action. Perhaps the Pride of the Rollins, or the Curse of the Footers, entered into it. Could it have been that he had a premonition of the following week when his friends sent him home from the Dance in odd shoes, one black and one tan; or was it just his natural slackness asserting itself? We must cease speculation, however, and bemoan the fact that we should have been deprived of what, we are sure, would have proved a model literary contribution. Having given the inner history of this affair, in strict confidence, of course, we will now pass on and become official once again.

We congratulate our visitors most heartily, not only upon winning a close scoring match, but also upon having the first three men home. It was a most enjoyable fixture and we look forward to the return date on Ealing's course. W. Mitchell showed something of his old form in finishing fourth, and a return to his running of a few years back, when he was Club Champion, should greatly strengthen our team. Ring and Saunders again attracted attention by their prominent placings, whilst Frank Tame and H. J. Fletcher also showed that they will have to be considered later in the season.

INTER-CLUB RACE.

Arthur Penny

Vice-Captain Arthur Penny gives his commentary on the first of the Inter-Club runs.

INTER-CLUB RACE. ASHCOMBE A.C., THAMES VALLEY H., BELGRAVE H.

A good crowd faced the starter on the occasion of the first of the Inter-Club runs held from our headquarters. Immediately H. S. Cook took the lead, closely followed by Carter, Jack Flower and W. S. Mitchell, all of the Bels. Going out towards the village, Cohen of Ashcombe crept up and took the lead when crossing the Golf Links. The runners were now beginning to thin out, but T.V.H. had a good crowd of their men with ours at this point.

Beverley Brook was passed and the Kingsmere reached, and here one of our men, Cecil Mains, went to the front, but appeared to be doubtful of the course. From here to the finish he was pressed by Cohen, who eventually won by a few yards. Well run, Cohen and Mains! Bicknell was the first "Valley" man to finish (9th); but as the Bels. placed their fifteen to score in the first 18 positions, we ran out very easy winners of the race.

Our "Juniors" ran well, and with conscientious training will greatly add to our strength.

H. D. Brown well deserved his handicap win, showing minutes improvement over previous running. Harry Shields, in finishing fifth in the scratch race, deserved some sort of reward for this, probably his best yet, and was placed second in the handicap, with Bill Mitchell, our second man to finish, third, with an allowance of 1 min. 45 sec.

THE SOUTH OF THE THAMES "JUNIOR."

Harry Shields

Harry Shields carries on with the good work and gives us his impression of

THE SOUTH OF THE THAMES "JUNIOR."

On Saturday, November 12th, three teams and a number of supporters made their way to Epsom for the South of the Thames District race. Some of those who represented us last year were no longer eligible, but on this occasion our "A" team finished third, one place better than last year.

All our "A" team men got well away with the leaders, Frank Tame, in particular, doing well until a strained leg caused him to fall back. Eventually Jack Fletcher, showing fine judgment of pace, led our team in, finishing 13th, closely followed by Shaw 14th, Cecil Penny 18th, and Walker 20th. This shows fine packing, although perhaps a little further back than we could desire. Ring, formerly walking, showed up well, and among the "B" and "C" teams could be seen many who are certain to carry our colours with credit in future races.

Both the winners, Reading A.C., and the second team, Royal Navy and Marines (Portsmouth) ran exceedingly well, both teams finishing their four men inside the first 11 position—splendid

Our boys did well to take third place with such strong opposition.

RUN v. METROPOLITAN POLICE A.A.

The Walking Hon. Secretary contributes a short account of the

RUN v. METROPOLITAN POLICE A.A.

We had a good muster of Police visitors, and our team was composed in the main of runners who have yet to make their places in our senior team secure. Two notable exceptions to this generalization are, of course, Club ex-champions in J. E. Flower and W. S. Mitchell.

Our runners were soon to the fore, once the race started. Harry Shields was in the lead for some distance with Jack Flower a few yards behind; but from the Windmill home they strode in together with "Bill" Mitchell a few yards in the rear. Then came the first of our visitors in H. Dyke, a Kent County runner. Our remaining runners packed in well to secure for us a very easy win scoring 10 aside.

MATCH v. EALING H., KENSINGTON A.C. AND MITCHAM A.C.

Joe Flower

Joe Flower has his say regarding the

MATCH v. EALING H., KENSINGTON A.C. AND MITCHAM A.C.

It is hoped that this fixture will be supported in a better manner in the future. The Ealing A.C., from whose headquarters this race was run, are a fine crowd of sportsmen and give our boys a very warm welcome both on the track and on the country. They have a great respect for the Belgrave Harriers so in future I appeal to our runners, especially new members, to make enquiries of their officers as to ways and means of travelling to the venue. This applies to all our away fixtures. It encourages the club spirit among ourselves, and gives our runners the opportunity of trying different country, which it is only way of building up a strong team of cross-country runners.

To get back to the job of reporting the race. Forty-eight runners turned out, eight only being Belgrave, although in extenuation it must be pointed out that we had another fixture elsewhere on this day. It was decided to hold two races in one, that is to say, three clubs competed in a 7 aside scoring match, and four clubs in a 5 aside competition. In both events we were last, which can hardly be termed creditable. The most pleasing feature from our point of view was the forward running of A. F. Mitchell, who finished fourth in the race and was first man home for us. When one realizes that he was only just beaten by Edwards, of Ealing, who was fourth the previous week in the North of the Thames Junior race, we can say our man did very well for one who has had little racing experience.

Ealing H. won both matches easily; and I would like to remind the Bels' that this is the third time in succession that they have beaten us. In fairness to them we must turn out in greater numbers and strength next time and then—well, wait till then!

In conclusion, on behalf on those present, I would like to thank the Ealing H. for their hospitality and a good run.

CRANFORD INTER-CLUB RACE.

And so on to the

CRANFORD INTER-CLUB RACE.

The T.V.H., Ashcombe and Belgrave H. inter-club run took place on Saturday, November 25th, resulting in a win for the club by a narrow margin of three points. It is worthy of note that of the 15 representatives of the club, 10 to score, many were in prominence at the finish. The course was in typical cross-country condition and eminently suitable to our men, for Carter made the tape in very convincing fashion, with 100 yards or so to spare. Mitchell, showing his old form, dropped into third place, with those three promising juniors, C. Penny, F. Walker and H. Shields, who seem never to be far behind, making a useful backing—their place being 6th, 7th and 10th respectively.

THE HON. SECRETARY CALLING.

W. L. Bayliss

Hearty congratulations to our team which won the Surrey County 3 miles Youths' race at Wimbledon on Saturday, December 3rd. These youngsters have been very earnest in their training and well deserved their honours. As expected, D. Martin got well away with the leaders and running in very good style finished third in the team race. He was well supported by G. Chantler, who ran well to finish 4th, followed by J. Barnett, 8th, and E. Pointer, 13th. The result should certainly be a tonic to our Surrey Juniors who will compete in the Junior Championship (over 18 and under 21) at Putney Heath on January 21st.

Now what of our 7 miles Championship for the "Blackstaffe Shield" on December 17. Arthur Allum, much to our regret and his disgust, will probably be a spectator. Arthur has just recovered from a serious operation to his ear and will not commence training for a few weeks. Footer and Penny, who are both running well, will no doubt be making the pace hot. They finished together at the head of the field in a match versus Cambridge University on November 26th. I suspect, however, that J. E. Flower, who is now commencing to shew his old form, and C. T. Carter will be troubling them. Then what of W. W. Mitchell? He is now training in earnest and if on form can worry the best men we have. With such a crowd of good boys as we have, anything may happen. Mains and Shaw tell me they are both keen on making a good show. This sounds encouraging with the "South of Thames" race in view. The form of H. Shields and A. J. Mitchell is very promising and I have no doubt that both of them will be giving us a pleasant surprises shortly. I look forward to a very interesting race.

The approach of Christmas reminds us of our Boxing Day Meeting. The usual handicaps will be run and sprinters are specially urged to try and support this half Athletic, half Social Function. Please make an effort to turn up and meet your Club friends the morning after the night before.

Entries can be posted or handed to W. L. Bayliss, the Hon. Secretary.

WITH THE WALKERS.

The Winter Season has opened quite successfully for us and we have certainly not gone back on last year's performances. Two of the three opening races have found us taking second place to Enfield A.C. in the scratch team races, but we have no doubt this state of affairs will alter as the season progresses in the same way as it did last year. We have had one winning and two second handicap teams in three races despite rapidly decreasing marks, and this is very satisfactory.

Tommy Green has led the field home once (at Enfield); and finished a creditable second in our own Open event. Len Fletcher has not succeeded in being in the first three yet this season in Open Event, but the New Year should find him back to his old form and all the better for a steady start.

In the individual handicap races H. Latter earned himself distinction (and no doubt a considerably reduced handicap!) by winning our own event, whilst J. G. Coleman did well in taking third prize at Enfield.

Several of our "Juniors" are showing considerably improved form. In particular our "Veteran Junior," Charlie Speechley, calls for attention, as does also A. H. Field. Many others have also improved upon their previous best and the result of our "Junior" Club Championship for the "Horwood" Cup should make interest reading.

Feature · page 19

ON OUR STROLLERS AND STROLLING.

It has been suggested that our readers might like to know of the exploits of a band of intrepid Belgravians who brave the elements on Sundays to catch a glimpse of Autumnal beauty, to feel the wind tugging at the hair roots, and to know that joy of living a little more intensely than the daily round permits in a work-a-day world.

But first let us state our very deep sense of gratitude to Bert Footer upon the heroic self-sacrifice which makes these Strolls possible. Our usual meeting-place is Earlsfield; not, as the uncharitable might suppose, because Bert lives nearby, but because he realizes that should the rendezvous be fixed at some more distant point, he might possibly delay the party. Rather than risk this he prefers the meeting-place to be within two minutes' walk of his doorstep. What nobility of thought! What consideration for others! What an example for those who never stop to think of one's conduct in relation to one's neighbours!

In all humility, I should like to ask "where would we be without Footer?" "On the footpaths, through the fields, away from the roads, enjoying ourselves," the cynic might answer, but we must not be too hard on our B.F. He has his limitations, his shortcomings, and there are moments of pathos such as when he asks to be lifted up above the hedges so that he, too, may see the rolling countryside and the wonderful panorama of Surrey landscapes. It is then that we realize that he is always condemned to be on a lower plane than ourselves. Stilts are his only salvation.

He reads a map in the same easy facile fashion he would a sonnet by Sir Rabindranath Tagore in the original; and believes that maps were published as badges of authority rather than as guides to wayfarers. Really a remarkable fellow whom, despite his idiosyncrasies, we are all proud to know.

And then there's H. W. Shields, who may be described as an antidote to Footer. Harry has a stentorian voice of which the thunder clouds are jealous; tremendous boots—seven-league ones straight from mythology—, and a gargantuan appetite. (The original Non-Stop. And what Variety! Onions and oranges. Bread, butter and blackberries!). He also possesses a broad grin, youth, a desire to argue (to put it mildly), immense reserves of energy, is an avowed misogynist (we have our doubts!) and has a mission in life—that of keeping H. E. Footer, whose name has, I believe, occurred before in these coloumns, in subjection and to curb his tendency towards Mussolini emulation. Should you want to know more of Shields, talk to him gently, take 10 paces away, and wait for the answer! Someone has said, not in any malicious manner, I assure you, that Harry may develop into a second Footer. He certainly has running potentialities, and is the equal of anyone in zeal for the Club weal.

Another regular member of our parties is Len Rollins, who introduced Marcel waves, plus-fours and the odd fashion of wearing odd shoes into the Club. In addition to Sunday walks, he is addicted to nocturnal wanderings and thinks nothing of walking from Wimbledon to Cricklewood (say via Walham Green) in the small hours of the morning. Rather eccentric you may think, but then it takes all sorts to make a world.

Then there's Micky Walker, who is smiling when you meet him in the morning and smiling when you leave him in the evening, and spends 60 minutes of every intervening hour in exercising his penetrating, infectious laugh. If I hadn't met Harry Shields I might have commented on Micky's appetite! But now I won't trouble.

Now meet smiling F. G. Ford ("Felicitous Freddy"), eyes twinkling, ever-cheerful, a song on his lips, as happy as the days are getting short, aren't they? I'm afraid Fred's winning smile would find him unplaced, but as I'm playing with fire it would appear advisable to change the subject!

There's Tom Cotton (complete with pipe) giving us the benefit of a much wider repertoire of song than we ever knew he possessed; and Alf Miller and Alf Taylor, who croon about Minnie, who's tall and skinny, and other ladies of their acquaintance. And there's Frank Haydon, who dearly loves to get his teeth into Footer (I write figuratively, of course. There are various reasons why it would be impossible physically), and "Doug" Sloper, who comes along when Circumstances (very charming ones, I admit) permit.

Anyone reading all the foregoing must wonder if the party has any saving grace, if there are any Belgravians who raise the tone of the party, give it distinction and respectability, create prestige and generally tend to keep the standard up when, but for their presence, it would inevitably sink lower and lower. The answer is—Yes! And who are these gentlemen of eminence, you may ask, whose nobility of character and high standards of honour and decency alone prevent the waves from engulfing, etc., etc.? They are the Hon. Editors of the "Belgravian," that celebrated gazette whose informative comments and brilliant journalism are eagerly awaited by an enthusiastic public every month, or two months, or even quarter (Advt.). They are our saviours, our defenders, our benefactors, and so on, and so on, and so on, culminating in a really devastating peroration.

And yet I'm not quite satisfied in my own mind about one of these two gentlemen, Frank Webb, formerly "Spider," because of his name! now "Super-Snake," because of his habits. No, on second thoughts I'm definitely not satisfied. It may be his employment ("Here's the rent, and would you like a cup of tea, Mr. Webb!"); or his nature; and I can't forget that we heard very little of that holiday at Paignton. And very few have seen the photographs that were taken! No, Frank, I'm afraid that upon mature consideration you must take your place with the rest!

So far I have dealt only with personalities and have left myself very little space to devote to our actual strolls and experiences. Let it be said that we cling slavishly to the Footer-tradition of doing one lap, or two small circles, as a prelude to the day's walk. As proof of what I have written about Harry Shields' appetite, let me mention the seat which gave way under his increasing weight and deposited him and two other gentlemen on Mother Earth. Then there was the disgraceful incident on Colley Hill when Harry most effectively put the damper on Bert Footer, and the occasion when "Doug" Sloper caused us to wade through the icy River Mole; and the attempt at self-strangulation by Footer (why does this name intrude so often?). using his haversack for the purpose, and many other memorable episodes.

Now to be serious. Any member who would like to join in these parties is cordially invited to do so. Just ask any of the regular party for the next Sunday's arrangements and spend a day with "Nature in the raw," not to mention Mascot Footer, eccentric Leonard, treble-forte-voiced Harry; Alfs one, two and three; T.C.; F.R.W.; D.A.S., and the others; and so add to and benefit from the sociability and Club spirit which it is our earnest desire to maintain and extend.

Column · page 21

ON THE SOCIAL SIDE.

A very successful Social was held on November 5th, when we had as guests old friends in Croydon H., Epsom H., Mitcham A.C., and new ones in the R.A.F. from Northolt. Many members of the last-named three clubs stayed to tea and the dance following; and, of course, there was the usual good crowd of our own fellows and their friends.

The dance was only interrupted once, although the ominous date and an unconfirmed rumour that Footer was to be publicly burnt had led me to expect more. The only "turn" was a series of turns by two gentlemen announced as "Castor and Oil, Continental Tumblers." They looked suspiciously like two Club members, "Nick" Carter and "Paddy" Donnelly—one clad in the height of fashion — plimsolls, football shorts, "dicky," "tails" and "topper"; the other in shorts and a tennis shirt with more rents than Frank Webb could collect in six months. The act consisted of some burlesque acrobatics, a slow motion boxing contest and similar fooling; performed all the while with serious expressions and with never a suspicion of a smile! The audience, on the contrary, laughed themselves into helplessness!

I am still ignorant as to who was Castored and who was Oiled; and also from which continent the performers came, although I suspect it is not very far from "The World's End!"

Our first Dance of the season was held at Wandsworth Baths on November 16th and proved a success. The attendance was not, perhaps, as great as some of those we have held in the past at Wandsworth Town Hall; but the arrangement of tables and chairs around the dancing area was appreciated, and all the dancers seemed to enjoy themselves to the full. Those who did not attend on this occasion will have another opportunity on February 15th.

Whilst on the subject of dances, an extra Club Dance has been arranged for Saturday, March 11th, 1933, profits from which will probably be allocated to the Belgrave Building Fund. The Dance is to take place at "The Queensberry Hall," Harrington Road, South Kensington. This hall is easily accessible from all parts of London. Buses from Putney, Clapham Junction and Hammersmith pass the door; while the District and Metropolitan Railways, and "Piccadilly Tube" serve the nearby South Kensington Station. Further details will appear later, meanwhile bear the following dates in mind :—

Jan. 28—Social at Wimbledon at which, I am told, "The Nonsuch Girls" will re-appear. Feb. 15—Dance at Wandsworth Baths. Mar. 11—Dance at Queensberry Hall, S. Kensington.

Any enquiries to W. A. Rice, 36 Ascalon Street, S.W.8.
