Editorial · page 3

COMMENT

Half a century has now gone by since W.H.Hare produced the first edition of the Club's gazette in April 1925. The opening lines of "The Belgravian" No. 1 read:

"It has long been the desire of many of those of our members mostly concerned with the management and welfare of the Belgrave Harriers that we might have a periodical publication whereby the activities of the Club could be more closely presented to our members and friends thus increasing their interest and developing the feeling of good comradeship which is so great a part of the Club's objectives."

Now, 50 years on, surely we can say that those original aims have been attained and kept to. The gazette's publication, thanks to successive Editors, has added much to our athletics and it is more than coincidence that nearly all of Belgrave's major club successes have come since that first issue. Of course, no one would claim that the regular appearance of "The Belgravian" has been directly responsible for any of our past triumphs, only individual determination and effort can cause that. But it has helped to provide us with heart. It has drawn us together and given us that comradeship during our competitive years and after, from which success can spring.

Long may it continue to do so.

W.H. Hare

You can't keep the COLEMAN family away from a Belgrave Open Walk. JOE was scudding around the course on a motor cycle and it is good to see him about again and doing the important job of judging. Brother LEN made the journey from Northern Ireland and was promptly snapped up to do some recording. Whilst waiting for the tail-enders to finish Len had to say something so he gave an account of how he beat Joe in a A.A.A. Seven Mile Championship in the thirties.

Making a welcome visit south over the Christmas period was JOHN BLAIR-FISH who led our small team home over at Blackheath. On Boxing Day he teamed up with his brother PETER and sister to enter the Chipstead Pram Race and finished a worthy second behind one JOHN THRESHER.

Veteran walker LEN EVANS has been in hospital for an operation and it was good to see him around at Imber Court on the day of the M.P.A.A. Open Seven. He looked remarkably well and no doubt when he's given the OK he will be in action again.

STAN DAVIES is now living at Haverhill (Suffolk) and is hoping to get in touch with WILL SPENCER who is also retired in that County. They used to enjoy Saturday runs with FRANK WEBB and Co.

Several visitors managed to call in to CHARLIE SMART's home and see him before Christmas. He is progressing reasonably well after his illness and is determined to throw away those walking sticks eventually. - Might be an idea to hold on to them Chas., you're going to need something to prop you up if you get through all that Christmas booze too quickly. A nice thought from the "Dukes Joggers" was to present him with a book about antiques.

Congratulations to CYRIL GITTINS on becoming a Grade I Timekeeper. Although his first allegiance is to Queens Park H he rarely misses one of our Battersea meetings where his services are of great value to us. He is also an active walker (and runner, when the occasion arises!) and serves on a number of committees.

We are pleased to learn that HARRY HATHWAY is making good progress after a successful operation on his hip. He takes regular walks and hopes to be timekeeping again this summer. In our last issue it was stated that Harry was not a member. This is not so as in addition to being an S.L.H. stalwart he is also a Belgrave member. Our apologies to you Harry together with our best wishes for your continued progress.

REG. HOPKINS (young Reg that is) and JACKIE are to be congratulated on their recent engagement. Jackie faithfully supported our track and field team at each league event last season, even travelling down to Exeter. We look forward to renewing her acquaintance this summer and issue an invitation to all other girl friends and wives to come and support the team.

Whilst on the subject of the Hopkins family, we must mention how pleased we are to see SUSAN jogging at Battersea Park on a Sunday morning, a much prettier sight than brother Reg.

Everyone will be pleased to know that HARRY SHIELDS continues to make progress and a recent check-up showed a marked improvement. Not only do we see him at various meetings but he is always doing a job and although retired from work he has found many new interests. You just can't keep chaps like Harry down.

At the A.G.M. last autumn LAURIE O'HARA and GEORGE PIDDINGTON were elected Life Members whilst BERNARD MERRY, STEVE WHITE and ALAN MEAD became Vice-Presidents.

FROM THE PRESIDENT - INTERIM COMMENT

RAY HALL

FROM THE PRESIDENT - INTERIM COMMENT

Most of you know I work in the Stock Exchange, where pessimism and euphoria go hand in hand, so after five months as President I would like to pass a few comments or observations.

The work of the Club seems to fall on a certain few people, some elected officers and others who are always available when called upon. But these willing workers are rarely thanked or named or remembered by the members in general. When I mentioned that I would appeal in the 'Belgravian' for the future commitments of the Club, the census of the workers was, "You're wasting your time." But I cannot believe a club of our size and ability has such apathy. There must be many members who could or would help if asked.

For the past several years we have taken space in the Merton Show. This takes place in Morden Park. Last year we raised over £100 profit with a bottle and jar stall. This year the show will take place over two days, Sunday August 24th from 12.00 to 18.00hrs and Monday (Late Summer Holiday) 25th from 10.00 to 18.30hrs. (the times are in the modernists idiom). So those few I mentioned earlier will have to give up their whole weekend and holiday because the stall will have to be erected on the Saturday. If you can assist in any way, help with transport, give bottles or jars, help erect or spend a little time behind the counter, perhaps you will give half a day to help. Please get in touch with John Baker or myself. Don't leave it to those few I've already mentioned.

Personally, I was disappointed (or would the word disgusted do?) at our turn-out for the South of Thames Senior at Swindon. Only three competitors turned out and they were outnumbered by Belgrave Officials, six if I'm included. The excuses were pathetic and one wonders whether the work involved is worth it. You must give, in return for the work done, some enthusiasm and support. Belgrave cannot live on history. The team spirit must come from the grass roots. In my heart I feel so sorry for Gordon Biscoe who works so hard and gets very little support. Please apologise to him for letting him down. The first feelings I had were to write an obituary for the cross country section. Even support for the Southern has dropped (only seven in the senior race) and it could be that we will find ourselves in division 2 of the Surrey Cross Country League at the end of next season. Gordon was not the only one to have little support. Clive Shippen sent out 60 cards concerning a league fixture for the youngsters - two turned out!

Included with this 'Belgravian' is the notice of an E.G.M., to vote on the proposal to increase the subscriptions. We, like all organisations, are suffering from inflation. Just think of the increases in costs of electricity gas, water, rates (two minutes silence please) that will be coming during the year. We as a Club must be half a step ahead of the game. In the past few weeks the Club has booked tracks for the summer season and some of the charges have risen by 60%. Also in the near future it is hoped to improve the facilities at Belgrave Hall. At present the Building Committee are working on proposals and costs and these will be put before the Main Committee soon. It's agreed by everybody that our money should be spent on our main asset Belgrave Hall before anything else. I don't agree with a Past President who I heard to pass the remark,"If you improve the Hall it will be used only by a few old runners on Saturday afternoons." That remark was destructive and not constructive. We must look forward and not back. But the Club must at all times be financially viable. To the reader I will ask one question. "Do you personally in any way support fund raising within the Club (i.e. football tickets etc.). Sorry if I made you blush. If you don't agree with my comments come along on April 21st and state your reasons. Come and stand up and be counted. continued on page 19 ........

CHAS. MANNING

points

Its time to chase a new leader. "Biscoe must go". As the instigator of the most abortive attempt to get runners out by the introduction of a points competition this was a hoot. It was the sloppiest bit of legislation dreamt up by a bloke whats got marbles instead of the usual grey stuff in his head. It makes the the Social Contract look like it was iron law. Its another sop to the good runner at the expense of his inferiors.

Now, this is how it should be done. Fairly to give everyone an equal chance. The season starts with the Yacht '3' and/or the '5¾'. Bill Lucas produces, in his inimitable and efficient (forget Peter North) style a handicap for nearly every Bel who's still warm. This list needs simply to be divided into three equal sections. Secondly the number of races; let's say for example 10 are nominated. Then, the first third start the season from scratch - the middle third are given 1 point per race, i.e. they start off with 10 points. The third group are given 2 points and consequently start off with 20 points.

All this means everyone starts off on equal terms. I would suggest that a further point per race be given to all Vets. and a bonus of 5 extra for me because I thought of it.

As far as administration goes it simply means that the handicap number is added on the big table that has to be prepared anyway from the first race. That's it.

So Gordon, make way for a more virile,dedicated, far-seeing man such as Doug. Maughan or John Plummer. Get in an unselfish worker like Doug. Jardine or Jim Rimmer. What's wrong with the intellectual talents of Paul Pringle and why can't Eddie Thorp or Alan Black display their talents of leadership? Remember - you've got a bloody good Vice-Captain. Doesn't he deserve better?

Veteran CHAS. HENN continues to run well over the country, year after year. One of the secrets of his success is now out, however. Contrary to normal advice about mixing cross-plys with radials, Chas. has been wearing studs on one foot and ripples on the other when the going is heavy!

As the cross country season draws to a close we must thank BOB TAYLOR for another winter of course marking. This onerous task, carried out in inclement weather as often as not, goes largely unrecognised by most of our runners. When travelling up to the Hall for the afternoon's race it is worth remembering that Bob is already out on the Common setting out the flags. And long after the winner has crossed the finishing line and the winter evening closes in, he will still be at work gathering up the markers.

APRIL 1950 OUR OWN HEADQUARTERS .....

It is said - "that he who plants an avenue of trees, cannot, in the nature of things, hope to enjoy them in their grandeur" - but it was a very happy occasion and a most gratifying result to the labours of all Belgravians responsible for it, when our popular Hon. Secretary told the Special General Meeting held at Belgrave Hall on Thursday, March 16th, that Belgrave Hall was now our own freehold property. What many of us years ago dreamed of, even dared to think possible, has now been achieved.

To Harry Hare, the originator of the idea, and others too numerous to mention, who helped to make it possible - we say - fervently - thank you, and we know they feel fully repaid for their efforts. They now hope that the wheels of officialdom will not turn too slowly and that in the near future we shall obtain a permit for rebuilding a Hall that will be a fitting and lasting monument to the greatness of Belgrave. Then, and not till then, will they, the men who achieved so much, so unselfishly, sit back and observe complacently - the dream that finally came true.

THE ENGLISH CROSS COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP, BEACONSFIELD, 1935 .......

A fortnight previously we had surrendered our Southern title to Surrey A.C. and whilst we had plenty of confidence that this result would be reversed, and that Salford would have to yield to us this time, we were not so optimistic as to think that Birchfield, holders for the previous seven years with eight internationals entered in the team, would be humbled and forced into fourth position! However, we anticipate. Let's start at the beginning.

Two hundred and ninety-five runners thundered from the starting pens, and in the first 50 yards Shaw and Shields had nasty falls over two prostrate Ealing men. They picked themselves up and had all the field before them. The first lap of one mile was soon covered, and then out into the country on the first lap of three miles of fast, undulating park land, with our boys fairly well placed.

At four miles we had another view of the runners. Jim Ginty was fourth at this point, and our other men all seemed to be weighing up their opponents and running strongly. An attempt to get an idea of the scoring was doomed to failure owing to the speed at which the men passed. We could see, however, that Surrey A.C.'s sixth man was behind our eighth. Salford had three men prominent, but where were the others? Birchfield seemed well beaten even here. Currie the Midland champion, had retired at 1½ miles, and they had two men in the first bunch, the rest nowhere! However, we have been deceived before by the wearing of white vests and so were not unduly jubilant.

At seven miles it was obvious that, bar accidents, victory was ours. Ginty had fallen back a little and was running with Bert Footer, Dan Patience being a few yards in the rear - all in the first 25 places.

Over the last lap many places were gained, our Taylor-Shields-Carter trio, in particular, pegging back many opponents. In the last mile our boys gave all they had, and we had the remarkable scoring sequence of 11th, 14th, 15th 18th, 19th, 20th places.

It must have been a proud moment when Bert Footer realised that one of his greatest ambitions had come to pass, and that he had led home a Champion team- not by a narrow,fluky margin - but with 56 points to spare. 1. Belgrave H (Footer 11, D.Patience 14, Ginty 15, Taylor 18, Carter 19, Shields 20) 97pts; 2. Salford H 154pts; 3. Surrey AC 156pts. A.A.H.

Richardson shows us how

DECEMBER 14th 1974 - WIMBLEDON - CLUB 7½ MILES CROSS COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP.

In heavy conditions that slowed down this year's times, second-claimer Bob Richardson won our 7½ mile race at a canter from Gerry North who sustained a heavy fall but nevertheless retained his championship. Fine running came from Colin Pearson who gained his first ever Club championship medal over the country. Also outstanding were John Davies and young Simon Holmes, whose steady and regular training over the past few months is beginning to pay dividends. New member John Dudman was another to run well, opening his account quite promisingly. A close handicap win went to Alan Mead who, amidst the mud, managed to rise above his regular rivals.

BELGRAVE AND THE 'BRIGHTON'

JOHN KEOWN

BELGRAVE AND THE 'BRIGHTON'

JOHN KEOWN writes

On Saturday 6th September 1975 Surrey Walking Club will promote their 50th Open London to Brighton walking race. Amongst the starters will be a number of Belgravians who will strive to uphold the proud tradition which Club walkers have established over this historic course since the turn of the century.

In 1897 Polytechnic Harriers organised the first Open Brighton Walk. There were 37 starters. The only Belgrave entrant was W. Poole who finished 7th in 9hrs. 38mins. 45secs. Six years later Club Hon. Secretary H.A.Smith finished 6th in 9hrs. 33mins. 08secs. in an Open Brighton promoted by the Hairdressers A.C. To the modern walker the 'Brighton' of those far off days would seem very strange. Leading the field was a trap containing the timekeepers, closely followed by the baggage wagon known to all as the ambulance cart. A further horsedrawn carriage called a brake was used to convey the judges. All the competitors were surrounded by hordes of cyclists who both helped and hindered their progress by acting as pacemakers and adding to the difficulties of walking on a rough dusty road.

Surrey Walking Club, who had since their formation in 1899 held a number of time trials for club walkers over the Brighton course, decided in 1919 to promote an open race from Big Ben to Brighton sea front. With the exception of the war years 1939-45 this race has been held every year since, during the month of September. All the great British long distance walkers and many of those from abroad have competed in this race, including Olympic medallists Tommy Green, Harold Whitlock, Lloyd Johnson, Don Thompson, Norman Read and Abdon Pamich. Although the course has down the years followed the same general route from Westminster to Brighton, various changes to the road such as the construction of Gatwick Airport have meant that the length of the race has varied from just under 52 miles to just over 53 miles.

Right from the start Belgrave walkers began to make their presence felt in 'Brighton' races by taking 3rd individual place in 1920, 21, 23 and 24 through J. Bellchamber, J.H.Allen, J.Miles and E.A.Wall, and providing the winning teams in 1921 and 24. Further encouragement was given to take part in the race when in 1923 Bob Ricketts presented the "Belgrave" Cup for a Club Long Distance Walking Championship to be held in conjunction with the Surrey W.C. Open Brighton. Jimmy Bellchamber, the Club's first great long distance walker, finished 2nd in the Brighton and Back race in 1921 in 20hrs. 53mins. 35secs. and so became our first Centurion. In 1927 Tommy Green, who later won the first Olympic 50 km Walk, began to carve a permanent place for himself in the history of the 'Brighton' when he finished 2nd to the 21 year old Italian Giani in 8hrs. 26mins. 44secs. and led the Club to victory in the team race. Two years later he recorded the first of his four wins and again led Belgrave to victory. During the 1920's Belgravian J.N.Thomas, who began his race walking at the age of 44 with North London Harriers, became the first walker in history to finish three successive Brighton and Back races.

In 1930 Tommy Green broke Harold Ross's 1909 course record of 8hrs. 11mins. 14secs. by 8 minutes and 19 seconds. But for the sheer force of the wind and rain he encountered during the last 2 hours he would have broken 8 hours for the 'Brighton'. A year later Tommy again won the race with a time of 8hrs. 05mins. 43secs. just 2 minutes and 48 seconds outside his own record.

Ross's record was beaten for the third time in 1932 when 25 year old J.H. Ludlow of Derbyshire Walking & A.C. clocked 8hrs. 01mins. 06secs. Tommy Green was an early pacemaker but the effects of his strenuous efforts at the Los Angeles Olympics soon told on him and he fell away to finish 3rd. A year later he was back in winning form with a victory in 8hrs. 01mins. 19 secs. Belgrave completed a hat trick of wins in the team race (now four to score) in 1932, 33, 34. On 23rd/24th June 1933 A.G.White finished 3rd in the Brighton and Back with a time of 20hrs. 48mins. 16secs.

Before the 1930's were over two more individual medals were won by Club walkers; by F. Bentley in 1934 and F. Rickards in 1937. Both finished 3rd. Rickards also led the Club to victory in the 1937 race. On 7th September 1935 Harold Whitlock (Metropolitan W.C.) broke the 'Brighton' record and became the first man to beat 8 hours for the course which had been slightly shortened because of the reconstruction of the road at Bolney. His time was 7hrs. 53mins. 50secs. Harold won gold medals in the 50 km Walks at the 1936 Olympics and 1938 European Games.

After the Second World War Belgrave walkers slowly re-established the Club as a force to be reckoned with over the Brighton course. In 1950 the great A.J.Stirling Wakeley, who a year later won the Brighton and Back in 18hrs. 46mins. 00secs., led the Club to their first post war victory in the team race. Two years later 128 walkers, the highest number ever to compete in the 'Brighton', faced the starter. Despite the fact that the Club had 13 starters we could only finish in 4th team position. The following year Norman Guilmant took Belgrave's first individual medal since 1937 when he finished 2nd. In 1954 the Club rounded off a triumphant season by taking both individual and team honours in the 'Brighton'. Norman walked a finely judged race to win by 2 minutes after 21 year old Don Thompson had led for 45 miles. Don, surely the best known British walker ever because of his famous triumph in the Rome Olympics 50 km Walk, scored the first of his nine wins in 1955 (eight in successive years). He broke the course record in 1956 with a time of 7hrs. 44mins. 52secs. and again in 1957 when he established the current record of 7hrs. 35mins. 12secs. Behind him Belgravian George Checkley was second in 1956 and 3rd the following year. The Club won the "Edward Knott" Team Shield in 1956, 57 and 58. George Beecham who was a scoring member of each of these teams later went on to collect four more first team medals. He was 4th in the 1958 race with a time of 8hrs. 37mins. 46secs. Belgrave's score of 13 points for four walkers in 1957 was the lowest on record. In this race Harry Shields finished 10th and outside the scoring four. Two years later he won the "Harry Preston" Cup which is awarded to the first veteran to finish the race.

The 1960's were great years for the walkers who wore the claret and gold. Although they failed to produce any individual winners they won the team race on six occasions, finished 2nd twice and 3rd once. They also won five individual medals. R.C. Third place in the open race went to Dave King in his first 'Brighton' in 1964 and to Pat Duncan in 1967 and 68. An American, G. Klopfer walking as an individual in Belgrave colours finished 3rd in 1969.

Ray Middleton ensured that the 1970's began on a high note for the Club when he became the fastest ever Belgravian over the historic Brighton course by clocking 7hrs. 50mins. 47secs. when finishing 2nd to Shaul Ladany (Israel). He also led the Club to their 20th win in the team race with a score of 13 points, equalling their previous lowest team score of 1957. In the next two years the Club finished 2nd in the team race. Belgrave only started four walkers in the 1973 'Brighton' and for the first time in the long history of this event the Club failed to finish a team. Despite the worst weather conditions for many years Belgrave bounced back to the top in 1974 when internationals Ray Middleton and Carl Lawton took 1st and 2nd individual places and the Club tied with Brighton & Hove A.C. on points in the team race but lost first place because the latter's fourth scoring man finished nearer the winner.

Down the years since W. Poole finished in the first Open London to Brighton race many Belgravians have followed in his footsteps. Some have only completed the distance once, others many times. The challenge which this Blue Ribbon event presents has ensured that all our long distance walkers, stars and others, have wanted to tackle the race at least once. Fifty Belgrave walkers have done so since 1946. Leading the table of 'Brightons' successfully completed since the last war are John Keown with 16 (consecutive); George Beecham with 15; Joe Wilson, Harry Shields and Bernard Eglington with 12 each; Gordon Doubleday with 11 (2 before the war) and Johnny Morris and Dave King with 10 each.

Belgrave and the Brighton -----

The success of the Club down the years in the 'Brighton' is only partly due to the efforts of our walkers. Little would have been achieved without the encouragement and support of a gallant band of helpers who ensured that the individual needs of each walker were continually catered for between Big Ben and Brighton seafront. Many Club members, and families and friends of members, have acted as helpers to each successive generation of walkers. Without their aid and encouragement there would be no Brighton success story. They provided the support necessary to enable Club walkers to give of their best. Unfortunately in recent years this support has been on the decline.

What a finish Belgrave walkers could write to the Club's success story over 50 years on the Brighton Road if on 6th September 1975 they could take all the individual medals and the team title! An impossible dream? Given that all our distance walkers are fit and available to compete and that there are no top class foreign competitors, that dream can become a reality. Ray Middleton and Carl Lawton are capable of repeating their successes of 1974 whilst John Moullin, absent through injury from the last 'Brighton', can grab 3rd place and give the Club the additional strength necessary to take top honours in the team race. Lucas leads Garnet Cup Team

FEBRUARY 1st 1975 - SLOUGH.

Through the wet winter, the walking section have held high hopes of winning the Garnet Cup for 1975. This race is junior by status. But hopes don't win championships. Luck helps but from Belgrave's point of view this disappeared the week before the race. Third place in a minor Scottish Championship ruled John Hall ineligible, a car accident put John Bromley on the side lines (delighted to see he's recovering from his injuries), the Irish had a demonstration so Doug. Fotheringham and Bob Carter were left behind in London to keep law and order.

Go west young man along the M4 to Slough. The Belgrave team was a mixture of youth and experience. It still had a chance to win on this mild February day.

Every one of the five Belgravians walked as well as could be expected on the day, our leading man being Alan Lucas. This likeable Australian walked his best race in our colours, always fighting his way through the field, finishing 4th and just one place short of his honours badge. A very solid performance and we are sorry to see that he is returning home soon but are pleased to have known him.

Our next man, Bob Tanner, has that old Belgravian theory that you must win every time. To go home from a race without a prize or a medal is sacrilegious. Feeling the after effects from his police cross country training, Bob faded a little in the middle of the race but fought back with great determination to finish 8th. His police colleague Martin Lewin, our third man, was suffering from a virus infection but he fought all the way, even suffering several attacks of sickness during the race. A very fine performance in his first ever ten mile race. I believe that with normal progress Bob and Martin can become Belgrave's next walking internationals.

The other member of the scoring team was Martin Yates. He walked his best race in our colours and with a little more class racing he could develop into a good walker.

These four received their first championship medals for second team and they were ably supported by Bob Picton making a comeback.

JOE SCAMELL, SENR.

We are sorry to record that Joe Scamell, Senr., whose illness was mentioned in the last 'Belgravian' , died at his home in East London on 4th December. He was 74 years old and joined the Club in 1925 - almost 50 years of membership. He was a member of our walking teams for a number of years and helped us to many a win in Open races. As a measure of his individual performances he won a First Class A.A.A. Standard over the old Two Miles Championship and gained a Middlesex County vest (Middlesex was a very strong walking county in those days), he also held the Club Record for our five mile course. With the passing of years he continued to turn out for the Club and if the speed wasn't still there the style was and it was obvious that much of his earlier success was due to his fine style.

He was by nature quiet and reserved but travelled to Battersea or Wimbledon (quite a journey in those days) to compete and enjoy the company of the Bels. He would invariably stop for a handshake after the finishing line was crossed, such was the warmth of his character. We have, not so long ago, seen Joe, his son Joe,Junr. and grandson Michael competing in the same race - three generations! He was an inspector in the Electrical Industry by trade, before he retired.

The family received many letters of sympathy from people in the Club, and, amongst the tributes was one of red and yellow chrysanthemums from the Club. The gratitude of his family has already been conveyed to us for the many words of kindness received.

G.S.D.

SPOTLIGHT ON CARL LAWTON

HARRY SHIELDS

SPOTLIGHT ON

CARL LAWTON

Carl was born in January 1948 and hails from the Dorset town of Shaftesbury which he left at the age of 19 to seek fame and fortune in the great world. This gives our first clue to his remarkable determination, partly hidden by a genial smile and the somewhat hesitant and shy manner which beguiles the tenacity which he adopts towards a job to be done and to his enormous athletic ability. One cannot use a question and answer technique with him. He is the exponent of expediency and those who have known him from the time he joined the club have more than a suspicion that especially in his early days money was tight. Yet rather than admit this he would cycle and walk enormous distances to a race meeting and still produce performances from 3,000 metres to London - Brighton that, given a points value, would make household names of the past seem small by comparison. Only Ray Middleton could compare.

Yet this man is still only 26 years old and at the threshold of his career. He fits into his working life an active administrative interest in the Club as a committee man,captain, walking secretary and one who steps in and does any job he sees wants doing. On race days he sorts out teams and gives out numbers in spite of the tension that must be present when he knows that the success of the team and the Club depends on his own performance. He is someone I am proud to know.

Ray Hall relates the story of speaking to Carl, in August 1967, dejected after being "pulled" in the Brockwell Park 5 miles. He had previously won the Duke of Edinburgh Gold Medal for walking. From this time the ugly duckling became a swan of immaculate style. His performances from a time of 57 minutes 33 seconds in the Belgrave Open of 1967 to the present day have been of steady and continuous improvement. Last October he won the Chippenham - Calne 6 mile race in a time only beaten in the Club by Stan Vickers during the year he won the European Games.

During 1974, apart from magnificent performances at shorter distances, he was 2nd in the Hastings - Brighton, 2nd London - Brighton, 1st Leicester 'Mercury' 20 miles, 1st Southern Area 10km track walk, 2nd Surrey 10km and 1st Surrey 3,000 metres. In all the National Championships, which with one exception, we either won or were placed, he was the king-pin together with his buddy Ray Middleton.

Amidst all this activity he has managed to court and win for his wife Barbara Inkpen, the first woman to have a high jump of over 6 feet ratified as a British Record. In many ways she has Carl's characteristics and is a delightful person. They never brag when doing well or complain when things are difficult, and are two grand people who deserve well of life.

The performance that has given Carl his greatest pleasure was his 3rd place in the Commonwealth Games Trial in which he set a new Club Record of 2 hrs. 34 mins. 44 secs. and was selected to represent his country in New Zealand.

Training for him is a matter of instinct, using the time he has available. My own hope is for him to have stability at work and that he continues to make use of his enormous ability with credit to his country, his club and himself.

With the exact opposite of my thinking of yesteryear, I hope that Barbara, together with our President's wife and many other ladies who do so much for the Club, can bring pressure for a women's section for Belgrave Harriers. Somehow I think that Carl and our President see hopes in this direction rather than amalgamating with other men's clubs.

HARRY SHIELDS

PETER CROSBIE

southern counties coaching course

On the 27th - 29th December three boys and myself attended a residential coaching course held at Crystal Palace Sports Centre, staying in the comfortable hostel especially built for this purpose. The course catered for Senior Boys up to the Junior Men's age group and covered all events. The events pursued by the four from Belgrave were: Triple Jump - Ivan Beaumont, High Jump - David Didino, 800 metres - Richard Danpure, and myself - Javelin.

The nucleus of the course were the 'practical sessions', these were simply where everyone split up into their own events. In charge of the groups were some of the best coaches in the country. They gave good clear advice on all aspects of the separate events. The practical sessions were hard work but enjoyable. To back up the advice films were shown of the world's best athletes. A small competition was run, competing against those in the same event. It took the form of a heptathlon and the seven events were, 60 metres box test (running and jumping), standing long jump, standing triple jump, medicine ball throw and 800 metres. Prizes were given for the first three in each event.

Overall the course is well worth going on. Apart from enjoying yourself, you also learn a lot about your event and other events and I personally recommend the course to any young athlete in the Club.

On behalf of the boys who attended the Course I would like to thank the Committee for their generous support.

MEET .. RICHARD DANPURE

MEET .. RICHARD DANPURE

Fifteen year old Richard Danpure goes to Rutlish School and became a Belgrave Harrier in 1974 after his father met some of our members in our secondary headquarters 'The King of Denmark'. He plays rugby for his school along with fives, basketball and of course athletics.

Soon after joining he began to make a name for himself by winning the senior boys high jump and 800 metres at our Club Championships, the former with a Championship Best Performance, and he went on to gain a A.A.A. 5 Star Award. He has had some advantage over other lads of his own age because of his above average height and weight but if he can continue to make headway when the others begin to catch up, then his success in athletics will be assured.

Now training at the Hall three times a week with Tuesday and Thursday road runs and weights on Fridays, he has no real preference for events but looks forward to the coming Sunday track sessions and the chance to tackle any event he can to find out his best.

He greatly enjoyed the residential coaching course at the Crystal Palace, although finding it hard work being the youngest person in the middle distance group. Most of his training on this course was repetition work over 150m/200m/600m and gave him some idea what real training is all about. He found himself so stiff after the first day that he could hardly move but would very much like to attend future courses if given the chance.

Richard's object is to enjoy his athletics. He likes belonging to Belgrave with its tradition and friendly atmosphere and when it comes to 'ribbing' he gives as good as he gets from the likes of Colin Pearson and Chas. Manning.

The track and field season is again almost with us. Due to some confusion at National level the A.A.A. Decathlon Championship was arranged to take place on the same day as one of the league fixtures. You may well imagine the consternation this caused - the thought of a league event without Reg. Hopkins and Snowy Brooks! We immediately wrote to the powers that be, and, not entirely due to our own actions one feels, the date of the Decathlon was changed. Now for the bad news: it was changed to the day of the National League qualifying meeting which of course we must aim to reach. Further action is in progress.

Travel to the matches at Portsmouth and Brighton will be by coach. With the price of petrol as it is, if we all go together it will be cheaper all round. Supporters will be more than welcome at all the matches, so if you are not in the team but would still like to come along, contact one of us a few days before each date.

The final death knell of trophy meetings seems to have been sounded with the end of the 'Brockman', formerly one of the highlights of the season. Herne Hill Harriers have gamely fought a losing battle by promoting the Brockman Trophy Meeting in the face of declining interest in these type of fixtures. They feel the time has now come to call a halt and we are sorry to see it go.

Please support our inter-club matches this year. We appeal particularly to those who do not regularly make the first team. Whatever your standard we can provide competition for you, so study your fixture card and especially note our home fixtures.

If you still have not entered the Surrey Championships get in touch with us. We have the entry forms and will be pleased to hear from you.
