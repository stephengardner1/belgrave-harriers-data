Benjamin feeling bold in Finland

Tim Benjamin is confident he can stand up to the physical challenge as he bids to make the final of the 400 metres at the World Championships following a season severely affected by illness and injury.

The 23-year-old sustained a knee injury before a back injection went wrong, resulting in fluid leaking from his spinal column. The problem confined him to bed for weeks, including two in hospital, with the Welshman suffering agonising headaches and vomiting.

As a result Benjamin did not train between January and May but his comeback race in June, when he unexpectedly recorded 45.17 seconds in Ulm, Germany, heralded a period of consistently good performances which culminated in a new personal best of 44.75secs as he inflicted a shock defeat on Olympic champion Jeremy Wariner at Crystal Palace.

Despite his lack of training Benjamin is happy with his preparations and insists his body can stand up to running two rounds, in order to make the final in Helsinki, citing his success at last month’s World Championships trials, where he ran the same schedule.

Benjamin recorded his first sub 45-second run en route to inflicting the defeat on Wariner in London but insists, however, he had not been surprised by his time, saying: “I should have done that a couple of years ago but I’ve never been able to train properly (because of injury). Going sub-45 is a natural progression.”

The Belgrave Harrier acknowledges he will have to replicate this sort of time if he is to achieve his intention of making the final.

“I will definitely have to go sub-45; it is going to be very tough,” he said. “You can sometimes end up in an awkward semi and then you have to treat the semi like a final.

“But people know now I have the talent - all the injuries have been out of my control.”

The current British record stands at 44.36 secs, set by fellow Welshman Iwan Thomas in July 1997, and while Benjamin does not believe he will have to record that time to make the final, he admits he may have to do so in order to win a medal.

Four Champions at Sport City

AAA Championships

Sport City, Manchester, 9-10 July 2005. a=9, b=10

Four Champions at Sport City

There was some dispute as to whether the ex-Commonwealth Games warm up track was really suitable for the AAA Championships. Certainly the stadium cannot take anything like the crowds that used to attend such events. But there was plenty of claret & gold in evidence and with four champions and nine other medallists it was a good championships for Belgrave and for our sponsors adidas.

A third successive javelin title went to Goldie Sayers who although a little down on distance in this competition has thrown a lifetime best of 61.45 metres this summer. Goldie is confident that she’s in shape to get to the World Championship final and maybe reach out to 63 metres.

Tim Benjamin was the favourite in the 400 metres in spite of missing much pre-season conditioning, suffering a cold in the run up to the event and facing up to a Woodford Green onslaught in the form of Davis and Hedman. He came through it all with flying colours although he admitted to having to work really hard in the home straight.

With Windsor’s James McIlroy in the field it was unlikely that anyone was tipping Tim Bayley as the 800 metres winner – but win he did – grabbing the lead while McIlroy faded, ultimately to drop out, and holding on grimly all the way to the finish. Now, if he can just get the qualifying time, surely Tim’s earned a trip to the Commonwealth Games in Melbourne.

A season’s best saw Ben Challenger in complete control of the high jump and in Helsinki a height of 2.30m, equal to his lifetime best achieved six years ago, is not out of the question.

Rugby put the boot in – no Cup final for Belgrave in 2005!

Golden Jubilee Cup

Semi-Final, Derby Moorways, 16 July 2005.

Rugby put the boot in – no Cup final for Belgrave in 2005!

“Are you sure there’s no-one else who could do the 400 relay?” Kevin Sempers implored of the Team Manager. “Well there is just one guy,” replied the bearded one, “but he's out there with two more laps to go in the steeplechase at the moment!” Kevin turned to see forty-two year-old Paul Beaumont plodding down the straight on his way to earning 7 points for a second place. “Ok,” said Kevin with a smile, “I'm knackered - but I'll do it.”

Multi-event star Kevin had pulled out of the team two days earlier, suffering from an adductor strain, but the night before the match he’phoned up to say that maybe it wasn’t quite so bad, and perhaps he’d come along to see how things went. He ended up doing six events, taking in four wins - and all this with an important decathlon lined up in two weeks time.

Kevin wasn’t the only hero. We’ve already mentioned veteran 400 hurdler Paul Beaumont turning out for the ’chase. Jonathan Blackledge injured himself while winning the 800m and had to pull out of the 1500 - but then David Anderson, with his Achilles tendon already sore from the 5k, stepped into the breach saying he’d jog round; of course he then couldn’t resist having a pop at the field on the last lap. As David limped off to catch his train home we could only hope he hadn’t done himself any long term damage. It was like that all afternoon: Scot Thompson also has an injury problem and would only do the discus - until he saw how a few more points might save the day and offered a “standing” shot put as well; an unfit Darren Chin won the 100m, ran the sprint relay - and then let himself be persuaded to run the long relay; Graham Beasley has been troubled with a trapped nerve but won the 200 and ran both relays; Chrissie Foster placed second in the 800m and then lined up for the very next event, the 100m, to avoid a blank on the team sheet.

The long relay boys were promised that we’d pull them out of the 4x400m if it was impossible to win the match but with a flurry of late wins including the women’s long relay, where we were boosted by the arrival of Hannah Stares and Jennifer Culley who’d hot-footed it from Loughborough, we could see that if our men won and Rugby placed fourth – we’d win the match. And then, as they lined up, we found that there were only three teams on the track. It was all over barring dropped batons. We did win the relay – but missed out on a place in the final by a whisker – two points.

There were plenty of nightmare events where points could have been gained. We had no runner in the women’s high hurdles and with only one competitor in the race we could have turned out someone to bunny-hop over the barriers to gain 7 points. We also suffered from a pole vault problem – no competitor in the women’s competition and Cameron Johnston coming in late and failing to clear a height in the men’s event.

The match at Loughborough (all credit to those who did that and the Cup), the BMC meet at Cardiff, a Heptathlon in Spain and the European U23 Champs. drew heavily on our teams, but whereas the men’s squad have the depth to cope with these demands, the women’s team don’t. Nevertheless, there were bright spots in the women’s team as well. Eva Massey blew the opposition away in the shot and Michelle Nestor, with long and high jump action already behind her, took the triple jump in a near best of 11.47m. It was great to see our men’s winner Farel Mepandy giving Michelle some advice which paid immediate dividends and also to hear respected coach John Anderson tell us what great potential she has.

Jacqui Loney was close to her best in the hammer and Louise Cooper came off the roads to gain second in the 3000m.

So, that’s it for the Cup in 2005. All credit to everyone on the day who did all they could, but now we can go on holiday in late August!

Win streak comes to an end

Bill Laws

British Athletics League

Match 4, New River Stadium, Haringey, 30 July 2005

Win streak comes to an end

Bill Laws writes:- Although there was still a statistical chance that we could retain our League title the real objective as we entered this match was to end the season with a win. And with a number of season bests and pb’s we almost made it, losing out to a victorious Woodford Green with Essex Ladies team.

Damage limitation achieved

The start was not promising. Only Mick Jones lined up for the hammer and once again finished second best to Andy Frost of WGEL. Points were also down in the pole vault after Cameron Johnson had withdrawn the night before due to injury. Damage limitation was achieved as William Sharman cleared 4 metres for a season’s best and junior Roy Collier equalled his best to get better than expected B points. The long jump saw a welcome return to the fold of Richard Danso - he quit the sport four years ago - who proved that his return was no flash in the pan as he recorded his first ever legal plus 7 metres jump to support a somewhat jaded Adrian Phillips who was recovering from his 30th birthday party celebrations. As Adrian’s head cleared so his jumps improved and two second spots were to mark the change in our fortunes.

Next up was Scot Thompson who opened the discus comp with a near 53 metre effort. A distance he was not to improve on and one which Inter-Counties champion Leith Marar chased but could not match; but third and first spots added valuable points.

Matt Douglas back in form - Ben Challenger supports the team

It was great to see Ben Challenger pair up with William Sharman in the high jump. Ben had delayed his departure to the World Champs warm up camp to support the Belgrave team and pronounced himself satisfied as he had withdrawn from the previous week’s competition at Crystal Palace nursing a slight injury. But here he was, winning, and in the process having some good attempts at 2.30m. William gained a good second place in the B event so the points continued mounting. But we weren’t getting away from WGEL who once again called out the evergreen Dalton Grant. Dalton turns 40 next year and can still clear 2.10m with apparent ease.

Matt Douglas has finally overcome a niggling injury and arrived pronouncing that he would fill in any event to gain points. Silly boy! His win in the A 400H was a comfortable affair after he had gone out easy and ran the home straight in style. But an hour and a half later he was called up for the A 110H as William Sharman felt hamstring pain during his warm up strides. Matt hasn’t run the event for two years and not trained over the high hurdles. His pb of 14.10 looked in danger until he hit the last hurdle but third spot in 14.17 was way above anybody’s expectations. Matt later put in a 48.3 relay leg to confirm that his troubles are over, but too late to make a bid for World Championship selection. By coincidence Dominic Girdler ran the same time as Matt to get second in the B race.

The 800m didn’t go to plan and Graeme Oudney could not explain why he was 7 seconds down on his season’s best as he trailed in last in the A race. With a paucity of middle distance men doing the League this year Chris Axe was a last minute replacement for the B race, arriving 5 minutes before the start and running within a second of his pb. He later ran his first ever steeplechase to make sure that Will Cockerell had a partner - and it was a surprise to all to seem him outkick Will who had, it must be said, just run the 5000m. For someone who has never jumped a barrier let alone a water jump Chris acquitted himself well. More to come?

Sprinters shine

Next to the 100m, followed by the 400m. Originally it had been planned to put Graham Beasley and Darren Chin on the starting line. But Graham wasn’t feeling too good and the arrival of World Youth double gold medallist Harry Aikines-Ayreetey for the guest 100m and relay prompted Graham to suggest a change to the line up. Harry duly obliged with a second place and in the B race Darren, running from lane one, also gained a valuable second place in exactly the same time as Harry. But whereas Harry’s race was just over the legal limit, Darren’s wasn’t. So a season’s best for Darren and a quick promotion to the B 200 as Graham suggested James Ellington take the A 200m. James duly obliged with his fastest ever run to gain second place and dip under 21 secs for the first time. Darren followed this with a big season’s best so the last minute changes proved a huge success. Set Osho, back from the European under 20 championships with a long relay gold medal, proved to all and sundry that his long awaited breakthrough has arrived as he set a new pb of 47.35sec which he followed later in the long relay with another sub 48sec clocking. In the B race junior Gavin Selway made his now trademark rocket start and headed the field at 200m only to be caught on the line but it was another great effort from this durable young man who still gained third spot.

Darren Gauson, fresh from a 3min 45sec pb in Belgium, joined Stephen Sharp in a tactical but classy 1500m. With third place in the A for Stephen and second in the B for Darren more good points as we briefly hit the front for the first time. A terrible triple jump then moved us back down the placings only for an excellent near max points in the shot for Chris Gearing and Scot Thompson to move us back up again. A high quality javelin competition meant less points than expected although Wesley Smith threw a lifetime’s best and James Everard competed even though he is due for a knee operation on Monday. His spirit exemplified the team’s efforts as at last we seemed to be firing on all cylinders.

Exciting relays

Finally to the relays. Graham Beasley ran his usual second sector, despite not being well, as the team struggled with another lane 1 draw. Darren Chin’s blocks slipped to add to the tension but then he, Graham, James and Harry clocked the fastest club time in the UK this year and finished some 0.65 seconds ahead of second placers WGEL.

The 400m relay was even more exciting. Matt Douglas led the first stage passing to Graham Oudney who fought hard and passed the baton to Set in third spot. Set cruised and then kicked hard to give Gavin Selway a two metre lead ahead of a chasing pack which ominously included Chris Rawlinson for Trafford and Ewan Thomas for the Beagles. Gavin held them until 30 metres from the line, finishing in third spot to record a season’s best for the club. A photo of that finishing straight shoot out would have a memorable place in Gavin’s album. Any shots out there?

And yet, after all these heroics, WGEL prevailed. Gladys Bird and her merry men were rightly ecstatic. They have come close to taking the BAL title on several occasions and finally they have achieved their ambition. Congratulations to the WGEL team - but we aim to get it back next year!

Division Four Champions

UK Women’s Athletics League Division 4

Match 3, Abingdon, 31 July 2005.

Division Four Champions

It was fitting that the last match of the year took place at the same stadium that twelve months earlier had seen us miss promotion by just 2 match points. This time everything came right and it would have taken an almighty disaster for the Belles to have missed out again. But it wasn’t just promotion that we were looking for – an unbeaten 2005 campaign would be nice. Blackheath & Bromley weren’t going to make it easy for us and when the opening event – the pole vault – saw two B&B stars and no other competitors, it was tantamount to giving them an 18 point head start.

Hannah Stares started the recovery by winning the 400 hurdles and then Claire Robinson took the 800m. As the jumps and throws results then started coming in, we crept slowly towards the lead. Double wins came from Sarah Wellstead and Katryna Euridge in the long jump, a double again in the “triple” with Sarah again and Michelle Nestor close behind her, and it was almost a maximum score in the high jump as well thanks to Hannah and Michelle.

With Julie Hollman in the line-up, no-one was going to get near our sprint hurdle duo, and as Julie then turned her attention to the shot where she partnered Eva Massey, it was top points again. It remained for Laura Fox and Jacquie Loney to do the business in the discus and we were away and clear.

Congratulations to all who made the league campaign a success - every point scorer - every judge and timekeeper - and especially Team Managers Cynthia and Bob who kept the momentum going. It'll be tougher next year in division three, but there's no reason why we cannot do it again in 2006.

National Junior Athletics League

You’ve got to hand it to our women’s under 20 team who although only small in number had a terrific season to top the Brent Division of the National Junior League – well done to Team Manager Kim Collier.

In 2004 our Junior men also won the Brent Division and as a result earned promotion to the Southern Premier Division. But the gulf in standard between the two divisions is huge and although we have some truly outstanding male juniors the rival attractions of World Youth Games and European U20 Championships meant that they mostly gave the NJAL a miss in 2005. As a result we were unable to do ourselves justice.

Organisation of the League changes next season when the competition becomes mixed. To form the new league structure the men’s and women’s points for 2005 have been added together and on the club rankings so calculated, Belgrave’s joint men’s and women’s team for 2006 will be placed in the Brent Division.
