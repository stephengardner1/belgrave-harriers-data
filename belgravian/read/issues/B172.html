EDITORIAL

A MERRY CHRISTMAS and a PROSPEROUS NEW YEAR to all Belgravians, wherever you may be. A special greeting goes out to those members in distant parts, whether they be in far off Australia, India and U.S.A. or in the nearby British provinces. We are pleased to enjoy your continued support and the good work you do as ambassadors of Belgrave. Special greetings also to those regular contributors who have assisted your Honorary Editor over the past year; and to our principal photographer, Ron Linstead,whose work plays such an important part in the Gazette's production.

As we leave another decade behind us this is an appropriate moment to remind our readers that the Club is still gathering together all material relevant to the history of Belgrave Harriers. If you have any photos, cuttings or other literature even remotely concerning Belgrave then we would appreciate the loan of such material. Eventually , an official Belgrave History will be produced---your contribution, however small, might well play a part. How about giving that old loft a 1970 springclean?.

On another page can be found our first advertisement. Each copy of the Belgravian you receive costs the Club about 1/9d, and costs rise annually. Advertisers help to keep those costs down so its in our interests to support our advertisers when we can. W. & G. HALES are not unknown to some of our members, but there are many who are unaware of the wide range of athletic gear available at this shop, one of whose suppliers is our own Gerry North. Open on Saturday afternoons, it is conveniently placed for buying shoes etc. on your way to or from Belgrave Hall. And don't forget to mention Belgrave when you make a purchase.

With a circulation approaching 600 and a readership probably nearer the thousand mark there must be many readers in a position to sell advertising space. Please contact the Editor if you have a recommendation to make. And if anyone is interested in becoming Advertising Manager. let him please step forward---quickly!.

HON. SECRETARY'S NOTES

A.A.H.

A letter of good wishes and a cheque for $ 25 has been received from Edmund P. Donovan, O.B.E., now of New York; and a member of the Club way back in 1911. He recalls with pleasure his visit to our Club Track Championships a couple of years ago, and hopes probably to renew acquaintance at our Boxing Day Meeting as he aims to be in England at that time.

If there was an award for our most loyal supporter it would go assuredly to MICK PYER, who must now be within months of fifty years of membership. Wherever our teams go MICK is to be found and we trust it will long be so. The Committee have invited him to serve as a co-opted member so that we may benefit from his experience and enthusiasm. It will be remembered by the few that it was one of Mick's proposals many years ago on our Committee that led to the inauguration of Youth's Track and Field Championships in the South.

Following the International Fixture Conference held at Bucharest recently there have been changes in the Summer fixtures. The A.A.A. Championships will be held on August 7th and 8th instead of July 10th and 11th as previously announced and as shown in our current Handbook. This change will accommodate the East German team who will visit this country on the dates previously announced for the AAA Championships, which will now be held after the Edinburgh Commonwealth Games on July 16th-25th.

There may yet be other changes in the domestic programme, but no doubt these will be settled within the next few weeks.

August 16th 1971 will be the centenary of the Wimbledon and Putney Commons Act which dedicated the Commons to the use of the public. Much of this land was previously in the ownership of Earl Spencer, by whose permission Thames Hare and Hounds held the first cross country races ever, celebrating their centenary last year, it will be recalled.

We have made good use of the Commons since before the first World War; and have been grateful indeed to have had country and road courses with so much natural beauty and so very convenient of access,on hand for pleasure and our sport.

Undoubtedly, as a Club we have benefited considerably in having the Commons at our disposal, with no pen of the speculative builder driving us outward. So many cross country clubs have fought losing battles with the so-called "developers"---especially is this true in Middlesex---that we must count ourselves very fortunate indeed to have conducted our sport for well over fifty years under relatively stable conditions.

The Ranger, Mr. F.G.P. QUIXLEY, has invited representatives of the various clubs and societies with interests in the Commons to meet soon and possibly arrange to play some part in the making of the centenary in 1971.

We congratulate most heartily Past President Harry Shields on being honoured with Life Membership of the English Cross Country Union. Fortunately Harry's health is now much improved and he is to be seen at many of our fixtures once again.

We also congratulate DOUG FOTHERINGHAM on being elected Chairman of the Race Walking Association, Southern Area Committee.

How very pleased to see TOMMY GREEN with his wife at Wimbledon recently, when he started our 50th Annual Open Walk. Tommy has his own particular niche in Belgrave history with his Olympic Games win, his London to Brighton and Manchester to Blackpool Walk wins, and dozens of other victories from two miles up to 100 Kilometres.

Tommy looks remarkably well and capable of repeating some of his wins of years ago! His age? Well he was 39 when he won his Olympic title at Los Angeles in 1932, so work it out for yourself!.

Seen in Town recently, Vice President Fred M. STONE. He was in London to lecture on Youth Movements, which seems just the thing for a 72 year old who has tackled almost everything in the world of sport. He will be remembered as one of the bravest Metropolitan Policemen ever, and the recipient of many awards for gallantry and devotion td duty, the last one from the hands of Her Majesty The Queen.

He was featured in the television series "This is your life" some years ago, in which the late Harry Evans also appeared. Fred sends his best wishes to all.

Also seen. Jack Bidgood and Pat Sapcote who send greetings to their many friends.

A.A.H.

THE PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE

Gordon S. Doubleday

Dear Belgravians,

I would like to thank all of the members who elected me President at the A.G.M. I regret that I was not at the meeting as I was in hospital at the time. I am stepping into the shoes of our Past Presidents, who have over the years given stirling service to the Club; I hope to be worthy of their example. I have had to miss some of our early fixtures and I am grateful to Past Presidents who have kindly stood in for me.

When I joined the Club in 1934 I was attracted by the good sport, the good sportsmanship and the good friendship which the Belgrave Harriers offered. These qualities are as strong as ever and, over the years, I have enjoyed them with countless others. I am now no longer active in the athletic sense and have transferred my interest to the official side, and it has been brought home to me how much our sport owes to its officials. Fortunately in Belgrave we have many who are willing to help in this direction and I am proud that I have recently joined their number as an Hon. Timekeeper.

Belgrave have always been renowned for their team performances and over the whole range of athletics we can claim to be second to none.Readers of the "Belgravian" are well aware of our present strength, but to me one of the brightest signs is the prowess and enthusiasm of our Boys and Youths and I hope that they will mature to be the team-men, the champions, and indeed the Internationals of tomorrow. I wish the Club and all its members a very happy and successful year.

Gordon S. Doubleday.

Feature · page 6

Our President

A.A.H.

In September 1934, Gordon S. Doubleday ran in a Novice race from our Wimbledon Headquarters. He duly won and was elected to membership the following month. So began 35 years of loyalty and conscientious hard work for the Club which have culminated in his election and installation as President. He is in every way worthy of this honour and we are sure that the Club will enjoy continuing success under his leadership.

Following his novice success "Doub" turned out regularly in our cross country events without any conspicuous success; but thoroughly enjoying his running and the companionship it brought him---and this is of course, the prime reason for our existence. In 1935 he climbed to higher positions in our races, and began to contribute regularly to the pages of "The Belgravian". He also joined our Sunday strolling parties and made his contribution to the flights of worldly and athletic wisdom that enlivened our weekly jaunts. Even at a distance of over thirty years there is recollection of frost-laden clear air, brilliant star-lit evenings and a good road under our feet---and Gordon's rendering of "David in the Lion's Den" and Ravel's Bolero"!.

Gordon Doubleday (left) and Alf Harley (right) try their hand at putting snow balls during one of their climbing holidays in the "Thirties". Alf Taylor is in the centre. The action photo shows our President after the war racing at Tooting Bec track, Christmas 1948.

In 1937 our President was persuaded to essay his first walk to Brighton and this he accomplished successfully, and repeated on later occasions. Now he applied himself to Walking at the shorter distance and accomplished an excellent Croydon to Godstone and back in his first season. In the Junior 10 miles Championship at Hampstead in 1938 when, let us remember, we had a record winning score with Len and Joe Coleman dead-heating for first place, Colin Sutton 6th and Reg Elson 7th, Doub finished 24th of the 127 finishers.

In September 1938 he accomplished an outstanding "double" in getting married and becoming Hon. Editor of the Belgravian!. His walking continued to improve and in the Surrey County 10 miles Championship he finished ninth--but was the sixth Belgrave man!. However, reward came in the 10 miles Championship at Manchester when he was in our winning team---and on later occasions he scored in our winning Surrey County teams.

The coming of War translated Doub to Exeter, where he became a Vice-Captain of Exeter Harriers, and he did his best to keep in touch during those trying years. Eventually, in happier days, the call of Belgravian duty persuaded Doub to be our Hon. Treasurer which he carried through for two years; and on his retirement from office he was made a Life Member--a proposition the writer was able to make on behalf of the committee at the AGM with great personal satisfaction and pleasure.

Doub's work for our sport did not end here. He has walked his hundred miles inside 24 hours to become a Centurion, and is now Hon. Treasurer of that exclusive body.

He is also President of the Road Runners Club, and well on his way to becoming a first class timekeeper.

Gordon Doubleday has won a Club Honours Badge for Marathon running; has strengthened our Walking teams; enlivened our strolling and our holidays; worked hard in two important offices (and now a third) within the Club; contributed time and time again to the Belgravian; served us conscientiously as a timekeeper; and made his services and abilities to other sporting associations. All this, plus his pleasing personality, his ability to enthuse, and his great loyalty to Belgrave make us fortunate indeed in our selection of President. May he enjoy good health to enjoy his year of office, and to give us the full benefit of his talents and enthusiasms!.

A.A.H.

Dinner & Dance

'CORDON BLEU'

The annual dinner and dance held at the Wimbledon Hill Hotel on the 15th November followed the successful pattern of recent years. Certainly the large attendance consisting of members of all ages accompanied by their nearest and dearest bore witness to the slogan "Nothing breeds success like success".

It was fitting that Sydney Wooderson, as President of Blackheath Harriers in this their Centenary Year, should be our guest of honour. In reply to his short but well received address Reg Hopkins displayed none of the nervousness generally encountered in maiden speeches, and achieved a fine blend of wit and invective. The overall brevity of speeches appeared to the connoisseurs to have resulted from an attempt to "keep 'em clean". Certainly there was a distinct lack of blue jokes and not even a single reference to Gerry North's support. Tributes were paid to both the retiring President and to the new incumbent.

The dancing that followed was enthusiastically enjoyed by all concerned and for those whose education in this art stopped at the twist the band's repertoire was sufficient. As a result of Ted Stimpson's persuasive pen numerous local firms and well wishers had contributed a vast array of prizes for the raffle. No wonder the dark mutterings from certain tables as prize after prize fell to Charlie Manning.

All in all a most successful 'do' appreciated by athletes, officials and guests.

One final suggestion, couldn't next year's menu be NORFOLK TURKEY AU VIN PECHE BONNE SURPRISE Only the names need be changed to protect the innocent.............

'CORDON BLEU'.

THE A.G.M.

CARL LAWTON

CARL LAWTON WRITES

With the attendance figure at around the 60 mark the A.G.M. of the Belgrave Harriers was convened at Belgrave Hall on the evening of Thursday, 18th September at 6-45pm. The retiring President Mr. Simmons was in the chair.

Apologies were received from various members of the club for non attendance, including those of the President elect, Mr. Doubleday who was suffering from a touch of the other in hospital, and Mr. Middleton who although performing magnificently in Athens at the time of the meeting regretted that he could not make it as he had walked enough *!?*! kilos for one day.

Mr. Harley proceeded to give a run down on the previos year's events in and around the club. He mentioned with regret the loss to the club of two outstanding and devoted men Mr. Rollins and Mr. Harley Senior. Their devotion to the club was exceptional and they would be missed. Mr. Harley made it known that he had been a member since 1926 and had watched its growth since that time. "The success over the years", he said "had been due to the supreme effort of the athletes and officials,and the past year had been no exception". The club was estimated to enter more individual events than any other club in the country but this gave no excuse for the disappointment of the club being relegated instead of promoted in the National League. The reason for this was the failure of a few, and only a few, to turn out when requested. the many successes of the club during the year would not be described as an excellent coverage had been provided in the Belgravian.

The General Hon. Sec. closed with a word of praise for our internationals during the year, Middleton, Thresher, Bicourt, Geoff North, Boosey and Lerwill as well as the promising juniors without whom the club would have no future. He then requested a vote of thanks to the retiring President, the retiring Hon. Treasurer, and the retiring Cross Country Secretary. They step down in the knowledge that the club is where it is due to their work over the years.

The Hon. Treasurer, Mr. Shepherd, in his report on the world monetary situation gave a detailed account of the income and expenditure, ably assisted by Balance sheets carefully distributed around the hall. After much deliberation we were informed that the club had made a loss of £3 in the last financial year. "This was a bit near the mark" he said. It was pointed out that much of the income was spent in sending teams to national championships and this cost was increasing annually. However it was concluded that so long as nobody removed the hall we were still in good financial standing!.

The meeting moved on to the election to life membership of Mr. R. Hall and Mr. C. Shippen. A brief review of Mr. Hall's stirling support of athletics both as participant and official was given by Mr. Harley and Mr. Maclennon. Perhaps his main achievement being one of a few who have beaten 8 hours for the Brighton. Mr. Wesch, showing his knowledge of English, wished it to be known that it was one of 6. Mr. Shippen was proposed by Mr. Lucas who described the efforts of the Hon. Editor of the Belgravian. He had worked hard for our benefit and his own athletics always took second place in the club. Mr. Manning seconded Mr. Shippen, but stated he was not going to say anything. Five minutes later he sat down.

TRACK AND FIELD — PETER HILLIAR (Track Captain) WRITES

PETER HILLIAR (Track Captain)

The 1969 track season was extremely disappointing and was climaxed by our relegation from a weak division of the National League to the first division of the Southern League. We only managed to annexe one trophy, The Jim Peters Trophy, which we only won with the considerable assistance of a strong contingent of juniors and youths.

What went wrong then, following our earlier anticipation of an outstanding season? It is difficult to put one's finger accurately on the pulse of the trouble, but I am tempted to hazard a guess that the modern athlete is so well provided with competition at all levels by his club, his area and national organisers, universities and various other bodies such as the BMC, that he can no longer raise any particular enthusiasm for competitions such as the National League. This naturally affects athletes belonging to other clubs, so it could be a case of loyalties and / or priorities, which I feel are not taken seriously by some members of our club.

To get down off the metaphorical high horse, I now propose to summarise events.

100/200 metres. Probably our strongest events on paper with Steve White, Jim Vivian, John Mitchell and Chris Martin and able assistance from Aris Sapountzoglou, Tony and Cliff Brooks. However, through injury and other commitments, we have usually been weak in top-class competition.

400 metres. The most exciting occasion was the emergence of Mel Ford with 49.3 in the Brockman. Otherwise, we had sound and loyal contributions from Tony Brooks, Philip Gee, Philip Smith and Dave Rees (a startling improvement to sub-55 by Dave).

800 metres. I think we could at last have some strength in depth in the future in what has been our "Cinderella" event since the retirement of Dave Cocks; through such competitors as Mel Ford (around 1-56 relay), John Boardman, John Kelly and Doug Jardine. Nevertheless, 1969 wasn't their year, unfortunately.

1500 metres. Our best performers in this event were all basically under-distance in John Thresher, Geoff North, Adrian Major and Bill Kerr, well though they performed.

5000/10,000 metres. Geoff North's brilliant Southern run was the obvious highlight, but at National League and Trphy level, we were generally under-represented. It was encouraging, however, to welcome Gerry North and Bob Smith back to fitness by the end of the season.

3000metre Steeplechase. John Bicourt continued his steady improvement and it was encouraging to see Peter Morris and Dick Piotrowski (despite a nasty mid-season injury) not far behind. Also, the season witnessed the resurgence of Alan Black.

To wind up, I should like to praise the enthusiasm of such members as Tony Brooks, Philip Gee, Dave Rees and Philip Smith, always willing to have a go at anything. Without them the season might have been disastrous. Finally, I feel a tribute should be paid to an immensely hard-working track secretary, Bob Taylor; a pillar of a great club.

BILL COUZENS writes on FIELD

BILL COUZENS

I am sure that everybody knows Belgrave are relegated from the National League. This time last year we were considering which club would be promoted with us into the second division of the National League, but when it came to it we fizzed out like a damp firework. Was it all bad luck? We were without the following athletes, in most cases in all three National League meetings, for reasons beyond their control.

Glyn Ratcliffe (Shot, Discus) posted to Ireland with the Grenadier Guards. Mel Ford (400m Hurdles, 4X400m Relay) also posted to Ireland. Steve Inman (Pole Vault) also posted to Ireland. Charlie Gillett (400m Hurdles, 4 X 440 Relay) Injured. John Mitchell (Long Jump, Sprints) Injured. Nick Head (Javelin, Shot) In Germany during 2 meetings.

With these men in the team we would certainly have avoided relegation, but what of our cert promotion prospects into the Second division? These could not be achieved without our top runners turning out at each League Meeting, so where were our sprinters? After all, this is where we should be strongest. Luckily we have a nucleus of men in Belgrave who will do two, three or even four events; their names can be seen quite easily in the results.

I, like others, left the last National League Meeting with a great lump in my throat. I hope next year we will be celebrating our return.

Dave Ansell writes:-

Few men could have been so near to gaining a full international vest as the athlete who heads our 100 metres and long jump rankings for 1968---JOHN MITCHELL. He must have been a marginal omission from the Great Britain team in the Finland and France matches.

John takes his final exams in medicine next year, but between now and then fall the Commonwealth Games in Edinburgh. He has both English and Scottish qualifications so must stand a good chance of appearing at the Games, should studies permit.

Our leading long jumper started his season in PARIS on May 10th, competing for London University, and recorded 10.8 (second) and 23'-7" (first). Four days later he chalked up 10.7/21.9/23'-2"; the 200m time being a personal best.

John was running into form nicely and in June won the B.U.S.F. long jump title, beating K. Snelgrove, with 23'-11". However, Snelgrove got his revenge in the Southern, 23'-7¾"/23'-6½" squeezing John into 4th place.

The height of John Mitchell's season was the A.A.A. Championships on August 2nd at the White City. ROAD RELAY ROUND-UP

Last year's Autumn road relay season was disappointing for Belgrave, but the 1969 season was most encouraging. There was more spirit this year, and whilst we only chalked up one win we did at least gain medals in the four premier events. The season went as follows:-

Once again, our worst turn-out of the year was at the Surrey race. With barring clauses, holidays, injuries and general apathy taking their toll it was a dismal occasion with Belgrave never in the hunt.

Each year, with the Surrey relay behind them our runners become transformed as though they've got something nasty out of their system and this was how it went with the Johnston relay. Coventry and Manchester were missing this year but the holders (Hercules-Wimbledon) who thrashed us last year were done down by our lads in a close fought struggle. This was our first victory in this relay.

The following week at Walton we faced our local rivals again, with Portsmouth thrown in for good measure. Lionel Mann was an impressive winner of the first stage, but by the third leg Dave Holt had taken Hercules-Wimbledon into their winning lead: they are a difficult club to beat over six stages. Our old enemies Portsmouth were never in the hunt really and finished 1¼ minutes behind in third place. Our strength in this well supported relay was indicated by our 'B' team which finished 6th.

(Photo:Ron Linstead) GEOFF NORTH hands over to brother GERRY for the last stage of the Walton Relay.

Such strength in depth augured well for Tipton's Dudley to Bridgenorth and back relay of ten stages, where we finished second to Derby. Our optimism was justified for, as in the Johnston , we beat the holders; but instead of taking the winning awards we were destined to be second again through the emergence of a strengthened North Staffs and Stone team now called City of Stoke AC. We were to hear more from them the following week and they are certain to provide very serious opposition in the coming months. Our second place should not detract from the fine running of our lads who finished inside the previous record and 3:48 faster than last year. In addition, new stage records were set by Bill Kerr, Gerry North and Peter Morris.

The short season was rounded off with what should have been it's major event---the inaugural 6-stage National Relay at the Crystal Palace. Not only was it the poorest supported event of all but it also produced the least enthusiasm. Six 2½ miles stages was far too brief for us to hold out much hope of winning and not only did the Stoke outfit score another win but Blackheath pipped us for second.

Although we were not exactly elated at our relay record this Autumn, many clubs would have been delighted to scoop one gold, two silvers and a bronze from five outings.

BELGRAVE WIN 'Johnstone'

Sunday September 28th

Owing to a proposed Southern Counties six man relay, which did not materialise, the entry for the Johnston was somewhat depleted with only nineteen teams starting. Coventry and Tipton have in the past contested this relay but were amongst the absentees. Nevertheless it was to be a hard fought battle between ourselves and our local rivals Hercules/Wimbledon who had won in 1968 in record time.

Pat O'Connor took the first stage and did well to bring us in third only eight seconds behind the leaders (Woodford Green and Ealing - Southall). Hercules/Wimbledon were at this stage back in 9th place.

Lionel Mann held 3rd place on Stage two by displacing Woodford but had to give way to an evergreen Ken Harland who took Cambridge H. from 5th to 2nd. Bivens of Ealing was the leader and Mike Fuller brought Hercules up to 4th.

John Thresher on Stage three took us up to 2nd and gained a few seconds on Ealing who were still out in front. John Sullivan of Hercules brought his club into 3rd place with a time one second faster than John.

Stage 4 saw us in the lead for the first time with a very good run by Trevor Hart who turned a 21 second deficit into a six second advantage.

Gerry North on the first of the long stages was well chased by Mike Barratt of Ealing but kept us in first place with a lead of 14 secs. Dave Holt gained a little ground for Hercules who were still third.

Stage 6 saw Hercules make a strong bid for the lead with a record breaking run by Mike Beevor who started 58 secs behind Bill Dance and brought his team up to a mere 9 secs behind us. We must again thank Bill for his ardent devotion to the Club by making the long journey from Huddersfield for this race.

Dick Piotrowski on Stage 7 was not in the least deterred by the closeness of Hercules and proceeded to open up a gap of 63 secs which later in the race proved invaluable.

Bas. Collins on Stage 8 gained two seconds on Laurie O'Hara, these two being by far the fastest runners on this stage. Ealing were now having trouble keeping in touch and were over two minutes adrift.

Although we all know the potential of Bob Holt on the road we also knew Geoff North to be running well as witnessed by his comfortable win in our own road race only one week earlier

We were not therefore prepared for Bob to catch up the 61secs deficit and turn this into a lead of 11 secs. It was not that Geoff ran badly, for he was credited with the second fastest time of the day, but that Holt was in his most devastating form.

Peter Morris was not in the least concerned about giving a start to Mullholland of Hercules and it was not long before he was running at the opposition's shoulder.

Entreaties from our supporters to pull away were accepted and Pete came into the Stadium with fifteen seconds to spare. Asking afterwards why everyone was panicking he was told that we did not wish to see him beaten in a desperate finish with Mullholland. His effort gained for him a stage record.

NATIONAL SIX STAGE RELAY

G.I.B.

CRYSTAL PALACE---October 25th

A'Damp Squib'---such is one headline already given to this race born in a rather wet Crystal Palace Sports Centre. Certainly it will go down as one of the non events of the year, with the title 'National' being a complete misnomer. Prior to the above date much controversy had centred around the introduction of this race, having been campaigned for by some of the smaller clubs for quite some time, but considered by others as completely unwarranted, in view of the existing 12 stage relay held at Leicester. However, with little or no consultation with clubs the date was fixed, in an already over-loaded calendar, only to clash with several important cross country fixtures including our own N.S. League event. The actual course of 6X2 laps (ie 2½m per leg) of the Crystal Palace motor racing circuit was also rather unimaginative. In view of all these factors several clubs decided to boycott the event as a form of protest. Although many of our members had varying views on the subject, it was considered we should support the venture rather than condemn it 'out of hand', although we would probably not have our strongest of teams out on the day.

Of the 19 clubs entered only 9 finally came under starters orders and from the start it was obvious that the main challenge was coming from City of Stoke AC, (our victors of the previous week in the Dudley to Bridgenorth Relay), and Manchester and District L.C, with Blackheath H. and Ealing and Southall AC providing further danger.

THAT OLD ONE-TWO AGAIN

5¾ mile road race---Wimbledon, September 20th

With Geoff and Gerry North on the start line one can always expect fireworks. No one has ever got within 20 seconds of their best times so the fireworks are usually confined to the family when they appear together. And so it was on this occasion, though Geoff ran out a clearer winner than Gerry would have liked.

Geoff set a brisk pace right from the start and looked as though he was after his record of 27:14. On the first circuit of the common a pack of nine detached itself from the remaining thirty five runners; the Norths, Morris, O'Connor, Mann, Thresher, Hart, Kerr and Piotrowski steadily moving clear of the ruck.

On leaving the Common,Geoff was five seconds clear with Dick Piotrowski showing up well, just ahead of the following bunch. At the bottom of Copse Hill the leader had stretched the gap to 15 seconds ahead of brother Gerry and Dick who were in turn 15 seconds clear of the next six. The rest of the field were now a further 50 seconds in arrears.

As usual the Cambridge Road drag and the "Kiss of Death" climb sorted every one out. Kerr was the first to tail off, then Hart, then Thresher. Dick did well to hold Gerry to the top of the hills but he faded badly. At the oak tree, with a lap of the Common to go, Geoff's lead remained the same but a strong finish increased his margin by 27 seconds.

Meanwhile, Mann, O'Connor and Morris were chasing hard and simply scorched round "the top" leaving the rest of their companions trailing. Lionel fired his booster rocket on the run in and nearly caught a surprised Gerry North; it's a pity the seven seconds gap wasn't six for then Lionel would have become only the ninth man to have broken the magical 28 minutes.

The shape of things to come was shown by Bill Curtin and John Stowe (both Youths) placing 11th and 12th---John scooping the handicap to boot!. Ray Beaumont was a deserved second and that popular "vet" Joe McDonald third. Joe was delighted at being amongst the prizes and also at beating his training companion, the evergreen Charlie Smart; though he would probably agree that their handicap allowances should have been reversed on pre-race form.

Yet another immaculate letter has been received from BARRY SAWYER in distant Australia (South Yarra, Melbourne). Barry, who is the Victorian State marathon champion has fought his way to personal bests from 1500 metres to the marathon, despite worsening achilles tendon trouble. Only when he was no longer able to hobble to the start line did this obstinate character give in and begin serving a medical "sentence" of twelve weeks off running. Daily swimming was recommended as an alternative and it will be no surprise to those that knew Barry to hear that he had soon reached 50 lengths a night!. Pat Duncan

There was a time, not very long passed, when it used to be a hard job getting together material for the Belgravian and pretty easy to get a mention - partly because I wrote it but mainly because there was not much competition. A YEAR TO REMEMBER

JOHN KEOWN

Belgrave's walking teams are becoming almost invincible, going from strength to strength with no limits yet in sight. Last year was highly successful with Belgrave winning three of the four National titles. JOHN KEOWN reviews that 1968/9 season and regards it as----

A YEAR TO REMEMBER

During the R.W.A. year 1968-69 (September '68 to August '69) Belgrave Harriers' Walking Section equalled, if not surpassed, the achievements of the great club teams of 1957 and 1960 The year ended with the club having won many races including the classic London to Brighton and three of the four National Senior Road Walking Championships. The walkers also secured second place in the remaining Senior event and a fourth place in the National Junior 5 Miles. In 1957 and 1960 when Belgrave teams won all three National Senior titles (a fourth, the National 20 Kilos, was added in 1965), the club had the advantage of being able to call upon the sevices of such internationals as Stan Vickers, Eric Hall and George Williams. Today, with only one international, Ray Middleton, among the active club walkers, success depends more on the efforts of the ordinary clubman and the drive and initiative of the Hon. Walking Secretary and the section captain.

Much of the credit for the success of Belgrave walkers during the year must go to Pat Duncan, the Hon. Walking Secretary. Pat is not only a hard working secretary but a capable leader and a very gifted long distance walker. Nothing is too much trouble for this tall Sussex man, for in addition to his other duties, he drives fellow Belgravians and others in his own mini-bus many miles, to races all over the country. Unfortunately his tremendous efforts on behalf of the club, during the period under review, meant a slight falling off in the standard of his own performances in such races as the Manchester to Blackpool and the London to Brighton. In June the previous year, Pat established a new course record of 7:57:01 for the 51¾ miles manchester to Blackpool race.

Ray Hall, the road walking captain, also contributed much to Belgrave's success. Ray, no longer as active a competitor as in the past, lightened considerably the burden of the Hon. Secretary with his help in the clubhouse, on the road and with transport. Alf Harley, Jack Goswell and Frank Jarvis, although required to devote a large proportion of their time to R.W.A. activities, played a not insignificant part in the Belgrave success story. Others who played their part were all those club members and friends who looked after the needs of the walkers during the middle and long distance races.

The story began on Saturday, September 7th, 1968 when the Belgrave team of Pat Duncan, Dave King, Bob Farley and George Beecham snatched victory from Bristol Walking Club in the London to Brighton by the narrow margin of 3 points (24 to 27). Pat finished third in the race, 23 minutes behind Bristol's Brian Eley, with a time of 8:23:24. This was the club's 17th victory in this race. Their first was back in 1921.

Since 1958, when Belgrave recorded their only win in the National Junior 5 Miles Championship, the club have had great difficulty fielding a team in this event. However on October 19th, at Manchester, Carl Lawton, Brian Ward and Paul Duckett filled fourth team position.

The winter season began well for Belgrave with Len Duquemin and the club taking top honours in the Highgate Open Seven. During the weeks that followed the team won the Enfield Open Seven and filled second place in both the Belgrave and Metro. Police Open Sevens. In November, whilst others struggled for top honours at home, Pat Duncan competed against the top long distance men of seven nations at Lugano in an International 100 Kms. race. He took second place behind S. Lindberg (Sweden) in 10:20:56.

Our ever cheerful West German, Will Wesch, set the race walking world alight in the early months of 1969, when he won the Southern 'Garnet' 10 Miles Championship, the Surrey Co. 10 Miles Championship, the Ipswich H. Open 10 and finished third in the Basingstoke A.C. Open 10. By winning the 'Garnet' he followed in the footsteps of Belgrave internationals, Stan Vickers and Ray Middleton who both began their rise to fame by winning this championship.

The club secured third place in the 'Garnet' team race, a very creditable performance in view of the fact that Belgrave had filled first place the previous year. Barring clauses in this Junior status championship prevent previous winners from competing again. Will Wesch led the team to victory in both the Ipswich and Basingstoke races. Unfortunately his mode of progression did not satisfy the judges in the R.W.A. National 10 Miles Championship at Morden on March 15th, so Belgrave had to be content with second place behind Leicester W.C. Three weeks later in the Metropolitan W.C. 20 Kilos. at Battersea Park the club tied with Coventry Godiva H. on points, each scoring 32. Under the A.A.A. rules first place went to Coventry as their fourth scoring man finished nearest the winner.

May was a great month for the walkers who wear the claret and gold. On the 3rd at Bexley in a two race, four a side international match between West Germany and Great Britain, Ray Middleton finished second in the 35 Kms. in 3:3:15 and thus helped his country to a team victory. A week later Ray led Belgrave to their second successive National 20 Kilos. victory. Other members were Len Duquemin, John Moullin and Will Wesch. At the end of the month the same team, this time lead by Will Wesch, who finished in 4th individual position, won the Leicester Mercury Open 20 Miles.

In June the club scored its 12th win in the National 20 Miles Championship. This was indeed a welcomed success for the walkers as they had not fared too well in this Championship since 1965 when they found themselves outside the medals for the first time since 1932. The scoring four were the same as those who took top honours in the National 20 Kilos. The forward walking of the other four members of the eight man team; Bob Coates, Pat Duncan, Bob Farley and Dave DeBondi enabled Belgrave to win the Nijmegen Shield awarded to the first team to finish its declared eight. During the month Bob Coates, an ex-Scottish Junior racing cyclist, covered 3,000 metres on the track at Brighton in the fast time of 13:16:4.

At the beginning of July Belgrave took first place in their own invitation Inter-Club Track Walks at Battersea Park. In the Birmingham Outer Circle 25 Miles Walk on July 5th, Ray Middleton finished second and lead the club to a very easy team victory. Later in the month the club were again in action in the Midlands, this time in the National 50 Kms. Championship at Redditch. Belgrave started 11 walkers including new recruit, G. Klopfer from the U.S.A. Klopfer walked as a guest and finished third just in front of Ray Middleton who was placed third in the championship thus making sure of selection for the European Games 50 Kilos Walk. Ray with the support of John Moullin, Len Duquemin and Dave King took the team title. This was Belgrave's 15th team victory in this race. In fact the club have only been outside the first three on 4 occasions since a team-championship was added to this event in 1932. Once again the reliability of our walkers was clearly demonstrated when they were the first team to finish their declared eight, thus winning the Milan Trophy.

The year ended with Belgrave recording a good victory in the Annual Hastings to Brighton 38 Miles Walk. The club finished 4 walkers in the first six and with only three to score easily retained their team title, with the very low score of 9 points. Ray Middleton took third individual prize whilst Pat Duncan in 5th position got the prize for being the first Sussex man to finish.

The year 1968-69 was certainly a great year for Belgrave walkers, perhaps they will make the present year an even more memorable one. Can they win all four National Senior titles? Their easy win in the recent London to Brighton certainly shows that it will take some very high class performances by their opponents to stop them.

Four Minutes From Bronze

RAY MIDDLETON

Three months have elapsed since the European Athletics Championships were held in Athens but the thrills of British successes are still fresh in our minds. Paul Nihill's great win in the 20 Kilos Walk tended to overshadow Britain's performance in the 50 Kilos event when two of our trio were unable to finish. But who better was there to carry the burden alone than our own RAY MIDDLETON. One of the Country's most reliable performers, and a seasoned international with a Commonwealth "silver" and two European "fifths" to his credit, Ray tells of that hot September day in Athens and how, after nearly 4½ hours of walking, he was just----

Four Minutes From Bronze

With so many good performances by British Athletes in Athens my 5th place seems a bit low in the order of merit. However, I was quite pleased with my performance. Having gone well in training since my arrival in Athens I thought that given a hot day I might have a good one; and sure enough the day of the race was hot. I had been stocking up with salt over the previous week as I no longer take salt on race days; only a small drop in drinks. I find that this cuts out sickness during the race.

The course was along the coast road with very little shade, and that blue sea to keep your mind off walking!. Hohne of East Germany went right from the gun with the Russians in the next three places, then two groups some way behind the leaders. I think the first 10 Kilos was the hardest bit as the sun was at it's hottest. I started out with sun glasses but could not see half the time so I had to whip them off. The first drink I had was a life saver, from that point on I could not wait to get to the next drinking point;in all I drank 14 bottles of Coke each one going down better than the last! It's true what they say about Coke("things do go better")--for me anyway!. At 20 Kilos I had got with the main bunch. Up to this point the course had been flat but to the surprise of many we found we were now walking up what seemed like the side of a mountain, and I do believe this was the turning point for many of the more fancied boys. At the turn I still felt quite good and I think I must have been about ninth at this point. I was walking with the two East Germans but I felt that once we were back on the flat I might have a job to match their speed. I soon passed one of the Russians who looked far from happy and this bucked me up quite a bit but at 30 Kilos the Germans put the speed work in and I found myself walking on my own once again . I passed our Hotel with 20 Kilos to go and this was just like walking past one's house but not stopping to have a quick chat to the wife and a quick drink, as I do at home. Once past this point I felt strong again and realised I had gained another place as I passed one of the Germans sitting in the road---disqualified. Despite the heat of competition I felt sorry for him as it had happened to me in Belgrade 7 years ago. But now I had the second Russian in my sights with 10 Kilos left. Pushed on by supporting John Webb I did all I could to nail him but with the sight of home ahead he just held me off. At first I felt disgusted at not catching him but he had walked well and that's all that counts in Walking.

It was a wonderful trip with great performances from British Athletes but I also remember the sadness of Dick Taylor and Bill Adcocks who, with so many good runs behind them, had to have a bad one on the big day. This also went for my own walking team who failed to finish the '50'---for them let's hope that they get another chance to prove their worth.

25 years ago

OCTOBER 1944------Since the last issue we have learned, with great sorrow, of the following deaths:- K.N. WOODS died from wounds in Normandy. He was a former Surrey Junior Cross-Country Champion and was placed in the South of Thames Junior race. KEN SHEAIL was killed in action with the Hampshire Regiment in Italy, having previously been wounded there. His favourite distance with us was the 880 yards. FRANK HANDLEY was killed in action with the Irish Guards. LIONEL PENNY was killed in action with the Recce Corps. He was one of our most promising cross-country runners.

Prisoner of War news is scant. LES BENNETT is still in Japanese hands. ARTHUR BEALES seems to have settled down to life in a prison camp and has "made himself at home"---if such a phrase applies!. FRANK HALLS was wounded in the legs before capture and it is feared that he will not beable to race walk again. DAN & ALEC PATIENCE were taken prisoners during the fighting around the Anzio beachhead and sent to Germany.(Dan had earlier been wounded in N. Africa and was home for a while). Their older brother GEORGE, with whom they were inseparable since the outbreak of war and have fought together during the whole of the North African and Sicilian Campaigns, was killed in the same engagement.

PAT DRISCOLL has been posted as missing with his fighter ten days after D-day-and REG SHEPHERD is still amongst the missing. BERT FOOTER has been right up in the front at Normandy and would welcome a rest in the rear. JOHN FLOWER is back in England after sixteen months sojourn in the "White man's grave", and now has one ring on his sleeve. LEN ROLLINS is on board H.M.S. Lochaibort.

ALF MILLER was slightly wounded in Italy and was pleased to have a clubmate as a visitor---ALF HARLEY!. We have recently learned with regret that he has been reported missing, probably a prisoner of war. LAURIE JACKSON was wounded in Normandy by shrapnel from a mortar bomb and spent a time in hospital at Bradford. His last copy of the "Belgravian" has a large hole in it---it was in his pocket when he stopped his "packet". We'll try and get the next copy on bullet-proof paper!.

ERNIE DUFFETT lost his home through a direct hit from a flying bomb. Mrs Duffett and some of their children were buried in the debris and were rescued, fortunately without injury. Ernie (a War Reserve Policeman) was on duty at the time. Prospective "pot-hunters" are warned that Ernie has recovered his handicap sheets!. CHARLIE COOK also had his home demolished by a flying bomb and sustained minor injuries necessitating a short stay in hospital. JOHN BROWN lost his mother and a sister when their home was destroyed, and we extend to him our deepest sympathies. THE MEN BEHIND THE TROPHIES — R.W. RICKETTS

A.A.H.

R.W. RICKETTS

Much has been written at various times of that great personality BOB RICKETTS, who probably wielded more influence on the Club than has any other man, alive or dead,in our 82 years of history. Bob had run in our events in the 1890's but it was immediately after the first World War that his sense of leadership caused him to work incessantly for Belgrave, and to lay the foundations for the greater things to come. As President of the Club he naturally seemed to be the dominant personality --in the pleasantest possible way. He had that great asset of being able to get others to share and work for his enthusiasms; and he was able to impress his personality on everyone he met.

He had run at most distances, was a boxer of no small repute; and on the persuasion of Jimmy Belchamber, had become a distance walker of outstanding merit.

However his greatest work for the Club lay in the planning and building of the Club for which he had so much affection. His powerful personality attracted other members, he fought the London County Council over and over again for a cinder track in Battersea Park, and when it was eventually installed fought again for adequate dressing accomodation to allow it to be used with advantage. He had a welcome for every newcomer; and had a keen eye to discover, and to use everyone's talents to the best advantage. And he worked and worked for Belgrave in the days when money was scarce indeed; and he himself was an example of intense enthusiasm for all the Club to see.

His outstanding personality, fortunately, was such that the Club was never in the position of becoming a one-man band. His qualities rather melded with the keeness and abilities of many other workers for the Club contemporary with him; and Belgrave gradually grew from an unimportant and relatively unknown group to a Club with a personality and a future.

Unfortunately Bob never lived to see the really great days for he died at the early age of 47, in 1927. The torch was passed to other hands.

During his lifetime Bob made many presentations to the Club which still endure. He gave us the "R.W. RICKETTS" Cup for our Junior 220 yards Championship; and the "R.W. RICKETTS" Shield for our 20 miles Walking Championship.

The "Belgrave" Cup for the Long distance Walking Championship also came from him; as did also the "DORIS" Shield (named after his daughter) for inter-club competition.

He gave of himself in ample measure in his years with the Club; and we still have the permanent records of his generosity in the form of trophies as detailed above to remind us, and to encourage us, and to enthuse us.

CROSS COUNTRY — Our new Honorary Cross Country Secretary GORDON BISCOE writes

GORDON BISCOE

Has Bill Lucas finally gone? Has been the question asked in recent weeks; although I don't think anything was implied by the word "gone". However after 17 years as cross country secretary one cannot just leave the matter there without first expressing our grateful thanks to him for all the efforts, frustrations and pitfalls--endured in that time, as well as the successes and triumphs. (For records please see 17 years of Belgravian! ). He hands over a legacy of hard work which I know will be hard to follow.

Since taking on the job, opinions have been expressed to me about a new approach and fresh ideas being a good thing---this might well be true, but whatever fresh approach there might be one fact still remains,---that anything we achieve will only be done with a maximum effort from all concerned. With training schedules now common place at around 80-100 miles (or more!) per week no one will appreciate just being told to train harder---(although if the cap fits-wear it!). But we must expect everyone to be fit at the right time for the right occasion and then having got there to give of his best.

We have, for example, already suffered two defeats this season at the hands of City of Stoke A.C., another 'new' club resulting from an amalgamation. This is the modern trend we have to face as well as the 'old enemies!. It is no use saying we have'strength in depth' and leaving it to someone else, for it is obvious there is no room for complacency or passengers. In short we must think and run like a team.

Once again in the new year we shall start the string of championship races leading up to the National. If every one does his best (we can ask no more) I'm sure success will be ours again.

A COUNTRYMAN'S JOTTINGS

Countryman

Many happy returns of the day CHARLIE SMART. On December 9th the inimitable Charlie reaches the magnificent age of 60. Nothing special in that alone, perhaps, but couple it with a 5:17 mile and it makes him the Nation's top athlete in his age group.

Charlie's long standing training routine of three mid-week visits to the Duke of York's track and Sunday mornings at Arthur Whitehead's has been a feature of the athletics scene for many years. Of late he can be seen regularly in the company of that other "character" and veteran, Joe McDonald. Thanks to each other they are both running better than for some time and Mac will be the first to vouch for the remarkable ability of his 60 years old companion. In his famous eloquent manner Mac described Charlie as "bloody great!". And few would disagree.

After 14 years at the helm of Belgrave's cross-country affairs BILL LUCAS has finally decided to call it a day. He had dearly hoped to see the Club win the National during his term of office but three consecutive fourth places was the nearest our teams could get. In this day and age this is frustratingly close so his optimism to the bitter end was fully justified. Bill must have written several thousand cards during those years and spent hundreds of hours of telephone time encouraging, advising, persuading and cajoling runners so that Belgrave could produce a truly representative team on the Saturday. His prescence at virtually every fixture made him a familar figure to athletes from all clubs.

Although Bill has relinquished the reins of the cross-country section he still remains one of it's most ardent supporters and already this season he has been seen fervently scribbling down race positions and times. He is still the Club's Hon. Handicapper, of course, so it will be some time before our runners are able to pull a fast one!.

With Charlie Smart having preceded Bill Lucas as our cross-country secretary this is an appropriate spot to say something of "new man" Gordon Biscoe. Gordon is already a familar personality to active Belgravians through his former office of Social Hon. Sec. and his present position as Road Running Hon. Sec. If the cross-country men get the attention and service enjoyed by their road running colleagues then they will have much for which to be grateful. Gordon produced several useful runs himself in the "fifties" and has by no means thrown in the towel yet, but the emphasis of his devotion to Belgrave has gradually switched to administration where, if he takes on anything else, he will become a Les Cohen Mark II!.

My picture was specially taken by Ron Linstead who had difficulty in getting Gordon's hand just right. Apparently the fingers have been trained to hold only pint beer mugs!.

It is with many regrets that we say au revoir to PETER MORRIS who, after 6 months with us, has returned to his native Blackpool. If running was the only thing in life then Peter tells me he would have no hesitation in remaining down South, but personal and business committments oblige him to make the move. During his six months with Belgrave he has given stirling service and has been a popular personality with our runners. We will follow his career with great interest, wishing him the best possible success and should he ever return South again he can be sure of a warm welcome from Belgravians.

The prize for the biggest anti-climax of the year goes to the A.A.A., inaugural six-stage National relay at Crystal Palace. Whether there was a strong demand for such a race in the first place is doubtful. Given that there was, then the consultation, planning, publicity and organisation in general was abysmal to say the least. Portsmouth were reputed to be one of the initiators of the six-stage idea, but they had pressed for 3 long and 3 short stages on a point to point course. The decisions on date, distance and venue were taken so arbitrarily that it was no surprise to those in the know to find a meagre NINE TEAMS on the start line. We were in two minds ourselves whether to appear.

The presentation "ceremony" was even worse than the debâclé at Leicester earlier this year. Although there were medals this time, there was nowhere suitable to present them.

For National championships to be handled in such a way is bad for the sport. They should be very special highly efficent affairs oozing with National "atmosphere". Unless this is done they are in danger of becoming just another race with no one caring very much who wins.

Once again we say farewell to New Zealander Kelly Diprose, who is off globe-trotting once again. This popular Kiwi would have been a strong force in our teams if he had been able to stay here long enough to settle down. There are no such things as plans with Kelly so no-one will be surprised if he suddenly turns up at the start of some future race. His main rival over here, though they rarely clashed, was Bob Smith. It is Belgrave's good fortune that Aussie Bob has decided to settle in England---and to prove the point he recently got married and bought a house in Purley!. However, we shall not be reaping any dividends from his move until the Spring as Bob returned to Australia this month to wind up his affairs there (we presume he means business ones!).

My picture of Peter Carton (Youth) and Richard Langley (Boy) was taken in Belgrave Hall. One day the backcloth might well be changed to that of a T.V. studio. Peter's progress this last year has been quite exceptional. Training with Bill Curtin induced him to haul himself up to Bill's standard in a matter of months, which was well illustrated by their first and second places in the Surrey County Youths 1500 metres this summer. Peter's efforts have induced others to follow suit and climb to the standards set by Bill Curtin, so instead of having a solitary spearhead and a group of supporting runners down the field we now have Curtin, Carton and John Stow with little separating them and Tony Binda and Les Harris determined to close that gap. If they can stick together for a few years and overcome the inevitable set backs then Belgrave has little to fear from the future.

Peter CARTON (left) and Richard LANGLEY (Photo:Ron Linstead)

Richard Langley is in an age-group behind Peter and is probably at his best on the track, where he looks a fine prospect. Nevertheless he is no slouch over the country and in 12 months time he should be repeating the present times of our top trio. Our wealth of Youth and Boy talent must eventually throw up a Junior team capable of winning the National.

Prospects for the coming major cross-country events are far from clear. There are always ifs and buts of course but will the loss of Diprose, the absence of Smith, the increased business pressure on Thresher and the moving house to Rochester of Geoff North have any serious effect?. It is difficult to say. Geoff's move makes him a near neighbour of Tony Fairclough so a training partnership could be beneficial to both. Mann, O'COnnor and Piotrowski are all going better than ever while Bicourt has been showing excellent early form. Bill Dance is keeping in fine trim up in Huddersfield, and Major and O'Hara are also running into form again. Last, but by no means least, we have that ever-present spearhead Gerry North and the reliable Trevor Hart. There should be enough combinations from that lot to produce a powerful team, but much depends on non-athletic influences---we can only hope that such factors will be kind to us.

G.L.C. - FOUR IN A ROW!

G.L.C. Cross-Country Championship--November 22nd

A few days of steady rain coupled with the venue of Parliament Hill and one immediately thinks of that familar sea of mud and heavy going. This was what Gerry North had in mind too when he set out to defend his individual title for the fourth successive year. However fate was against him this day for on arrival he found Spring-like weather and very firm going. An hour or so later his title had gone to a fit Bob Richardson of Blackheath Harriers. Richardson had opened a gap of about 10 secs on Gerry at the half way stage which he maintained to the end.

Lionel Mann showed Pat O'Connor (and everyone else!!) that he means business on the country as well as the road by being our next man home, whilst Trevor Hart was having a few shocks---being pressed in the latter stages by Laurie O'Hara and with Adrian Major, now returning to something like his proper fitness, breathing down his neck.For O'Connor, Fairclough and Kerr it was not their day. Worth noting was Jim Rimmer's good run in his first main outing of the season and Bob Glover's triumph over arch rival Brian Gorman.

Although Gerry lost his title he nevertheless led Belgrave to victory again for the fourth successive year.

MANN TO MANNING

Club '7'---Wimbledon, December 6th

Lionel Mann means business. Up to now he has been regarded as a road man providing useful support over the country, but this year he intends to wear a cross-country label, as of right, and show his heels to those who reckon they make our scoring six. In annexing the "Blackstaffe" shield on a cold and wet December afternoon he showed a useful bunch of runners where to get off and stretched the field to such an extent that it was a quarter of an hour before near-namesake Manning closed them in. (It is rumoured that Charlie is to grow a beard and visit Somerset House!.).

Thirty two runners started, getting away this year before the inter-Varsity race crossed their path. Forty eight seconds later a breathless Laurie O'Hara appeared at the start with the unenviable task of chasing the field.

After the first lap (14:21) Adrian Major was towing Piotrowski, Mann, Dear and Fairclough round in a tight bunch. A hundred yards back were Black, Rimmer and Smith, and 150 yards further behind was John Kelly---with Laurie O'Hara hard on his heels. Laurie must have covered that first lap a good 8 to 10 seconds faster than the leaders!.

With two laps complete it was Mann (28:37) setting the pace from Major, both 10 yards clear of Fairclough with John Dear running the race of his life another 50 yards back. Although still making places, O'Hara paid the penalty for his fast start and dropped about 40 seconds on the leaders.

Adrian hung on doggedly during the final circuit but was outrun in the closing stages by the narrow margin of 5 seconds. Tony Fairclough was a none too cheerful third followed closely by an exuberant John Dear who looks set for his best ever Winter.

The handicap went to D. Trow, who ran some five minutes faster than anyone thought him capable and who was fast closing on Charlie Walker in the finishing straight. Jim Rimmer ran better than his fourth handicap place suggested so perhaps we will be seeing more of him than usual this season.
