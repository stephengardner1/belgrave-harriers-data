WELCOME TO MATTHEW STICKLES

With fine performances in the Surrey League and Junior Southern C.C. Championship Matthew Stickles has shown what a fine addition he is to Belgrave's middle distance ranks.

Born in February 1969, Matthew is still a junior in the Country in the UK, but is too old now to compeete as an International under the different International age groupings. He started running at the age of 12 and has performed with distinction at all age groups. In 1987 he was 3rd in the Junior AAA's 3000m in a p.b. of 8.18 and gained his first GB Junior vest as a result when he ran 8.20 in a W.Germany & Swiss triangular match. He came third in the '88 Junior later. Counties and his track p.b's include 800 - 1.53.7 and 1500 - 3.51.2. He has never run a 5000m although under Coach Steve Gosling's guidance this will be remedied in 1989. Certainly Matthew has wintered well, including a strengthening programme under the aegis of Mike Winch at the Belgrave Gym. We look forward with anticipation to Matthew's summer performances.

NORFOLK WELCOMES BELGRAVE AND BELGRAVE WELCOMES NORFOLK

Five years ago we had a Cup Match in Norwich and past President, Tom Morrell and his family, who had moved to the area many years earlier, backed up our travelling supporters. At that time a few of us could have anticipated that in 1988 several of Norfolk's top runners would chose Belgrave as their club. Bill Laws has talked to our latest batch of Internationals who you will be seeing in action in the near future.

PAUL EVANS - Age 27:

Paul Evans started running to get fit for football which he played for Lowestoft. His training involved 2 days per week and it was with some reluctance that he entered his first road race - a Carnival 10km in August 1986. To everyones surprise he finished 7th in 33.33. A month later he ran 32.30 and was hooked. Hugh Elder took over as coach and within 6 months Paul had run two ½ marathons, the second one in 65.30. In August '87 he gained his first England vest when he ran in a Home Counties ½ marathon in Scotland. He finished a very creditable 6th reducing his p.b. to 64.31. In 1988 he ran his first 3000m and became the first Brit. to run a sub 8.00 in a debut race. Since then he has represented the ECCU in several overseas races. His track p.b's (as of February 1989) are: 1 mile - 4.02, 3000m - 7.57, 2 miles - 8.32.2, 5000m - 13.57, 10,000m - 29.08.

NEIL FETHERBY - Age (stopped counting at 27)

Neil Fetherby is a true ultra-distance runner who thrives on the 120+ miles/week training schedules imposed by coach Bud Bolders. His International career started in 1986 when he represented England in the Aberdeen Marathon. He ran the Berlin Marathon twice and on the second occasion set a p.b. of 2.17.35. He gained a full GB vest in the 87 Koscite Marathon following wins in the Wolverhampton and Norfolk Marathons. Since joining Belgrave in 1988 he has been dogged by injury and only now is getting back to past form. His 10KM is 30.20.

DARREN MEAD - Age 20 years

Darren Mead is a legend in his own young life time. Twice winner of the Youths National C.C. Championship he gained his first England vest in the 1986 Junior World Championships where he finished 12th. He gained a GB vest in 1986 where he ran the World Junior and ran 14.12.3 for 5000m at the age of 17. 1987 saw a period of re-trenchment and it was not until 1988 that Darren started to re-gain form. He finished 2nd in the National Junior C.C. Championships and following a move to Sutton joined Belgrave and immediately made our British League Team running 800, 1500 and 5000m. His current p.b's confirm a surprising turn of speed which could well prove his most valuable weapon in the years ahead. At present he is self-coached and self-employed in the carpet business. P.b's: 200m: 23.7, 400m: 50.5, 800m: 1.52.6, 1500m: 3.45.8, 3000m: 8.08.0, 5000m: 14.12.3, 10,000m: 29.20, 10 Miles 49.03.

IAN WELLS - Age 26

Ian Wells has been the Civil Service 400mH champion for the last 4 years. He has joined Belgrave to increase his opportunities for top class competition which is limited in the Great Yarmouth area. His choice of Belgrave was as a result of meeting fellow Civil Service athletes Abi Ekoku, Jay Busk and Derrick Williams. He aims to train at Battersea Park with Bob Bridges squad at least once per month. His p.b's are: 110H: 15.5, 400H: 54.72, 400m: 50.6 and Decathlon 4602 pts.

Leo Coy, President 1989.

Bill Laws

The election in September 1988 of Leo Coy as President gave particular pleasure to those who know how much work he has put into reviving Belgrave's Track and Field fortunes.

Like so many of our officials Leo's arrival at Belgrave and involvement in the management side was as a result of a series of coincidences. He started running seriously in 1968 following encouragement from TVH's International 800m man Peter Browne. He so easily could have joined TVH. But no, he 'phoned the AAA's who thought he might consider Belgrave, met Alf Harley at Battersea and in 1969 was in!

Belgravian reports of the time spotted this 'new talent' and he was soon making teams which included past greats like Gerry North, Bill Curtin, and Peter Carton. He ran in two 6 stage National road relay teams and five Southern 12 stage road relay teams. Somewhat later he won the Club 3000m s/c Champs.

But already other interests were encroaching. In Charlie Manning's Presidential Year - the 'infamous '74' - Leo was cajoled to organise a fund raising fashion show and did so with great success. In '75 he became an official NUT and since then has been responsible for compiling the UK Top 100 performances in the steeplechase 5000 and 10,000m. In '77 he was elected assistant Hon. Gen. Sec. first to Peter Hillier and then Ray Hall.

In 1980 when our 'A' team T&F side were struggling at the bottom of Southern League Div.1 Leo decided something had to be done and embarked on a team rebuilding exercise which virtually put paid to his own serious training plans. The success of his efforts could be seen as Belgrave moved from 35th in the National Rankings to its current 3rd place in Div.1 of the British Athletics League. 8 years of solid hard work. Add to that 4 years as Young Athletes Secretary and his current job as Coaching Secretary and you will appreciate just how much Leo has done for Belgrave.

During all this he has been ably and loyally supported by his family who on many occasions have been seen together at T&F matches looking after entries, recording, scoring and the myriad of jobs needed to ensure successful organisation. While wife Sue and daughter Nemone are strictly back-room helpers, son Jon has run for Belgrave over many years and is a rapidly improving Junior.

So well done Leo, you thoroughly deserve the Honour.

Bill Laws

Editorial · page 7

From the President.

LEO COY

Thank you for electing me President of this great Club. I am deeply honoured to undertake this role for this great Club. The position of President in the Club is unique in that you are not given a description of what you should be doing, you are elected and left to get on with it. There are a number of things that I would like your help to achieve, that will be enjoyable for you and very beneficial to the Club.

Belgrave Hall One of my aims as President will be to increase the use of Belgrave Hall by Club Members. The Hall is being given a much needed facelift, thanks to the efforts of Tom Carter. Mike Winch is basing all his activities under one roof at Belgrave Hall and this will give us a boost in income and make it a unique Centre of Excellence. A spin-off from this is that the Hall will be open for training on seven days a week. So, get up to the Hall as often as possible, get out for a run, and have a cup of tea or a pint afterwards.

New Members For a Club with a membership of over 600 you may be surprised to learn that we desperately need new members, of all ages, particularly young athletes, male and female. All new members should be encouraged to train from Belgrave Hall or from Battersea Park Track and this in turn will develop a foundation for the Club to progress in the future.

Belgrave Committee The organisation of Belgrave is placed by you in the hands of the Committee which is elected each year at the AGM. There are about six people who sit on this Committee, that virtually run Belgrave with very little input or ideas from the Club Membership. Should one of these stalwarts decide that they have had enough and cease their activities, then the club would be in real difficulties. We need new administrators, new ideas and more Club Members involved in running Belgrave. If you can spare an hour a week please let me or a Committee Member know. Any help however small would be much appreciated.

Young Athletes At one time we had a regular influx of youngsters to fill our teams. This influx has dried up and we now have very few young athletes, girls or boys. We have also got nobody to organise the Young Athletes, which is an unbelievable situation for a Club as distinguished as ours. It is for you to decide whether we continue with running a Young Athletes Section, if nobody comes forward in the very near future then we will have no alternative but to restrict the age of our new members. Please help us to avoid this happening.

Thank you for your time and your support so far in my year of office. Remember that no club can survive for long if everybody just turns up for a race or a training run. We need your help now to form a solid foundation for this our second century.

We have a great deal going for us at present, the performances of our Junior Cross-Country Teams throughout the eighties is the envy of most other clubs in the country, our Track and Field Team is our best ever. Have you been to support our Juniors or Track and Field side? You don't know what you are missing.

Good luck throughout 1989.

LEO COY President

IN MEMORIUM: R.M. 'MEL' THORPE (Born March 1938 - died November 1988)

CLIVE SHIPPEN

It is with sadness that we must report the death of Mel Thorpe. Mel was an integral part of the Belgrave running teams of the 1960's and, with his wife Mary, ever present at the numerous social functions the team enjoyed in those days.

When he knew he would be moving South from Coventry Godiva Harriers he joined Belgrave and was elected to membership in August 1963. By the time he had got himself settled in it was March 1964 and his first appearance for us was at the Bexley Relay where he was put in the 'C' team as a 'feeler'. His performance proved to the selectors that he was a serious candidate for our 1st team and he confirmed that view in his first season with us.

His enthusiasm for running carried him across a wide spectrum of distances, from one mile on the track to 20 miles on the road, all in that first season with us which culminated in Mel helping the Bels to a fine second place in the News of the World "Southern" London to Brighton Relay.

From then on he was ubiquitous, cramming in to four seasons what many would take double that time to achieve. Hardly a team was fielded without his presence. His best performances on the track were 4:19.8 for the mile, 9:04 for two miles and 14:13 for three miles. In the "5 3/4" he ran 28:34 and in the Yacht '3' the second fastest time ever of 14:37, only Gerry North having run faster.

But after 4 years of wearing Belgrave colours over track, country and road - and giving us sterling support in all our affairs - his job took him back to Coventry. He retained second-claim membership of Bels for very many years and he and Mary always looked forward to their re-union visits South.

The Belgravian of March 1967 wrote:-

"We suppose it was inevitable. To hope to hang on to Mel Thorpe indefinitely when he now lives in distant Coventry was too good to be true ..... he will now probably run against us in the National Road Relay wearing the colours of Godiva. Mel writes 'I thank all those at Belgrave for the happiest four years of my life'. It was a pleasure to have you Mel".

Our condolences are extended to Mary, Karen and Richard.

CLIVE SHIPPEN

EDDY BOTJLER

BELGRAVE HARRIERS - BRITISH BOBSLEIGH CHAMPIONS?

Through the 'Belgravian' I would like to record the sporting achievements of certain members of Belgrave Harriers. Normally they are seen exploding out of blocks or being part of our sprint relay team in the British League. I am of course referring to Dave Armstrong and Lenny Paul who, in November of this year, were part of the team which, travelling at speeds of around 70 mph, won the British Bobsleigh Championships in Austria.

The 'Two-Man" competition saw Dave Armstrong as the brakeman, with Mark Tout as driver, win the British Two-Man Championship. Two weeks before, this highly regarded team, scored another success by breaking the British record for the 'Two-Man Push Start' competition at Winterberg.

Both Dave Armstrong and Mark Tout were joined by Audley Richards and Lenny Paul to a very comfortable victory in the 'Four-Man British Championship. (Mark Tout being a decathlete while Audley Richards is a 49 seconds - 400 metre runner, both plan to be members of Belgrave Harriers next season.

These results mean that for the remaining competitions this season which includes the World Cup - European Championship and World Championship, we will go into the draw as Great Britain 1 and will, therefore, always be in the top section which is a very important factor.

For those who may be unaware, the purpose of the crew is to accelerate a Bobsleigh weight, either 390 kgs Two-Man or 630 kgs Four-Man, over a distance of approximately 50 metres. The greater the speed that can be achieved at the start, the faster the 'bob' should travel on its downward path over 1500 metres. Hence the need for sprinters with strength.

Competition between teams to gain the fastest push-start is very competitive with some teams recruiting athletes of the highest standard. The Canadians boast of two athletes that were part of their sprint relay team in Seoul, plus a Canadian decathlon champion. The Swiss two-man bobsleigh team has the Swiss spring champion as their brakeman.

If there are any readers who think that they might like to have the opportunity to bobsleigh and believe they possess the necessary physical qualities, bearing in mind the need to become used to warming up in temperatures of minus 10 degrees centigrade and feel they would like the challenge of sitting in a bobsleigh travelling at about 70 mph they should contact the author of this article for further information.

SOUTHERN LEAGUE ROUND-UP by Bob Bridges

The season got off to a bad start when I had to struggle to get a team out, even though the fixture was in London, but all credit to those who did, especially those who 'doubled' or 'trebled' up who threw or jumped 'out of event' as we managed to finish 3rd.

The third match was cancelled due to a waterlogged track at Huntingdon which we were declared the winners due to Belgrave being the highest placed in the league of the clubs involved in that fixture.

Our fourth match at Portsmouth was going to be out toughest match as two of the teams were 2nd and 4th in the division and so it proved as we finished equal third. We seemed to be getting stronger on the track and weaker in the throws. This match saw for the first time in Belgrave colours, Phil Sedgrove who sprinted the final 100m to win an exciting 5000m and Trevor Blanchard who won the Long Jump and injured himself on his first Triple Jump but still managed to finish 2nd.

For the fifth match we had to travel to Reading. Although we won 12 out of the 20 track races, due to athletes getting stuck in traffic and British Rail being British Rail, we virtually had no throwers (yet again) and very few jumpers and lost the match to South London Harriers by just 3 points.

We had to win the last match at Battersea to gain promotion. This was always going to be difficult as Milton Keynes were the division leaders and though there were only two teams in it there was only one winner ... and it weren't us. This match saw the debut of Matthew Stickles in Belgrave colours in which he won a physical and later a verbal 1500m and was also placed 2nd in the 800m. A look through the results will show the high standard of this match and that we did not give up without a fight with many pb's or seasons best being performed, notably Chris Cornwalls 2.03m in the high jump.

Although it was a disappointing season in the fact that we just missed out on promotion there were several encouraging performances that looks good for the future, notably Trevor Blanchard in the long and triple jump, Alistair Grant in the 400H, Gareth Allison who amazes with his talent and versatility, the French twins in the sprints and Joe Minetti who was finding his old form and discovering he could also run 400 just before he got injured towards the end of the season. We need more throwers and pole vaulters and I am indebted to our ex 400m and 400m runners who filled these events during the season.

J.G.C. (Ed.)

So ended the 1988 British Athletic League - Division 1 and the Club had had its best ever track season. Now let us give due accord to those two men who had engineered this success.

In alphabetical order Leo Coy and Bill Laws are the two Team Managers.

To organise two teams of track and field men for four matches each of 19 events, the whole gambit of disciplines, is no mean task and considering such things in a club such as ours where members live and work over such a wide area, co-ordinating the travel arrangements for at least 30 per match is only the start of things after the team has been selected and agreed.

The venues for the matches meant that in one case flights to Edinburgh had to be arranged, plus accommodation, Birmingham had different arrangements and the two London fixtures likewise. All this organising was done by Bill and Leo, at great expense in time and energy and no doubt money and also considering that they have their own professions to consider, we owe them a great debt of thanks and gratitude for a marvellous job well done. Thanks Leo and Bill. This tribute to Bill Laws and Leo Coy is in line with the sentiments expressed by the General Committee of Belgrave Harriers.

23rd Belgrave Relay - 26th March 1988

Colin Pearson

Stuart Paton started us off in this years relay and what an excellent start he gave us coming in second place and recording our fastest long leg of the day 24.34.

Graham Adams took over from Stuart and justified his selection into the team with his run 16.33 coming in six place "cries of come on me ole cocker" could be heard on his run in.

Francis Ward ran the third leg not a bad run from Frank proving to me that he can run well when he gets his act together, but also losing places coming in eleventh place with 26.12.

Martin Forrester played his part well holding onto eleventh place running 16.33 on the fourth leg a time which most seniors would have been proud of Martin should be a contender for a long leg next year Seniors beware these Juniors mean business.

Mike Hayes ran the fifth leg also holding onto eleventh place with 26.07 Mike gave a good chase but just failed to catch the Exeter man.

Not the usual scene for John Booth three miles? but showing the strength of character to run 17.02 on the six stage John lost one place finishing twelth always willing to show his loyality to the club.

The seventh leg was run by Dave North in 27.18 Dave was well under par for this years race, feeling the effects of little training and moving house, coming in 17th place we all know Dave can run better than this and hope to see him fitter next year.

Simon Maynard set off with a vengeance gaining four places giving us the lift we needed at this the Eighth Stage 16.19 faster than I had expected from him, showing the experience of a seasoned relay runner, unlucky not to catch the Luton man in front.

John Wright ran our second fastest long stage 24.49. I was impressed with John's run even though he had run a half marathon the week before, he took us up to Sixth place on the Ninth Stage.

Matthew Chaston running the Tenth Stage was another runner who excelled my expectations with 16.01 proving a point to me that he is a natural relay runner.

The Ladies.

Harry Porter

Report from Harry Porter

Firstly, I would like to congratulate Leo Coy, on behalf of the Ladies Section, on his election as Club President for 1988/89.

Once again, there was a disastrous start to the Women's Southern League Division 2, in the first match, when at Kings Park, Bournemouth, we failed to field our best team. Again we struggled in the following matches to maintain our usual mid-league position. Instead, we were fighting against relegation until the last meeting at Battersea Park which took us back to a respectable mid-table position for the end of the season. However, if we had had good results in our first meetings, by starting in mid-table, we may have finished at the top, rather than as we did, start at the bottom and finish in the middle. Also, of course, we have a depleted Junior side, meaning that every Junior is needed to turn out and support the team from the beginning of the season.

So - come on Ladies, let us start 1989 with a show of strength from both age groups.

Following on from that, our Senior Ladies once again did us proud in the G.R.E. competition. They were the only Non-Division I League Team to reach the semi-final where we duly finished 8th. We were congratulated by three or four of these leading clubs on our reaching the semi-final stage.

In the Rosenheim League we won all of the meetings except the final. That was a very close match indeed and we were pipped in the last event by Epsom's win in the Javelin by two points.

This means, of course, that we failed to the trophy that we had won for the first time in 1987, the inaugural year for the Ladies Competition also the Belgrave Harriers Centenary Year.

In the Thameside League, there is little one can say because of our lack of strength in the girls and minor girls, having only a few members in this.

This can also be said for the Boys and Colts sections. So if anyone reading this paragraph knows of any Girl or Boy in these age groups - please bring them along we need them to help ensure the club's continuing success in the future. Therefore, I would like to charge every member, male or female, to introduce one girl or boy in these younger age groups into the club this season. If everyone made an effort on the Club's behalf, we would be the envy of all the club's in the Southern Counties.

The Ladies Walking Section has continued to gain in strength and success, winning several team and individual awards for the club during the year.

Well done Ladies - keep it up.

There have been several good performances this year, most notably by Sian Spacey who finished third in the National 10K Road Walk. She also set new club records in the 2K, 3K, 5K and 10K Walking in the space of 3 months.

Andrea Coore, after being out of action, injured, all the winter 1987/88 and half of the summer following with a knee injury, came back with a bang to break the Club Long Jump record 3 times in 3 consecutive jumps bring it up to near the 6 mtr. mark with 5.86m.

Congratulations also, to Cindy James for winning the "Endeavour" Cup for amassing the most points throughout the summer season and for supporting every meeting. Well done and thank you Cindy. Runner up in the "Endeavour" Cup was Michelle McIntosh.

"Centenary Cup"

This prestigious cup is awarded, in the judgement of the committee, for the best performance by any female Belgrave Harrier in the year. The 1988 award went to Sian Spacey for her excellent 3rd place in the R.W.A. Womens 10Kms Road Walk Championship.

Runner up, once again was Michelle McIntosh for her fine win in the Southern Counties Girls Long Jump.

At this point, I would like to thank, firstly, Derek Jones for all the "backroom" work and for organising the meetings; an endless and thankless task which few people appreciate has to be done. Keep up the good work Derek, and many thanks on behalf of myself and the club.

WITH THE LADIES

Also those two Ladies - Win Shepherd and Jean Hutcheson who turn out to do the results recording on every occasion when we need them.

I would also like to thank Cynthia James, my assistant team manager for her enthusiastic help and support throughout the year. My thanks go, of course, to all those officials who have turned out to support the Ladies Section and especially the Officials Hon. Sec. Derek Crookes for his excellent work. To all the Athletes who have turned out and supported the Ladies Section throughout the season, my special thanks. Keep up the good work for 1989 and good luck.
