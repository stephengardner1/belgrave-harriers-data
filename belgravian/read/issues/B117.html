EDITORIAL

YOUR EDITORS.

Dear Fellow Belgravians,

In reading this issue it may be you will notice a slight undercurrent of melancholia. We are unable to report many successes in any section ; on the contrary, among other things, for the first time for many years, we are not now the Surrey County Champions at either Cross Country or Walking.

But, do these reverses give us cause for the rumblings of pessimism we hear. Your Editors do not think so ! In our Editorials we endeavour in a few sentences to review many aspects of the Club and we feel our intended words of wisdom do much to emphasise the necessity of certain things and alternately to soothe any unwarranted apprehensions.

Frank Thomson (whose obituary is published in other pages) with other pioneering Belgravians, were, in their days never deterred by pessimism, although they were up against tremendous odds. With due respect to those Belgrave stalwarts of yore, there was no possible chance to achieve anything much more ambitious than a club match win. Yet cheerfully they kept at their task and the full fruits of their endeavours were eventually realised in our many championship successes. Why, then, should we be pessimistic because we are having a few reverses ? What Frank Thomson and his colleagues did in the early days with a handful of enthusiasts we can do again, particularly with the splendid team of up-and-coming youngsters which we now have. So, away with the blues. We are still at the top of the tree in all branches of sport, the only difference being that we are sharing that position with others. Let us welcome this new state of affairs, and let it give us a greater incentive to regain our lost honours in the knowledge that it will be a much more praiseworthy achievement.

YOUR EDITORS.

Frank Thomson

YOUR PRESIDENT SPEAKS ON THE WINTER SEASON

E. A. DUFFETT, President.

The winter season just passing has not shown the Club in a very good light as regards successes in open events.

Both our runners and walkers have sadly declined in performance as reckoned on previous years, but on reading through a copy of the December, 1931, issue of the "Belgravian," I came across the following comment, which seems to me to meet the present situation :—

"The true strength of any club is its numbers, enthusiasm, sportsmanship, and standard of ability, which are the true index to a club's pre-eminence, and we need have no misgivings on these points."

As I stated in our last issue, we have unbounded enthusiasm as regards our Youths and Juniors, ; we also have the numbers, as witness our large fields in Club races. I do not think that anyone would question our sportsmanship, and I think our standard of ability is quite high.

What, then, you may ask, is the reason for the apparent decline both in our running and walking sections ?

Firstly, I think, is the inevitable retirement of some of our well-known members in both spheres, and their places are hard to fill right away, although we have the right material coming along.

Then we have the numerous studies being undertaken by our members in order to help their business careers, which curtails their training time, and then we have the marriages, which are bound to come along and unless they can "school" their wives to take an interest in athletics, any time taken for training is naturally resented by their better halves !

It is rather ironic that I mentioned our cycle of successes in the last issue, but it does seem that for the moment our run of victories has been halted, although some people may think this is good for the sport as a whole.

That we shall triumph again I have not the slightest doubt, but it does require the support and co-operation of all our members, which I sometimes think has been lacking in certain quarters, and let us hope that next season we shall see the "Bels" back on top once again in both the running and walking worlds.

E. A. DUFFETT, President.

E. A. Duffett

Obituary · page 3

F. G. THOMSON

A. A. H.

Once again the Club has suffered a very heavy blow in the death of Frank Thomson, which occurred on February 1st, in his eightieth year.

Frank was not a Founder Member of the Club, but joined Belgrave—he was a Westminster resident—in its early days. He had many successes as a sprinter and, as was the custom in those days, he entered races at all distances and "had a go" at most. He played an important part in the management of the Club and in 1893 was accorded the honour of Life Membership. In 1895 he was Assistant Hon. Secretary to F. A. Bool, happily still alive and living in Chelsea, and was also a Vice-President and Hon. Auditor.

In 1903 he had an operation which cut short his active career, and business and other interests claimed his attention in the years that followed.

In the early 1920's Frank returned to his first love of Amateur Athletics and it is doubtful if anyone worked harder in the cause of our sport during the next twenty years. Almost every day throughout spring and summer he was at Battersea Park Track, and during the winter season he was always to be found at our mid-week quarters on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and at our Wimbledon Headquarters on Saturdays—and always working in the Club's best interests. For a while he was our Gen. Hon. Secretary, when the Club was bounding ahead, and later became Hon. Treasurer of the Surrey County A.A.A., building up the first bank balance that Association had ever known. For several years he was (jointly with F. W. Blackmore) Hon. Handicapper for Road Walking events in the south and in permanent demand as chief scoring judge at most big walking events. He was well known as a member of the Southern Committee A.A.A., and had special interests on the handicapping Board of Control. In 1935-36 Frank became President of the Road Walking Association—the first Belgrave Harrier to hold that high office. He was a Past Chairman of the R.W.A. Southern Committee and highly honoured by being elected to Life Membership of the Road Walking Association and of Les Marcheurs Francais, in tribute to his work for walking internationally.

He became President of the Belgrave Harriers as the war clouds burst upon us. A partial breakdown in health in 1941 caused his retirement from business and removal to the Slough area. But still his interest in athletics persisted and he was frequently to be seen at our events. He was at our track championships last year, at the Club Dinner in October and our Open Walk in November—still showing that keenness which had always epitomised all his activities. He derived tremendous satisfaction from the Club's successes over the years with its succession of Championship titles and prominent performances, which had sprung from the few enthusiasts of the early years of the Club.

Many of us will have plentiful and rewarding recollections of our old friend. We recall Frank wielding the megaphone at our track meetings, giving helpful advice to members on training nights explaining the rudiments of the Field Events and his assiduous work at our various races and on the results sheets. He will be remembered for his self-less work at some of the early Nijmegen Marches and for his hours well spent in fostering and fathering the Southern Amateur Athletic League Field Events competitions, which may truthfully be said to have been the forerunner of the greatly improved performances we see to-day. We shall remember him with the open-top bus he hired for us on Brighton Walk and Brighton Relay days ; as Trustee of the Club, conscientious and hard-working for the Club's ideals. We shall think of him, only a few months ago, with sponge and drinks at Slough Feeding Station on the Bath to London Walk, where he spent many hours helping competitors.

Above all, we shall remember Frank Thomson as a great Belgravian who attained high office in the realms of athletics, yet worked effectively and unceasingly for Belgrave in fair weather and foul. Increasing years did not dull his enthusiasm, and his example is one we can all well take to heart.

Frank's daughter has kindly offered us a trophy in memory of her father, and this we shall gladly accept as a perpetual reminder of one who served the Club so well and so long. R.I.P.

A. A. H.

Expressions of sympathy and appreciation were received from the Amateur Athletic Association, Road Walking Association, Surrey Walking Club and many who had worked with Frank over so many years.

The following is an extract from a letter received from "Timber" Woodcock, Surrey W.C., and typifies the enthusiasm for our sport which Frank always showed :—

". . . I remember when I first started walking in 1930, Frank Thomson was known to me as the Southern Handicapper. In 1931, when I was a member of the Surrey Team to go to Nijmegen, I well remember that Frank was in charge of the R.W.A. Team and, with our Capt. Wilmot, he took great care of our welfare while in Holland. I remember a nine o'clock foot inspection at the end of our beds just before lights out every night. Who was first up every morning ? Why, Frank Thomson. We usually woke to find him fully dressed and having a go at those who were inclined to stop in bed at 3 a.m. Who looked after the team when on the road at check points ? Why, Frank again. And when it was all over he was the proudest man at the final ceremony when the awards were made. . . ."

CROSS-COUNTRY MEETING—FEBRUARY 14th

With a certain amount of trepidation, it is ventured to state that an informal meeting to discuss cross-country racing was called for the first time for some years by the Cross-Country Captain, Jack Brown.

The idea basically, was to stimulate interest in winter activities and to this end two items appeared on the agenda :—

1. The progress of the Cross-Country Section this winter. 2. To discuss plans for the future.

At this juncture it would probably be as well to say that there was an attendance of about 25 runners, and conspicuous by their absence were some of the great names of the Club.

The meeting was opened by Denis Brickwood outlining the somewhat docile outlook of the runners to-day which had its reflection in a degree of Club stagnation. This was not to imply that we were worse than any other club—in fact, we were still more virile than most. Progress, he pointed out, could be divided into two sections : 1, The progress in ability of the individual. 2, Progress in Club outlook and spirit. He had decided that there was progress in the ability of certain runners and this implied that the training had been good. There was nothing particularly controversial about this and most speakers agreed. This naturally led to the second type of progress and Ted Stimpson, soliloquising on the past glories of the Club, said that the spirit which led them on then, was required now. Efforts had been made to get people to run in organised packs and the outcome was poor. A view was expressed that various groups did their own training which did not differ in any way from organised training. Doug Briggs and George Stace spoke most ably in their own defence, but at the same time did not disagree with the necessity for having an organised run with the pack once a week. Jack Brown, the Captain, whilst not doubting the truth of Ted Stimpson's views, ventured to say that the nature of athletics had altered in some degree and the individual performance was now considerably more important than before the war. Perhaps this was not desirable, but you had to keep pace with the times. On pack running it was explained by Denis Brickwood that it was an opportunity for the selectors to see how men were running, and this non-attendance on Saturdays had caused mistakes in selection to be made. During the season changes had been made in the organisation of packs, such as changing pace-makers periodically, but it was surprising that some members were not aware that this was being done. It had to be explained that the pack-leader ran in the body of the pack and was able to watch individual runners. The pacemakers were varied in order to afford experience and add variety to the runs. In addition, of course, it did tend to teach judgment of pace and it certainly kept the pace at a pretty high rate. To those that had not indulged in these packs, Denis Brickwood extended a cordial invitation, and assured possible clients that they would benefit from it, and even enjoy it.

Passing on to the future, there was a general feeling that more thought could be given to the season before it actually started. It was conceded that the responsibility for about everything was left to Ernie Duffett, and whilst this was appreciated it did mean that, when anything was not to members' requirements, it just had to be Ernie's responsibility as no one else was involved. This was unfair and it was up to members to come forward and give their ideas to those concerned.

George Chantler said that the Captains and Vice-Captains were elected to do a job and it was up to the runners to back them up. He knew the day when, if the Captain said, "We're going to run 10 miles," everyone did it. He wanted to see that once more.

On fixtures, it was reported that support had been pathetic and it was lucky that there were three Bishop brothers and Bob Taylor. The average turn-out was about seven per away fixture and this was not good enough for a Club of our size. Perhaps this type of fixture was not wanted, or was it the venue ? A suggestion from Ernie Duffett was that fixtures be confined to clubs south of the Thames, and this was, in the main, agreed on by the meeting.

Another point raised was the desirability of nominating the Captains well before the Cross-Country season started, so that they could take some part in making plans for the ensuing season. It was even suggested that a small Committee should be formed to properly organise the season's activities. The suggestion almost amounted to heresy in club official circles, but it was considered that it would make members more conscious of the work that was necessary in a club of our size and which was now done by a matter of half-a-dozen officials.

The meeting discussed many other matters and it would take many pages to put it into print. The meeting was undoubtedly a success, and although it lasted about 1½ hours, it could have gone on much longer. It was somewhat difficult to end the meeting, but it was obvious that those who wanted to let off steam had had their chance.

And now a final word to those who were not able to be present. You must have some views on cross-country running—you must have given it a thought. So, watch the notice boards, or the press, or News Letters, or this admirable magazine, for the date of the next informal meeting.

FIELD EVENTS

J. CHAPPELLL, Hon. Field Events Secretary.

We are now approaching the Field Events season and I am hoping that the members of this section will get together and support George Powell, Captain, and P. Luxon, Vice-Captain. We have a very full programme, and I trust it will prove a successful one. Plaques are to be awarded, as suggested last year, for Club records. As you must know, to win one is very hard work, but it can be done and I hope 1953 will give you the opportunity.

I must say that Frank Simmons and Franz Stampfl have worked hard all the winter on Saturdays and Sundays at Battersea Park, and I am sure they will be all the more pleased if their work produces good results.

We have received and accepted an invitation to compete in the Sward Trophy (all field events) next May. The Club has not yet won this trophy, but we can win it if the field events men train hard enough. The events are 120 yards hurdles, 220 yards hurdles, 440 yards hurdles, 2 miles steeplechase, long jump, high jump, hop, step and jump, pole vault, discus, javelin, and hammer and shot. Entries will be sent in by the Club in April. We should very much like to cover every event, so carry on with the training and see what you can do. Further particulars can be obtained from Frank Simmons or myself.

One important thing I would ask of you, and this applies equally to every member of the Club : If you are selected to represent the Club in a match, do please turn up and give us your support, or else let your Hon. Secretary or Captain know in good time so that they can fill the vacancy. Many valuable points have been lost in the past, merely because we had no one to represent us, so, whatever you do, don't let the Club down.

Here's wishing you good luck and good sport.

J. CHAPPELLL, Hon. Field Events Secretary.

P.S.—Will any non-active members who are willing to act as officials, please get in touch with me before the track season starts ?

"BLACKSTAFFE" SHIELD RACE

The Club 7 miles c.c. championship was held on December 13th over our usual 2-lap course across Wimbledon Common, and although the field of 29 starters may be good by comparison with other clubs, it was a poor turn-out so far as the last two races for this trophy are concerned.

NEW CLUB CHAMPIONSHIP

A few years ago Tommy Green presented to the Club a very fine trophy, which was allocated to a Junior 5 miles Walking Championship. Unfortunately, owing to the shortage of Junior walkers, it has not been possible to hold a championship race until this year. It has now been decided to hold the first race for this trophy on Saturday, 18th April, the same day as the Club 20 miles Championship. So far there are about six Club walkers eligible, i.e., under 21 years of age on 1st October, 1952.

Some of our foremost walkers in the past have started as runners and we now invite any member of the Club who is eligible by age and feels he would like to swell the entry, to take part in the race. This may attract some of our young members who are not quite making the grade at cross country, and who may be potential walking champions. As an added incentive, our Hon. Treasurer, Syd. Roberts, has offered to present a prize for the first competitor to finish who has not previously competed in a walking race. Why not have a go and see what you can do ?

Make a note of the date NOW : Saturday, 18th April.

Notice · page 21

THE SUMMER SEASON

This is Coronation Year, and we can expect the usual crop of additional meetings due to this fact. We have already been invited to take part in meetings on Coronation Day itself, but do not think our members would appreciate having to turn out on such a day.

Our first Club meeting will be at Battersea Park, on Saturday, 2nd May, and the Surrey County 7-mile track walk will be included in the programme.

On Wednesday, 6th May, we visit Paddington Track for a match with Queens Park Harriers ; start at 6.30 p.m.

Then on 9th May, at Battersea Park, will be held the Surrey County Youths Track Championships, with the 6 miles run and Pentathlon also at this meeting.

16th May, at the new Wimbledon Park Track, is the date fixed for the Brockman Trophy meeting, and on the same day we are holding our open 20-mile road run from Belgrave Hall, start at 3 p.m., and we are hoping for a good entry.

On Monday, 18th May, we have a Club meeting at Tooting Bec, and have a Senior and Junior match against the Polytechnic H. and South London H. ; start at 7 p.m.

Whitsun Saturday and Monday we see the C.A.U. and British Games meeting at the White City, and there are also the usual number of “ open ” meetings on this day.

30th May is the day fixed for the Surrey County Senior Track Championships at Motspur Park, with the Junior and Youth Relays, also the Junior Hammer Throw.

On the same day we have accepted an invitation to compete in a Coronation Sports meeting at Aylesbury, with the usual generous six prizes in each event given by the Hazells Club.

6th June sees more County Championships, also a glut of Coronation meetings, and we have promised to support a meeting at the newly-opened track at Carshalton, and also to visit Walton A.C., at the Stompond Lane ground for an inter-club match.

On Wednesday, 10th June we have a Club meeting at Battersea Park, start at 7 p.m., and hope to put on our annual Schools Relay at this meeting.

Saturday, 13th June, is “ Kinnaird ” Trophy day, with our Club marathon championship being decided in conjunction with the Poly. race from Windsor to Chiswick.

Tuesday, 16th June, we have a match with Sutton and Cheam H., at the Sutton Stadium, and this is in the nature of a “ B ” team fixture.

17th June is the date of the L.A.C. Inter-Club Relay meeting at the White City, and it is to be hoped that our members will give full support to the various events.

Saturday, 20th June, is the day of the Southern Senior Track Championships, due to be held at the White City, and owing to the past large entries it may be necessary to introduce some qualifying conditions for this year's meeting.

Wednesday, 24th June, we have another Club meeting at Tooting Bec Track, start at 7 p.m., and if it is decided to include any handicap events we trust they will be better supported than in the past by our members.

27th June is the day of the Surrey County Junior Track Championships at Wimbledon Park, and the Senior relays will also be held at this meeting. Also on this day there are “ open ” meetings at Weybridge and Broomfield Park, Southgate, which should attract good support.

Wednesday, 1st July, we have a match at Victoria Park against the local club and the United Hospitals A.C.

On Saturday, 4th July, we have the “ Heathfield ” Trophy meeting at Stompond Lane, Walton-on-Thames, and we are the joint holders of this trophy.

Also on this day will be held the Southern Area Junior Championships, the venue for which has not yet been decided upon.

Wednesday, 8th July, is the date fixed for our open Inter-Club Walking Competition at Battersea Park Track, where the accent is on competing for one's club, with no awards given.

10th-11th July we have the A.A.A. General Championships at the White City and tickets for the Club stands will be available from Frank Webb, as in past years.

On 18th July there is the “ Waddilove ” Trophy meeting at Birmingham and we have also promised to send a team to Guildford on the same day, which should give a chance to all our members.

Wednesday, 22nd July, we have a Club meeting at Tooting Bec Track, and hope to include some members' handicap events on the programme.

The A.A.A. Junior Championships, also the Marathon and Relay Championships are due to be held on 25th July at Maindy Park, Cardiff, and the question of fares will come into what support our members can give this meeting.

Saturday, 1st August, is Club Championship day at Battersea Park. See summer fixture card for list of events, and we hope our members will support the various events, for which standard medals are awarded.

August Monday we have the “ Hazell ” Trophy meeting at Aylesbury and, seeing that our members did so well last year, I am sure that we shall not lack support for this fixture.

Wednesday, 5th August, we have a Senior and Junior match at Charlton Park Track against Cambridge Harriers, and although this is the holiday season, we hope our members will endeavour to turn up for these matches.

Monday, 10th August, we have a Club meeting at Tooting Bec Track, and as the evenings are drawing in at this time of the year, we shall only have a small number of events at this meeting.

Saturday, 15th August, we visit Alperton for a match against Thames Valley H. and Mitcham A.C., and our Committee have agreed to the “ Doris ” Shield being put up for competition once again. There will also be a Junior match at the same meeting and as we have several members in this area this should give them a chance to represent the Club.

22nd August is the date of our final Club meeting, due to take place at Battersea Park Track, and we have arranged a Senior and Junior match with Cambridge Harriers, and it is likely that the Club 3 miles track championship will be decided at this meeting.

On Saturday, 5th September, we visit Imber Court for a match against the Metropolitan Police A.C., and this will more or less wind up the track season, except for the evening floodlit meetings at the White City and the L.C.C. Championship finals, which are due to be held at Battersea Park on 12th September.

We have a new track captain in Len Marchant, who most of our members will remember as a half-miler of near international class and, together with vice-captain, Dicky Bartlett, they will do their best to make sure that every member is given a chance to show what he can do.

It is to be hoped that members will avail themselves of the opportunities offered them by the Club, and if any new member is in any doubt as to the method of approach, I shall be only too pleased to advise him in this direction.

All entries for Club events should be sent to me at least ten days in advance of the meeting date. A card will do, and any handicap fees can be paid on the day of the meeting.

Any enquiries can be sent to : E. A. Duffett, 57, Harcourt Road, Thornton Heath.

Frank WebbLen MarchantDicky BartlettE. A. Duffett

HERE AND THERE

Archie Bedford was first home in the British Workers' 5 miles c.c. championship, with Ron Mitchell in second place.

John Burr won the L.B.H. Novices c.c. race, and Eric Bishop was second in the Inter-Banks Junior event.

Brian Trower finished third in the Portsmouth A.C. open 5 miles road handicap race, with a start of 3 min. 10 secs., and has also been running with the R.A.F., Sopley.

E. T. Paterson is another doing his National Service training and has been running for the R.A.F., Yatesbury, team.

Another member to do well in R.A.F. events is P. N. G. Gilbert, and he captured the course record at Hednesford and also broke the R.A.F., Padgate, record. He has also twice run second to Sgt. McMinnis, the R.A.F. champion, and as he is still a junior, he should do well in future events.

P. J. Rossiter is now in the R.A.F

J. Hills finished second in the R.A.S.C. c.c. championship, but has now been posted to the Middle East.

R. D. Knights also writes to say that he has been posted overseas with the R.E.M.E., and sends best wishes to Alf Taylor and S. Jefferson.

Geoff Goode is now with the Royal Signals at Catterick, and ran in the Northern Command championships and finished 8th, and as K. L. Norris, of T.V.H., was 4th, it was evidently quite a hot field.

Brian Rice, who is at Cambridge University, was reserve in the 4 x 880 for the Inter-Varsity Relays in December last, and Alan Sexton ran for Cambridge in the 4 x 110 yds., 4 x 220 yds., and the 4 x 440 yds., and led his team to victory in the latter event.

A. J. Snoad was sixth in the London University c.c. race, and it is a pity that studies have prevented him from turning out for the Club, but he hopes to remedy this for next season.

P. R. Bagnall was first home in an Inter-College race when running for S. Mark & S. John, and he beat several well-known runners, and should be an asset to our teams on the road and over the country.

Congratulations to George Oliver on winning the Civil Service Road Walking championship, and he beat R. Carpenter, the holder, with A. H. Cotton, the Olympic walker, in third place.

Syd James was second in the L.B.H. 7 miles road walk at Wembley Park, with Harry Churcher, the holder, a non-starter.

We were pleased to see that B. C. Michez and M. A. Phillips, two of our youngsters from the Raynes Park County School, were selected to represent Surrey Schools XV.

While on the subject of Rugby, we see that Basil Walden and D. R. Childs are playing for the very successful Streatham R.F.C., and J. Powell-Jones has now joined this club, while Alan Sexton recently turned out for his Old Boys' side

There is a rumour that L. N. Bailey, the Streatham club's full-back and Surrey County player, is going to join the Club, as he wishes to go in for half-miling on the track.

We send our best wishes to Vernon Blowfield, who, now that he has permanently moved down to Oxford, has resigned from the Club, and has joined the local Oxford City A.C., of which club Derrick Burfitt is also a member.

We extend congratulations to Chas. Walker on once again being selected to run for Surrey in the Inter-Counties c.c. race, also to Bob Belmore, who is a second-claim member, and to Jack Brown, who was a scoring member of the Kent team, who were third in the team championship.

Also hearty congratulations to Les Cohen on heading the poll for the 20 elected members for the Southern A.A.A. Committee, and to Alf Harley, who was fourth in the voting list.

At the Inter-Varsity Sports Meeting at the White City on 14th March, Alan Sexton, our quarter-mile champion, was second in the 440 yards to A. Dick, the Olympic runner.

Ted Smith recently won the London Transport 7 miles walking championship.

Geoff Gregory, who decided a short time ago to turn to running, has now given up the idea and is training hard for walking again. He made a good start by easily winning the Tobacco Trades 7 miles Championship in London in March and is looking forward to a successful season on the track.
