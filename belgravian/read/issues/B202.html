Editorial · page 3

THE WAY AHEAD

Slowly but surely the improvement in our fortunes continues due to the dedicated hard work of a handful of officials and our active athletes. Sure enough it often seems that we take 2 steps forward to be driven back by the news of a key person either moving or giving up support due to personal reasons. Nonetheless we are now emerging with a younger, more dedicated management and athletics team able to face whatever the future holds with greater confidence than for many years past.

We can see now that it has been a close run thing. There has been, and there still is, a preponderance of Athletic Clubs in the S.E. of London. As a result officials and active athletes are spread thinly. In the recent past many people have pressed for amalgamations and groupings into stronger units. Indeed several senior Belgrave Officials still see this as the best way ahead. Not so, we trust, for the majority of you.

The way ahead is to make Belgrave strong and secure. In this issue we outline some of our plans which are being implemented this summer. What we urgently need is your continuing and resolute support.

grapevine

The day after the Surrey C.C. Championships Tom Carter spotted a familiar face at the Belgrave Hall Bar. His memory provided the answer - David Main whose name is on our International Honours Board. David finished 14th in the English C.C. National in 1946 and was selected for the Irish team. Tom was 16th that year and we won after all these years the National Title from Birchfield. David has re-joined Belgrave.

Geoff Pearson, who ran 25th in the same 1946 National astounds us all by still competing and produced a very creditable time in the March 81 5¾ at the ripe old age of 63.

We are all sorry to learn that Syd Spencer, former Police Long Distance walker and nowadays a Walking Judge has been in hospital with leg trouble. Unfortunately he had to have one leg amputated below the knee. We hope that he makes a good recovery and is soon back at the roadside.

Charlie MacLennan was knocked off his cycle and sustained injuries which caused him to be "off duty". However, you can't keep Charlie and his camera away from Walking events and he has been clicking away in Battersea Park.

Edmund Donovan had to cut short his visit to the U.K. last year. His wife, Daye, wrote to us on his behalf after he had had an operation on his hip; however, come Christmas we find him sending greetings in his own handwriting with a hope to visit us again this year.

Seen at a recent Police Dinner was Bob Readman, one of our many police walkers of the past, a former Club Champion and team member. Now living in Sussex, he sends his regards to old friends.

MONEY MATTERS

MONEY MATTERS

Late Subscriptions - Pay Now - Run Later

While the Dinner - Dance features high on everyone's priority list the paying of subscriptions apparently does not. At the February 1981 Belgrave Committee Treasurer, Jim Heathfield reported that in the current year 250 Members had not paid their subs., while 90 members still owed for the 1979-80 season. In view of the high cost of sending literature through the post to members, as well as the increasing printing costs for fixture cards, the Belgravian Magazine etc. your Committee has reluctantly decided that members owing 2 years subscriptions will be asked to resign from the Club. We really cannot afford passengers at any time, but particularly not now.

Fund Raising - 100 x 1 mile relay - May 31st 1981

Once again Gerry Sexton has taken up the challenge of trying to raise some interest in obtaining funds in sponsorship of the 100 x 1 mile relay. With this issue of the Belgravian you will receive a sponsor card. The chances are that you did not trouble to interest friends in raising money last year so why not really give it a go this time. Lets see what a concerted effort by everyone can do to bring that much needed money rolling in. Good Luck to you all.

Bill Couzens - Mine Host

In charge of the Belgrave Hall facilities is ex-President Bill Couzens. Aware of the need to utilise our major asset as much as possible, Bill has been leading a one-man campaign to raise funds by hiring out the Hall. At present an Art Class uses the Hall for two afternoons each week while football clubs use the Hall on Monday and Wednesday evenings. The Hall is now open, therefore, 6 days per week. A particular attraction for our sporting guests is the Belgrave Bar manned by our old and not so old faithfuls so that refreshments !!! are available to reach the worn out parts that need rejuvenation!

SOCIAL

SOCIAL

Dinner - Dance:

The currently difficult economic circumstances make it imperative for all of us to watch day to day expenses and in many cases some pruning of "luxuries" has been necessary. The Annual Dinner - Dance is not, we are glad to say, considered a luxury, but is an essential event where old friends meet and a great social time is had by all. This year, now under the complete and excellent control of Ron Langheim and family, proved no exception with close on 200 members and guests at the Dog and Fox. The cool damp November evening seemed far away as the revelries continued through to 1 p.m. and all too soon it was farewells all round - for many until next years event.

That everything went smoothly, and after a final count-up profitable (there was a positive cash balance to boost Club funds), was due entirely to the Langheim family who organised not only the Dinner itself, but also the increasingly popular Belgrave Draw. The top prize of £100 was won by none other than by Reg Hopkins who no doubt will be waiting to greet you all at the Belgrave bar. We shall see!

... and 40 years ago

From a letter by Edgar Good - Life Member, recalling training during the period 1902-1914.

"After a race or training spin you arrived back to a nice hot bath, prepared by our trainer, Bill Harland - that is if you were first home; after that it got thicker and thicker, probably the forerunner of the 'mud pack'. The trainer then got busy with the flesh gloves. Once up and down on the front, taking the legs in his stride, two on the back and a smack on the rump, finished you at 2d each. We made our own oils and used them. We all had different ideas, and the smell of whisky, turps, vinegar and what not, made the place reek."

In Memorium

In Memorium

George Chantler: We regret to inform our older members of the passing of a longtime friend and member.- George was a very active team member in his younger days and would have been a valuable member of our management team. However, fate took a hand. He moved away, was a founder member of Feltham A.C. in 1957 and helped to build up what is a now Borough of Hounslow A.C. He always retained a keen interest in Belgrave affairs and despite poor health over later years made every attempt to attend our Annual Dinner-Dance and renew acquaintances.

Tom Gladwin: Tom's interest in Belgrave Harriers was more recent than George's and naturally coincided with son John's increasing involvement in our sport and club. However, it is not the length of involvement that counts - it is the level of support, encouragement and friendship which one brings. All these qualities Tom displayed in full and in a very short time he built up a wide circle of friends who join with the Gladwin family in their grief. We all knew of the pleasure which Tom obtained from John's achievements and that Belgrave should have been the vehicle for this will remain a matter of pride for us all. He will be sadly missed.

Carl Lawton

WALKING

Except for the Chippenham we failed to win an 'Open' this winter, but were always placed. The reasons we did not pull off a win were through our inability to turn out a full team due to illness, injury and work difficulties and not through non-appearance. In the G.L.C. we did at last all get together and won comfortably from Surrey W.C. Our other main win was of course the National Junior at Coventry, fortunately we had three able to turn out.

This year we shall attempt to retain the McSweeney Trophy, but to do this we must produce good performances in the Senior Championships, including the 100 km. to be held this year at Stoke Mandeville, and then hold on to the Under 20s.

Paul Blagg has been the star of the winter for us, with Richard Dorman and Carl Lawton often on the side lines. He has produced some excellent performances including 71.26 in the G.L.C. for 2nd and winning the Met.Police 10m Championship in 72.15. With these sort of times one can anticipate some promising long distances from him this year.

Richard Dorman produced a stimulating performance to win the National Junior at Coventry in a championship best performance.

News of world traveller Allan Callow, he has now taken up full time residence back in his native Isle of Man and reverted back to Boundary Harriers, but still retains 2nd claim to Belgrave.

Congratulations to Doug Fothingham for his performance in winning his age group in the Worlds Vets Championship in New Zealand.

OCTOBER 11TH 1980 - CHIPPENHAM TO CALNE - 6 MILES

Gordon Doubleday

OCTOBER 11TH 1980 - CHIPPENHAM TO CALNE - 6 MILES

Saturday afternoon in Chippenham is a busy time with shoppers plus a continuous stream of vehicles. In addition the annual Chippenham to Calne walk contributes 144 walkers plus officials. Shortly before 3 p.m. a policeman halts the traffic and the lady competitors line up and are sent on their way by the Mayor. They are soon followed by 130 men and the 1980 race is under way.

Soon in the car to transport timekeepers to Calne we get caught in the traffic hold up. Eventually we catch the tail enders and we are not travelling at the speed of the 8 m.p.h. leaders. A left hand turn takes us away from the A4 and the walkers and eventually back on the course but now ahead of the field.

The finish on the Green at Calne where the Mayor awaits has separate funnels for men and women. Soon the warning cry arises "here he comes" and looking at the watches the time is just under 41 minutes as the leader reaches the top of the Green and Steve Barry (Roath) competes the final distance to the finish to break the course record set by Ken Matthews in 1962 of 42m 47s by no less than 1m 25s. The wind was favourable but certainly not to the extent of the margin by which the record was broken, and so the new record holder wins the Gold Medal which has been waiting since Ken Matthews record was made.

Next, only 9 seconds outside the old record, came Richard Dorman and 8 seconds behind Carl Lawton, superb walking by our two. Mike Scamell and John Newnham are soon in and our hopes of retaining the team title look good, Steve Marshall, Mike Nicholls and Edie Crossingham are next home all under 50 mins. and then Martin Hills and Joe Scamell.

On to the prize giving at the fine sports centre where once again we collect the team prize which include the monster pie. Richard also wins the 1st Junior award (his time was 3rd fastest ever). The Handicapper managed to spread the 10 prizes amongst 9 Clubs with Steve Marshall taking one. We drew blanks in the raffle but we must not grumble and maybe just as well judging by Carl's well laden van, trophies, grub and competitors etc.

Yet again another well organised event and of course Jack and Joan Goswell were in on producing the results.

Gordon Doubleday

NOVEMBER 22ND 1980 - WIMBLEDON - BELGRAVE OPEN 7 / THE BERMAN CUP

Gordon Doubleday

NOVEMBER 22ND 1980 - WIMBLEDON - BELGRAVE OPEN 7

THE BERMAN CUP

Most of our Club Trophies are named after their donors mostly prominent Belgravians of the past. The 'Berman Cup' is awarded by the Club. Originally it was competed for in the Open '7s' in the thirties being allocated to a different event each year. This mattered little as our team led by the late Harry Churcher used to make a clean sweep of the Opens and by winning it for three consecutive years it became our trophy and nowadays it goes to the team which wins our own Open 7 miles Walk.

The 61st 'Open' was held on 22nd November and good weather prevailed. Our President, Carl Lawton started the race which had the usual array of Club Officials, Chief Judge was Joe Coleman who himself was a regular member of our winning teams. He was third in the A.A.A. 7 Miles Championship in 1938 and in 1939, also in 1939 he was awarded the Fowler Dixon Style prize. Many remember his dead heat with his brother Len in the R.W.A. 'Junior' Championship in 1938. Amongst the judges were George Bentley, Fred Denny, Barbara Dunsford, Sid Spencer, Bill True and Joe Wilson and refereeing was Ray Hall.

Again the course was changed using three laps of the hills using a less steep hill than previous. New Zealander Mike Parker (Brighton) and D. Jarman (S.W.C.) were soon clear of the field and as the race progressed the further ahead they were and at the finish we had to wait nearly 2½ minutes before the third man arrived. Our first man in was John Hall (7th) with John Newnham doing well for 12th. We can perhaps note that not only were we without Carl but Richard Dorman, Paul Blagg and Mike Scamell were non-starters. Nevertheless Mike Nicholls and Steve Marshall (16th & 17th) came through well to complete the team. We were the first club to finish four, but our Midland rivals, Leicester W.C. soon had their fourth man in to give them a three point victory.

With eight handicap prizes to compete for, our other walkers had plenty to go for and the ever consistent Doug Fotheringham was rewarded with 2nd place, Steve Marshall 4th and John Dunsford (with Doug preparing for their trip to New Zealand) 8th. With the addition of John Hall we also won handicap team.

The London Business Houses Championship was held in conjunction and John Hall (Civil Service) and John Dunsford (Lon.Transport D.L.) were 3rd and 5th. An all Belgrave team John Hall, Bromley and Keown were second team.

Once again, Jack and Joan Goswell produced their immaculate result sheet which showed 110 starters and 15 teams. Finally our thanks to Carl under whose guidance the race went without a hitch.

Gordon Doubleday

MARCH 7th 1981 - BATTERSEA PARK - SOUTHERN AREA 10M

SOUTHERN CHAMPIONS AGAIN

The S.A.R.W.A. held their Championship Meeting at Battersea Park on March 7th, the main event being the Ten Miles Senior Championship, preceded by a Ladies 5 Kilos. We had no representatives in the first race - a situation we hope will soon be put right. By the time the Ten Miles started it was raining and unfortunately, we were without Paul Blagg, whose accident is reported elsewhere, John Hall too was an absentee. Nevertheless, we had quite a good line-up to defend the title we won last year over the same course, basically five laps of the road round the Park.

After the first lap we emerged from our shelter under a tree to see that Carl and Richard (who won the Individual title last year) were going well in about sixth position, later came Edie and Mike, also together, and a good team placing was in the making. Others were banging away over the familiar circuit. As the laps went by our positions were consolidated and with a lap to go, Carl was fourth, moving in top gear and we weren't surprised when he had picked up a place to finish third. Richard, getting back to form soon came in and it was good to see Edie maintaining his recent good form with a powerful and stylish finish, to be followed soon by the consistent Mike to give us a team win again. Happily the rain eased off over the last stages.

As usual we had good performances from our others, our Vets, led by World Champion Doug Fotheringham with John Bromley and Joe Scammell would surely have beaten any other Clubs trio. With so many walkers "barred" we couldn't field a team in the Garnet Cup, held in conjunction.

Championship Double

JANUARY 3rd 1981 - HAPPY VALLEY COULSDON - SURREY C.C. CHAMPS.

Following last years successes in winning both the individual and team Surrey Youth's titles it was expected that this years races would be something of an anti-climax. Gerry Sexton who had finished 6th in 1980 as a first year Youth had experienced bad luck through injury and illness while the transition to Junior ranks was expected to prove too much for the rest of last years winning Youths team. Add to that the last minute withdrawal of Stuart Paton with 'flu and even the most optimistic supporter would have given odds against a medal of any colour.

How different it was on the day. Our Colts finished a team for the first time in many years. Michael Blackmore ran a very creditable 18th in the boys race and then Paul Richley pulled out all the stops to win the Youths title having followed every attempt by the talented Andy Leyland to drop him around the hilly course. In the event it was Paul who broke away up the last hill and coasted home to retain for Belgrave the title which Dave north had won last year.

The revelation in the Junior race was John Gladwin who is now showing tremendous stamina to add to his blistering track speed. With the leading group from the start John held on to Hensby and Ledger until the half-way mark when Hensby made a distained break. Soon after pre-race favourite Ledger found John too hot to handle and it was an individual Silver for the Belgravian - a remarkable and possibly unique feat by a first year Surrey Junior. It was the team result, however, which took everyone by surprise. Woking closed 3 men in 10 but had to wait till the 40's for their 4th man. In the meantime a best ever run by Junior Captain Mark Waller ably backed by Dave North (on only 30 miles training) and Mark Sinclair ensured a surprise victory for our team. A quick check confirmed that this was our first Surrey Junior team title since 1963. Congratulations to all our youngsters.

ROAD AND COUNTRY

ROAD AND COUNTRY

We entered the winter period with the pleasant prospect of an improvement in our fortunes. Sure enough the better of our middle distance runners were getting a bit long in the tooth but nonetheless we expected some solid performances as usual. Add to this our talented batch of up and coming junior athletes and our stay in Division I of the North Surrey League seemed assured.

In the event it turned out badly. The talent was there but in practice we could not get more than half our top best twelve out at any one time. And so from a promising start we slipped to the bottom of the league and are once again relegated. A close perusal of the names of our scoring teams and the relatively close margin of 329 points between 2nd and 7th will show you how easy it should have been to stay up.

The bright spots were there. Our younger runners performed exceptionally well (See Young Athletes Section) and out of the blue came a new member - Graham Macky - a very talented and enthusiastic runner from New Zealand, who has revelled in the competition with which we have provided him.

A modest but encouraging sign that we are on the road to recovery is that for the first time in many years we completed all teams in the National Cross Country Championships.

And finally we got it together with a much improved team effort in our own Haig Southern 12 stage road relay in which we finished 17th . It was pleasing to see Chris Steer qualifying for this race during a three week visit from the U.S.A.

BERT FOOTER / "IN TERROREM"

Gordon Doubleday

BERT FOOTER

"IN TERROREM"

This was the title of an article which appeared in the Belgravian many years ago, its author was H.E. (Bert) Footer, the man chiefly responsible for our wins in the E.C.C.U. Championships (The National) in 1935, 1939, 1946 and 1948. He was trying to urge the lads on to greater efforts. Now 76 years of age he is living in retirement at Ramsgate, we are sorry to say that he suffers from arthritis in both hips and has trouble with his breathing, he has to walk with the aid of sticks. It became his ambition for Belgrave to win the National and in so doing he collected many honours for himself, Surrey Champion, Inter-Counties representative many times, Southern Champion and International finishing fourth at Caerleon in 1933 and fourth again the following year at Ayr. In these two races he was a member of the winning English team, also in the team was Arthur Penny, they both did much to further the good name of the Club. He was also Club Champion and his name can be seen on the Macher and Blackstaffe Shields.

So, how in the thirties did he get the rest of the team to such a peak that they could call themselves National Champions? The chief ingredient was training as a pack. If you were in the team, or even if you weren't, he expected you to train with the pack Saturdays at Wimbledon, or on what the Fixture Lists described as "Social Runs" with other clubs. Although not races they seemed to be the next best thing! Other Clubs would join us at Wimbledon or we joined them, the Grandstand at Epsom Downs was a favourite venue with a run out to Walton Heath and back. Midweek it was Tuesdays and Thursdays round Rotten Row (Hyde Park) from the "Rising Sun", Ebury Bridge Road - through Belgravia! On Mondays and Wednesdays you were encouraged to train from home, and Bert and a few others had a tough course in the Southfields - Wimbledon Park area. Friday was rest day, but Sundays - no matter how hard you had gone on Saturdays he and others used to do an all day, stroll over hilly rough country. In case that wasn't enough he had a schedule of home exercises to strengthen the chest, shoulders, abdomen etc. even those who did hard manual work performed their exercises daily.

For many years Birchfield Harriers had virtually monopolised the National and in the early thirties Bert's team began to figure in the placings with corresponding performances in the Southern. Not only did we score many wins in the Surrey County Championship but our Middlesex members (running as Cadogan A.C.) would win their County title. We won the Southern in 1934 and in 1935 we travelled to Hall Barn, Beaconsfield for the National, having taken second place to Surrey A.C. in the Southern, held at Hassocks (Sussex). As the runners entered the final lap a quick check up showed us to be in the lead and, shouted on by a coach load of supporters we gained the day and Birchfield's monopoly was ended. It was a proud moment when Bert led the team to collect the winning trophy and their medals. Congratulations to Bert and the Club poured in from all quarters.

Our hopes then went forward to the London to Brighton Relay, held a few weeks later. There was no National Road Relay, but whoever won this famous race (promoted by the "News of the World") could regard themselves as Britain's Number One Club in this department. Having won the race in 1934 our Midland rivals were out for revenge! The race from The Houses of Parliament to the front at Brighton consisted of twelve stages of different lengths and of different terrains regarding hills. Not only did you have to have twelve good runners, but you had to put them on the right stages. Here Bert's knowledge of the team enabled him to deploy them to the best advantage, he himself doing the eleventh stage of over five miles from Hickstead to Pyecombe at the top of Dale Hill. Birchfield put up a great fight but Bert was first up Dale Hill to hand over a lead which we maintained to the finish.

In 1936, 1937 and 1938 the team trained hard but Birchfield won at Alderley Edge and again at Stratford-on-Avon Racecourse, next year our local rivals, Mitcham A.C. won it back for the South at Reading. However, in 1939 on a muddy course near Manchester we gained our second National win. During the summer Bert took part in and won many road races and figured in our track teams, who regularly won Open Team Races over two and three miles - a feature of the Open Handicap meetings which were very popular. He also competed in the A.A.A. Six Miles Track Championship, and in 1936, was first reserve for G.B. team for the 10,000 metres at the Berlin Olympics.

He turned his attention to the Marathon and prepared himself with his usual thoroughness for the 1939 Poly Marathon. Third to arrive at Chiswick Track was a very tired Footer, and the vocal encouragement of Club Supporters helped him over an agonising lap of the track.

In September World War II started and he joined the Royal Marine Commandoes - whose tough training must have appealed to him. He was in some tough assignments and when the war ended he resumed his running. The first post-war National, held at Leamington in 1946 saw us win again, but he was not in the scoring team. Next year the Lancs. Club Sutton H. pushed us into second place over a snowbound course at Apsley, (Herts) but next year at Sheffield we won again. After a long distinguished career as a runner, he joined us in 1922, and as cross-country Captain and six years of war he was past his best and retired from the sport.

The Club honoured him by electing him an Honorary Life Member and a Vice-President. He was offered the Presidency but was unable to accept. A great pity - our best wishes to you Bert.

Gordon Doubleday
