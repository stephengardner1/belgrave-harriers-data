SUE MORRISON - OUR NEW SECRETARY

Eric Crutcher

For the first time in the club's illustrious history we have a lady secretary. Sue Morrison has taken over the reins from Joe Coleman who, following the tradition of past secretaries, put in some sterling work on behalf of the club. Although Sue is a relatively new member, having joined in early 1986, she has already become an enthusiastic and loyal member of the flourishing ladies walking section in addition to taking on the duties of secretary.

Sue's involvement with athletics goes back to 1982 when she took up running and completed a half marathon. Having "got the bug" for running it was only the following year that a marathon was tackled and successfully completed followed by participation in the London Marathon. Her introduction to walking came in 1984 when she competed for the Inland Revenue in the Civil Service Championships. As walking gradually took over from running she joined Belgrave (encouraged somewhat by John Hall and Eric Crutcher!) where the further persuasion of Carl Lawton made the transition complete!

Sue's involvement on the administrative side of athletics also started in the early 1980s when she accepted a place on the Inland Revenue Sports Association Committee (Eastern Region). This soon led to the National Committee where she was Publicity Secretary from 1983-87 and Assistant Treasurer from 1985-87. This also included responsibility for compiling their National Sports Bulletin twice a year (could Sue follow Joe and "do the double" - Secretary and Belgravian editor? Although this Committee work covered sport in general Sue still sits on the IR Athletics Committee and in January 1986 accepted a place on the Civil Service Athletics Association Committee where she now has responsibility for the ladies representatives teams.

There is no doubt that Sue is a great ambassador for Inland Revenue and Civil Service athletics and equally I am sure that our new Secretary will prove a great Ambassador for Belgrave Harriers.

Eric Crutcher

From the President.

Bill Lucas

Dear Members

Our Centenary year has drawn to a close and as your president I would congratulate, on your behalf, all those who have contributed in any way towards its undoubted success. I must however make special mention of Clive Shippen who was chairman of the Centenary Committee and fronted for most of the year's activities. We are deeply indebted to him for all his hard work and the immense amount of time he has devoted to the club not only in 1987 but in the years leading up to our anniversary. Many of you will have contributed to a small memento which, by the time you read this, will have been presented to remind him of BELGRAVE 1987.

The year as far as I am concerned ends in disappointment with the inability of our winter teams to uphold the prestige gained by the track and field team during the summer. The great name of this club was built on our successes on the road and over the country but I fear that over the past few years that name has slipped badly through lack of support of the winter fixtures by our leading middle distance runners. We have the names on paper but where are they when they are wanted.

Your cross country secretary prepares a programme and works hard to enter teams and appraise the members of each fixture only to find that the numbers taking part are hardly worth his effort. I fail to understand how members can call themselves Belgravians and yet not give of their best to ensure that the name of Belgrave remains prominent in our sport. The number of fixtures at which a major effort is required are very limited viz Country, Area and National Cross Country Championships and Area and National Road Relay races - only five. All other races including club championships are designed to bring an athlete to fitness for these events. Surely it is possible for all middle distance runners to arrange their training programmes around specific dates which are well known in advance - it has been done before with great success both for individuals and club teams - why not again!!!

I cannot believe, for it has been disproved on so many occasions that running over country and on the road harms the summmer performances. Such names as Ovett, Moorcroft, Pirie, Cram, Lewis, Hutchings and Bedford to mention only a few bear witness to the benefits of winter competition. It is not too late for runners and their coaches to rectify our poor start of the 1987/8 season. Get together with Colin Pearson and plan a recovery so that the name of Belgrave rides high. What ignominy to be relegated from Division 1 of the Surrey League!!!

No-one can participate in British athletics unless he or she is a member of a club. Give back to this club the devotion it deserves and help all its members to share in the glory of which it is undoubtedly capable.

Best wishes to you all for 1988.

Bill Lucas President

IN MEMORIUM - H. F. KING.

Gordon Doubleday

We are sorry to report the death of one of our oldest stalwarts, Harold King. He must have been in his eighties and for many years was a Grade I Walking Judge and was a familiar figure on the roadside and on the track. Harold was a quiet modest man who was liked by all and whose decisions were respected by competitors and officials alike.

Like most Walking Judges long years as an official followed an active career - indeed a distinguished one. In 1922 at Regent's Park not only was he the Individual winner of the RWA "Junior" (by qualification based on past performances) 10 miles Championship, but he led the Belgrave team home to victory. The following year he was third in the AAA 7 miles (now 10,000m) Championship held at Stamford Bridge. He also won Kent County Championships, and was a regular Club team man for many years.

His work as an engineer took him to East Africa where he contracted an illness which put an end to his career as an active walker. Fortunately his enthusiasm for the sport was channelled into Judging. Harold was a keen and loyal Belgravian, a quiet man who will be remembered with respect and affection.

Gordon Doubleday

Obituary · page 7

JOHN BROMLEY

J.G.C.

John Bromley passed away suddenly and peacefully in December 1987 at Perth, W Australia after competing in a walking race. He was visiting his family at the time. John joined Belgrave Harriers in 1949 whilst serving in the army as a PTI and stationed at Aldershot. John competed for the club on the track, road and cross-country running and in 1970 after a series of Achiles Tendon operations and a replacement big toe joint he transferred his activities to race walking and had competed with distinction in that section up to the time of his passing.

He was, up to the time of his retirement, Physical Training Officer at Hendon Police Cadet School and in that capacity he was in charge of all physical training and sport and many fine athletes owe much to him for their earlier instruction.

Some of John's wins as a runner were 1944 - Welsh AAA Youth 1 mile, 1946 - Welsh AAA Junior 1 Mile, Kent County Junior 1 mile, 1947 - Singapore AAA 880 yards and 1 mile,1949 - Lancashire County AAA 1 mile, 1953 - Surrey County AAA 2 miles Steeplechase (this record still stands), 1954 BELGRAVE HARRIERS 880 yards and 1 mile, 1955 - Singapore AAA 880 yards, 1957 - Singapore AAA 1 mile and 3 miles (new record).

He was a fully qualified AAA Coach in all events and also as Track and Field Judge, Starter, Timekeeper, Meeting Organiser and Administrator and had been Army Senior Athletic Coach and Senior Instructor at the Army School of Physical Training and had acted as Team Manager and Coach to several British Modern Pentathlon "B" teams at home and abroad during the preparations for the Mexico Olympic Games 1968. A quiet spoken man and exceedingly modest. A great all-rounder and it will be hard to find his equal.

Obituary · page 7

Joe Wilson - Centurion No 164

Regrettably Joe passed away on February 2nd 1988 aged 79. He joined the club in September 1939 being introduced by Chas. Churcher our then Road Walking Captain. Joe took up Race Walking at the age of 30 having spent his earlier years as a amateur footballer of some note and also as a prize winning Ballroom Dancer at which he also acted as a demonstrator and judge in dancing competitions. He developed into a good team man and featured in many of our longer distance successes from 20 miles, 50kms to London to Brighton and London to Brighton and back. He competed and finished in 14 London to Brighton races and finished in all of them in under 10hrs. In 1951 he became a member of the podigious Centurions Club when in the Brighton and back race he completed the 100 miles in 21hurs 59mins 30secs. In 1957 "Brighton" he was hit by a car and spent 3 weeks in The Royal Sussex Hospital with a broken jaw and facial injuries.

Although primarily a road walker Joe did compete on the track and became a very efficient coach and when his competing days were over he then devoted his time as an RWA Grade I judge in which capacity he officiated at Club, County, Area and National Championship events. The likes of men like Joe Wilson with such club spirit are hard to come by and he will be sorely missed.

grapevine

The fact it was Belgrave Centenary year did remind some elapsed members who had in mind that once "a Belgravian always a Belgravian" to contact the club and make themselves re-known and fortunately there happened to be a couple of us who could go far enough back to recall them.

One such man was Bruce Hayman who as one of our top sprinters in the late '20s and early '30s. His athletic career was cut short when he had a severe operation on his head which left him severely incapacitated and in consequence his parents moved back to their original home place in Devon and after some time in convalescence and rehabilitation he regained reasonable good health. Bruce wrote to the Club Secretary asking for details of the Centenary Celebrations. Luckily the Hon Sec (Joe Coleman), and Chas Jones had been members with Bruce in the Belgrave Junior Track teams in 1928/29 era and readily recalled him and so on the Track and Field Championships day Bruce came up to Battersea Park from Devon to meet the Club and to renew his acquaintance with the track that he had not seen since he left London after his operation in the 1930s. You can well imagine his surprise at the difference. When he left it was a three lap to the mile ankle deep dust heap and costing 1 penny to use the dressing room and now the 400 metre lap all-weather track with all mod cons and costing a small fortune to use. Having had a most enjoyable day making himself known to everybody and meeting most of the club officials Bruce returned to Devon, complete with his coveted 60 years old club lapel badge vowing,to keep in touch.

Another "contact" came as the result of a lady member of the Cornwall A.C. telephoning the Hon Sec on behalf of a son of an old member. The son (living in Hayle, Cornwall) wanted to buy a club tie as a surprise Xmas present for his 80 year old father who never ceases to talk about his times with the "Bels". Things were sorted out and the 80 year old turned out to be Cecil Mains (not to be confused with Dave) who was a cross-country man and my records show him as finishing 4th to Bert Footer, Arthur Penny and Arthur Allum in the 1933 10 miles Club Cross-Country Championship. Cecil wrote me from home in Dorset to thank me for my services and wished to be remembered to all and in particular Arthur Penny, Bert Footer and Arthur Allum - I have contacted Arthur Penny but unfortunately Bert Footer and Arthur Allum now do their training at extremely high altitude and I do not expect to see them for some time yet.

Chris Fairfield now a second claim member and living at Stalybridge, Cheshire, promptly sent in his subs with an accompanying newsletter. He had run a 3 hrs 6 mins in the London Marathon but brought home the bacon when he won a £1,000 worth food voucher in a competition organised by a Bacon Company. Eat your heart out lads. At the time of writing he was leading in his 1st Claim Club,Saddleworth Runners, road running championship (best of 5 out of 8 races) with one race to go. So hang in there Chris. Keep it up.

CENTENARY CELEBRATIONS

Clive Shippen

Clive Shippen reviews the Centenary Celebrations

The last issue of the Belgravian covered the first half of our year's celebrations and outlined the plans for the remainder of the year. This issue is able to report on the second half of our celebrations, though detailed reports will be found elsewhere in these pages.

As we forecast in June, the Club Track and Field Championships proved to be a memorable occasion. The weather was fine, there was a large attendance, and a lively spirit prevailed throughout the afternoon. In addition to the very full programme of athletics events for men and women, boys and girls, there were pole-vault and discus invitation events, children's races and a special re-enactment of an 1887 track sprint - complete with authentic moustachioed runners in their Victorian garb. Great pains were taken to "string" the lanes, and the race was the highlight of the day.

A marquee was erected to display club trophies and for the sale of Centenary mementoes; and the announcer and recorders enjoyed the luxury of a tented stall on the special day. Mark Shearman, an athletics photographer of international repute, spent the day with us and took dozens of memorable action shots. His final ones being of the large group which stayed on to the very end and released their helium-filled balloons at his command. Anyone wishing to see contact prints of the day's photographs and desiring to place orders for prints (from en-print size to poster size) should see Carl Lawton at Belgrave Hall. The group photo in poster size (36"x24") is an excellent souvenir.

Only seven days elapsed before we were once more busying ourselves with a celebratory occasion. This time the 36th Club '20'. A souvenir programme outlined the history of the event and the part played by its founder, Arthur Whitehead. In recognition of Arthur's service to the event our President presented him with a Belgrave Centenary wall plate. At the presentation, held in the forecourt of Lauriston Cottage, were The Mayor of Merton and the winner of the inaugural event in 1952 - Dave Fotheringham of TVH, who had travelled up from the Isle of Wight.

The race sported "Start" and "Finish" banners for the first time in many years and several other extra touches helped to mark the event as a special occasion - not least being the award of centenary mugs to the first 25% of finishers. Surrey County AAA and the Southern Counties AA acknowledged the celebrations by incorporating their own championships into the race.

A busy July closed on the 26th with the Thames River Trip from Putney Pier. It was a great pity that this should clash with our British and Southern League matches, but this alone could not account for tickets being undersold. But for those who went it was a night to remember. Lots of spare food, the best disco of the year and a very high-spirited evening saw a happy 111 Belgravians disembark in the early hours of Sunday morning.

The introduction of the AAA McVitie's 25x1 mile relay in early October caused the planned 100x1 mile relay involving ourselves, Blackheath and TVH, to be cancelled. It did not seem appropriate to try and use the McVitie's race for our own celebrations, so the Centenary relay slipped from our programme.

And so to Wednesday 7th October - the Club Birthday. It was difficult to estimate how many would attend Belgrave Hall for this get-together. There was no music. In the event an estimated 140 came along and it turned out to be a very enjoyable evening with many re-unions. Bill Webb, at 93 our most senior Past Presidsent, unveiled the new Centenary Bell which had been donated by Charlie Manning. The Centenary cake, beautifully made by the President's daughter and sporting a perfect replica of our Centenary logo, was expertly sliced into the appropriate number of pieces within seconds by The Queen's pastry chef, Lionel Mann. "Champagne", provided by The President, enabled all present to toast the Club's health and look forward to the start of our second centenary with keen anticipation. Especial thanks went to Charlie Manning and daughter Linda who donated and served the evening's fine buffet spread.

Bill Webb has got an "ology" - Campanology - as he inaugurates the "Centenary Bell" which bears the inscription: "This bell was unveiled by the most Senior past President - W.G.Webb - on the 100th Birthday of the Club 7 October 1987". The Bell was donated by Chas.Manning Past President.

The pinnacle of our year, was reached on Saturday, 14th November, when 250 members and guests gathered at The Gloucester, South Kensington, for our Centenary Dinner Dance. It was a magnificent occasion. Bill Couzens and Brian Pritchard excelled themselves as organisers and made this the most memorable of our celebrations. Many members, old and not so old, travelled from all parts of the country to be present. But these were out-done by three who flew the Atlantic especially for the occasion; Daye Donovan from New York (how Edmund would have loved to have been present), Chris Steer from Seattle and John Thresher from Toronto.

Our Patron, Eddie Kulukundis, and Susan Hampshire delighted us with their presence and amongst the Club's official guests were Rachel and Steve Ovett, the latter rounding off the speeches appropriately with typical Ovett humour. The revelry continued with dancing until 1.00a.m and a disco until 2.00a.m but the farewells were still going on at 2.45a.m! It was an occasion that wanted to go on for ever, and would be very difficult to follow.

But our 68th Open '7' Walk promotion was not put off by the previous act. The organisation had been coming together nicely during the preceding weeks and once again we enjoyed the presence of The Mayor of Merton, Councillor H. Turner, who started the race. To mark Centenary year a novices race over 5 miles was introduced and this was successful enough to warrant inclusion next year. Our final souvenir programme featured a history of the event and all previous 67 results. Our Race Walking celebration showed that Belgrave is a vital part of the UK Race Walking scene and it could well be that our introduction of a novices race in our Centenary event will prove to be a milestone in the revival of UK Walking fortunes. It was fitting that Belgrave should win both the men's and women's scratch team events.

Bill Lucas presents the Open 7 Miles Walk winning team awards to our Ladies - Gill Brackpool, Jill Borthwick and Sue Morrison.

"Centenary" President Bill Lucas and the Mayor of Merton Clr H Turner who graced us with his attendance at the 20mls Run, 7 miles Open Walk and 12 stage Relay.

As was expected when the first review was written, the London-Brighton Relay plans had to be abandoned through lack of sponsorship. A study group had followed the course stage by stage on the corresponding Sunday of 1986 and had logged the carefully timed trip complete with supporting photographs. Much other work went into the early planning including the agreement of the three police forces involved. Hopes were raised and dashed on several occasions over the last two years and it was with much disappointment that the Centenary sub-committee had to acknowledge that the required sponsorship would not be forthcoming.

Some mementoes are still available for purchase but no further stocks will be ordered. Sue Parrott and Eric Crutcher should be contacted for details of remaining stock.

And so, after years of waiting, Centenary year has suddenly slipped behind us. Ahead lies the untrod ground of a second century with another celebratory year at the end of that. The revellers will all be different, but they will be linked to us by a common factor - the club we love and know as Belgrave Harriers.

Clive Shippen

The Centenary Dinner Dance

Bill Couzens

14th November 1987 Venue - The Gloucester, Harrington Gardens, London SW7

This occasion was to be the pinnacle of our Centenary year with two hundred and forty Belgrave members and guests attending.

The evening commenced at the reception with each member or guest being individually announced by the toastmaster and greeted by our president, Bill Lucas and his charming wife Sheena. The finery of the occasion was evident, with many dinner jackets and bow ties on display and the ladies exotically putting "Dynasty" into the shade. The dinner itself by consensus of opinion was excellent and lived up to the high standard of The Gloucester.

Throughout the meal background music was played by the band Tuxedo Class with the occasional drum roll to announce "The President will take wine with all internationals past and present", another drum roll, "The President will take wine with all past presidents" another drum roll "- with the Committee" "with the ladies" etc. I should think for the President the art is not in public speaking but in finding how to remain sober. Then came the speeches, the first by Clive Skipper who gave a most interesting and enlightening portrayal of the club from the first meeting of a group of athletes on Friday 7th October 1887 who formed Belgrave Harriers to Belgrave Harriers 100 years later. Clive also designed the menu card that provided a short written history of the club.

All raised their glasses to the toast "Belgrave Harriers".

The next speaker was our President, Bill Lucas who talked of the club and its successes together with proposing our guests.

The response was by David Denton President of the ECCU.

A surprise speaker was Steve Ovett who provided everyone with a most entertaining speech showing his admiration for a club achieving 100 years and also for a certain Belgrave runner who, a number of years ago ran Steve into the ground on a training run across Wimbledon Common. Steve described the runner as "eyeballs out" and in full racing gear, a whisper of "Brian Gorman" was spontaneous. Finally the Tommy Green Cup for the most meritorious performance of the year was presented to John Regis and the equivalent Ladies trophy the "Centenary Cup" was awarded to Frances Guy for her fine marathon run.

With the formalities over everyone was ready for the dancing and Tuxedo Class went into full swing, noticeably there was a great convergence onto the dance floor with many letting "their hair down" and getting stuck in, not the place for the faint hearted.

At 11.30pm a well earned rest came just in time for some of us "old uns" with the drawing of the raffle, 1st prize was a microwave oven, other prizes a magnum of champagne, a carriage clock and many other excellent prizes.

From midnight to 1am dancing continued to the music of Tuxedo Class then with thanks for the band on came the disco "Travelling Gramophone". I was advised previously by this time most people would be gone in fact the majority stayed and the dance floor was packed. At 2am with the music throbbing in our ears and a farewell from the President the Centenary Dinner Dance was over. Truly a night to remember.

Bill Couzens

FEBRUARY 1963

Gerry North, the holder, was given little hope by the Press of obtaining a forward position in the Inter-Counties race at Reading. His defeat by Snowdon in a Surrey League match and sixth place in the Lancashire County were the basis of these views. When, seven days before the big race, Gerry only just held off Shelley (N.Staffs) in the Mitcham '15' it was said he was not the force of last year. How wrong they were. Despite suffering from a bad cold he beat all the recognised opposition only to lose his title to Portsmouth's T.K. Johnston who, revelling in the Artic conditions, chose this day to make the biggest break-through of his career.

I wonder how many of our senior athletes have noticed that Gerry can race week in and week out yet still produce the big effort for the big occasion. Perhaps, if the lesson is learned, we will soon field our strongest team more than once a season.

The first season of North Surrey League fixtures has ended, and was undoubtedly a success. These matches give the bulk of club athletes a chance to experience the big race atmosphere and, with 10 to score, an opportunity of counting for their team.

The league was born at the "3 Kings" Mitcham in May of last year. The proposal was put forward by Mitcham AC but, because of traditional fixtures being affected, it was not over-enthusiastically received by the other six clubs present who were, Belgrave, Walton, Surrey, Hercules, Herne Hill and South London. Eventually, however, all except SLH agreed to give it a trial.

Track and Field. Section Report

Leo Coy and Steve Williams

Track and Field. Section Report by Leo Coy and Steve Williams.

The track and field season has drawn to a close and we can look back with a great deal of pride and satisfaction at the achievements of our teams and individuals in this historic year.

In finishing fourth in the 1st Division of the British League we have established ourselves among the very best T&F clubs in the United Kingdom, a marvellous achievement when you think that five years ago we were just an average Southern League outfit. We have had a wonderful run through the British League and our arrival in the top Division for our 100th Birthday couldn't have been better timed. But there's to be no resting on laurels - we must continue to look forward. To break the present grip on this competition held by Birchfield, Haringey and Wolverhampton & Bilston will take some doing but we are already building on the season just gone. We will have new members eligible to compete next season and our strength in depth and in quality will be greater. We shall of course continue to need the utmost support from our Internationals at this level and while nobody expects them to compromise their training towards peak performances in the major championships, we do look to them and their coaches to give every consideration in their programmes to fitting in as many of the four matches as they can, if not necessarily in their primary events. Let's now start to have in mind that league title bringing the chance to represent the UK in the European Club Championships. There is no higher honour for a club in track and field.

Returning to the season gone, perhaps the one disappointment was getting knocked out in the semi-finals of the GRE Cup (though the fixture did clash with the AAA's). On the one per event basis of this competition, we should at least make the final even if only some of our top men are available.

The 'B' team continued to provide valuable competition for our second tier athletes and a springboard to the 'A' team for those good enough. Promotion wasn't achieved but we'll be having another go next season; a big effort in the first match next season will get everyone in the right frame of mind. In our midweek competition there were encouraging turnouts at most of the matches, with the use of Tooting rather than Croydon playing a part here. Unfortunately we just lost the final by one point.

Individually our athletes have never achieved more in one season with John Regis' performances of course uppermost in the mind and Marcus Adam another to have a tremendous year capped by his European Junior win. International honours again fell to John Gladwin and Ernie Obeng while Abi Ekoku becomes a new name for the club honours Board with an England Under 23 appearance, as does Felix Sandy who ran in the World Championships for Sierra Leone. John Gladwin won AAA and UK Championship medals and became Britain's 6th fastest ever miler, while Gary Staines and Dave Swain brought top quality to our 5000m running with Dave winning a World Student Games medal and Gary surely exceeding even his own hopes in his first season at the distance. Stuart Paton, Phil Lovell and Ken Onuaguluchi again won Southern Counties medals as did John Wright for the first time and there were innumerable Area and County representative honours. We provided five County Champions and club records were broken, re-broken or equalled on no less than 28 occasions.

'Centenary' congratulations to all on making it a season to remember.

EVENT ROUND UP OF INDIVIDUAL PERFORMANCES

100m Our sprinters were outstanding in 1987. Over 100m we had the sight of two of our men making the final of the AAAs and although it was Ernie Obeng who had collected the fastest semi final time (10.26) John Regis was first Bel across the line, collecting the Silver Medal and a pb of 10.37, ahead of not only our own number one for the past decade but also Wells and McFarlane. Only the American Dwayne Evans could edge out our rising star. In a blanket finish, Ernie was 5th in 10.41. John also finished ahead of Ernie, 10.45 to 10.56, when they took 4th and 5th in the Dairy Crest Games later in the season but timewise it was Ernie who had the fastest clockings. In addition to that 10.26 he had a 10.29 in Budapest and a remarkable 10.14 with marginal wind assistance when 2nd to Christie in the BR Sprint Trophy at Portsmouth in June. That's Ernie's fastest ever discounting a 10.1 manual timing. Earlier that day, Ernie had filled the same position in 10.30 for England v Czechoslovakia and Italy while in July Ernie was 3rd for England v USA in 10.38 and in August, 3rd at the IAC Meet in 10.39 for another couple of quality performances.

Our next fastest man was Sierra Leone International Felix Sandy who had signed membership after the Commonwealth Games last season. Felix spent most of the year in the USA where we understand he recorded a best of 10.38.

Our leading Junior, Marcus Adam, had a spectacular season. Over this distance he was 3rd in the UK's, 2nd in the BR Junior Sprint Trophy in 10.44w, had a fine win in the AAA's Juniors over Jamie Henderson, took 5th in the European Juniors, after collecting the club record from John Regis in the heats with 10.46, and won for GB Juniors against West Germany and Poland in 10.48. But perhaps Marcus' best 100 was his storming last leg to bring the GB Junior 4x100m team into the Gold medal position to beat FRG by one hundredth in the European Junior Championships. He had to do that of course to complete a Belgrave hat-trick of Junior Championship relay Golds!

Phil Goedluck found fewer top quality opportunities in his first Senior year but he was 6th in the UK's and in the BR Sprint Trophy (10.47w) and ran a seasons legal best 10.58 in the AAA's making the semi final amid hot competition. Joe Minetti won the Surrey Junior title and reduced his best to 10.8 while Mike Bazire, Dave Armstrong and Len Paul - a new member who trains with Dave and is a member of the British Bobsleigh Squad - were also under 11 seconds. And to follow in the footsteps of these, a young man showing much promise is Freddie Ocrah who ran a club best (manual timing) for a 'Boy' with 11.7.

200m John Regis commenced the season with a series of moderate runs by his standards while rounding into shape after injury and the first sign of the form to come was shown when he duffed up Derek Redmond over 300m in late July. That preceded John's brilliant run in the AAA's Championship where he just held on to an early dip to outlean the fast finishing Dwayne Evans in the astonishing time for Crystal Palace of 20.25 - a CBP and UK All Comers Record. That assured him of selection for the World Championships and three more 200's - 20.54 2nd to Jefferson at the IAC meet - 20.50 4th in Zurich - 20.78 2nd to Christie at the Dairy Crest - put the finishing touches on his preparations for what turned out to be our individual highlight of the year!

In ideal conditions in the 1960 Olympic Stadium, John sailed through the heats and semis getting progressively faster (Hts 1st 20.76, Rd2 3rd 20.60, Sf 1st 20.54) to take his place alongside the World's elite for the final. Let's allow Ron Pickering's TV commentary to pick up the story as the gun goes for a remarkable race.

"No problems with the start, Da Silva has gone off well, John Regis has closed down on Floyd Heard, John Regis is running a superb bend, John Regis is coming off the bend and Krylov in the red shirt, Regis and Da Silva, Regis is there and left Floyd Heard, Heard is coming back, John Regis hanging on, its going to be very close and John Regis is going to get there, John Regis gets there!"

If only Ron had been right! Reviewing the slow motion replay, the commentator recovered from his excitement...

"In the early part of the race, John Regis destroyed Floyd Heard's chances of this race, he runs a magnificent bend, he also does Da Silva of Brazil another of the close favourites. Krylov is fighting Queneherve in the blue and white and Calvin Smith has the terrific finish. John Regis has a magnificent three quarters of the race and look at his chest forward, if he could just duck like Jon Ridgeon then he may have made it but Calvin Smith is the fast finisher on this side. What a superb run from the 20 year old, whatever medal he gets it has been magnificent. His concentration never deserted him. What he's done to World class American, Brazilian and Canadian sprinters here is no man's business and they were into a breeze against of .49 all the way down that long straight so that's an incredible time."

It certainly was. When the photo finish had been sorted out John had collected the Bronze medal in a new UK record - the first Senior UK record in track and field to be held by a Belgravian. 1. Smith (USA) 20.16; 2. Queneherve (France) 20.16; 3. John Regis 20.18; 4. Da Silva (Bra) 20.22; 5. Krylov (USSR) 20.35; 6. Heard (USA) 20.25; 7. Pavoni (Ita) 20.49; Mahorn (Can) 20.78.

And lets hear from John's TV interview also as he talks almost as well as he runs!

"... I dipped again too early but hopefully for next year I'll be able to rectify that. I wanted that line to be there so badly, I saw the dotted line so I thought dip now but again as I said that's about 10 metres before the actual line is so I was dipping way before I should have - that's what basically lost me the race but in saying that I've got to be pleased now, I've got the British record, ranked number one, so it's been a great World Championships for me really... I was drawn in lane 2 which wasn't necessarily a good draw for me; I had Robson Da Silva and Floyd Heard outside me so I just powered round the turn and then coming onto the straight I just concentrated on the finishing line, making sure of my high knee lift and just trying to keep form basically. But I knew the other guys would be coming on me slowly and slowly and as you can see there they're beginning to pick me up and unfortunately I start dipping 'there' which just cost me the gold medal but even so I'm pleased with my performance today ... With guys like Floyd Heard and Robson Da Silva who I've raced three times previously and never beaten and to beat them on an occasion like this has just been a Godsend to me really. I couldn't have predicted that I'd get in the top three but in saying that I've trained hard, worked hard with my coach, come back from injury and now I'm in effect the third best in the World at this moment."

You certainly are John and well done from all of us at Belgrave.

While John nearly conquered the rest of the World, Marcus Adam absolutely conquered the rest of Europe's Juniors at this distance when he was in blistering form at a damp Alexander Stadium, Birmingham in August. A time of 20.92 in the heats followed by 20.95 in the final was more than the rest could handle and taking a title previously won by Valeriy Borzov was pretty fair compensation for missing a medal over 100m earlier in the week. Marcus was in fact undefeated in all his Junior races over 200m. There were International wins for GB Juniors v West Germany and Poland (21.00) for The England Under 23's in Norway (21.26) and retention of his AAA Under 20 title in 21.30. But that emphatic European title was the one Marcus will savour.

Unbeknown to many, Felix Sandy was a second Belgravian selected for the World Championships at 200m. Felix represented Sierra Leone and placed 6th in his heat in 21.67. Making it a great year at 200, we also had Joe Minetti winning the Surrey Junior title, Mike Bazire the Sussex Title also placing 4th in the Inter Counties, and Freddie Ocrah equalling the Boys Club record!

400m New member Paul Cuff from New Zealand was our No. 1 with solid 48's through the season. Jay Busk was also under 49 a couple of times, placing 2nd in the Wilts County Champs and representing his County while Mike Bazire was inside 50 for the first time and took 3rd in the Sussex Champs. Mike Alleyne ran a 49 in May but was injured most of the season. Good progress was made by a number of youngsters at this distance, notably Steven Garcia, Alistair Grant and Adrian Ross.

800m John Gladwin won the UK Championships at this distance before a pelvic problem affected the heart of his season. John did however run a series of 1.46's and came back to run one of his best ever 800's at the IAC Meeting in August where he was a fast finishing 3rd ahead of the eventual World Silver medallist and the reigning Olympic Champion. Stuart Paton was an easy winner of the Surrey 800, took silver in the Southern Championships and was twice under 1.50 while John Booth made a fine early season breakthrough winning the Surrey Juniors (with Graham Adams in 2nd) and running 1.52. Phil Walker and Andy Moore were inside 1.54 and 17 Bels were under 2 minutes.

1500m/Mile It was sad to see John Gladwin unable to produce his best form in time to gain World Championship selection. However, a philosophical John was not displeased to have come through to 3rd in the AAA's in all the circumstances. Fortunately he shook off his injury sufficiently to have a great run in Zurich where he so very nearly pulled back Bile over the mile, recording 3.51.02 (a run that must give food for thought in view of what followed in Rome) and he followed up with Mile wins at the Dairy Crest meet (with Gary Staines in 5th) and in Munich. In the Welsh Championships, Justin Chaston won the Junior title while brother Matt placed 4th in the Senior event, both significantly improving their best times during the season. Dave Swain became our 4th fastest ever 1500m runner with a 3.40.95 at the Rome Grand Prix while Glynn Tiernan was runner up in the Surrey Juniors.

3000m/5000m No sooner had the season started than we learned of Dave Swain's clearance for membership and the first club record of the season had fallen as Dave had chased home Sydney Maree in Philadelphia on 11 April in the fine time of 13.37.8 to remove Eire International Gerry Deegan from the record books. (That will please those members who preferred to recognise Geoff North as our record holder because Deegan never actually ran for the club. He was a paid up member for a year or so though). Back home Dave guested at the Portsmouth International meeting in June with a 3rd place in 13.58.43 and then collected his second club record with a 7.57.60 3000m at the Peugeot Talbot Games on 10 July before setting out for the World Student Games in Zagreb as Britain's only representative in the 5000m. Sadly he was unable to produce his best form there but he did collect the bronze medal. Meanwhile news had filtered through of a 13.45 debut 5000m from Gary Staines down under in Australia where Gary had been spending about 6 months at the Canberra Athletic Institute. A scientific diet under their advice to bring his weight down to around 9 and a half stones and his engagement to Long Jumper Nicole Boegman were two factors obviously doing him no harm. Gary returned home in July and our first view of him in action confirmed the point. Showing his best ever form at any distance Gary had a superb win over a classy 2 miles field at Gateshead, taking the notable scalp of Buckner, for another club best of 8.30.09. That was a week prior to running another blinder in the AAA's 5000m to take the silver medal, beaten only by a resurging European Champion. The club record had fallen again to 13.30.53 but the previous holder didn't go too badly himself as 16 seconds behind Gary came Dave in 12th place. These two then reduced their best 2 mile times; Gary to 8.26.15 at the IAC meeting and Dave to 8.33.92 at the Dairy Crest Games. Dave then called it a day for major competition but Gary was still rounding to a peak and took himself right into the reckoning for an Olympic Games place next year with a brilliant 13.20.00 at Crystal Palace in September with again only Buckner in front. We knew Gary would go well when he moved up from 1500's but that sort of time was remarkable - only a few seconds faster in their whole careers were such household names as Foster and Bedford! Not neglecting a 'workout' at a shorter distance, Gary then took a 4th place over 2000m in Lausanne beating a host of International stars in another fast time (4.53.69) having gone through 4 laps in 3.55! Gary returns to 'Oz' in February for a short period and all we can say is let's have more of the same next Summer Sport!

10000m There were few runs of note over this distance but in the improving John Wright we had the Southern Counties bronze medallist and in Kiwi Daryl Fantham the Surrey County 3rd placer.

3000mSc Illness curtailed Mark Sinclair's season but he had time to retain his County title with our only sub 9. Mike Hayes also had a quiet year but Simon Maynard made good progress.

Hurdles Gareth Allison became our fastest ever Junior High Hurdler in the last BAL match while Alistair Grant smashed the Youths 400mH record with 57.7 in the Public Schools Championships in Birmingham. We watch their progress with interest. Of the Seniors, over one lap, Ian Bland ploughed a somewhat lone furrow for us, his fastest being 54.51 in the Southern Championships. Tony Pamah was absent all season and Chris Knight was injured after a promising 3rd place in the Surrey Championships. But was that really John Regis who ran 24.0 for 200mH at Thurrock in September? Watch out Ed Moses!

Jumps Chris Cornwell equalled the club High Jump record with 2.00 in the GRE Cup while Phil Lovell's best pole vault result came with another 2nd place in the Southern Counties. Derek Brown was injured much of the season but went over 15 metres for a fine Triple Jump 4th place in the Inter Counties Championships and placed 6th in the 'Southern'.

Throws Abi Ekoku made good progress this year. In the Shot he placed 8th in the 'UKs' and 6th at the Pearl Invitation meet at Gateshead before breaking through the 16 metre line for a fine new club record at a Southern Counties match in August. With the Discus Abi was 7th in the 'UKs', 4th in the 'Southern' and 8th in the AAA's. He had a fine best throw of 53.42 and certainly kept Ken Onuaguluchi on his toes all season. Abi also made his International debut for an England Under 23 team against Belgium and Norway, placing 3rd with 52.42. Ken spun the plate out to 53 a few times but was slightly down in distance on previous seasons. Nevertheless he was among the medals with 2nd places in both the Inter Counties and Southern Championships while his best shot came Indoors at the IAC Shot Day at Crystal Palace as early as February - a fine pb of 15.78.

A new name at the backend of the year was Nigel Bevan, one of the country's leading Javelin throwers, who became a member on 17 August and five days after that set new club figures for a Senior and Junior with 71.66 representing Southern Counties in Norway. That throw was with the new Javelin and superseded our previous best with the old model by a healthy 4 metres. That's some performance and we look forward to seeing Nigel in Claret and Gold next season.

ALL TIME TOP 100 INDIVIDUALS OVER THE 'FIVE AND THREE QUARTERS'

Steve Williams

As promised in the October 1985 edition of the magazine here are the 100 fastest men round our long established 5 3/4 mile road course; one man for each of our hundred years. Virtually anybody who's been anybody in the way of Belgrave distance running is on the list and no doubt some of the names will evoke many memories for our older members. I believe the compilation to be accurate even though some results over the years were either not fully reported or omitted from the 'Belgravian' but let me know if you have any queries.

The first race was held as long ago as 23 March 1929 and the 'Belgravian' of the time tells how it came about. "In view of the London to Brighton Relay, the committee decided to stage a time trial on the road a fortnight before, in order to assist in selecting the team. As the stages of the relay vary from 4 1/2 to 6 1/2 miles approximately, the trial was run over a course at Wimbledon covering a little more than 5 miles representing about the mean distance. The course was the same as is now used by the Walking Section consisting of two 1 mile laps of the Common then down Copse Hill, Coombe Lane and back via Cambridge Road, finishing with another lap of the Common; a very suitable course containing a few hills and mostly good surfaces, similar to those on the Brighton Road."

Interestingly, the runners for this first race were despatched singly at 15 second intervals, the order of starting having been drawn for, with the object of simulating as near relay conditions as possible. Twelve men completed the race with T J Flower fastest in 31.27. The following year the 'yacht' start was abandoned for a straight race while a sealed handicap was held in conjunction and the winning time came down to 29.46 which was very respectable for 1930. A year later the winner was one Arthur Penny in 30.16 while the 'Belgravian' reports that "Carter again showed that he is a distinct acquisition and his forward running gained him second prize in the handicap". "Carter", of course, was 'our Tom' then a novice! It's marvellous to be able to record, 56 years later, that these two stalwarts are still very much part of the Belgrave scene.

In the next three years Arthur Allum completed a hat-trick while Bert Footer took the record to 29.30 in 1935. The 'Belgravian' report that year captured the finish of the race in the style of prose of the day like this. "Carter leads Footer by a yard at the top of Copse Hill and they stride up the steep incline like horses with no sign of fatigue.... Round the triangle they go.... In this space, Footer managed to get 50 yards away from Carter and win. We looked for the winners usual smile but alas! even the group of charming ladies at the winning post could not inspire the dull dog to cast a little sunshine on the scene!"

The 1937 race was run over a shortened course and the times are therefore not relevant but the 'Belgravian' congratulated Ted Stimpson "for his fine stylish run" and W E Lucas, "who won the handicap in splendid fashion." Both men were of course destined for International honours. Arthur Penny reclaimed the race in 1938 in a new record and Tom Carter gained his sole success the following year after having been three times a runner up previously. Later that year saw the outbreak of war but one more race was possible in 1940 before the number of members called to the service of their country caused a necessary curtailment to the full programme of club activity. Len Herbert, another International, won, shaving a second off the record.

When the race resumed in 1947, standards were lower because of lack of training in the preceding years but Ted Stimpson brought back respectability the following year, winning in 29.33. In 1949 the first two men home, Vernon Blowfield and Jack Brown went slightly off course and were effectively disqualified but Verne took the record down to 29.09 in 1950, and following the cancellation of the 1952 race through snow bound roads, Ray Tooby opened up the 28 minute era with 28.50 in 1953. But 1954 was to see what might still be regarded as the most remarkable feat of running on the course when Ron Linstead, only just 18 years old, smashed that time with 28.24. Ron was too young to gain selection for the 'Brighton' but a sign of the quality of his run was that the men behind him went on to place 2nd in the National relay. Unfortunately Ron never improved on that time but even now there are few who have beaten it. In October that same year it was decided to hold a second annual race over the course to assist with selection for the newly introduced October London to Brighton Relay which initiated a championship for 'Southern' clubs. Once more a short course was used for this first 'Autumnal' race.

Four and a half years then passed before Pat Newell took the record to 28.21 and, in March 1960, 28 minutes was beaten for the first time by our regular correspondent and visitor, Ranjit Bhatia, who showed the form which was to take him to the Rome Olympics, with 27.56. Ranjit's time was astonishing to club members then but only two years later another man at the peak of his powers was to go over 200 yards faster. Gerry North was just two weeks away from winning the National Cross Country title and in superb shape. He smashed the record with 27.15 over two minutes ahead of the next man, Charlie Walker, causing the 'Belgravian' reporter to question "How many of our older runners ever dreamt they'd see the approach of 27 minutes on this course?". Gerry was in fact still (just) a first claim member of Blackpool at the time but it would be pedantic not to count his best time in the list below as he was to become one of the great Belgravians.

The Junior record was taken to a fast 28.01 by Tony Fairclough in March 1964 behind one of Bill Kerr's four wins while 1965 saw the first of Laurie O'Hara's record six wins (four in the March race). Then in 1966 the present record was set. Geoff North was known to be going for his elder brother's time and the 'Belgravian' reports he was so on edge that when it was suggested to Geoff that he might like someone to knock in some starting blocks it failed to raise even the merest smile! "No sooner had Bob Taylor's starting arm dropped than Geoff was clear of the field ... Geoff kept his form well on the homeward run and a 'break it or bust' sprint down the finishing straight sent up an ooh! from the watchers that momentarily did not indicate which side of the record his time was. Relief all round (Gerry wasn't there) as it was announced that Geoff had beaten the record by just ONE SECOND!".

The following years saw many a pitch battle between the evenly matched runners who formed the backbone of our successful teams of the late 60's and early 70's but only three or four runs, from Geoff and Gerry and Bill Weller approached the record in that time. However two 'age' records fell in the 70's - to Junior Bill Curtin with 28.00 in 1971 and Veteran Laurie O'Hara with 27.40 in 1973. In the mid 70's, John Stow ran up a record four consecutive wins with Oliver Foote taking three on the trot in 82/83; but very few Bels of the 80's have intervened at the sharp end of the list, only John Wright having made the top ten with an impressive solo 27.44 last March. So we await the day when Gladwin, Staines, Swain and company might, in the not too distant future, set seriously about this race and take us below 27 minutes! It would be nice to see! In the meantime, imagine this lot below all gathered at the end of Lauriston Road for the same race. Getting a good team out for the relays would be easy!

ROAD RACE ROUND UP

The odd success is coming the way of the road runners as efforts are being made to consolidate our entries for open races and word is spread of who is doing what! However communications still need improving if we are to do ourselves justice. There are so many races available these days that we should regularly be able to pick up some team prizes. It doesn't even need our very best men to be involved although we still look to them for a lead. A word in the right ear could enable us to get the back up brigade to join in and everyone could benefit. The guys running down the field will make that much more of an effort if they know there's something in it at the end. Martyn Head is your new Section Secretary. Contact him on [phone number withheld].

Two successes missed the boat for the last edition. These were the win in the City of London 10 Miles, where Don Anderson in 5th was a late entry non scorer, and in the Alton 10 Miles where Martin Lake, who was the holder, led the team home in 2nd place this time. In the Fleet Half Marathon, John Wright was representing the Army and didn't score for us but he finished 10th in a fast time with some respectable running behind from our team which placed 8th. Best performance in the London Marathon came from Canadian Peter Anthony who moves into 25th place in the club all time list with his time. Another prominent showing came from Dave North with a 2nd place in the Portsmouth 10 Miles in May in 54.22. Must have been tough, long or both Dave! When he lined up alongside John Wright in the Brighton 6, there was a good chance of high placings from the pair and thats how it turned out, with John winning in 30.03 and Pete in 3rd. Young Athletes Report

Jim and Dave Mote

Young Athletes Report by Jim and Dave Mote

Out came the Wellingtons, Waterproofs and Umbrellas. In went the Sun. Yes it was the summer season again.

The 1987 Young Athletes season started with the traditional enthusiasm and expectation. Cross-Country runners chipped the dried mud from their spikes and the sprinters blew the cobwebs off theirs. All prepared for the season's first engagement at Tooting Bec.

3rd MAY 1987 TOOTING BEC

Dark clouds greeted us at Tooting Bec and it was not long before the new centenary umbrellas were in use and greatly appreciated by all who huddled under them. It was good to see some new faces mingling with the stalwarts from last year but a head count soon showed that our Boys and Youths teams were far from strong in numbers and the Colts section was almost non-existent.

Nevertheless everyone competed with great enthusiasm and after a long wet afternoon the fine efforts of our team were eventually beaten by the superior numbers of the athletes from the recently amalgamated Swale Combined Club. We managed to stay ahead of our South London rivals Herne Hill but sadly missed our Youth sprinters.

24th MAY 1987 BATTERSEA PARK

Our home match was our chance to show our mettle but on a warm afternoon with everyone expecting we would prove victorious we somehow let it slip and finished second. Once again we were pipped at the post by a team that could field more athletes on the day. Our faithful nucleus ran, jumped and threw with great gusto but were beaten by the numerical superiority of Kingston and Polytechnic Harriers.

7th JUNE 1987 DARTFORD

On another dull threatening day we set off by coach to Dartford with a team which this time had only five youths and one Colt but our spirits were high and an impromptu game of cricket preceded the athletic match. Unfortunately our lack of numbers again made the match an uphill task from the very beginning but once again enthusiasm countered these problems and once again we finished second. An added bonus was the fact that the rain which threatened us all the afternoon never materialised and there was an air of satisfaction amongst the tired athletes on the journey home.

28th JUNE 1987 HORSHAM

The sun came out! So shocked were the team that only 10 athletes turned out. Outnumbered by Belgrave parents, coaches, officials and other sundry supporters and not to mention the other teams, our athletes manfully battled against insurmountable odds. Just to add to our problems the three teams we were opposing were Bexley and E Grinstead who were at that time first and second in the league and Horsham who were the host club. Apparently we were hit by the holiday season (not applicable to our opponents) and our results reflected the depleted nature of the team. But we all came home with a lovely sun-tan!

Our only individual winner was S Abbeysinger in the Boys High Jump (B).

Final Result (1) E Grinstead 268 (2) Bexley 2445 (3) Horsham 90 (4) Belgrave 58

26th JULY 1987 TOOTING BEC

Our final match on a fine afternoon gave us our best team in numbers of the whole season but still it was not strong enough. Our close rivals Hercules Wimbledon and Surrey Beagles had even bigger teams and after H.W. had taken a commanding lead early on we engaged in a close battle with Surrey Beagles for second place before losing our grip when our athletes had competed in their maximum number of events and left the Beagles to pick up the spare points. For the first time this season we had competitors in the Youth sprints but still failed to field a sprint relay team.

Seventeen teams competed in our South Central Division and Belgrave finished 8th.

It has been a hit and definitely miss season in which the consistent nucleus has performed with commendable enthusiasm and enjoyed their individual achievements. Unfortunately some of our talented athletes have been unable to attend on many occasions for various legitimate reasons and this is where our weakness lies Baba and Nadu Khan, Dan Winson, John Coy (Team Captain) and Michael Bowers competed in every match and I would like to thank them for their unswerving loyalty. In reporting the matches above I note that Dan Winson does not appear as a winner at any time but he is a winner in a different because he was always there always finished and always scored valuable points - well done Dan!! Another special mention goes to Michael Bowers our up and coming middle distance runner who injured his foot and could not run in the second part of the season - he came along and "shot-putted" instead!! What more can one say?!

Think about it lads. You have a great deal of talent. A little more consistency in your appearances and the league could be yours for the taking. And finally many, many thanks to the parents, coaches, officials and would-be officials who supported us (not forgetting the supporters from the ladies section who graced us with their presence on many occasions). Have a good winter season and come back fit and raring to go!

Belgrave Thameside League

This league was an interesting innovation five years ago when Belgrave decided to provide additional competition for the younger boys in some local clubs. Its development has been quite remarkable with four very competitive meetings taking place over the short athletic season. Our own teams (Colts and Boys) have not been once sucessful in the competition but have enjoyed the opportunity to compete in a league with surprisingly high standards. On average the turn out of Belgrave athletes was better this year and one encouraging aspect was the increase in the number of Colts taking part. Only two arrived for the first match but by the last one our team had grown to eight.

Six teams competed in each of the divisions both of which were won by Croydon. Our Boys finished fourth and the Colts were fifth but we have great hopes of improving on this next year.

A number of Belgrave officials work very hard to present this competition and must be applauded on the high standard they have achieved. We trust the Belgrave athletes will show their appreciation of these efforts with an even greater and more consistent turn-out next year.
