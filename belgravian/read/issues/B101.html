Editorial

We are now at the end of what can be termed the first full post-war winter season. From the afternoon of the season's opening meeting at Belgrave Hall the atmosphere was set: time seemed to have slipped back and the years of the war became, in many minds, non-existent. As one who "arrived back" that afternoon not having been near Belgrave Hall since 1940, it was a startling joy to see again so many well-remembered faces and to be greeted with such a warmth of welcome. Many things have changed these past seven years and this club has lost some of its dearest and best members, but we are still a club with enthusiasm and unity about us. At present most things are opposed, or unhelpful, towards the pursuit of Athletics. This country, unlike many others, gives no official or material support to Amateur Sports Clubs of any kind. Whether this is for better or for worse is a debateable question, but it is our heritage that such clubs as ours should be free to control and run their pursuits without any outside interference or aid, therefore what we achieve can verily be called our own. We have much to achieve, both for the benefit of the Athletic world in general and for the glory of this Club. The task of this Club in the future must be to build a strong, thoroughly ALL-ROUND Club, setting an example of fair sportsmanship that will make our name a byword for such things and influencing all those who have contact with the Club. In 1948 the Olympic Games will be held in London. Between now and then it must be our object to build a Track team as strong as our Walking and Country teams have been for so many years. If we can achieve such eminence in these two spheres there is no good reason why we should not do likewise on the track. We have the talent, some of it already shown, some of it dormant, but to mention only these few names is to comment on a strength which could do much: Broadbent, Botwright, Burfitt, Blowfield, Nankeville, Marchant, Herbert, Read, Lucas, Stimpson. To reach our object on the track our training must be made more rigorous, new methods must be tried, progress must be noted and used to advantage, and above all we must train together and help each other. It is the only way that standards can be compared and raised.

Such must be our objects. They have in turn a more profound aspect, for Athletics is one of the few truly amateur sports left. It requires much selfless endeavour for no material gain and it can only be successfully pursued if loved, and to love it is to love something outside yourself. Thus it helps to form an essentially unneurotic outlook in a world where the selfish and neurotic is ever more shrill and powerful in its beseeching. To help counter this modern disease even in our small way, is to achieve something of a rare sanity these days.

Whilst this Edition is in progress the Southern C.C. Championship has been run. Len Herbert ran a magnificent race to win the Senior race, and Derrick Burfitt found a fine finish to win the Junior race. A weakened team in the Seniors were second to Aylesford Paper Mills and the Junior team ran very well to finish third to S.L.H. and T.V.H.

Obituary

It is with the greatest regret that we have to announce the death of TED JACKMAN at the early age of 35 years.

Ted joined us in 1930 and quickly showed that he had walking ability. During the summer of 1931 he was placed frequently in Club Track Handicaps and, improving apace, was successful in Open Track Handicaps, his win in the Ponders End Fete and Gala being particularly memorable. His walking on the road also showed improvement, and although never attaining championship rank he was always a dependable club man. His personality was pleasing. A gentle modest bearing, combined with sterling character and excellent sportsmanship endeared him to all, and he will be sorely missed, for we can ill afford to lose such a splendid club man. Only the day before his death he was talking of his many friends within the Club, of which he has been a proud member for seventeen years. We are the richer for his friendship and loyalty, the poorer for his passing at such an early age. Frank Elson, Charlie Churcher and Charlie Poulter paid the Club's last respects at the funeral, and a wreath in Club colours testified to the esteem in which he was held. Our profound sympathy is extended to Ted's parents and sister in their very great loss.

Column · page 4

Commentary

On page 6 will be found a short note from the Hon. Treasurer, Bill Webb. It is hoped that any guilty members will take note of the paragraph on subscription.

Dave Martin Shepard and the Social Committee are doing great work now and are putting the Belgrave social side on its feet again. The repairs to Belgrave Hall have been completed and its use for frolics in the future will be as deplorable as ever was!

The Belgrave News Letter, edited and printed by Frank Elson, is at its 69th Edition. It is a very well edited Letter and the idea behind it a sound one, for it does keep all members of the Walking Section in the closest touch with the doings (or would you prefer the word "victories," Frank!), of this illustrious Section.

The Club Annual Dinner was most successful. The attendance was a record, the speeches complementary to all and not too long, the food good and the party spirit most excellent.

We won the Surrey C.C. Championship again and Cadogan A.C. (our friends) were third in the Middlesex. Surrey Colours were won by Tom Carter, Len Herbert, Bill Lucas and Leslie Stokell. Middlesex Colours by Vic Blowfield and Geoffry Pearson. In the Kent Championships George Lucas was 5th and in the Herts. Championship Albert Frost was 4th. Both these Belgravians won their County Colours.

War-time members Jimmy Green and Peter Moughan won their County Colours for Kent and Herts. respectively.

Congratulations to our Junior Captain, Eddy Short, who was 4th in the London University C.C. Championships: George Lucas, 3rd in the North Kent District Race; Geoffry Pearson, 2nd in the Banks Championship.

The Walking Section is winning everything at present in a most glorious fashion. All the big Southern "Opens" have fallen to us and Harry Churcher, closely followed by Jack Rutland, has once again proved himself unbeatable over his distances. The National races are yet to come, but we can own a measure of confidence in our ability to do well in these.

In the Britannia Shield race last November, Len Herbert, running for the R.A.F., was 5th and 2nd Englishman home. Our Belgian friend and member, Etienne Gailly, was 7th, running for the Belgian Army, and our Poly member, Dick Towndrow, 15th, running for the British Army team.

This Club extends its most sincere congratulations to Mr. E. J. Holt, of S.L.H., on his being made an Officer of the Order of the British Empire for his services to Athletics, which have indeed been great.

We won the South of Thames "Junior." Bill Lucas and Len Marchant ran fine races to finish 1st and 2nd.

In the South of the Thames "Senior" we were 2nd. Our score of 87 points, the lowest ever recorded for 2nd place, would normally have won us the race. Our congratulations go to the Aylesford Club, who turned out such a good performance to beat us. We won the Coleman Cup for the lowest score for 12 men home.

Michael Broadbent and Derrick Burfitt have been mentioned in the press as being British Olympic hopes. We believe they can do it given the chances, and we hope others will join them by 1948.

In view of the above it is interesting to note that Derrick, and Arthur Bruce, are awaiting their call up any moment now.

Your Hon. Editor is receiving copies of The Scots Athlete. This is a first-class journal dealing very fully with events over the border. It makes extremely good reading. It is published by W. J. Ross at 69 St. Vincent Street, Glasgow, C.2. Annual subscription 6/6.

It is good to see Arthur Penny running again and running as well as ever. Such veterans as Arthur are the salt of this club and the fact that they can come back, when not far short of 40 years, tells that past training has been good. All younger running members can learn a thing or two from such as Arthur.

Ted Stimpson has recovered from his operation and is training again. We give Ted our best wishes for the future Track season—and what about the 1948 Olympic 10,000 metres, Ted?

We were pleased to see Dick Murphy starting the Ten Mile Walk and presenting his shield to the winner, Harry Churcher.

No mention was made in the Handbook of the winning by P. J. Briggs of the Surrey Junior Hurdles Championship last season. This was a most regrettable omission and we give our apologies to Briggs, who is now serving in the Army, and is stationed at Larkhill.

Victor Blowfield is showing international form over the country. His winning of the Club Ten was magnificent. With good training Vic is going to be a menace to the best in the country during the next Track season.

Also congratulations to a new member, Leslie Stokell, on his fine and ever-improving running this season. There are also big things in store for Les in the future. This column would like to see him try the 2 miles Steeplechase next summer.

Hon. Treasurer's Notes

W. G. W.

SUBSCRIPTIONS. Some misunderstanding appears to exist as to the date on which the club Annual Subscription becomes due. A number of members have assumed that the club Financial Year runs concurrently with the calendar year and that January 1st is the date on which subscriptions are due; others have regarded the date of their election to Membership as the governing date for payment. The clarification of this point is best served by a reference to rules 7 and 9 on pages 18 and 19 of the handbook, where it clearly states that the club financial year ends on September 15th and that subscriptions become due and payable on the date of the Annual General Meeting. It is important that outstanding subscriptions should be paid immediately. The normal running expenses of the club have to be met and a reference to the last balance sheet will show that they are no mean liability. Subscriptions should be forwarded or handed to the Hon. Treasurer. Alternatively they may be handed to the Assistant Hon. Treasurer, Mr. T. F. Morrell, or, in the case of walking members, to the Hon. Walking Secretary, Mr. S. Roberts. The official receipt will be issued by the Hon. Treasurer in all cases.

Whilst it is good to reflect on the growing strength of the Junior Section, we cannot overlook that their lot is not particularly happy, having regard to the inevitable operation of the National Service (Armed Forces) Acts at a very critical stage in their athletic careers. It behoves us to keep them fully informed of club activities and events, and ensure them of an easy resettlement into club affairs on their return to civil life. The committee do not enforce the payment of club subscriptions during the period a junior member is called to service under these Acts. It is hoped, however, that junior members will notify the Hon. Secretary immediately they are called to service and keep him posted with subsequent movements.

DAMAGE TO CLUB TROPHIES. In the event of a club trophy becoming damaged whilst in the safe keeping of the holder, no action should be taken regarding the repair of the trophy without prior authority of the committee. Any such action is likely to prejudice a possible claim through the club Insurance Policy. Damage to trophies, however small, and also any loss of trophy should be reported to the Hon. Treasurer immediately and a detailed statement of the casualty given.

ENGRAVING OF CLUB TROPHIES. Although most holders of trophies have had my circular letter, I would here repeat as a reminder and as a guidance for future holders, that it is desirable that all trophies should be engraved up to date before the date of the next competition. Where practicable it would be appreciated if holders would arrange for the delivery of the trophy to the club engravers, The Alexander Clark Co. Ltd., 38 Leadenhall Street, E.C.3, together with the particulars of the records to be engraved; any outstanding particulars can be obtained from the Hon. Running Secretary, Mr. E. Duffett. In those cases where the records are engraved on small silver shields affixed to the trophy or to the plinth of the trophy, it will suffice if the blank small shields to be engraved are removed and posted to the engravers together with the necessary particulars to be engraved. In these cases, however, it is important that the last engraved shield should also be sent to serve as a specimen for the engraver. Where holders find this arrangement impracticable perhaps they would be good enough to arrange to let me have the trophy at a convenient date so that I can make alternative arrangements.

ENGRAVING OF MEDALS. On the question of engraving of medals won by members, the last ruling given by the committee on this subject, which is still extant, is as follows: That the club undertake to engrave (1) All club medals; (2) All team race medals awarded by Athletic bodies and Associations to which the club is affiliated.

BADGES, ETC. Vest badges 2/6, and Coat metal badges 2/-, are in stock. As these are sold at cost price it is requested that applications by post should be accompanied by return postage. It is regretted that there is no immediate prospect of obtaining supplies of vests and ties or other clothing. Any helpful suggestions from members on this vexed subject will be appreciated.

FINALLY do not forget those subscriptions. A small item, but a very important item. You may win an award in the next club event and it would be very disappointing to find at the last moment that it was withheld because you had forgotten to pay your subscription. Be on the safe side and post it to me at once.

W. G. W.

Field Events Gossip

T. C. J.

The winter field events activities have been confined to the Coaching School, which has been functioning since the beginning of November, 1946, at Belgrave Hall. Unfortunately it has not been as well supported as was anticipated, but it is hoped all who have been regularly attending will put to good use the things they have learnt when they start their summer training. The S.A.A. League (Fields Events Section) programme has just come to hand. A Junior League will also function in which Field Events will be included. In addition a Decathlon competition has been added this year for individual competitors.

An interesting new competition is announced by The Polytechnic Harriers to take place at their Stadium on May 10th. The programme includes all the usual field events, three hurdle races and a steeplechase, comprising 11 events in all. The competition is both for individuals and teams. In the team competition four men score, and in this and the individual competition each competitor will be allowed to compete in not more than five selected events. Scoring will be by points from 20 points for first place to one point for 20th place. The "Sward Trophy" will be awarded to the winning team. It is hoped we can put up a very good show.

Report · page 8

With the Walkers

Our walkers started the winter season on the road in no uncertain manner, twenty-eight members turning out for the opening Club 5 Miles Handicap.

The first open, Highgate Harriers promotion, made it clear that we were making a rapid return to our pre-war predominance, at least over the shorter distances. Four in the first nine with the majority of clubs at full strength was certainly very promising. As time went on we won many other opens, Harry Churcher taking most of the individual honours with Jack Rutland a close second. As yet unbeaten, our lowest score was in the Cambridge Harriers open, when our placings were 1, 2, 3 and 5.

So far there has been little opposition from the Midlands and North and presumably we shall have to wait for the new 10 miles championship to find out their present strength. One thing is certain, all the leading clubs in the country will do their utmost to become the first 10 miles champions and our succession of wins in open races will no doubt make our opponents all the more determined to prevent us from crowning our successes with National Honours. We therefore look to all concerned to see that the next few months re-establish us as the premier walking club in the country.

As the National Junior Race will be over before this issue is out, little need be said, but young Mac's present form is good enough to annexe the individual title and that he will receive good support from Bert East, Harry Rouse, Joe Wilson and others. The team race on paper looks a very open affair, but once again there is not much indication of the opposition likely from the North and Midlands.

With the two longer distances being held in the South, we shall not this year have the disadvantage of travelling. Although we were placed in both these races last year, with the combined efforts of all concerned, we should not be content with less than first place and a return to our pre-war position as National Champions at all distances. We have plenty of good material; all that is required is plenty of regular training, and especially for the longer distances, lots of strolling. Regarding the latter, your Road Captain, Charlie Churcher, is anxious to get a good muster for Sunday Morning strolls. With the approaching spring the weather should be more suitable for this, and it is hoped that this side of training will receive full support. The venue is Putney High Street at 9 a.m., but if you want to join in somewhere en route, get in touch with your Road Captain and he will be pleased to make the necessary arrangements.

As a result of a Novices Race promoted by the M.P.A.A., two more Police members were enrolled. P. Sapcote and M. Archer, both of whom show every prospect of becoming useful walkers. George Bently, Fred's brother, back from the Forces, is with us again, and George Atkinson, also of the Police and a son of ex-Chief Inspector Atkinson, a former Barking to Southend winner, has joined us. Also Mac's young brother Sid, and Fred Herring, now first claim, will soon be available for teams.

To our new members we extend a very hearty welcome and wish them many happy years with the club. Our aim is 100 active walkers, so it's up to everyone to do their bit to increase our numbers still further and thus provide some champions for the future.

The Olympic Games are gradually creeping nearer. We already have men of Olympic class; these can quite easily be added to, and there is no reason why we should not produce another Olympic Champion, or perhaps even two—track and road. So good walking to you all and may our successes continue.

S. R.

Report · page 9

With the Juniors

On October 19th, at Wimbledon, we had a match with the junior members of Surrey A.C. and Dartford Harriers.

We had the first three men home in Derrick Burfitt, Eddie Short and Arthur Bruce, who came in together, and they were followed by Len Bishop.

Scoring three aside, the points were—Belgrave H. 6, Dartford H. 19, Surrey A.C. 25.

In a further match of five aside between Dartford and ourselves, we won with the lower score of 25 to our visitors' 41.

On November 23rd our lads made the journey to the Guards Depot, Caterham, where they were entertained to a match with the India Cadet Company. Eddie Short and Arthur Bruce came home together in first place, followed by a couple of Cadets, and then came the two Bishops brothers, Eric and Len. Next followed a whole crowd of Cadets, and eventually our other three runners, who were R. Garwood, K. E. Aspinall and B. C. Potter. The latter had lost their way and therefore their positions could not be counted in the team race. In view of the fact that we had four lads in the first six home, the Cadets very sportingly called the match a draw. Our lads then sat down to a real "Naafi" tea, and a good time was had by all!

We had a team entered in the Surrey County Youths' Championship, which was held at Coulsdon on December 7th, but only managed to field four runners. Their finishing positions were—13 L. C. Bishop, 25.1; 17 E. T. Gilbert, 25.50; 39 K. E. Aspinall, 27.48; 55 L. Cheesley, 32.52.

We finished sixth out of an entry of twelve teams, so our lads can be said to have done well, for with the exception of Len Bishop, this was their initial effort in on open event.

The club also entered a team for the Surrey County Junior Championship, held at Earlswood on January 4th. Owing to Service calls, the age for this race being 18 to 21, we only fielded four runners, but they can be said to have done well in being beaten in the team race by the narrow margin of 2 points. Arthur Bruce ran well to finish third, with Eddie Short fourth, then Len Adams scored fifth, and Eric Bishop seventh.

The course was very heavy and most of the runners had trouble in keeping their shoes on, a point to be borne in mind for future races.

Finishing times of our runners were—3 A. H. Bruce, 30.24; 4 E. A. Short, 30.32; 6 L. A. Adams, 32.27; 8 E. M. Bishop, 32.50. Team scores were—S. L. H. 17 pts., Belgrave H. 19 pts.

On January 18th our lads visited the Surrey A.C. at their quarters at Kingston. The match was over about 2½ miles and once again our trio of Derrick Burfitt, Eddie Short and Arthur Bruce, came home together in first place.

Len Bishop duly followed in fourth place, and with the match scoring four aside, we won with the lowest possible score of 10 points to Surrey A.C.'s 26.

Belgrave Open Walk

NOVEMBER 30th, 1946

Our own open did not attract quite as large an entry as had been anticipated, but of the 176 entries 158 started on a signal from the Club President. Harry Churcher contented himself with accompanying C. Morris of the Surrey A.C. for the first lap, after which he began to make a lead. Jack Rutland, on returning to the common, then went up into second place, and with McMullen, Joe Coleman, Len Fletcher and Bob Readman not far behind it soon became apparent that, barring a number of accidents, we should add one more to our list of successes. Harry continued to walk in excellent style until about half-a-mile from home when, to the surprise of everyone, he was ruled out, leaving Jack Rutland in the lead. This unfortunate occurrence gave Len Fletcher, competing in his first open for a considerable time, an opportunity of sharing in the team honours. Despite Harry's misfortune we won easily from Woodford Green by 21 points to 55 points, with Leicester Walking Club third with 57 points. From the result of the Enfield Open it was evident that competition between the "A" and "B" teams for handicap awards would be very keen. This proved even closer than was anticipated and resulted in the "A" team taking first place with the "B" team 1 second behind. Colin Sutton, starting from a longer mark than Len Fletcher found his way into the winning handicap team at Len's expense. Joe Scamell took the Veterans' prize, and Bob Readman the special prize for the first club member not gaining any other award.

The account would be incomplete without recording our most sincere thanks to the following for their donations to the prize fund:—Messrs. H. Evans, J. Bellchamber, A. A. Harley, A. Harley (Senr.), A. Beales, P. Wright, C. Sutton, F. Elson, C. Churcher, H. Churcher, J. Crump and F. Thompson.

Also to Mrs. Churcher for presenting a special prize. Lastly we are indebted to the R.W.A. Officials and Hon. Handicappers, to the many club members who assisted as stewards and pointsmen, and to those clubs and individuals who supported the event and helped to make it the success which it has always been.
