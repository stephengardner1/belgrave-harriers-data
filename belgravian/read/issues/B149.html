EDITORIAL

The New Year was heralded in by the announcement that British field athletes were to benefit from a generous £10,000 grant by Messrs. Courage, Barclay and Simonds Ltd. to boost jumping and throwing standards over the next five years. Athletics Weekly commented "----it will certainly be a great help to many athletes and give them every chance to reach the top IF THE RIGHT MATERIAL IS THERE". Is the right material, in fact, finding it's way to the National team? The B.A.A.B. use the Athletic Clubs of this country as their source for Internationals but to whom must the Clubs look for their material? Mainly, of course, the schools. It is this end of the scale that needs the attention. Every schoolboy plays soccer or rugby but how many have had the opportunity of pole-vaulting or even seeing a hammer? How many good-class rugby players have ever handled a shot or discus?-- They might have well missed their true vocation.

When the majority of schools in this country can field a team of two strings in all recognised events then the real cream will surely filter through to the top. Army units can find 2 men per event for the Inter-Unit Team Championships, and from the rawest of materials too, so the schools surely can. If 100 boys all throw the hammer for the first time, however crudely it may be, the best two or three are very likely to follow up their prowess if given the right encouragement. The seeds of enthusiasm must already be sown before a youngster will join an athletic club to improve his ability and seek competition. The field events sections of most Athletic Clubs are in dire need of recruits,---and it is upon school-leavers that most clubs depend. The number of Clubs in this country that can field 2 men in all field events can be counted on the fingers of one hand. It is no wonder that the National team suffers in turn.

If all schools, particularly those at the lower levels, would take a bigger interest in field events then they would be doing as big a service to the sport as the "Courage Awards". Perhaps our schoolteacher members are the best equipped to campaign for this end.

THE CHILDRENS' PARTY

C.S.

JANUARY 6th - BELGRAVE HALL

Crackers, jellies, balloons, sweets, ice-cream, cakes - games with 'Uncle' Bill Lucas and 'Uncle' Bob Taylor - music, dancing, more sweets, more balloons and, inevitably, more and more noise! Phew! Training is needed for this type of event just as much as the athletic kind and, thanks to Ted Pallan and his social committee, everything was kept under some sort of control by the many adult helpers.

The deafening din reached a crescendo when the puppet show got under way and 'Mr. Punch' went home eventually with an inferiority complex - there is no hope for the clown. When the conjuror had finished his penal task, there was barely time to clear the chairs away before the floor was taken by the children once more. More noise. Then silence!

Father 'Nutty' Christmas had arrived. The children watched his slow plod with an air of apprehension as though they feared he might not approve ( having been kept hanging around for 12 days; but their fears were quickly dispelled when the lights went up and the presents were handed out. By the same token, Santa Claus himself was forgotten (for the next 11 months at any rate) when the presents were opened.

Bedlam broke loose once more; this time accompanied by the roar, rattle and crash of the new toys. It was a joy to see so many senior member[illegible] scuttling across the floor with aeroplanes, cars and balloons - to see Ron Linstead engrossed in the puppet show and Bob Taylor leading the 'conga'. Ye[illegible] for the sake of our Senior members, we must keep the children's party going.

A COUNTRYMAN'S Jottings

COUNTRYMAN

Gerry North, the holder, was given little hope by the Press of obtaining a forward position in the Inter-Counties race at Reading. His defeat by Snowdon in a Surrey League match and sixth place in the Lancashire County were the basis of these views. When, seven days before the big race, Gerry only just held off Shelley (N.Staffs) in the Mitcham '15' it was said he was not the force of last year. How wrong they were. Despite suffering from a bad cold he beat all the recognised opposition only to lose his title to Portsmouth's T.K. Johnston who, revelling in the Artic conditions, chose this day to make the biggest break-through of his career.

I wonder how many of our senior athletes have noticed that Gerry can race week in and week out yet still produce the big effort for the big occasion. Perhaps, if the lesson is learned, we will soon field our strongest team more than once a season.

* * *

The first season of North Surrey League fixtures has ended, and was undoubtedly a success. These matches give the bulk of club athletes a chance to experience the big race atmostphere and, with 10 to score, an opportunity of counting for their team.

The league was born at the "3 Kings" Mitcham in May of last year. The proposal was put forward by Mitcham AC but, because of traditional fixtures being affected, it was not over-enthusiastically received by the other six clubs present who were, Belgrave, Walton, Surrey, Hercules, Herne Hill and South London. Eventually,however, all except SLH agreed to give it a trial.

I understand that South London are now looking for a "Mr Heath" to negotiate their entry to the "six". I wonder if Bill Lucas would make a good "De Gaulle"!

* * *

Cliff Roffey arrived in this country a few weeks ago for a spot of leave from business in Kenya. I saw Cliff before the Club '10' and he said he did not feel as fit as on his last visit. Personally, I think his effort in the Championship compared favourably with the first race of his last visit.

Few will need reminding of the success he enjoyed last time. He went from strength to strength and in his final outing clocked fastest time of the day on Stage 10 of the Brighton Relay. That other overseas stalwart, Ranjit Bhatia, handed over to Cliff that day and for both that was the last time they ran on the Brighton Road. Unfortunately, Cliff returns to Kenya just 2 weeks before the National relay in April, so must undergo another period away before he can return to the Belgrave team.

Our other exiles, Mal Robinson (U.S.A.) and Ranjit Bhatia (India) are also keen to return for a spell. Who knows? they might all arrive together.

* * *

Clive Shippen, who we have not seen competitively since last August, tells me he is making a determined come-back. Clive, who is 31, is not satisfied with what he calls "a career of near misses". Although he gained a Surrey Co vest in 1962, not a single team winners medal has come his way. He has a silver and bronze for the South of Thames Junior, silver and bronze for the London County, silver for the South of Thames Senior, bronze for the Surrey County, bronzes for the London/Brighton National and Southern Relays and narrowly missed a Club Honours Badge when he ran a 9:28 steeplechase the very season the AAA reduced their Grade 1 standard from 9:30 to 9:25!

Should he be successful in adding some "golds" to his collection an interesting set would be the South of Thames medals, for, to obtain a complete set, the gold must be won last of all-- the winning team being barred for good.

* * *

Of the newer members that have been appearing at our recent fixtures Pat Bradley is perhaps the most promising addition. Pat, a Scot, was introduced into the Club by Bill Kerr and has already shown useful form. Next season could see him amongst our leading runners.

Paul Binns, still a junior, is not yet as advanced as Bradley and our other Juniors, but after speaking to him recently I am convinced he will make the grade. He has been running for nearly 2 years now but has only just started seriously. if he can stick to his 40 miles a week training he should reap the benefit in a year or two.

Martin Edwards, a junior also, has wasted little time in carving a niche for himself in the ranks of our Junior team---a team which can be expected to perform well against the tough opposition they will meet in the Southern and National. Before these jottings are read the former event will be over and I shall not be surprised if a set of medals comes their way;nor for that matter the senior team also.

SIX MORE MARKS IN DELHI STATE ATHLETICS RANJIT BHATIA SETS THE PATTERN BY A STAFF REPORTER

You can't keep a good man down! The headline you see here is reproduced from an Indian newspaper, and reads on as follows:-

"Indeed, the day's outstanding performer was a Senior---Ranjit Bhatia of St. Stephen's College, who represented India at the Rome Olympics It was Bhatia who began Saturday's record-smashing---and in conditions hardly conducive to such a feat.

Bhatia began a "dead-cert" for the 5,000 metres. In fact, he had to race all the way against himself---his nearest rival was more than 400 metres behind---but he did this so splendidly that the whole Railway Stadium had to applaud him.

He had clocked 15:4.1, 10.3 secs better than the old mark, yet he looked still fresh at the finish. Later, he was still fit enough to help his team take second place in the 4X400 metres relay--------"

Ranjit, who lectures at St. Stephen's, introduced cross-country running to the College team and their runners have improved immeasurably, winning the state title. He tells me he also ran 1:56.7 (800 m) and 4:02.7 (1500 m) at the same meeting---surprising many pundits who regard him as semi-retired.

International politics rarely affect the affairs of Belgrave Harriers yet India's border dispute with China is an exception. Ranjit was planning to spend next summer's holiday in London but will now have to do a 4 month spell of military training instead. Thus, a reunion to which we all look forward must be postponed once more.

CHRISTMAS 1962 — CHRISTMAS DAY PAARLAUF

Eighteen pairs took part in the annual 30 minute paarlauf from Lauriston Cottage and 12 of the teams were from visiting Clubs. This was the 9th year the race had been held and its popularity grows on each occasion.

The winning pair, Gowan and Derham of H.H.H. were the only set of these two-man relay teams to complete 8 circuits and in so doing they set a course record of 6 miles 1094 yards. It is customary for the Belgrave runners to draw for partners - one from the better half with one from the lesser - but with our first couple finishing only 8th the time has probably come for our stars to partner each other.

BOXING DAY HANDICAPS

Held at Battersea Park on a bitterly cold day this meeting was still able to command a fair turnout. It was as though those in attendance had friends in the Met. Office who had informed them that this would be their last chance of performing on firm going before the snow storms and freeze-up which arrived that afternoon.

There were 7 starters in the 120 yards sprint and Pat Cornelius (off five yards) steamed home in a brisk 12.7. Later he made full use of his 5ft. 6" allowance in the shot to beat all but one of the 8 competitors with 33'0". Not a performance to worry Dallas Long but not a bad effort after all that Plum Pud, Turkey, and cigars!

A grand total of 25 starters went to their respective marks for the 2 mile run handicap and John Leaver, a youth, emerged a popular winner by 7 seconds from Dudley Gordon who manages to find time between his continental jaunts to'get up' in these handicaps. Chris Steer and John Bicourt had a really ding-dong battle with Chris just getting the edge over his younger rival for 5th place. One of the most heartening features of this event was the fine running and encouraging support of our younger members.

In the 3 mile Walk Handicap there were 10 starters. Eric Hall was back marker with an allowance of a minute and P. Wilson was first away with 7:30. Wilson held on to his lead and finished almost a minute ahead of Hall who had worked his way through the remainder of the field with deceptive ease. J. Wilson came under the Judges' eye on the 2nd lap and George Piddington better known in the cross-country section, decided to stick to running after 6 tough laps of " heel and toe".

South of Thames Junior

BILL LUCAS

BILL LUCAS REPORTS:

The race was held this year from one of the regular venues for this event, namely The Guards Depot Caterham where the dressing, bathing and catering facilities are somewhat better than usual.

The conditions were very damp and slippery underfoot after continuous rain in the morning which had hampered the operations of the officials in preparing the course.

Our teams were very depleted and although fifty two names had been entered for five teams only twenty two went to the start. This was most disappointing as this race is designed to give a chance to the "lesser lights" of appearing in open competition. Belgrave accept this opportunity with open hands and enter everyone able to put one leg in front of the other.

In the absence of Barry Sawyer, Ken Miller, Clive Shippen, Laurie O'Hara, Derek Crookes and George Williams from the A team our hopes of a place as in the 1961 race were reduced to nil and the best we could do was to take 12th position out of the 69 teams that finished.

Tony Fairclough confirmed his early season form by running a steady race throughout to finish 19th and be our first finisher. Another junior, Chris Steer, also performed well to finish 55th with John Moore 59th and David Jones 89th. These were the scoring four who amassed 222 points as compared with Guildford and Godalming's 81.

Many of our runners were short of training and probably the most disappointing position was that of Cyril Forey who after a fair start fell right back to 195th whereas he should have been up with the leaders. What a pity Cyril is wasting his natural ability by lack of concentration on training.

Good performances were put up by Alan Morrison (115th) and Fred Paget(148th) and Keith Angus a new member showed great promise in finishing 170th.

It was interesting to note that four juniors were amongst our first seven finishers--a good omen for the future.

The B and E teams were incomplete to score .

THE CLUB '7'

Gerry North earned the right to hold the magnificent 'Black Staffe' Shield for 12 months, when he won the Club 7 mile cross-country championships at Wimbledon on December 1st. Although the winner finished over a minute ahead of the field, the race was full of interest throughout and many observers were speculating as to the humdinger that would ensue if the absent Kerr and Newell were able to get amongst 'this lot'.

Thirty-five runners set off in the murk of the afternoon over the three lap course with Walker, Black and Thresher forcing the pace. After one circuit these three, with North, Davies and Sawyer tucked in behind were clear of the field. Another half lap saw the fading of Davies and Sawyer and by the time the final circuit was under way a revitalised Walker had shaken off all but North.

It was at this stage that Gerry moved away like a Rolls-Royce in a scooter rally and it says great things for Charlie's temperament that he battled on undeterred never allowing his pursuers to gain an inch when many lesser men would have been thoroughly demoralised. Gerry stormed round that last lap finished 63 seconds clear of Chas. John Thresher was troubled with a minor leg injury and did not appear to be his usual fiery self but he nevertheless, finished a comparatively close 4th. Jack Brown had to make full use of his experience to pull back Tony Fairclough and John Davies in the later stages but eventually he got away from them and was fast catching Barry Sawyer in the run in. Thirty runners completed the course and the handicap was won by Niel Aston from Chas. Walker and Alan Black who finished equal second.

SURREY Co. CHAMPIONSHIPS — Great Win For Juniors

W.E.L.

REIGATE PRIORY---JANUARY 5TH 1963

Heavy falls of snow during Christmas and New Year covered the course to a depth of 8/9inches with deeper drifts in parts--this, with a bitter cold wind, made conditions far from attractive for both runners and spectators although the latter were able to obtain some inner warmth from a welcome stall selling hot Oxo (no advertising intended).

The afternoon's activities started with the Junior race for which we were fielding our strongest team made up of a number of the runners who had in 1962 taken the Youths championship and the remnants of last years Junior team. It soon became evident that we were possible winners for Tony Fairclough was with the leaders followed closely by Chris Steer with David Cocks and Jackie Hampshire not far behind. Tony ran a gallant race in atrocious conditions and fought every inch of the way with Stebbings of Croydon and Gregory of Walton before being forced back in 3rd position which he held at the finish. The others also held on to the early places and we took the team honours from Herne Hill, the favourites, by a mere two points.

Martin Edwards, a new member, ran very well in finishing 32nd.

Next came the Senior race in which we were hoping for a place despite the fact that our best runners are so spread that they do not qualify for Surrey In fact we had runners in the Lancashire, Middlesex, Hampshire, Kent and Sussex championships on the same day.

Alan Black set off at a great pace in an endeavour to secure an early good position but a slip sent him sprawling in the snow and it took two miles before he settled down again. It says much for his fitness that he worked his way up to 8th to again secure his County vest which he was first awarded in 1961.

Charlie Walker confirmed his good form by running a steady race to take 11th place. Our next runner was John Thresher in 25th position; a little disappointing as he had been running very well in earlier races, but perhaps understandable for a track man in such conditions. Don Maclean excelled himself to be our fourth scorer only a short distance behind John. Then came a gap before Ken Miller appeared--Ken is slowly working his way back afer a long lay off through injury and by the time the big races come round should be a great force again. Our sixth scorer was Eric Gebbett with Ron Linstead (placed 70th) just behind--this was too far back for a runner of Ron's calibre in a field of only 120 runners, but it was obvious that the deep snow was not to his liking. In the end we took 4th position in the team event in a race where form went by the board.

Feature · page 11

Is AMERICA'S College Set-Up The Answer?

MAL ROBINSON

MAL ROBINSON WRITES FROM UNIVERSITY OF SOUTHERN LOUISIANA, U.S.A.

The fact that the United States of America produces so many sports stars is no accident. But it is hard for the lay-man to understand why the U.S. can head the world list at so many sports. A look at its High School and College set-up provides some of the answers.

In American schools sport has an important place. There is no time for a "playing is the important thing" attitude. Each school maintains a sports department with a specialist coach for each sport for the express purpose of providing a winning team. Usually a school has its own stadium ( often better in quality than most British First Division Football Stadia) and each football game is attended by upwards of 10,000 spectators. The coach is normally a College graduate holding a degree in Physical Education as well as some other academic subject.

All this is made possible by alumni (old boys) and local businessmen. They provide the money to run the athletic programme but as is often the case, " he who pays the piper, calls the tune" and a non winning coach will often find that his contract is not renewed at the end of the season. A coach of a state championship team, however, can bargain for his contract.

Old boys( sometimes for tax exemption purposes) donate money to obtain a winning team and local merchants do so because a winning ball-team provides publicity for the locality. Every state is divided into areas, and within these schools are graded according to pupils strength. Normally any school will confine its competition to schools within its group. At the end of the season each area classification champion plays off on a knockout basis to determine overall state supremacy. The newspaper reporters then draw upon all teams in any one classification to determine a state team - similar to an England representative side. This is further developed with regard to area and then national teams - "All-American High School Team of '62". For the individual school there is great prestige in being respective class champion, for the individual it is a great honour to be picked for a representative side.

Also at season's close a certain number of the school team will be selected by the coach for a letter award. This is the letter of the school (I am an S man of Southwestern). This is a great honour and one which if properly earned is of considerable value to a high school or college graduate. One of the biggest high school advantages takes the form of college scholarships. There are however, different types of scholarship awarded with varying degrees of coverage with regard to the payment of fees. To ensure that the college secures the best boys for its various teams an elaborate talent scouting system is operated and in some cases "under the counter" inducements are even offered.

Once the boy is at college he is expected to concentrate on two things - training and study (often in that order). During the season the athlete will train hard each day and this continues, though admittedly with less intensity, in the off-season. He is expected to be in his dormitory by 10.30p.m. and no smaking and no drinking is the general rule. Contrary to general belief in the U.K. a certain scholastic standard must be maintained and theoretically a student is not allowed to compete in the college team if he does not maintain a C average. Therefore the coach is expected to keep an eye not only upon athletic progress but also their studies.

Bearing all this in mind it is not difficult to see the sort of environment that has produced so many Olympic and world champions. The essential difference between the American and British sports systems is the comparative absence in the U.S. of the small amateur clubs and district associations and the concentration of athletic activity in highly subsidised and professional-minded colleges. In Britain we used to depend upon Oxbridge as our source of National Champions but whilst we still find our Metcalfes, Richard Sharps, and Chris Davidges, the amateur clubs throughout the country are now producing the lion's share of our stars. Ineviteably there is a different approach resulting from each system. Despite the insistence on the maintenance of academic studies the American athlete is at least semi-professional and as such has a far greater need to win, whilst his English counter part, although perhaps nowadays not an amateur of true blue quality, treats his sport more as a hobby and this would probably account for his not having quite the same 'killer' streak as his Trans-Atlantic rivals.

WALKING

There was a time when any talk of Belgrave walking centred upon Hall and Vickers. Then came Williams and Middleton to hog the limelight. It was almost like a procession of music-hall names---rumour has it that Morecombe and Wise even considered joining our walking section. But this winter it's fair to say that one name more than any other has made Belgrave walking news---Vaughan Thomas.Vaughan is yet another walker who looks like gaining regular international honours before very long. He has led the way home for our boys in all the 'sevens' so far this season and obviously is a born fighter. The two Rays, Hall and Middleton, have also walked extremely well, and this part of the season,with its relatively short races,is not Ray Hall's particular favourite. John Knifton and Eric Hall have also been in winning teams and illustrate two ends of a scale---the enthusiasm and fire of the youngster and the determination and experience of the older man. There are many who are worthy of mention but for the sake of brevity we would conclude by mentioning a name to look out for in future---Dave King. He's provided a few upsets already and looks likely to unseat a few more established favourites before he's finished.

The cross-country section certainly seems to be emerging out of the depths again and successes have not been comong so frequently to the walkers as they have in the past five years. But the talent is still there and certainly the spirit has not disappeared. If Thomas maintains his form, Middleton gets really fit, Ray Hall gets going over the slightly longer distances, and our youngsters Barnes, Knifton, King etc. continue to improve then we shall be taking the gold instead of the silver and bronze.

Junior Section

Fairclough heads the Juniors---Bicourt the Youths------Hayes the Boys. This is the position at the present time, but who knows how long it will last when we have so many other youngsters striving to depose them? Hayes is expected to show some pretty hot form when he gets amongst youths next season, for this lad could well be a worthy successor to Bicourt. But this season has seen Fairclough and Bicourt fighting it out for honours in their respective divisions with Fairclough perhaps having the edge by virtue of his excellent 3rd place in the Surrey County Junior Championship. Then came the "Southern" at Parliament Hill. With a beautifully timed run Bicourt took 3rd place-----an effort of great merit. He thus becomes our 'Cover-Boy' for this issue;the first Youth to grace the front page. Fairclough's 18th place was also a fine effort and endorsed the consistency this scraggy fleet-footed hope for the future has shown this season.

Bicourt has received admirable support this year from Simmons, Edwards and Leaver. John Leaver is not yet quite a match for Eugene and "Noddy" but this tall, stylish runner seems to have temporarily outgrown his strength (a common occurrence amongst tall young athletes), but once his strength has been built up to match his physique then we could well see some startling progress from this likeable youngster. Patience is the key, and this applies to all of our younger runners. Remember, you all develop at different rates and if your rival appears to be improving more rapidly than yourself it doesn't necessarily mean it will remain that way. Many of you, after a period of encouraging progress, will find yourselves "marking time" for anything up to a whole season---dont be discouraged, for this is merely a period of consolidation. You cannot climb to a higher plateau until you have established a firm foothold on the lower one. Routine plays an important part here. Hang on to your fitness by resolving never to miss a training run--and if you don't feel like training? give yourself the luxury of an "easy" until you do, but still go out!

The National Championship, to be held on March 2nd at Cambridge will conclude the 1962/3 season and we look to our Youths and Juniors to round off this season on a high note.

Bels. Win Mitcham '15' Again

JANUARY 12th

Running in near Arctic conditions Belgrave "froze" the field for the 2nd successive year and again took both team awards ( 3 to score and 6 to score The remarkeable thing is that only one of last year's successful trio was in our scoring 3 and only 2 of last year's 6 in our first half dozen - so much for our depth.

A few places back Sawyer had passed Newell in the closing stages and Chas. Walker had held on well to 14th place. THE SOUTHERN (Senior)

DISAPPOINTING 8TH BY BELS:-

Held at Parliament Hill Fields it was touch and go on the morning of the race whether the race would be held. A partial thaw during the preceding week had softened up the ground and melted the surface of many large expanses of snow---but Saturday, February 16th, heralded the return of those icy North-Easterlies and everything turned to solid ice. It took the organisers a long time to re-route the course and avoid the hazardous ice-rinks but several balancing tests could not be missed and those with unsuitable footwear risked, and in some cases sustained, bad injury.

On paper we had high hopes of being second to Portsmouth AC but once again we were unable to field a truly representative team and the ten that did start were dogged by misfortune.Those that were unable to appear were Linstead (injury), Thresher (cold), Newell (work) and Roffey (flu). Gerry North had had tendon trouble for over 3 weeks and was ill advised to run, especially as he had also been down with gastric trouble on the Thursday and Friday. However, he and Barry Sawyer (foot injury) were plucky starters in our depleted team. We still had fair hopes at the start but the course disposed of them as well and Belgrave were written off in 8th place, for Sawyer had to retire and Bill Kerr's shoe was split by the ice, forcing him to withdraw as well.

Gerry nursed himself along from 30th to 11th at two laps, but on the final circuit was obliged to give away 7 places. Alan Black got an excellent start, being placed in the first ten at a half-mile, and our supporters were hopeful that he would reproduce his scintillating form of the previous week when he won the final league match, but Alan also was affected by the Belgrave jinx and moved steadily back to the 40's---a position he held over the last circuit. David Jones was nowhere near his recent form and John Moore, inexcusably, was left at the start. Charlie Walker ran well and moved through the field with great determination---fast catching Alan at the finish.

Our footballing runner, Laurie O'Hara, was taking part in his first race over 9 miles and did very well to finish 92nd. He was not in the least distressed and was soon talking about the Sunday League football match he would be playing next day! The only good thing about this weather is that it releases Laurie from his Saturday game to run for us.

Jack Brown, who appeared only as a reserve, was a gallant 109th in his first race since the Club '7' in early December. Close behind was Don Maclean (118) who made ground impressively in the latter stages to complete our scoring six.

To finish 8th without Kerr, Linstead, Sawyer, Newell, Thresher and Roffey just shows what potentiality we really have, We can only hope our bad luck came all at once and that we shall have a 100% top-form turn-out for the forthcoming 'National' and 'Brighton'.

On the overall results of Seniors, Juniors and Youths only T.V.H., as last year, had a better record---their positions of 2,2,4, beating our 8, 4,6, Ponders End's 5,12,3 (!) and Shaftesbury's 11,1,8. Mitcham recorded 14, 19,2 whilst Portsmouth, Woodford and SLH were not strong enough to finish teams in the first 20 of each section.

Perhaps there are a few seeds of comfort amongst these figures after all.

'FLASHBACK!'---------- 9 years. THE SOUTHERN 1954

This picture was taken by Ed Lacey at Parliament Hill just after the start of the Southern Senior Championships of 1954. That man out front?----Why, "Jumbo" Masters of course.The "Pied Piper of Parliament Hill" is seen here propelling that Belgrave vest to the very summit where T.V. cameras were positioned to screen their first ever "Southern".

The North Surrey League

FINAL RESULT (Subject to confirmation):-

This was the League's first season and it consisted of four fixtures in which all six clubs competed. Any number can run but only the first ten from each club count in the scoring (the "University" scoring method).If a club fields less than 10 men then their score is made up by the addition of positions 60,59,58 etc.

An individual award goes to the man who, competing in all 4 races, scores the lowest number of points.

The four host clubs this season were Belgrave, Walton, Surrey, and Herne Hill, in that order. But for the Surrey match there were over 100 starters in each race and the biggest team turn-out was Belgrave's 30 in the final match.

RACE NO.1----Wimbledon, Oct 27th.

This was held in conjunction with our "Opening 5", and Gerry North was the first winner to go into the League's record books---this was also his first win over the country in Belgrave colours.

Charlie Walker ran exceedingly well to finish 5th and was well supported by Pat Newell and young Fairclough. Walton's packing, 7 in 17, was too good for the Bels.

RACE NO.2---Esher, Dec 15th.

This was the toughest course of all and the going was really heavy. Gerry North won by a street and was this time supported by John Thresher (8th) and Pat Newell again (11th). This was Bradley's first outing and his 20th place just behind Sawyer and Fairclough was a fine effort. Bill Kerr was lying 5th on the last lap when he fell with a badly sprained ankle.

Walton again took the team-race, but by a smaller margin from the Bels who scored one point less than in race No.1 but had to go back to 54th place for their 10th scorer. But for Bill's accident we would have won.

RACE NO.3--Kingston, Dec 29th.

Held in Richmond Park over a snow-covered course Surrey A.C.'s Snowden, revelling in the conditions of his home course, held off a fast-finishing Gerry North by 1 second. Both runners were nearly 1½ mins ahead of the next man. Weather conditions were responsible for keeping the field down to 75 finishers.

RACE NO.4.---Addington, Feb 9th

This was to be the race where Gerry North would clinch the individual title, but Gerry was injured and unable to run so the award went to Walton's Bob Roath.

North's absence did not affect the team award, and it was ironical that we should score our first league win without him. What's more, Alan Black showed everyone that he could be a "North" if he liked and produced a power performance that made the rest of the field look like hacks. On a course of heavy snow and occasional patches of frozen plough his effort drew forth superlatives from all sides. Bill Kerr and David Jones, both getting back to fitness again, provided excellent support with their 4th and 12th places.

In the four races, 21 Belgravians contributed to our total points score, thus illustrating our considerable depth. Unfortunately, it also indicates how rarely we manage to field our best runners all at once.

25 Years Ago

16th JANUARY 1938---THE INTERNATIONAL C.C. CLUB RACE AT LA CROIX DE BERNEY, PARIS.

A further invitation having been given by the Union Sportive Metropolitaine for us to compete in the above event was accepted and the following team was chosen to represent the Club---Carter, Cohen, Footer, D. Patience, J. Parker, Penny, Tobin and Verney, accompanied by W.G. Webb and H. Parker as Club Officials.

--------and soon we were to hear the voices of our President, and Tom Carter, Bert Footer and Arthur Penny coming over the air from the Radio City Broadcasting Station in Paris, telling the French folk why the Belgrave Harriers were in their gay city.

--------38 teams were to compete in the race, including the Belgian Champions (Union St-Gilloise) and ourselves, the only British entry. After the parade of the runners the "Off" was given and soon we had the pleasure of seeing Parker leading the field. At 3 miles Parker was still leading with Penny & Footer coming through to the front. At this stage, however, it seemed that our other boys were further back than they should be and one began to wonder if that awful sea-crossing was affecting their running. At 5 miles Parker had dropped to 5th place and though Penny and Footer were running strongly and Carter and Cohen striving hard, it could easily be seen that we could not win. The team of Algerians (U.S. Belfortaine) was running wonderfully well and had 3 men in the first 4.

--------In the last lap of the race Penny came up to pass Parker and finished first man for us in 7th position.

RESULT 7th A.W.Penny,9th J.Parker,11th H.E.Footer, 23rd C.T.Carter, 27th L.J.Cohen 32nd R.W.Tobin, 34th D.Patience, 117th H.G. Verney.

--------during the race our President (W.L. Bayliss) was introduced to members of the German, Austrian and Italian governments. Are the Belgrave Harriers to visit one of these countries for International Honours?! (Eds' Note: "Yes, some were, but regrettably not in the role of athletes---21 months later we were at war).
