Editorial

Your Editors

Dear Fellow Members, A VERY HAPPY AND SUCCESSFUL NEW YEAR

We are honoured to become your editors at the same time as the election of your new President Tom Carter, to whom we are indebted for his typically modest article.

What greater pleasure could be given to this wonderful veteran of so many individual and team successes in Belgrave colours, than for us to regain our supremacy over the cross-country clubs of Britain?

We are already London County Cross-Country Champions and we are pleased to see the great form of Jack Brown, Charlie Walker and Charlie Dabbs. Let us see how many more titles we can annexe during 1957.

Owing to our inability to edit non-existent material, we were almost driven to publishing our personal memoirs. Whilst this would doubtless prove most indigestible reading, it does seriously show how much we depend on articles contributed by section leaders and in fact by any members.

We welcome material from any source, sent to us within reasonable time of the event to which it refers.

With your co-operation we hope to be able to maintain the high standard of editorship set by our predecessors.

Your Editors.

Tom CarterJack BrownCharlie WalkerCharlie Dabbs

Letter · page 2

From Your President

C.T. CARTER. President.

Dear Belgravians, Thank you all for the great honour which you have bestowed upon me in making me your President for this year.

To me, this wonderful Club of ours has no equal, for what other club can point to so many successes in all branches of athletics.

There is no need for me to remind any of you as to these achievements, all you have to do is to read about them in your winter handbook or ask any oldtimer (I know!) of Belgrave who would willingly devote a few weeks to telling you.

I should like to draw members attention to our motto, "Semper Paratus" which means "Always Ready". To me this means always ready for anything for our Club; I hope that this means the same to you also.

Something that I should like to see done, is that of the turning out of teams in club vests. When representing the Club, be proud of it, let rival club performers fear the sight of claret and gold vests and Persil-white shorts.

I cannot close these few remarks without mentioning our two Olympic representatives; congratulations Eric and Vic.

When you read these few words of them, you will know how well they have performed and have given of their best.

Here's to a successful year and once again thank you for making me a very happy and proud Belgravian.

C.T. CARTER. President.

C.T. Carter

Column · page 3

On the Track

Frank Simmons

Once again I am accepting, with mental reservations, the unenviable job of Track Captain. I say reservations, because I am not really satisfied that the 1956 track season, though successful, has not been accompanied by the support which I expect of Belgravians; in fact I wondered at times whether it was worth the trouble trying to instil some enthusiasm into you.

Perhaps my forthright approach to you was not the correct one, but like the leopard, I don't suppose I will change my spots. My one thought is to try to assist you on the track, to get you to enjoy ATHLETICS, to coerce you to run, throw or jump for the club. This of course, should be unnecessary, for it is always an honour to represent a club such as ours.

We need more endeavour and fewer excuses, then we shall show all that Belgrave is the best club in the country.

The talent is within our ranks, it only needs self-confidence and club spirit to bring out the best in all of you. Remember, you are the club, you Seniors, Juniors & Youths. Your officials do their best on your behalf, give them full support and their task will be lightened, for without that it is sheer hard work.

I look forward to a more successful season in 1957, but we can only achieve this if all give their unstinted support, both in home and away fixtures and in major trophy competitions.

Frank Simmons

Report · page 4

Floodlit Meeting - 24th. October

Frank Simmons

On a cold damp evening at Tooting Bec, the first inter-club "Floodlit Meeting" was held for the "Melbourne Trophy".We had entered three teams, Senior, Junior and Youths.

Inability to turn out Junior and Youths sprinters weakened us, but those who ran in the longer events gave of their best, indeed, once again they demonstrated to our Seniors the art of Team running.

It was a source of satisfaction to their advisers, and to me, to see our lads running as a team, as Belgrave used to run, our juniors packing in their one mile race 4th, 5th and 8th and the youths in their's, 5th, 7th and 8th, gaining minor team placings. This shows signs of better running when these boys have had more experience, they were the youngest teams running and it should be noted that they all wore their "Belgrave Vest" proudly. We were weakened again in the senior events, Ron Holtum having the flu and Jack Brown unable to be present. Bob Taylor tried to contact Charlie Walker but failed.

However, after much persuasion Bill Bird made our three in the 3000 metres team race, but not being at his best he finished well down. Ray Tooby ran well to be placed third (How about that Club Vest Ray?) and George Davidson was fifth. We were third in the team race. In the absence of Holtum I decided on a gamble and ran Clive Ryan in our senior 4 x 110yds. team. This team of C.R. Jones, D. Carter, Ian Forrester and Clive ran well to finish second to the Poly, who had Shenton on the last leg, Clive hanging on for dear life for second place and gaining some valuable experience against top class sprinters.

Our 4 x 220yds. team again finished second to the Poly, being beaten by 8yds.

The forward running of D. Carter in both sprints and the running of Roy Glenn who is always ready to have a bash should give us hopes for next season.

The tit-bit for us, was the mile medley relay. Dudley Gordon ran his best quarter ever. Not knowing who was in front of him, he just "went" and surprised us all with his determined running, handing over second to Dennis Butler for the first furlong. Dennis gave us the lead but Pat Fox, inexperienced as he was, lost ground and handed over to John Davies 40yds. down. John set about his task in workmanlike style, running in his usual relaxed way, he reduced the lead on the first lap and in the back straight of the final lap came away to give us a win. John's time was 1min.55.8secs, truly wonderful running on such a cold night. Our field events lads performed well, M. Ash and D. Ansell gained first team points in the long jump and Ash and C.R. Jones second points in the high jump.

We finished third in the competition as a whole, points being added from matches. It was surprising the number of our members who always seem to appear just too late to be asked to run, or have the flimsiest excuses as to why they cannot, yet are always willing to "hold" a watch on somebody else and then criticise them when they were not willing to have a go themselves.

Frank Simmons.

Feature · page 5

Do Not Despair

During the last few years a considerable number of promising members have disappeared from competition long before age should have caught up with them. What is the cause? Is it married life, studies or just a spell of poor form, resulting in loss of interest. Perhaps they just felt they were too old.

It need'nt be thought that because one has reached the age of 30 or even 40 one is necessarily past it. Indeed there are instances too numerous to mention of athletes well past 40 putting up class performances. Our own Charlie Smart being a good example.

So don't despair those of you who think you are past it, try those shoes on again and take a few gentle training runs. What could be more enjoyable than a spin on Wimbledon Common on a Saturday afternoon? You will probably surprise yourself and a good many others too.

Charlie Smart

Report · page 6

S.L.H. 30miles road race.

Saturday Sept.8th. saw us with a full team at Old Coulsdon for this classic event and we were greeted by that elusive body called the sun.

The promoters very sportingly delayed the start to enable MacDonald, who had arrived late, to take part, and we finally got away at 2-45p.m.

At the end of the first lap Collin Collis and Eric Gebbett were running with the leading bunch of men, with W. Wortley, A. Penstone and A. Bishop not far behind, while A. Byford and J. MacDonald were some way back.

After 15miles Collin and Eric were still with the leaders in a time of 1h.29m.30s., but at 17miles Eric began to drop back and at the end of the third lap was some four minutes down. A. Bishop was now our third man and at this stage Wortley and Penstone dropped out, leaving us with five men still in the race.

On the last lap Collin held on to third place to finish in the magnificent time of 3hrs.2m.33s., while Eric moved up from ninth to sixth.

In spite of having two in the first six we could not prevent Thames Valley's powerful trio from winning the team race.

A. Byford was most unfortunate to miss standard by a mere half minute.

The actual winner was A. P. Keily of Derby & County A.C. in the record time of 2hrs. 59 mins. 8 secs.

56 started and 41 finished.

Report · page 6

London to Brighton Running Race

Yet another resounding success was achieved by the Road Running Section when they won the team event in this classic race. The runners concerned were the indomitable trio of A. Bishop J. MacDonald and W. Wortley who had finished second last year.

W. Wortley ran with the leaders for about 17 miles when he began to fall back. A. Bishop and J. MacDonald started more soberly and at 10 miles Bishop began to go ahead. Passing 20 miles in 2 hrs. 16 mins. he was 4 mins. behind Wortley, while MacDonald was 3 mins. further back.

Wortley was by now beginning to have a rough time but was still under three hours at the marathon distance, and at 27 miles he was overtaken by Bishop. However, he continued to press on in true Belgrave fashion in spite of being reduced almost to a walk. Bishop although fading somewhat after 40 miles managed to hold on to 12th. place and so be our first man home.

Wortley and MacDonald were never far apart after 35 miles and finished, in that order, with less than one minute between them; the latter improving on his last year's time by no less than 40 minutes!

Our fourth runner A. Byford, did very well to complete the course in 30th. position but said afterwards that he had found it a very long journey.

The winner was R.F. Hopcroft of Thames Valley in the very good time of 5 hrs. 36 mins. 26 secs.

Report · page 9

Walton - 20th October, 1956.

This was to prove a great day for Belgrave Harriers, in the two events which were so admirably organised by Walton A. G. and The Road Runners Club. It is impossible to speak too highly of the accurate timekeeping and attention to detail which is always evident in events promoted by these two organisations.

THREE HOURS TRACK RACE

Ten runners, including our own Colin Collis and Wally Wortley, lined up for this event which was to prove a succession of new English Native Records.

10 miles in 51m.48secs. by Jack Heywood with Colin then fifth in 58m.6secs. and Wally in 61m.4secs. was to lead to a new English Amateur Native Record for 15 miles in 1hr.17m.25secs. by Heywood who then retired. Colin, with his sights set on the full 3hr. run was progressing carefully to complete 15 miles in 1.26.44, 20 miles in 1.55.57, and 25 miles in 2.25.48, truly wonderful pace running.

Wally had now retired. Eric Smith had also departed, with a new English Amateur Native Record to his credit for 25 miles of 2.21.19, leaving Colin in second place 3 mins. behind Arthur Keilly and himself leading Beale by the same margin.

The testing time had come and Colin was putting everything that willowy frame of his possesses into his last 5 miles. For the first time he was to fall outside 6 minutes per mile but his work was done, for at 3 hours he had covered 30 miles 952 yards 2 feet, a truly magnificent performance having himself broken the then existing records for 25 miles, 30 miles and 3 hours. Arthur Keilly, the winner covered 31 miles 877 yards 11 ins.

10 MILES ROAD RACE

As the Manchester Guardian stated "Jack Brown of Belgrave Harriers........made a startling entry into the ranks of short distance road runners when he took second place to O'Gorman in the Walton A.C. 10 miles event".

Thus we had two individuals finishing second at Walton on the same day. Jack and O'Gorman left the field after 2 miles and a rare tussle between these two resulted in O'Gorman leading Jack home by only 8 seconds in a new record time of 50m.17secs. beating the previous best by 38 seconds.

Charlie Walker and Eric Gebbett ran extremely well. to finish 12th and 13th - Charlie is returning to something like his best form. However, we had to go back to 30th position for our fourth scoring man, Dave Baulch, and consequently we were 3rd team with 47 points behind St. Albans A.C. 39 points and Walton A.C. 45 points. Considering that a field of 130 faced the starter, our lads performed extremely well. A special word of praise to Jim Heathfield, a newcomer to this distance on the roads who covered the course in 55m.55secs.

Road Relay Running

Olympian

LONDON TO BRIGHTON RELAY (SOUTHERN CLUBS) OCTOBER 13TH.

The "Gods" again smiled on us and provided us with another fine day for the second of our bi-annual visits to that famed Regency resort,Brighton. The reporters in the Sunday newspaper organising these races,gave us no chance of winning (as always) which in itself automatically assured us of at least a place. The better-informed thought we might be second to T.V.H, but Herne Hill,now appearing near the front with alarming regularity, proved them wrong by a mere 16 secs.However,this is anticipating our story,so let us proceed.

Len Bishop,in place of our regular first runner,P.Bagnall, who was injured,rose well above the form he had shown of late, giving us a reasonably good start finishing 6th behind Poly,H.H.H Aylesford, Hampstead,and St.Albans in that order. Eric Gebbett,more used to 15 and 20 miles than a mere 5,soon proved that he has a turn of speed,quickly passing St.Albans and Hampstead.Poly unfortunately lost their way at tha start of this leg,and dropped to 10th.At Wandsworth H.H.H led from Aylesford and Woodford with Eric not far away.An ominous cloud,in the form of Ken Norris of T.V.H was looming up,and not dismayed by their 10th position on the 1st. leg,he ripped through the field and headed the race before the take-over,as if the hounds of hell were at his heels. He established a lead of 89secs. and chopped 54secs.off the stage record.Eric was undeterred however,and ran steadily throughout to hand over 3rd.behind T.V.H and Woodford,having made up 3 places. Charlie Dabbs,on the long leg from Mitcham to Purley,had the misfortune to start only a few seconds ahead of Ken Caulder of Surrey, Sando, and Towl of H.H.H. Charlie wisely ran his own race,arriving in Purley in 6th. place,just over 2 minutes behind the leaders T.V.H Came George Davidson and hopes that some of the leeway would be made up as George had run so well in the recent road race. Although by no means disgracing himself,he could make no impression and was forced to give way to Thorpe of St.Albans.The experience should be invaluable to George in the future. Jumbo Masters,who we were glad to see out in the team again, set about the opposition through Redhill,passing St.Albans,now exhausted of their supply of good runners,and Surrey,handing over well within striking distance of all but T.V.H.Jumbo had put up the fastest time on hig leg. Ray Tooby,another of our experienced runners who had missed a few Brightons,took over on the flat 5mls.run to Lowfield Heath with three runners in sight.He ran steadily,but his effort lacked spirit,and we made no further headway.We were now 2½ mins down. On the longest leg of all,Jack Brown ran a "blinder",making up 2 places and 30secs on T.V.H.His time was surpassed only by St. Albans' O'Gorman,at that time well behind us.Jack gave Heywood of H.H.H a fright and was unlucky not to catch him before Handcross. The traffic here was exceptionally thick and a slight contretemps with a large car might well have proved serious to our runners. Pete Newall took over on the second longest leg from Hickstead, and although making good progress down Handcross Hill,later regretted this fast start,as many have before him.He quickly caught Ron Clarke, but experience soon prevailed,and Ron steadily pulled away.A game but very tired Newall handed his baton to Geoff Goode at "The Castle" 3mins behind T.V.H and 1¼mins behind H.H.H but still in 3rd position. Geoff was another newcomer to the Brighton trail,and after a good start seemed to be suffering badly on what is thought to be a fairly easy leg,but which is in fact a " snorter " as the writer, in company with other distinguished Club members can well vouch for. However,Geoff pulled himself together and moved well over the last mile in sight of the South Downs. At the foot of Dale Hill,Charlie Walker waited,as usual, wasting much energy asking all and sundry," what do you think?" Would Charlie pull one out of the bag and put us back in the running for 2nd place? By now the winners were almost a foregone conclusion. It was certainly Walker's day when, 97 secs.behind H.H.H., represented by promising J.Davis, he climbed Dale Hill like a champion, and with the aid of continuous encouragement from supporting occupants of cars, the most prominent being the President, he produced the fastest time of the day and took us to within 64 secs. of the "Hill". T.V.H. unfortunately, also had a good performer on this leg and only 6 secs. was pared from their lead, which mattered little as they were now over ½ mile ahead.

Dave Baulch, whose running during the summer was a source of disappointment to many (including Mavis) set out to prove his fitness, and with a time of 17.40 on the last leg, a time beaten only by a few Belgravians, raised hopes of our finishing 2nd. Unfortunately the race could not be extended to Black Rock or we should certainly have pegged back those red and black hoops. As it was, a mere 100 yds. separated us from the 2nd team and Dave did the fastest of the day.

We can be well satisfied with our effort. Experience has been given to newcomers to this event, which will help us next April, and now that S.L.H. have lost the great Gordon and we can improve on our team by the inclusion of Davies and Hallowell, it can be hoped that last year's 5th position in the National Relay can be bettered by at least 4 places.

Result: 1st. Thames Valley Harriers 4h.34m.58s. 2nd. Herne Hill Harriers 4h.37m.33s. 3rd. Belgrave Harriers 4h.37m.49s.

Walking Notes: Chippenham to Calne 6 Miles.

Eric Hall.

Not quite the same star-studded field as in previous years tackled this tough course in lovely weather. We had eleven starters but were without Stan Vickers.

Wally Emery put a Belgrave vest in front early on, but unfortunately split his heel and had to retire. Belgrave men, it seemed, were everywhere, and if all had been eligible to score we would have won the team event; as it was we missed third place by one point and were only eight points behind Metropolitan Walking Club, the winners.

George Coleman added to his list of triumphs and missed the course record held by R. Hardy by only ten seconds. His opposition were disqualified early on and he won by 2m.39s. in 44m.19s. Next came Eric Hall followed by second claim member George Williams in 47m.38s. Ray Hall gained revenge on Ray Switzer, Norman Guilmant smiled his way through and was last seen selling old football jerseys to the timekeepers. Dennis Russell showed us that he will get his seven-mile badge very soon. It was pleasing to see Doug Woods again and we hope that this is the first of many appearances Doug. Ray Switzer got another crafty fourth handicap and Ray Hall crept in at the last with tenth place while Charlie Speechley was only just behind. This shows an improvement on last year so what about next year lads, lets have our share of the pork pies which go with the first team prize.

St. Dunstans One Hour Walks

J. Goswell.

Parliament Hill 22nd. Sept.

Quite a good muster of 26 members took part in the various sections. Highlight of "D" section was John Knifton's fine effort in finishing second with a distance of 6 mls. 795 yds. Ben, his father, competing on the same afternoon, must have been pleased with his 17 year-old son's performance. George Webb gained his 6 mile badge, whilst Bob Readman only just missed his 7 mile. Surely Bob has earned the name "Evergreen"- a grand effort. Ray Switzer, ably backed up by Ray Hall, Dave Stevens and Eric Froude gained his 7 mile badge in this section.

Peak of this meeting, is the "A" section, and we saw a grand performance by our lads. Stan Vickers led from the start, followed by George Coleman and at the mile they were 6 m.56s. and 7 m. 3s. respectively. Although Coleman got within 60 yds. of Stan, he could get no nearer and Stan went on to a meeting record of 8 mls. 120 yds. With fine efforts from Eric Hall, Ted Smith, George Checkley and Bob Newson, the latter practically killing himself to take Stan over the last couple of laps, we won the team race from Woodford Green. Well done all concerned - a fine show.

Report · page 14

Pre-Olympic Walk 10 Miles - Brighton 3rd.Nov.

J. Goswell.

Although this race was not taken too seriously,everybody was anxious to see how Stan Vickers had recovered from his leg trouble. In the previous weeks,rumours had flown thick and fast concerning him.After watching Stan and Eric Hall in this trial,we feel sure they will be a credit to their Country and Club,at Melbourne. Stan kept with Hardy and Coleman in the lead until 8-8½ mls but then dropped 5 yards or so.He recovered again and was with the leaders when they went off course after being misdirected,and lost at least ½ ml.After retracing their steps,they decided to walk back together to the stadium. K.Matthews,who had been some 500yards behind when the leaders went off course,was only a few yards behind at the stadium,and whilst lapping the track,decided to pass the three men,which really meant nothing;they were content to stroll in chatting together. Their time 77.16 for over 10½mls indicates the early speed of the race.Eric Hall had walked a very well controlled effort and gradually moved through the field to finish as the 1st Kilos man home in 79.47 for 10mls. We can be justly proud of Stan and Eric,who fully justified their selection.Good luck to them both!

J. Goswell.

Report · page 15

John Bromley wins big walk.

S.C.R.

We are indebted to a member of Thames Valley Harriers for the report of a Walking Race from Singapore to Jahore,a distance of just over 17 miles,which was won by none other than John Bromley former steeplechaser and Brighton Relay runner in the very good time of 2hrs.56mins. This race,which was promoted by the Singapore Free Press,was held on a Sunday morning,when no less than 650 competitors lined up for the start at 6am. According to press cuttings,John won this race last year,but has apparently kept his achievement up his sleeve as this appears to be the first intimation that he has turned to road walking.We do like to hear of the activities of our members who are overseas and hope that any others who are carrying on the good work will let us know about it. Of the 650 starters,430 finished the course,and John received once again the handsome trophy presented for annual competition by Messrs Bata,the shoe people,and the prize of a cinecamera presented by Twentieth Century Fox Films Ltd. Well done John. Bill True is making note of the result in case you continue walking on your return to this country.

S.C.R.

Notice · page 16

For the Attention of all Juniors and Youths

A. W. Penny. (Secretary) Junior Section.

This article is primarily for the benefit of the Youth and Junior Section of our club, of which you, the members, have nominated me as Secretary. There are a few points I would like to make which I feel some of you would like to know.

ONE. is that I am always ready to listen to any suggestion as regards the functions of our sections.

TWO. I am endeavouring to form one, or more training packs during the Winter months; at the moment, we are training from Wimbledon on Tuesday and Thursday nights at 7-0 pm. and Saturday afternoons.

I am grading the packs to enable runners of varying ability to enjoy these runs and also to develop each individual without his being run off his legs. This is most important as every Champion will tell you pack running is the foundation of Team running by which we can endeavour to win the Major Events during the Cross Country Season.

There are two results which apply to our Section:-

3 Mile Road Race at Wimbledon 29.9.56

K. Miller is a new member and shows every promise.

4 Lap Road Relay at Battersea Park 21.10.56.

This race was organised by M. Pyer. and was arranged to give our younger members a chance to show what they could do. Their ages ranged from 12½ to 15½ years. One of our members, E. Penny, recorded second fastest time over the course, which is approximately 1¾ miles. His time was 8 mins. 49secs. which is very good running, but a Mitcham lad did even better- his time being 8 mins. 42 secs.
