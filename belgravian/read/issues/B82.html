Editorial · page 3

The Track season, now completed, must surely be one of the most successful we have ever had. Our teams have won the "Reading Evening Gazette" Trophy and the "Waddilove Trophy," and in the "Kinnaird Trophy" Competition we made our best show to date. The "Doris" Shield and Inter-Club Relay Shield have fallen easily to us; and many of our members have been prominent in the important events of the Season.

Perhaps the performance which gave us most pleasure was that of our team (Bird, Botwright, Jarlett, Pack) in taking second place to Achilles A.C. in the A.A.A. 4 x 440 yards Relay Championship. This was an excellent performance and an improving team may bring us our first A.A.A. team title next year.

Gradually our track teams improve and in a year or two we may well be challenging for possession of the "Kinnaird Trophy." It is certain that in a complete athletic programme comprising all track and field events, Marathon and Walks with, say, two or three representatives in each event, we should be very prominent, and probably win such a competition if it were arranged.

* * * * *

Congratulations and very best wishes to our members who have taken unto themselves wives. They include : T. H. Scrimshaw, G. S. Doubleday, C. T. Carter, R. G. Hillier and J. Fletcher.

* * * * *

Members will remember that in these columns from time to time the question of a Coach to direct training has been raised; and we were forced to the conclusion that really capable coaches were very rare, and those that did exist were already fully committed. During the past months, however, we were fortunate in securing the services of a Continental coach, Herr F. Stampfl, whose vivacious enthusiasm and expert knowledge soon endeared him to our members. A fine all-round athlete himself, he is not content to tell others what to do, but gives personal demonstration and evidence of his own capabilities. Besides giving our track runners the outline of correct training, which should prove of great value to them in the future, immediate improvement was shown by some of our field-events men. Skelley and Melchoir, to mention two men, accomplished performances late in the season which two months earlier had seemed almost impossible.

If it can possibly be arranged, we hope to have Stampfl's services during the Winter on the Field-event side; and for Track and Field events when the Track season re-opens next Spring. It cannot be definitely stated at this juncture if we shall have his assistance, but we hope we shall be able to continue in the same splendid way that was initiated this season.

* * * * *

The "W. A. Rice Memorial Trophy" subscription list has now been closed, and in due course a Cup will be presented to the Club for the Junior 880 yards Championship. This trophy will be a perpetual reminder of one who served the Club well in many capacities, and whose splendid qualities made him one of the most popular men the Club has known.

Those who subscribed to this lasting symbol of appreciation of the friendship of one they were glad to have known were : J. B. Belchamber, L. G. Bennett, E. A. Duffett, F. H. Elson, E. Gordon, A. A. Harley, A. E. Harley, G. M. Murray, A. Patience, D. Patience, R. Patience, G. F. Pepper, G. H. Powell, C. W. Redgrave, W. Redgrave, T. H. Scrimshaw, H. W. Shields, A. E. Taylor, P. G. Wright.

* * * * *

Congratulations to F. E. Bentley upon his selection to represent Great Britain in the 50 kilos Walk at the European Games, and to C. F. Sutton upon being chosen as a reserve. Both Fred and Colin have well deserved these honours.

* * * * *

Congratulations also to Eddie Pack upon his selection to represent England at Dublin. Eddie has shown consistent form throughout the season, and he is unfortunate in having such a number of first-class quarter-milers contemporary with him. A few years ago Pack's times would have won A.A.A. titles. There can be little doubt that he was somewhat unfortunate in not receiving an invitation to go to the European Games, and when it was found that Bill Roberts could not accept, we should have thought that Pack would have been given preference over Horsfall (incidentally, an Australian). Pack's presence in Paris would also have strengthened the 4 x 400 metres relay team, which was beaten into second place by the German team. Pack in place of Barnes would, in our humble opinion, have represented a few yards improvement. However, we are not in the favoured position of the Selectors, and we can only hope that next season our man will secure his full share of honours.

* * * * *

The Club Annual General Meeting takes place at Belgrave Hall, Denmark Road, Wimbledon, on Tuesday, September 27th. It is hoped that all members will make an effort to attend, and take their part in the election of next year's Officers and Committee.

* * * * *

A few laggards have still to pay the current year's subscription, and our Hon. Treasurer would be glad to hear from them. Others, no doubt, will be anxious to be the first to pay their 1938/1939 subscription, which becomes due at the Annual General Meeting.

* * * * *

Congratulations to L. W. Herbert and J. C. Smart upon gaining their Club Honours Badges. Both succeeded in gaining A.A.A. Standard; Herbert in the Mile, and Smart in the 3 miles; and so qualified for the Club badge.

* * * * *

In a season that has given us so many successes, it is a matter for regret that we have been unable to regain our rightful place in the First Division of the Southern Amateur Athletic League. In the early races of the competition we scored heavily, but at the one mile team race only three of our runners materialised. and we were unable to start. This automatically robbed us of the leadership and our opportunity to regain First Division status. The presence of even one of our slower men would have enabled us to start in the Mile race, and some points would have been gained! An occasional setback of this nature is, we suppose, inevitable; but it is to be hoped that next year we shall not suffer in this way.

* * * * *

Now that the Track season is over, it is an opportune time to express our thanks to those officials who have made our meetings possible. Without running through the list of all who have helped, we must single out for special mention our old friend, Bill Fish, for his exceptional services to us all. A. G. Fell, always present as Starter, and A. C. Cook, our newly-found Announcer, have also helped considerably.

Report · page 6

With The Walkers.

Our walkers made a brave show in the A.A.A. Championships. Cooper won once again, with E. A. Staker (Highgate H.) 2nd. Harry Churcher was third, Percy Wright fourth, and Don Brown and Joe Coleman also finished inside first-class standard time. Len Coleman was just outside, and we had five men in the first eight places.

We had hoped that Don Brown would beat A. A. Cooper (as he had done twice earlier in the season, in the "Kinnaird" race and Inter-Counties Championships), but after winning the London Business Houses title ten days previously he was taken ill and confined to bed with a severe chill. The after-effects were obviously still upon Don in the A.A.A. Championship, and it was noticeable from the first ten yards that he was not himself.

So severe were the effects of the chill that Brown did not race again on the track after the Championships. However, he has now started again for the road season, and we trust he will have more luck in avoiding chills next season, and claim an A.A.A. Championship.

* * * * *

On July 6th we were invited by Surrey Walking Club to meet them in a 3 miles race at Tooting Bec Track. J. G. Coleman and N. A. L. Burt, of S.W.C., had a spirited tussle, but Joe,s speed and strength prevailed and he led us to a win. Scoring six aside, final points were Belgrave H. 34 pts., Surrey W.C. 44pts.

* * * * *

The Road season is with us again, and there is no reason why it should not equal in success our last record season. Our training and racing programme is on similar lines to previous years, and over a number of years we seem to have arrived at a programme which gets our men fit, and fit at the right time, which is of the greatest importance. We are sure that given the keenness and perseverance which are so essential, we shall continue to do well.

* * * * *

We shall again have out all those men who have brought the Club honour and distinction in recent years. It is most important, however, that we also have some new recruits with the necessary qualities; and we trust all our members will be on the look-out for youngsters likely to embrace the sport. We have some very promising younger members, but there is always room for more.

Remember that we hold a novice race early in the season!

* * * * *

Can we win the "Junior" two seasons in succession? An affirmative answer depends upon the keenness of the eligible men. Bridger, Heather, Thaxter, Roberts, Doubleday, Snell, and others, supporting Bert Clayton, our Eastleigh member, who may win the individual title, is certainly a team of possibilities. However, March is a long way off yet; and we shall have further opportunities of discussing this very intriguing question.

* * * * *

The Southern Committee of the Road Walking Association are holding their Annual Supper on Monday, November 21st, at the Empire Restaurant, Victoria. It is for men only and the charge is 4/-. We hope Belgrave will be well represented.

* * * * *

All members will shortly be in possession of their fixture lists, and any member requiring further information, or wishing to enter any of the races, etc., should write to the Walking Hon. Secretary.

The Annual Inter-Club Walking Contests.

Our annual fixture was held at Battersea Park Track on June 8th, and again we had a splendid evening's sport. As Woodford Green A.C. and Valentine's S.C. had their own meetings on, they were unable to send teams along; but we were glad to welcome Cambridge Harriers and Highgate Harriers for the first time for some years.

The first race was held at four miles (reduced from the usual five miles) and we were represented by R. F. Elson and C. E. Churcher. Harold Whitlock (Met. W.C.) won from Neilson, of the Poly. Our walkers finished third and sixth and gave us a lead on points. Our score was 25 points, while Highgate H. and Polytechnic H. scored 23 points each.

Report · page 9

The Brighton Walk.

This year's classic found us poorly represented, and it was evident that our team would do well to take third place; and that we had not one chance in a million of retaining our hold on the Shield. Circumstances combined to weaken our entry. Fred Bentley was, of course, in France representing Great Britain at the European Games. Jack Bidgood found the pressure of work in a new position prevented him from training. Jock Martin, a stalwart for many years in this race, was indisposed, as was also Fred Rickards. Readman and Roberts were also prevented from entering and we could only muster six entries against last year's eleven.

Our great hope was Colin Sutton. Unfortunately, a severe cold laid him low a week before the race, necessitating a day in bed, but Colin started in this race as though in perfect health. At 18 miles he held a good lead, but the effects of the cold began to show and he fell back. Pluckily he carried on, despite a very bad time lasting for some hours, and eventually finished eighth in 8 hr. 44 min. 52 sec.

Bill Urry, as last year, gave a splendid show of even walking and finished tenth in 8 hr. 56 min. 58 sec. He was the first "Service" athlete to finish and, as last year, won the special prize.

Our third man, in 18th position, was Harry Thaxter. He completed the course in 9 hr. 11 min. 7 sec., and so gained a first-class standard medal. Thaxter is young in walking experience and is to be congratulated upon an excellent performance.

Our team was completed by E. L. Reeve, a Club-champion at shorter distances some years back. Reeve, together with Don Willis and G. S. Doubleday, finished inside ten hours and so qualify for second-class team medals. Doubleday improved an hour and a half over the previous year's performance, which was incidentally his first walking race.

The race for first position was one of the most exciting and interesting seen on the road for many years. Henderson, after taking the lead from Sutton, went well ahead and it seemed impossible to catch him. He was pegged back, however, and the leadership was continually changing. Eventually Tom Richardson (Woodford Green A.C.) who had created new World's Records the previous week at various distances, including twelve hours, came through to win in 8 hr. 8 min. 1 sec. At Crawley he had been over 10 minutes behind the leader.

Leicester Walking Club were worthy winners of the Team contest, with the promoters, Surrey Walking Club, second, and ourselves third.

Of the 93 entrants, 89 started and 75 finished—an excellent record!

All our six entrants finished, and Colin Sutton won the Club Long Distance Championship and Belgrave Cup. Second and third medals go to Urry and Thaxter.

Next year we hope to give better support to the race and place a stronger team on the road. Our team on this occasion did well—no one can be said to have walked below form—but we have others who would strengthen our representation. Perhaps next year will see some of our younger walkers making their first efforts in this annual race—one of the finest of sporting events—and in the Club Championship held in conjunction.

Once again we thank in all sincerity those cyclist attendants who helped our men along—a feat of endurance, patience and self-sacrifice in itself. A Club bus was on the road and supporters, walkers and attendants travelled back together, tired but eager to be on the road again next year.

Well Walked!

W. H. SNELL.

[Hon. Editor's Note.—Bill Snell is the first member for some while to burst into verse in our columns, and whilst his metre sometimes falters, and occasionally the rhyme is suspect, we congratulate him upon the sincerity of his lay, and the trouble he has taken. We should like other members to polish up their iambic pentameters, their dactyls and spondees, and maybe we shall find a Belgrave Laureate].

Congratulations to all you Belgrave men, The Coleman Brothers, Joe and Len. In the "Junior" they dead-heated, A hundred others were defeated.

Sutton and Reg Elson completed the team, Smashed all records—they stand supreme. In all the "Sevens" and Valentine's "Ten," First scratch team were Belgrave men.

Harry Churcher is the the "Champ" of them all, Of the many champions from Belgrave Hall. Six consecutive "Opens" has this chap won, A marvellous performance, never previously done.

They went to Colchester to walk mile after mile, Our men smashing records in amazing style. Then up to Birmingham to contest the "Twenty," Our chaps showing stamina that they had in plenty.

We've often been second, or sometimes third, But this time it's ours—at last!—I heard. Filling positions two, four, eight, and nine, They've won the Shield—"Excellent! Fine!"

Bentley, Sutton, Bidgood and Rickards, At distance-walking, you know they are wizards. The "50 Kilos" at Bradford we won, Completing a "season" that takes the "bun."

And now all praise to A. A. Harley, And our Captain, good old Charlie. Hard work they've done behind the scenes, Selecting the teams—you know what that means!

They must feel proud of the work they've done, After seeing the Cups their teams have won. To all you youngsters this sounds very nice, But to get to the "top" take Harley's advice.

To all new walkers, we start in September, From Belgrave Hall (a place to remember!) You may now be slow, but plug away, Hard training will get YOU first one day.

Down to Roehampton, or round the "Hills," Keep with the "pack,' road sense it instils. In future years, you've lots to do, To uphold our prestige, and we're looking to YOU!

Report · page 12

Field Events.

The season just finished has been the most successful ever in the Club's history. For the fourth consecutive year we have won the Southern Amateur Athletic League competition, scoring 243 points. This represents eleven points out of a possible twelve from each event in which we have competed.

Charlie Melchior did well to win the Javelin event in the "Waddilove Trophy" Competition at Birmingham—our only win—and so contributed in no small way towards the winning of the Bowl. In match events, too, our field events men have scored more points than our opponents, so helping our match wins.

Club Championships Day saw records go in the High Jump, Shot Putt and Long Jump. Pack had an easy win in the High Jump clearing 5ft. 5in., and Skelley's 22ft. 8in. in the Long Jump compares favourably with performances in International matches. If he maintains this form next year he should gain his place in our national teams.

S. Jones had an excellent win in the Shot Putt with 39ft. 1in.—the best performance yet in Club events by "home" talent—only Grassau and Pfenninger having beaten it.

L. J. Worrad is the new Discus champion. He has recently joined us and has already proved himself a great asset. He was also second in the Weight (37ft. 0in.) and narrowly beaten for third place in the Long Jump, with 19ft. 10in.

H. M. Quackenbos is the new Javelin holder. Melchior and Powell were unable to throw, owing to the claims of work, but Quack's win is the reward of persistence. When he matures physically, he will throw 160ft.

At the Blackheath H. meeting at Motspur Park on August 20th, Melchior was third in a Javelin handicap. His scratch throw was 166ft., his best yet. We congratulate S. Jones upon winning the Shot Putt event in the Surrey and Middlesex match on August 27th.

S.A.A.L. RESULTS.

We were first on 11 occasions, second four times, third three times, fourth four times. Fourth was the lowest position ever occupied.

On July 6th we had a Field Events match v. Herne Hill H. at Tooting Bec Track. A most enjoyable match ended in a tie, each Club winning three events. We hope to arrange more fixtures of this nature next season.

Our Field Events men were prominent in the match v. London Associated Electrical Undertakings. Sutton won the Shot Putt, T. C. Jones the Long Jump, and Quackenbos the High Jump.

At Charlton Park v. Cambridge H. our men were first and third in the High Jump (Quackenbos and Marchant); first and fourth in the Discus (Sutton and Jackson); first and fourth in Javelin (Quackenbos and Jackson); and second and fourth in the Long Jump (Skelley and "Q").

Other good work was done against the Old Rutlishians at Merton; and v. Southgate H. at Battersea.

We would again emphasise the welcome awaiting new recruits to the Field Events side. In particular, we should be glad of some more High and Long jumpers. So if you know of anyone, please inform :—

Column · page 14

This And That.

A very pleasant evening was spent at Southfields on July 2nd, when we met Kensington Argyll A.C. We had an easy win and outstanding recollections are of Alf. Orton winning the 880 yards handicap, our Walking Hon. Secretary winning the Mile Walk Match, and of Albert Smith running a "slow-motion" half-mile in the Relay, which we won. Certainly, a very pleasant evening.

* * * * *

On July 18th we visited Merton and opposed Old Rutlishians A.C., who won the match. We received a fine welcome, enjoyed ourselves immensely and hope for a renewal of the very attractive fixture next season.

* * * * *

A club "B" team visited Norbury on July 7th, to meet London Associated Electrical Undertakings. We managed to win most things; and we understand our Track Captain will long remember being passed in the last few yards of the Mile!

* * * * *

Our Juniors, both in numbers and quality, have been very prominent this year, and we are confident that there is much first-class talent here. On July 6th, at Tooting Bec, they scored a very impressive win over the Juniors of Herne Hill H. and Mitcham A.C.

* * * * *

Our teams excelled themselves at the Ponders End Fête and Gala when we won two trophies. Our Relay team (Bird, Blunden, Pritchard, Pack) win in fine style; and our lads in the Three Miles race (Smart, Cohen, Carter) gave us another team win.

* * * * *

Stan Botwright has shown improved speed this season and his wins include the Civil Service 220 yards title, two B.W.S.A. titles, two Club titles and a medal in our second A.A.A. 4 x 440 yards Relay team. The absence of Les Cohen and Pat Parker robbed the Mile of some of its interest, but an exciting race between W. Lucas and L. W. Herbert held the attention of us all. Lucas led the way right from the start, Herbert being content with keeping just behind. Thus they went until the bell, having dropped the rest on the last lap. Herbert attacked down the back straight. Lucas held him for a while, but on entering the final straight Herbert put in a powerful finish which Lucas could not quite resist. This is not only Herbert’s first Club Championship win, but a new Club record of 4 min. 29.2 sec. The field were battling for third place, Charlie Smart proving the best of the bunch. Standards (4 min. 50 sec.) were gained by Dan Patience, Ted Stimpson, Alf Taylor, Tom Morrell, Tom Carter, Harry Shields and Syd Ring.

Then the 220 yards finalists lined up and the Juniors again gave us a keen race. In a very good time—23.8 sec.—R. B. Shepherd led R. S. Turner and J. A. W. Scott.

Stan Botwright again showed the others a clean pair of heels, winning in 22.7 sec. (new Club record) while A. S. Blunden, F. W. Pritchard and Tom Wiley—finishing in that order—gave us a close race. Eddie Pack, Alec Mewes, S. Lyons and Les Bennett gained Standards (24 sec.) in their heats.

In the absence of C. Melchior, G. Powell and E. H. Finneron, the Javelin resolved itself into a close contest between Quackenbos and S. Jones, the former winning with 133ft. 2in. and Jones throwing 131ft .1in. I’m sure that Quackenbos’ prowess is chiefly due to a close study of the finer points of Javelin throwing.

A rather small number lined up for the Two Miles Walk. Harry Churcher and Joe Coleman led from the start, with Percy Wright in close attendance until he incurred the Judge’s displeasure. Len Coleman and Bill Treacher were battling for third place until Treacher was disqualified at 1½ miles. Harry was a little too fast for Joe, and won in 14 min. 36 sec. Reg Elson followed home to gain a First Class Standard, but no other Standards were obtained. The holder, D. Brown, did not start, and I am sure that his presence in the race would have added considerable interest.

The last race was the 440 yards final. As was expected, Eddie Pack had a comfortable win, and cantered home in 51.6 sec. Sid Jarlett again gave us a fighting finish to beat Alby Bird and Dick Michael. Jarlett’s improved running has been one of the features of the season. A. H. Orton gained another Standard, having beaten 56 sec. in his heat.

Meanwhile F. Skelley had jumped 22ft. 8in. to retain his Long Jump title. This is surely one of the best jumps by an Englishman this season. T. D. Wiley and F. W. Pritchard were second and third.

No account would be complete without a hearty vote of thanks and a strong recommendation for a big drink and a box of throat tablets for our Hon. Announcer, Arthur Cook, who gave us a sterling performance on the megaphone. Our other officials, too, deserve warmest thanks from both competitors and spectators. Last, but not least, we thank the competitors whose performances will, no doubt, urge them on in quest of greater honours—sufficient unto the day is the attainment thereof.

Report · page 28

The Final Evening Meeting.

As usual, we held an evening meeting at Battersea Park in August and on the 10th we held the following handicap events.

This year we experimented with a 300 yards race, but entries were very poor, due mainly to holidays, and Alby Bird, off 10 yards, ran a good race to just beat Stan Blunden, off 6. Tom Wiley (8) was third while among the also rans was—yes, that man’s here again—Percy Wright.

In the Shot Putt “that man,” or in other words, P. G. Wright, was the winner with 38ft. 11in. He received 15ft. 6in. R. Stride, Snr. (16ft. 6in.) did 38ft. 4in., while Alby Bird (15ft. 6in.) gained third prize after dead-heating with the scratch man, S. Jones, both doing 38ft. 3in. As Jones did 39ft. 1in. in our Club Championships, a repetition of this would have gained him first place.

A good race was seen in the 880 yards Junior handicap and L. Marchant made good use of his 10 yards allowance to beat N. C. Shrubb (15) for first place. Our Junior champion, D. L. Mumford, was third off the scratch mark. These three juniors are three extremely promising young runners, and Shrubb was recently selected to represent the Surrey Juniors.

The 2 Miles Walk saw a rather small field and Charlie Speechley (450) scored a popular win, with A. H. East, off a much reduced mark, still keeping among the prize winners by being second off the 330 yards mark. Reg Elson walked in his usual fluent style to gain third place with 190 yards start. Harry Churcher walked well from the scratch mark and eventually worked his way through to 4th place.

A popular distance with our lads is the 2 Miles race and Ted Stimpson showed he is working back to his best form by winning off 100 yards; while Tommy Morrell (160) ran well to gain second place. Frank Webb (170) was third. To Frank it would appear that the distance is immaterial, be it anything from 300 yards to 10 miles.

Another feature of this race was the fine running of L. W. Herbert who, off 50 yards went through the field in fine style and only lost third place in the last few strides. He just failed to overtake Frank Webb, and as the time of the race was 9 min. 30 sec., Herbert must have been doing fast time as he was close up to the leaders at the finish.

Notice · page 29

The Forthcoming Winter Season.

After one of our most successful Summer seasons ever, we look forward with confidence to the forthcoming Winter season.

We have arranged a full fixture list and I trust our members will give the fixtures their fullest support, both at home and away. We are also extremely fortunate in possessing our own hall—Belgrave Hall—at Wimbledon and here again the Common is ideal for running purposes.

I hope all members, both new and old, will come along to Wimbledon and sample the delights of a run over the country, there is nothing better for getting rid of that “tired” feeling, never mind what the advertisements say!

We have a fine bath of hot water for use after the run, and our capable masseur, Tom Easton, is also in attendance, if required.

Saturday runs always start about 3 p.m. and for those who would like to do some mid-week training we have our quarters at the rear of the “Rising Sun,” Ebury Bridge Road, which is quite near Victoria Station. We go out from here every Tuesday and Thursday evening and run over the adjacent roads into Hyde Park, when we go round Rotten Row, which makes for very useful country training, especially if we have had some rain, and then back to the quarters. Hot water can also be obtained here and while conditions are not quite so ideal as at Wimbledon it must be borne in mind that suitable mid-week quarters, especially in this vicinity, are extremely difficult to obtain.

Now a word as to our fixtures. On the first Saturday in October (which is October 1st) we hold our annual 3 Miles Road Run and 5 Miles Road Walk. Both these events are handicap races and all members have an equal chance of success, so be sure to enter in good time.

The following week, October 8th, we hold the usual Novice races open to Unattached men, and also members of our own Club. If you have any friends who would like to try their paces, bring them along to Wimbledon on this day.

We are then away to Ealing Harriers, and then on the 22nd we have a home fixture with London University C.C.C. In conjunction with this race we shall hold our usual 5 Miles handicap. We expect to have a good field out.

This season we visit Cranford for the annual Inter-Club race with Thames Valley H. and Ashcombe-St. Georges A.C., the date being October 29th.

Then on November 5th we make our first visit to Aldershot for a run with the Aldershot Command team. We are also paying a visit to Cambridge for a run with the University Hare and Hounds and in each case a picked team will represent the Club.

Tom Easton
