EDITORIAL

The first post-war track season has been a busy one, and the results show that Belgrave can still be reckoned a major club. This is not surprising when we consider our wartime track performances, and with men coming back from the Forces we should be stronger still next year. Some of our returning men have not come up to their pre-war performances, but they all appreciate that their acclimatization to civilian life takes some time, especially those who have been overseas. A number of them were at their best right at the end of the season, and they are confident that with judicious winter training they will surpass their previous performances. In this respect the Wednesday evenings Physical Training and Lectures at Belgrave Hall should be helpful, and it is hoped that as many members, especially Juniors, will attend.

A full programme of Championships was held, but none of our members gained any wins for the Club in either the Southern or A.A.A. Senior Championships. Harry Churcher was unfortunate to come up against one of the Swedish cracks in the 2 and 7 Miles' Walking Titles, both of which he won in 1939. Harry was second in the 2 and third in the 7, and we are all very pleased to see him back in his old form. Coming to the Junior Championships, we find, once again, the name of Derrick Burfitt at the head in the 1 Mile, he won both National and Southern titles. Derrick will still be eligible for these titles next year.

The Club Championships and Handicaps gave us some keen racing and there were some good performances in Club Matches. There is still room for improvement in the support given to Club Handicaps. In days of old we had to have heats in a Club 880 yards Handicap to prevent overcrowding; there are plenty of gaps the Hon. Handicappers will be pleased to fill.

The thoughts of the Athletic World are turned towards the next Olympic Games, to be held in London in 1948. The thoughts of those athletes who are aspiring to being selected should be there already! The peak of condition required of an Olympic athlete can only be gained by careful planning and training. The selectors no doubt have their eyes on all possible contestants, and several of our crack men are being watched and their performances noted. The European Games, recently held in Oslo, give some idea of the standards to be aimed at.

At the Annual General Meeting a number of new officers were elected. One of the key positions is that of a responsible Senior to take charge of the Juniors. A great deal of thought has been given to the Junior Section. The choice of Juniors for official club posts is made difficult owing to National Service; a year ago we elected Alan Bishop as Junior Captain, and he was promptly called up! This years' Junior Captain is Eddie Short, and we hope that he will have the active support of the Juniors, and the moral support of the Seniors. The administration of the Juniors, i.e., notification of fixtures, travelling arrangements, etc., will be in the hands of the Sectional Hon. Secretaries, who will work in close touch with Frank Webb, who will be keeping a fatherly eye on them in general. We are very proud of our Junior Section, and, judging by their performances, they will be shaking the Seniors up before they are much older.

The Club Officers are an extremely hard-working lot indeed. Much of their work consists of correspondence. All members can help them by replying promptly to all correspondence requiring an answer, e.g., when you have been selected for a team and cannot turn up, the sectional secretary should be notified so that substitutes can be arranged.

The winter season is now in full swing and we are getting good musters at Belgrave Hall. Our runners will do their utmost to retain their many Championships, including the English and Southern titles. The walkers will strive to regain the Championships we have held in the past. Our respective captains, Bert Footer and Charlie Churcher, will have watchful eyes over their men and will be pleased to advise any member on training, etc. The year 1947 will be the Club's Diamond Jubilee, and it would be very fitting if we were to make a clean sweep of all the National Team Trophies; it would also make it a memorable year of office for our esteemed President, Harry Parker.

NOTES

Belgrave Hall is open for training every Tuesday and Thursday evening. Tooting Bec Track is open on Wednesday evenings from 7 o'clock. Tuesday training round our pre-war course over Rotten Row, Hyde Park, will be held from The Duke of York's Territorial H.Q. Track Dressing Room, King's Road, Sloane Square, S.W.3 (nearest station, Sloane Square) at 7 o'clock, with Bert Footer, pack leader. There will also be a training pack for walkers.

* * * *

The question of Club Badges, normally in the hands of the Hon. Treasurer, is now being handled by his assistant, Tommy Morrell. Vests are not yet obtainable but badges (cloth and metal for buttonhole) can be obtained from him.

* * * *

It is most important that the Club should get publicity in the local press. We already have a number of members doing very good work in this direction, but more volunteers are needed. The President or the Hon. Secretary will be pleased to hear from anybody who can help. Club Notes already appear in the South London Press, South Western Star and Wimbledon Borough News.

* * * *

The new monthly magazine, Athletics, is now well established and needs no introduction to most members. For those not acquainted with Athletics, it is published by John Langdon Ltd., 40 Maida Vale, W.9, and is edited by one of our members, P. W. (Jimmy) Green; the annual subscription for twelve issues is ten shillings, post free. The walkers are still catered for by Race Walking Record, which has now been going strong since 1942. The subscription is seven shillings for twelve monthly copies (H.M. Forces, five shillings), from A. D. McSweeney, 102 East End Road, Finchley, N.3.

* * * *

The Club cannot yet resume its full programme of Social events, as Belgrave Hall is not in a fit state to undergo the strain of a Social Evening. When the repairs have been done our Social Secretary, Dave Shepherd, will be gettings things going. Before the war Belgrave was famed for its Socials, and Dave is confident of restoring our Social Side to its former state. With the co-operation of all we can look forward to some jolly and riotous evenings.

* * * *

Harry Shields has been re-elected to the Committee of the Southern Counties' Cross-Country Association. Jack Chapple, our Mitcham friend, and our Poly member, Jimmy Wood, were also elected. Ernie Duffett was re-elected to the Committee of the South of the Thames Cross-Country Association, and is now classed as the "Senior" Committeeman—well in the running for a Vice-Presidency. Syd Roberts has been elected Chairman of the Southern Committee of the Road Walking Association, whilst Alf. Harley has been elected Hon. Treasurer of the Road Walking Association.

* * * *

Dr. Christopher Woodard, recently demobilised from the Navy, is now on the Staff of the Middlesex Hospital, Mortimer Street, W.1. This hospital is taking a particular interest in athletes and offers sunlight treatment, gymnastics, and physical treatment, with a view to building up British athletes. Any Belgrave Harrier who sustains any injury or who has any physical weakness or disability is invited to call at the hospital any evening between 6 and 7 p.m. and ask for Dr. Woodard. In a short talk given to members before the A.G.M., Christopher emphasised the need for prompt and correct treatment for physical injuries, and he hoped that any member needing treatment would come along. He has been a Belgrave Harrier for many years past and has now graciously accepted the post of Hon. Medical Adviser.

* * * *

Harry Hare's many friends will be glad to hear that he is on the way to recovery. Frank Thomson is better and was a spectator at the finish of the Brighton walk, and was also at the A.G.M. Bill Fish was also at the A.G.M. and was very pleased to be amongst the lads again. Charlie Cook is recuperating and Will Inge has been ill. George Clay has been troubled by an old malady—an after-effect of being gassed in the 1914-18 war.

* * * *

Once again we must express our gratitude to our starter, George Fell, who has been as sprightly as ever. We are indeed fortunate in having such a competent man with the gun, for the starters' job is an important and exacting one.

* * * *

Our President was very much amused to receive, when he was Hon. Secretary, a communication addressed to Mr. H. Sam Reve. The address was correct, but the name, of course, is Parker. Incidentally, Harry maintains a large fan mail with Club members, especially those serving or away from home. For those seeking his address, it is: 3 Fritham Close, New Malden, Surrey.

* * * *

Here is a reminder to all concerned that the Club Rule is that only First Claim members are eligible to compete in Club Championships, and three months' membership immediately prior to the race is essential if a member has previously belonged to another athletic club. Where there is a Club Handicap in conjunction, those not eligible as above can compete in the Handicap only.

* * * *

Bert Button, writing from Sydney, New South Wales, sends greetings to the Club and all those he knew years ago.

* * * *

Heartiest Congratulations to Colin Sutton and Jock Martin upon their performances in the 24 Hours' Walk, held at the White City on July 14th-15th. Both covered 100 miles within the 24 hours and so qualified to become members of the famous Centurions' Club. Their times were: Sutton, 20 hours, 40 minutes, 11 seconds; Martin, 22 hours, 16 minutes, 57 seconds, and both have been presented with a suitably inscribed Certificate from the Club. Both these walkers have been prominent members of our teams in long-distance walks and they well deserve their new honour. The winner of the event was our Poly member, Percy Reading, who set up three new records, viz., 19 hours: 105 miles, 466 yards, 110 miles: 19 hours, 57 minutes, 16 seconds, and 20 hours: 110 miles, 410 yards. Percy also beat the unofficial record for a non-stop walk, covering 129 miles, 749 yards. We offer Percy our heartiest congratulations for he is very proud of his membership of the Club. The race was held in appalling weather conditions, the track was rain-soaked and the competitors had to walk several yards wide, which for four hundred laps must be a fair distance. Our veteran walker, already a Centurion, Jimmy Chalk, covered nearly 80 miles before retiring, a remarkable achievement for a man well past 60. It is interesting to note that Jimmy Belchamber gained his Centurion's Badge over 20 years ago when he was second in the London-Brighton and back walk. Our Vice-President, Mr. Ernest H. Neville, is also a well-known figure in the Centurions.

* * * *

George Clay has been carrying on with the good work at Motspur Park Track. In response to one or two enquiries he has asked us to point out that the training schedules given in the last Belgravian were not his, in fact some of the items were much different to his teaching and practice.

* * * *

Another renewal of our pre-war activities was the Supporters' Coach for the London to Brighton Walk (details elsewhere). A number of members and friends made the trip and the encouragement they gave to our walkers was much appreciated. Thanks are due to the organiser of the coach, Syd Roberts. From the walkers comes further praise for the splendid services of Charlie Poulter at the baths after the race. The competitors when they arrived after their 52 miles' walk, the last 20 being walked in the pouring rain with a wind which, apart from its slowing effects due to its sheer force, made cold bodies and limbs even colder, were heartened to see the genial and grinning Charlie all ready to bath, rub and bandage them. He used five yards of adhesive tape on blisters alone! Charlie will be at Belgrave Hall, or travelling with our teams (runners and walkers) and his services are at the disposal of any member who wants a rub down or first-aid treatment.

* * * *

The R.W.A. has just announced a new National Championship at 10 Miles. This will be a Senior Championship, and it is suggested that it should take place four weeks after the "Junior" 10 Miles (i.e., March 29th, 1947). Belgrave will make a strong bid to become the first holders of the title, and our chances of an individual winner are very good indeed. With Harry and Charlie Churcher, Jack Rutland, Joe Coleman, Bob McMullen, and others, we should see a number of Belgrave vests at the front.

* * * *

The Hon. Secretary would also be glad to have programmes, fixture cards, cuttings, etc., etc., giving details of Club activities in the pre-1914 era, for inclusion in the Club records.

* * * *

Tom Carter was unable to do any serious racing this summer owing to the leg injury he sustained in a road relay. He has had an operation on his leg and has started training again and we hope that he will regain the form he showed when he led the field home in last season's Southern Cross-Country Championship. Owing to Tom's inactivity we had no representatives in the 2 Miles Steeplechase Races. This is an event at which cross-country runners shine; have we any potential steeplechasers amongst our new cross-country stars? Tom put some good steeplechase performances before the war, and no doubt would have been prominent this season.

* * * *

"Having joined the Belgrave Harriers in the year 1930, I never for one moment thought I should have the great honour to be made a Life Member at the last A.G.M., for which honour, gentlemen, I really sincerely thank you. I have always enjoyed walking races and training with the Belgrave lads and I will certainly do my best to bring the teams forward in the athletic world, and more so, to endeavour to have the Belgrave Colours in the next Olympic Games. I thank you once again for the honour bestowed upon me, and I wish Belgrave Harriers every success in the coming years."

HARRY G. CHURCHER.

WINTER COACHING SCHOOL

It was suggested to the Committee, and it has now been decided, to organise a Winter Coaching School at Belgrave Hall, which will be held every Wednesday evening from 7 p.m. to 8.30 p.m., commencing on November 6th.

The programme will consist of physical training, the study of the technique and coaching of field and track events.

The physical training will be under the expert tuition of Dicky Dumbrill, who was a member of the Army Physical Training Corps, assisted by Frank Simmons. The field events coaching will be under the supervision of Charlie Jones, assisted by Sid Jones, and Bill Sutton, and George Clay and others will give advice and instruction to sprinters and middle-distance runners.

In addition it is hoped to be able to arrange for eminent coaches and International athletes to give lectures. One of our members, Dr. C. R. Woodard, has also promised to give talks in connection with injuries usually sustained by athletes and their treatment.

The full programme is available and is exhibited on the notice boards at Tooting Bec Track and Belgrave Hall. It has also been circulated to Junior members for whom this coaching is mainly designed. It is hoped that as many as possible will come along to Belgrave Hall on Wednesday evenings so that when the Summer season comes round they will be in a position to start their training on the right lines.

Now is the time to look ahead to 1948 when the Olympic Games will be held in London and we hope that this coaching will be able to set some of our members on the right lines so that they may be able to improve their performances sufficiently to be considered as candidates for the British team for the Games.

Any further information can be obtained from the Field Events Hon. Sec., T. C. Jones, 6 Broomwood Road, S.W.11.

NEWS OF MEMBERS

Max Quackenbos is very busy in the U.S.A.. He is working hard in research with plastics, but has found time to resume running after a year's absence from the track. He got down to 4.33 for the mile and 10 minutes flat for 2 miles. With a small band of enthusiasts, he is starting a new club, the Newark (New Jersey) Track Club. He was thrilled to read of our "National" win, and marvels at the fitness of the "old hands."

Willie Nankeville has been training with a club in Hamburg, except when he has popped home to show us his heels. Amongst his many brilliant performances this season were winning his Corps championships at 800 and 1,500 metres in 2 minutes 0 seconds, and 4 minutes 9 seconds, respectively. Represented Britain at the International Meeting in Antwerp, and was third in the 800 metres in 1.58.8. Won the 1,500 metres in the Armed Forces Olympic Games, held in Berlin, in 4 minutes 1.2 second (equivalent roughly to a 4.20 mile). FIELD EVENTS REVIEW

We welcome the return of Sid Jones and Bill Sutton from the Forces and genial Jerry Thompson, after suffering from sugar diabetes, has been one of the features of the Section's activities during the past summer. Unfortunately, neither one of them was able to help us in the first of the post-war S.A.A. League contests, but they all made their presence felt as soon as they did get back into competition.

As far as can be ascertained (without the official table of results to hand) we finished second in the S.A.A. League. We were rather unfortunate in not being able to turn out our best available men in all events, which made all the difference between first and second places in the League. All who turned out are thanked for their support and next year we hope to be able to go one better and show the other clubs in the South that we are the best-equipped Club in this Section of the sport in team competition.

The Junior Section of the S.A.A. League was run for the first time this year and Field Events were included therein in which our Juniors showed up very well.

Charlie Melchior again, and Alan Dunn, one of our most promising younger members, were our mainstays in the Javelin, gaining maximum points in a number of matches. They were ably backed up by veteran George Powell, and our Field Events Captain.

Until the entry into the competition of Sid Jones, Bill Sutton and Jerry Thompson, Charlie Melchior was our first string in the Shot. Jerry Thompson, after a long lay-off, did not show quite such good form as previously, but managed one or two good performances. WITH THE RUNNERS

On looking back over the past Track season, I think we have every reason to be well satisfied with the progress of the Club and its members during the summer months.

We won most of the Inter-Club matches, although the winning or losing is not so important, in my view, as the important point of giving our members an opportunity of testing themselves against other clubs and of encouraging the team spirit, without which any club cannot expect to make headway.

Our Juniors were a good example in this direction, and we have got together a very fine lot of youngsters who should stand the Club in good stead in the years that lie ahead.

Derrick Burfitt, was, of course, outstanding in his performances, and we look forward to a continuation of his successes, both on track and country.

Len Herbert was our most outstanding distance runner, and it was a great pity that Tom Carter, owing to a leg injury, was unable to give of his best. Among the middle-distance men, Len Marchant and Willie Nankeville were very much to the fore, and with John Brown and Vic Blowfield showing great promise, we can look forward with confidence to next season.

Among the sprinters, Teddy Read improved by leaps and bounds as the season progressed, and was given good support by Dicky Dumbrill and Jack Frost. Stan Botwright, back in civvy street, took things fairly quietly and near the end of the season Michael Broadbent made a re-appearance on the track.

Alan Sexton and Dick Negus give promise of better performances to come, while Arthur Bruce was outstanding over the middle-distance races in our Junior matches.

Space does not permit of my mentioning all the many very promising members, but Junior and Senior, that we have in our ranks, but I am confident that next season will see our members more outstanding than ever.

So to the cross-country season, and if the gathering at Belgrave Hall for our opening race can be taken as a guide, I think we are in for a bumper season. We cannot win more races than last season, and it must be borne in mind that for several of the events complete new teams will have to be found, as the winning team each year is barred from competing again. However, with a turn-out of 55 members for the 3 miles road race handicap, I think these can be found, and it is especially pleasing to see so many old faces once again appearing at Belgrave Hall.

We are holding the usual Club Championship and Handicap events, also a 3 Miles' Novices Race on November 2nd, and in addition to the various Inter-Club matches, we have arranged several matches for the Juniors, under 19 years of age.

Bert Footer is our cross-country captain, and as a runner who has thrice represented England in the International Cross-Country Championship, I am sure our members, both new and old, will reap much benefit from his learned advice and experience.

With Alf Taylor and Frank Webb as vice-captains, we again have two men of much experience, and the latter is giving a special eye to our Junior Section.

Eddie Short is our new Junior captain, and in his first season over the country, Eddie put up a magnificent show in the England Youths' race, and is certain to give the Junior Section a good lead in this direction.

Coming to our fixture list, we have again arranged runs with very old friends such as Thames Valley Harriers, Metropolitan Police, Finchley Harriers, Surrey A.C. and Mitcham A.C. Towards the end of the season we have arranged runs with the Cambridge Harriers and London University Tyrian Club. This will give those members, not in our championship teams, an incentive to keep training, and not slack off as is likely towards the end of rhe season.

We again have the usual Road Relay races, and we also understand that Ilford A.C. are hoping to stage their annual race, so our road runners look to be in for a busy time.

For the championship events, the Surrey County are going to Earlswood, and the South of Thames is being held in Windsor Great Park. The Southern Counties will be on Ascot Racecourse, while the English Championships will be held at Apsley, Herts. For the benefit of those who think they stand a chance, the International race is being held in Paris, so they had better "brush-up" their French!

We are again holding our Annual Track Meeting at Tooting Common on Boxing Day morning, and I trust that members who intend competing will let us have their names in good time for handicapping purposes.

Thus it will be seen that there is plenty of work ahead for all members and it behoves everyone to give the Club captain their best support by turning up on Saturday afternoons whether at home or away, and also by coming along and training in mid-week, so that he can sort out the various "packs" that will assist us in retaining our proud title of the premier English cross-country club.

All enquiries and enquiries to E. A. Duffett, Running Hon. Secretary, 57 Harcourt Road, Thornton Heath.

"WADDILOVE" MEETING

Held at the Alexander Sports Ground, Perry Barr, this Inter-Club meeting promoted by our old friends the Birchfield Harriers, was watched by a large crowd. We had sent up a team for the Senior contest, and as past holders of the trophy were keen on putting up a good show. This year they had a Junior contest in conjunction and it was also decided to support this. However, our team was sadly weakened by the absence of Marchant and Frost, who were on R.A.F. duties, and the terrific queue at Paddington looked like ending our journey before we had started. Eventually six Senior and seven Juniors arrived at the ground, and Ted Read did well to gain second place in his heat of the 100 Yards. Syd Jarlett also ran second in his 440 heat, and then Charlie Jones gained third place in the Long Jump, which he followed up with second place in the Javelin.

Our three remaining Seniors were in the 2 Miles Team race and Len Herbert ran second to Sydney Wooderson in the individual race and with Bill Lucas fourth and Dick Tobin eighteenth, we finished third in the Team race, beaten by a margin of 4 points for first place.

MARATHON NOTES

Our long-distance runners have had a busy season and some good performance have been recorded. The Finchley 20 Miles Open, held on a four-lap course at Ruislip, on April 27th, attracted a big field, and it was encouraging to see a number of Belgrave supporters. Dan Patience ran well to finish eighth, White and Doubleday, short of training, faded out over the last two miles and were both outside standard time (2.15). Will Spencer, one of our Police runners, made his first attempt at this type of race, but retired at 15 miles. The revived Surrey County 20 Miles Road Race was held at Mitcham, on May 18th, on what was roughly a two-lap figure-eight course. The race was started by His Worship the Mayor of Mitcham. For the first 10 miles the field kept together, then came a short sharp shower and the field split up. The winner was F. Harmer, of Herne Hill Harriers, who led one of his clubmates home in 2 hours 4 minutes 45 seconds. Syd Ring was third in 2.5.54, Bert Mitchell fifth in 2.8.10, Fred White seventh in 2.8.19, Gordon Doubleday eighth in 2.10.4, and Will Spencer eleventh in 2.17.21. Our finishers all gained County Standard Medals (within 20 minutes of the winner). Spencer deserves congratulations for his performance, and no doubt with more long-distance training he will show faster times; he has an easy flowing style which should suit him well for this type of race. In the team event, three to score, Herne Hill beat us 11 points to 14, with Mitcham A.C. third.

In the Kent County Open 20 Miles, held at Chislehurst, on May 25th, Dan Patience ran his best 20 miles to date to finish third in 2 hours 0 minutes 13 seconds. Fred White and Bert Mitchell both finished inside Standard (2 hours 15 minutes), and the team (three to score) was third.

The Poly Marathon was held on June 16th, from Windsor to Chiswick. Before the race the runners were inspected by H.M. the King, who acted as Starter to the 99 competitors. Fred White ran his best and finished eighteenth in 2 hours 58 minutes 50 seconds, Bert Mitchell twenty-seventh in 3 hours 7 minutes 0 seconds, Gordon Doubleday fifty-fourth in 3 hours 30 minutes 25 seconds, and Jim Watson sixty-fourth in 3 hours 41 minutes 37 seconds. Dan Patience and Syd Ring retired when well placed owing to sickness.

The A.A.A. Marathon was held on the pre-war out and home course from the White City Stadium. Bert Mitchell was thirteenth in 2 hours 59 minutes 45 seconds, and Doubleday fourteenth in 3 hours 4 minutes 2 seconds, both gaining A.A.A. Standards (3 hours 5 minutes). Fred White had an "off-day" and retired. If he could have produced his previous form he would no doubt have gained a Standard too. As we go to press it has been announced that next year's Marathon Championship will be held apart from the Track Championships. If this means some refreshments and pointsmen along the route it will be a welcome step, as these were entirely lacking in this years' race, except for a few volunteer pointsmen.

An Invitation "Marathon" of 15 miles was held at Salisbury on August Monday. The course included a mile-long climb over the Wiltshire Downs. Doubleday was ninth in 1 hour 34 minutes 56 seconds, and White eleventh in 1 hour 36 minutes 0 seconds, the winner being our old Tipton friend, Jack Holden, who covered the course in the very fast time of 1.22.05.

THE ANNUAL GENERAL MEETING

This was held at Belgrave Hall on Saturday, October 5th, with R. J. Savage in the Chair. The President opened the meeting by welcoming both those who had returned to the Club from the Services and our new members. He then paid tribute to the progress of the Club, especially the Juniors. An apology for absence was read from H. E. Thaxter.

The Hon. Secretary then read his report. Mention was made of the great victories of our cross-country teams, of the gaining of International vests by Len Herbert (England) and David Main (Ireland) and of the great leadership of our Captain, Tom Carter, the Southern Champion. Of the walkers Jack Rutland was outstanding with seven individual wins in Open Road Walks. The Track and Field Events Sections had also performed well. The Juniors had maintained very good progress, with Derrick Burfitt again pre-eminent. For his win in the A.A.A. Junior 1 Mile Title—and many other excellent performances, the Club had awarded Burfitt an Honours Badge. The recruitment of new members was very satisfactory, 107 having joined us during the year.

The Hon. Treasurer then presented his Accounts and Balance Sheet. The return to our normal programme had brought us in extra expenses, and he was pleased to be able to show us a balance on the right side. He expressed gratitude for the work done by Les Cohen in connection with the Building Fund. Before the Balance Sheet was put to the meeting and adopted, the Hon. Auditors, J. B. Belchambers and T. Cotton, paid tribute to the Hon. Treasurer's sound and efficient accounting.

The next business was that of honouring two of our members with Honorary Life Membership. The Committee's nominations were Harry Churcher and Charlie Jones. In proposing the former, Syd Roberts recounted Harry's joining the Club in 1930 and of his rapid progress, culminating in his two seasons of invincibility on the road over the shorter distances, and of his winning the A.A.A. 2 and 7 Miles' Titles in 1939, having been placed to foreign winners in previous years. WITH THE WALKERS

The summer season provided a full programme for our walkers, both on the track and in the long distances on the road. All events have been well supported with creditable results to the Club. In the two National Championships on the road we had to be content with a minor placing—in the 20 Miles tieing for second place with Met. W.C., and in the 50 Kilos, third to Leicester, who scored a splendid double. Owing to "V" celebrations, our Police walkers were unable to assist in the 50 Kilos, resulting in the team being weakened. Another handicap has been the foot trouble of our Road Captain, Charlie Churcher, which eventually resulted in him giving up walking for a period whilst undergoing treatment.

On the track Harry Churcher has made rapid progress during the summer in regaining some of his pre-war form and although he lost both his A.A.A. titles at 2 and 7 Miles, put up the best time of anyone in the country this season when finishing second in the 2 Miles. In the 7 Miles the following morning, Harry was seriously handicapped by having to work almost until the start of the race, but even so, finished third.

Our Inter-Club match of 1, 2, 3 and 4 Miles, a popular feature of our pre-war programme, did not receive the support we hoped for, but a very keen struggle developed betwen us and Highgate Harriers, which resulted in a win for the Club by 3 points. Since the date of this match, however, many clubs have been considerably strengthened by the return of further men from the Forces, and we look forward to a good entry next year.

The 24 Hours' race, promoted by Met. W.C., resulted in Colin Sutton and Bob Martin qualifying for membership of the Centurions' Club, and both are to be congratulated on their excellent performances under most adverse conditions.

The Club Championship on the track proved a great success, five of our walkers gaining 1st class standard, with Harry Churcher wresting the Championship from Joe Coleman.

In the Inter-Club match at Motspur Park on 31/8/46 against strong opposition and without the assistance of our track champion and Bob McMullen, both on holiday, we scored another success, Joe Coleman and Jack Rutland finishing second and third respectively, backed up by Bob Readman in seventh position.

Feature · page 38

NUMBER 100

A. A. H.

We are all rather inclined to accept an issue of the Belgravian when it arrives as something that just "happened," and seldom do we think of the hours of preparation that go into each issue, or of the care and attention which somebody must devote to every sentence on every page before it passes into print. This issue of The Belgravian is No. 100, and an appropriate moment for wishing ourselves an ever-increasing measure of success for record in the next hundred issues, and to glance back quickly over the years which have given us our first hundred Belgravians.

A Club Gazette of sorts existed in the earlier days of the Club and copies of a duplicated 1908 issue have been preserved; but our first printed Belgravian came off the presses in April, 1925. Harry Hare was the first Hon. Editor, and found time in addition to his other multifarious Club duties to give our Gazette an excellent start in life. After producing nine issues, in October, 1927, Harry handed over the Editorial blue pencil to Ben Knifton, who gave us thirteen issues, now in resplendent claret and gold colours. In April, 1929, Alf Harley took over and remained in the Editorial chair for nearly ten years, until becoming General Hon. Secretary in September, 1938. During those momentous years in the Club's history, 59 issues made their appearance. Our present Hon. Editor, Gordon Doubleday, followed on, but the coming of the war and an enforced move to Exeter rendered his task difficult, and its performance all the more praiseworthy. The cares of Editorship furrowed other foreheads for single issues during the difficult days of war, but in the main "Doub" was in charge of this important feature of our Club life, which became trebly important with our membership dispersed through the world on war service. He has now brought us up to No. 100, which you have before you.

"Doub" has announced his resignation from the Hon. Editor's position to take effect immediately, and our very sincere thanks go to him for all his considerable work on our behalf, often under extremely adverse conditions. We hope he will be a frequent contributor to the Gazette he has graced as Hon. Editor during past years.

And now a quick glance back to our early editions, just to remind you of the Club's progress during 21 years. The walkers had already known the thrill of winning National Championships, but in 1925 the runners were recording their first real successes, in winning the South of the Thames Junior Race and the Surrey County Cross-Country Championship. In 1926 we essayed our first London-Brighton Relay and finished fourteenth of 19 competing clubs. Our scribe wrote: "The prospect of competing next year is not hopeless and with some little enthusiasm among a few more members we shall find that we can show vast improvement on this year's performance, satisfactory as it may seem as a first attempt." Later we were to win the event three years in succession against the finest competition in the British Isles—and the history of this one event is akin to our Club improvement in all spheres.

We have gone a long way since those early successes were recorded, and we all know, those who have assisted in any degree in our subsequent climb to fame. There are still further rungs to ascend, and may the Belgravian continue to record our successes (and our disappointments, lest we tend to lose any sense of proportion), our Club life, and all that goes to make Belgrave mean so much to everyone of us! And so, on from our first hundred Belgravians, towards our second century—with the goodwill and help of every loyal Club member!

A. A. H.

Letter · page 39

GORDON S. DOUBLEDAY.

You will have read in the above article that I am resigning from the post of Hon. Editor of the Belgravian. Producing this gazette has been a pleasure—a feeling which can be ascribed to anything connected with this Great Club. My work has brought me in close association with both officials and athletes, both in peace and in the trying days of war. We are, in a way, a motley crowd, we come from different walks of life, have different jobs, different outlooks and points of view, but, when we get together those are forgotten and we unite in the cause of the success and good-fellowship we have learnt to associate with the Club. During the war there were many incidents of kindness and sympathy between members whose only bond was just that they were fellow members of the Belgrave Harriers. The Committee, in the early days of the war, decided to continue publication of the gazette as a means of keeping members in touch with the Club. It has been pleasing and gratifying to read the letters from the lads who went overseas. Now I find that I have not the time at my disposal that I used to, and am taking the obvious step. I am extremely grateful to those who have helped me from time to time. I hope that I can be of some assistance to future Hon. Editors—I am confident that the lads will provide excellent material to write about. Keep it up! Belgrave.

GORDON S. DOUBLEDAY.

Report · page 39

At the Blackheath Harriers Open Meeting our team was third in the Invitation 2 Miles, in which Sydney Wooderson attempted to lower the British Record. The scoring was very close, Blackheath 18 points, Finchley 19 points, and Belgrave 20 points. Len Herbert ran very well to finish second to Wooderson. Charlie Smart and Ted Stimpson completed the team. In the 4 x 100 Yards Relay our four ran second to Achilles, our runners were Teddy Read, Dicky Dumbrill, Jack Frost and Michael Broadbent.

Another Relay success was at Guildford, Lulham, Hawkins, Osmint and Read each pulled away from their rivals. At this meeting Read won the 220 Yards and Osmint the 100 Yards (under 18) off 3½ yards, in 10.15 seconds against a strong wind.
