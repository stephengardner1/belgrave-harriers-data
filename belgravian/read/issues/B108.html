Editorial

YOUR EDITORS.

Dear Fellow Belgravians, It is said—" that he who plants an avenue of trees, cannot, in the nature of things, hope to enjoy them in their grandeur "—but it was a very happy occasion and a most gratifying result to the labours of all Belgravians responsible for it, when our popular Hon. Secretary told the Special General Meeting held at Belgrave Hall on Thursday, March 16th, that Belgrave Hall was now our own freehold property. What many of us years ago dreamed of, even dared to think possible, has now been achieved.

To Harry Hare, the originator of the idea, and others too numerous to mention, who helped to make it possible—we say—fervently—thank you, and we know they feel fully repaid for their efforts. They now hope that the wheels of officialdom will not turn too slowly and that in the near future we shall obtain a permit for rebuilding a Hall that will be a fitting and lasting monument to the greatness of Belgrave. Then, and not till then, will they, the men who achieved so much, so unselfishly, sit back and observe complacently—the dream that really came true.

And now for a subject that is causing some people a little concern—our recent defeats over the Country. Many are the reasons advanced, some unjust, some pessimistic, advice is freely given, some good, much bad. Let us not worry unduly. Let us take our defeats as gentlemen of sport, let us be ready to congratulate our victors and above all appreciate that competition is now much keener owing, no doubt, to the desire of our friends our opponents to vanquish a club with a reputation that we enjoy. That, we believe, is perhaps the biggest reason—the desire of David to vanquish Goliath—and now that we have been vanquished, shall we say temporarily, let us now turn about and endeavour with all the enthusiasm we can muster, to regain once again our former premiership in our Sport. We are passing through a period of gradual change over from a number of old and tried runners to a newer school of robust young runners, but without perhaps the experience of the old school. This experience will undoubtedly come, but in the meantime we must expect one or two reverses. No, we are not dead yet. Let us say we are a little unwell. Let us not try to find the cause, but certainly let us find a cure. And the first part of a cure is a tonic. Again, let us look upon our recent defeats, as in the nature of a tonic—the cure will be completed by 100 per cent. Club Spirit—you'll see.

Finally, if you turn to page 20 you will find an article dealing with this matter by a well remembered columnist namely " Observer." Some years before the war there was a considerable amount of conjecture as to who was the person writing under this nome-de-plume, and although sometimes his writings were outspoken and critical, they were, nevertheless, always eagerly read. We, the Editors, welcome the return of this mysterious writer, and having been allowed to share the secret of his identity, we pledge ourselves to keep it.

YOUR EDITORS.

(As a footnote to the above Editorial—a personal message from the " Frank " side of the Editorial Staff to Harry Hare. " Please do come along and say hallo to your old pals and have a look at—' YOUR HALL '.")

Harry Hare

In Memoriam

It is with profound regret that we have to record the sudden death of R. (Jock) Martin who was killed on Thursday, March 23rd, 1950, whilst on Police duty on his motor cycle.

Jock joined the Club in 1933 and was one of our most popular walking members and a very efficient official. He was also a Centurion, qualifying for this honour at the White City Track in 1946. He had put up some wonderful performances over the long distances, among them a 6th position in the London-Brighton Walk in both 1933 and 1934 and held the Club Long Distance Championship in 1936.

He was also a popular member of the M.P.A.A. Walking Section and was responsible for the introduction to our club of a number of our other Police walkers.

He will be mourned by all who knew him and the Club extends its deepest sympathy to his wife and daughter in their sad bereavement.

A floral tribute was sent from the Club and Club representatives attended the funeral.

R. Martin

Coaching

T. C. JONES, Chief Coach.

In the last issue of the Belgravian I mentioned that the Club had been able to arrange to have the exclusive use of the track at the Territorial Army Headquarters, Duke of York's Barracks, Chelsea. It has been decided by the Committee that this track shall be used solely for coaching purposes. This will enable really specialised coaching to be given without the disadvantages which appertain on ordinary training nights at other tracks.

The coaches have studied the possibilities which this will offer and have planned these special sessions at which all the Club coaches will be in attendance. These sessions will be held on Wednesday evenings from 7 o'clock till 9, commencing on April 12th.

In view of the fact that the authorities have placed a restriction on the numbers who can use the track on these evenings, a programme has been prepared covering all events over the summer period. This programme showing dates and events is enclosed with this copy of the Belgravian.

Owing to the restriction on numbers, only those men whom the coaches consider will most benefit from special coaching can attend and the coaches or section captains will inform their men before the date of the first session. Also in view of this restriction no training can take place unless under the guidance of the coaches and in the events which are being covered on the particular night. It is regretted this must be enforced but we are restricted and in addition, of course, the coaches desire to be able to concentrate on the programme mapped out.

This programme gives everybody ample opportunity and it will be seen that each event is given about eight evenings. The coaches have worked extermely hard on this programme and the schedules of training for each session, and it is hoped full advantage will be taken of it.

The use of the track has only been obtained at considerable cost to the Club and if in the opinion of the coaches any member is not taking full advantage of the facilities offered he will be excluded from the sessions, and his place filled by a more enthusiastic member.

Each member attending will be handed a card upon which to record his performances throughout the year and this card will also serve as an admission ticket to the track. Upon the information recorded on these cards, it will be possible to trace improvement in performances over the season.

Any enquiries for admission to the coaching sessions and details thereof can be obtained from the Hon. Secretary of the Coaching Committee, F. Simmons, 69, Monckton Street, S.E.11.

We have four coaches who are now qualified A.A.A. Coaches. George Clay has passed as a Senior Coach and Frank Simmons, Mick Pyer and Ben Lulham have obtained their Honorary Coaches badge. The other coaches will be taking examinations during the coming months so that eventually we hope the abilities of all Coaches will have been recognised by the A.A.A.

All coaches will now be concentrating on the summer programme and will generally also be available on Tuesday and Thursday evenings and Sunday mornings. Our main training track is at Tooting but coaches will also be available at Motspur Park and Alperton.

The full list of coaches with the events they cover and the tracks they attend are set out below. To enable everyone and particularly new members to find the coach for their particular event, each coach can be identified by the coloured shoulders on his track suit—these colours are also set out below.

At both Motspur Park and Alperton, club champions who train at either track will be only too glad to give any assistance to you in events not covered by the coaches.

To the Juniors

FRANK SIMMONS.

Dear Juniors, The track season is fast approaching and I hope many of you will be regular for training and coaching, for it is only by regular attendance that you can improve on your last season's efforts. We had a good season last year, but I am hoping for an even better one, it can be you know, if you really get down to it. There have been matches arranged for you which I hope you will endeavour to attend, but I will tell you all now, that you will be picked on your merits and that you will have to earn your place in the teams. I looked upon it as an honour to be asked to run, jump or throw for the Club, and I hope you will look on it in that way too. I shall be at Tooting on Tuesday and Thursday evenings from 6 p.m. until I am more or less turned off the track, and I shall be very happy to help one and all to attain a good standard in any events they train for. The Club has been presented with a magnificent cup which is being given for the Junior quarter-mile championship so I hope you lads who fancy yourselves as quarter-milers will prove that it was not given in vain and that Belgrave can still turn out good lads at that distance.

The field event lads cannot find a better coach than Charlie Jones. He will always be there to give you the benefit of his long experience, and teach you the correct technique, with which, plus a little determination, you should give a good account of yourselves.

Starting on April 12th at Duke of York's Headquarters, the SPECIAL COACHING SCHOOL begins. The time will be from 7 p.m. until 9 p.m. every Wednesday.

Train well, work hard and UP THE BELS!

Good luck, FRANK SIMMONS.

Our Own Headquarters

A.A.H.

By the time the Gazette is in your hands, the purchase of the freehold of Belgrave Hall will have been completed, and an ideal of many years' standing will have been realised.

In the 1920's, with the late Bob Ricketts as President, and many others (happily still with us) striving hard, a small reserve of £200 to £300 was built up and this was always looked upon as the beginning of something which might eventually lead to our own headquarters. The possibility seemed so far away however, that no one ever indulged in speculation.

Later, Harry Hare, who succeeded Bob Ricketts as President after occupying most official positions in the club, initiated a scheme to save halfpennies—many members can proudly show their contribution cards to this day—and our funds began to grow. Frank Elson helped with a football scheme, Tom Cotton, Teddy Gordon, Wally Bayliss and others contributed their ideas, a Smoking Concert was held at the old mid-week quarters and still our Building Fund, as it was now called, grew. Collecting boxes known as " bricks " were issued to members who undertook to turn out their spare coppers and by all sorts of means our balance increased.

The purchase of the leasehold of St. John's Hall (as it was then known) in 1935 gave a fillip to our ambitions, and now Frank Elson and Tom Cotton, assisted by countless other members, really got going and our available money in reserve reached the thousand's column. Les Cohen now gave a hand and a spell of as fine a work as any amateur club has ever known, was begun. Fortunately for us it still continues and our gratitude to Les knows no bounds.

These humble beginnings and the application of Belgrave team work at its best over many years has now had its reward and we now own our own headquarters—free from debt, debentures, mortgages and the rest of it. Our grateful thanks go to all who have helped and we look for your continued support in the future.

We are entitled to feel the flush of satisfaction on the attainment of our ambition.

Just after the first world war, a member advised the then Hon. Sec. Oscar Horwood, that he was resigning to join another club " who held Club championships and entered open relay events." Oscar said the day would come when Belgrave would hold Club track championships, enter teams in open competition and generally be a power in the land, but Oscar's confidence and optimism were contemptuously received. The first two track championships were instituted in 1922 and have been added to almost annually until we have a range of which any club could be proud. We have produced teams to win National Championships and athletes to earn distinction and respect over a wide range of events. We have built up a reputation for sportsmanship in victory and defeat. At the moment, a Belgrave Harrier, Harry Parker, holds the highest official position in the Cross Country world and another former Club President, Frank Thompson, has been President of the Road Walking Association.

Now we have acquired our own freehold headquarters and have money in hand to proceed with development when this is permitted. Who would deny our older members that flush of satisfaction they now feel?

Our headquarters have the advantage of being easily accessible and building developments are unlikely to drive us further and further out of London as has happened to so many clubs. Our courses, both on road and country are excellent and the threat of being moved on, especially now we have our own headquarters, is practically non-existent.

As conditions improve, we shall endeavour to rebuild nearer the heart's desire, and thus complete the grand work begun by those who have striven so hard in the past. This is dependent on the support and enthusiasm of every member and I am sure we shall not be disappointed in our efforts any more than they were.

A.A.H.

Did You Know That—

It is now just 41 years since HARRY EVANS was aproached by a fellow baker and asked to support the Bakers' Charity Athletic Meeting at the old Kensal Rise track by competing in a walking race. Harry agreed, and surprised himself by finishing a close second to the trade champion.

Another baker named Savage, who had been the world's professional champion and whose records made under the name of " Billy Franks " in 1882 still stand, thereupon became interested in Harry and, having a son in the Belgraves, he got Harry introduced to our club.

Up till this time the Bels had never been a power to be reckoned with in Club Championships of any kind. It is true that their stationery and Entry forms showed that once they had been placed third in the South of the Thames Championship, that was their sole team honour but the Club's numerous team victories have all been achieved since the days when Harry showed his fellow clubmen that true sportsmanship demanded that one should give his utmost in every race.

The Walking Captain of the Club at that time was F. W. Coomber, a fine walker and a good sportsman, who quickly realised Harry's potential ability. He taught Harry so thoroughly that in the next Club race to be decided the pupil walked away from the teacher. So Harry was urged to enter for the Two-mile handicap at the Stamford Bridge Open " Good Friday " meeting. Hot Cross Buns, being a National necessity at that time, Harry had to put in a stretch of 36 hours on them. During this period he had only two short rests. The race commenced only a few hours after he left the Bakery but Harry won the race by 75 yards.

Shortly after the Good Friday race Harry competed at Herne Hill and was a close third in the very fast time of 13 mins. 32 secs. Next he won a 2 mile Walk at Redhill. Here Tommy Eaton, whom many of you will know as our pre-war trainer, was the virtual scratch man together with Alf Pateman. Harry followed with a second prize at the Tottenham Hospital Sports and a first prize at Sutton.

This Sutton race was memorable inasmuch as Harry was receiving a small start from A. E. Rowlands, the New Zealand Champion, who was over here for the 1908 Olympic Games and who had been recording some very fast times. He was a strong favourite for the race and actually caught Harry, but Harry fought on to gain the victory by a yard.

This race was followed by a second place in a race at Epsom. During this season Harry had won every Club handicap from the scratch mark. During the Winter Season he won the Club 10 miles Championship in 78 minutes.

Following this he walked in the A.A.A. 7 miles Championship and recorded the fast time of 54 mins. 30 secs. and in the 2 miles Championship recording 14 mins. 10 secs. He followed this up with a win at 2 miles on the old White City track of three laps to the mile. This was a very fast track, certainly faster than Stamford Bridge, but not quite so fast as the present track there.

A second place in the L.A.C. Open walk at Stamford Bridge came next and this was followed by a first at Clapton. This was undoubtedly one of Harry's finest performances. E. J. Webb, a winner of five A.A.A. Championships gave Harry a short start and caught him with a lap to go. Harry held him and one of the judges ran the whole of the lap in case either broke contact. It was neck and neck all the way with Harry just winning.

By now we have got to the Winter Season of 1909 and here we find Harry winning the first Junior Individual and Team race promoted by the Road Walking Association. The Belgraves lost the team race by one point, but hereafter there was another honour recorded on our letter headings.

About two miles of tar-macadam and eight miles of rolled granite were generally to be expected in a ten mile race at this time, and in this race the course was typical. In winning, Harry recorded the splendid time of 81 mins. 37 seconds.

Following this, the Club arranged a fair number of inter-club races and in these, although Harry was generally first man home, our successes were few as usually only three or four Belgraves turned out.

Now came a long break in Harry's athletic career. He married, and a growing family, his wife's ill health and night work kept him out of the sport for some time. This period of inactivity was followed by the 1914-18 World War. Harry was an early volunteer for the forces. He was captured and spent a long while in a German prison camp. On returning to England in 1919 he tells me he " was talked into training again by Jimmy Belchamber." Although he did not get back the pace that had formerly made him so formidable he soon became a very sound and reliable team man at seven and ten miles.

He found pace enough also to win some more prizes at 2 miles on the track, a first at Paddington just after the 1920 Olympic games being especially commendable. In this race the British, Australian and South African Olympic walkers were competing. Harry's handicap mark was by no means a long one. On the road he was awarded the style prize in the Islington Borough Walk in March, 1920.

Twice Harry attempted the 20 miles R.W.A. Championship but found that this was not his distance. This was before 1914. When he made his after-war come-back I could not persuade him to try this distance.

As a walking judge Harry has no superior. He has been on the R.W.A. list since early in 1922 and has officiated at many A.A.A. Championships. His worth to the Club as an adviser on style and fair walking is inestimable. As a Committee man he has served a lifetime. He has held the Presidency of the Club worthily for two years.

Finally, Harry's advice to all young walkers—" Get a good style before attempting speed work. That will come later, and remember, the game is the thing."

Surrey County C.C. Championship

Held at Banstead on January 7th over a 3-lap course, which must have been nearer eight than the stated seven and a half miles.

Frank Abberton the holder of the individual title was a non-entrant, as he is not doing anything over the country this season, but even without him we were fairly confident of retaining the " Pickett " Cup, which is awarded to the winning team.

At the end of the first lap, Stone of Dorking St. Pauls was away out in front and then came two Surrey A.C. runners followed by Len Herbert and a bunch of five Belgrave runners, and at this stage of the race it looked a walk-over for us.

Stone had increased his lead when they came round again and although Len Herbert was still with Butler and Wheeler of Surrey, with Les Stokell close behind the rest of our chaps had fallen back and we were hoping for a usual last lap Belgrave effort.

It was Surrey A.C. however, who supplied this, and their team came through splendidly over the final lap and they eventually finished their first five men home in the first eleven placings, which is truly great packing, and although our supporters seemed to doubt that we had lost the team title, the fact that Surrey had five men home against our two, is sufficient to convince anybody that we were beaten by a better side.

Actually, our total of points was less than last year when we won the race, but seeing that this was Surrey's first success since 1932, when incidentally both Stan Belton and Tom Carter were in the respective teams, and were still in this year's, I am sure that no one will begrudge Surrey their success.

Seeing that we had been placed third in the Junior race, and were second in the Youths event, our second team placing here equalled our record of 1938, when we were also placed in all three of the County cross-country events.

5 Miles in 1 Hour Talent Spotting Walk

On Saturday, January 21st, we staged a Talent Spotting Walk for beginners at Wimbledon. The race was over a measured level five-mile course. No prizes were given, but a Certificate issued by the Road Walking Association, duly attested and signed, was to be presented to all competitors finishing the race within one hour.

The enthusiasm of those responsible for advertising the race was amply rewarded by a magnificent entry of 80, and of these, 60 faced the starter. It was a cold day and a keen wind was blowing, but otherwise the conditions were favourable.

There were no disqualifications, and only two retirements, whilst of the 58 finishers, 57 beat the hour. Seven beat 45 minutes, and the race was notable for many thrilling finishes. So close were some of these that the same time had to be recorded for the 1st and 2nd; for the 3rd and 4th; and also for the 6th and 7th; 11th and 12th; and 13th and 14th places. Yet although so many joyous bitter-end battles were fought, they were all conducted without infringement of our definition of Walking.

In fact, the style throughout was of a very high order, and the promise shown by a large percentage of the competitors augers well for the future of our sport.

The start was a fairly fast one and at a mile the first half-dozen were not far apart, but thereafter, A. Johnson of the R.A.F. Halton—a tall stylish walker—forged ahead and soon led by 40-60 yards. He kept the lead for most of the journey, but with about 1¼ miles to go, G. Towers, also of the R.A.F. Halton, made his effort and began to close the gap. With a half-mile to go they were level and it was only in the last few yards that Towers, who was very strong and had walked a well judged race, managed to get a stride ahead and won in the good time of 43 mins. 10 secs.

Meanwhile, Eastwood and Cummings, also of the R.A.F., were having a similar neck and neck fight for the 3rd place. The 5th man was about 30 yards behind them, and 100 yards further back a like tussle for the 6th place was being waged between another R.A.F. man and H. Jeffery, the first Belgrave to finish. Here, also, about a yard separated 6th and 7th.

The result was a great triumph for the R.A.F. lads from Halton, as they filled all the first six places, and were backed up by others finishing 13th, 14th, 21st and 28th. Fifteen R.A.F. lads from Uxbridge also walked well, and it is indeed pleasing to find that our sport has taken root firmly and is flourishing strongly in this branch of the Services.

Another gratifying feature was that about fifty of the sixty starters were in the neighbourhood of 20 years of age.

So from every point of view the promotion was an unqualified and outstanding success. An especially welcome competitor was J. Bidgood, the son of our dual long-distance champion, whilst it was also pleasing to see George Powell—for many years our field-events expert, all-rounder and many times champion—giving his javelin a day off and trying himself out at still one more branch of athletics.

Following the success of the five miles race, it has been decided to hold a similar race on Saturday, April 29th, 1950, in which competitors will be invited to walk SIX miles in one hour. This is an excellent opportunity for members to bring along their friends for a " Try out." The last race produced 60 starters and we look forward to a field of similar size on April 29th.

Over the Country

by Observer.

A review of the cross-country results shows that we have fared none too well this season from a team point of view.

Starting with the South of Thames " Junior " race we finished fifth as against last season's fourth place, while we lost both the Surrey Senior team and individual titles.

Our Middlesex members also lost their team championship and were beaten out of a place, but we did manage to secure places in all three Surrey County championships over the country.

We were holders of the South of Thames Senior title, but as the winning scoring team are barred, there was some excuse for our dropping to 12th place in this year's race.

The Southern Senior title was also lost and here again we were beaten out of a place, but our Juniors showed a big improvement on last year's 18th position when finishing fifth this time. Also our Youths improved from 17th position to 2nd place this season, and this is really the outstanding performance by our cross-country section.

In the " National " we dropped from sixth place last year to eighth this time, but our Juniors improved from 24th to 21st, while our Youths who were 29th last year finished a very creditable seventh this time out.

To try to find the reason for our dropping off is partly explained when we find runners like Len Herbert, Derrick Burfitt, Bill Lucas, Frank Abberton, Reg Ruddick and Eddie Short not taking part in the big races and although we are a club of considerable numerical strength, it takes time to build up a really strong team.

Also we find these days that many of the lads deem fit to bring along their wives and girl friends on race days, and although we are always pleased to see these at our Club socials, I personally think that on race days a chap should concentrate on the job on hand and not have any females to distract him.

Of course, the wives may threaten to go home to mother, the only snag here being, that when they come back they usually bring mother with them!

A hope for the future is the considerable promise of our Youths section, under Micky Pyer's tuition, and a return of the old club spirit among all classes of members, both those who have gained their International honours, and those who are hoping that they may one day wear the red rose on their running vest.

A general striving in that direction must result in an improvement as a team and I trust that the day is not too far distant when Belgrave will once again be the premier cross-country club in the country.

Congratulations to Alf Harley, our nominee, and Les Cohen, nominated by the London Business Houses S.A., on being elected to the A.A.A. Southern Committee.

We note with interest that Ernie Duffett has been re-appointed one of the official A.A.A. Handicappers for the forthcoming season.

Eddie Short selected to represent the English Universities at Glasgow in the Varsity C.C. International Race on March 18th.

Archie Bedford, who won the London Transport 5 miles Cross Country Championship.

Ken England and Jim Kennedy on their recent marriages.

DAMAGE TO HALL. As you will now have learned, the Belgrave Hall is now entirely your property. Reports have been received of minor wanton damage and your committee earnestly request all members to co-operate fully in preventing further damage.

A.A.A. CHAMPIONSHIPS. July 14th-15th. Tickets may be obtained. as soon as available, from Frank Webb, 50, Longford Avenue, Southall, Middlesex, at 3s. 6d. each. Early application is advised and only applications accompanied by cash can be considered.

OVERSEAS CUP. It is regretted that there was a very poor response to the appeal in our last issue for donations to this cup. We are nevertheless grateful to C. T. Pateman, A. Penstone, F. H. Elson and R. Towndrow.

Special welcome to Ron Kemp, who will in due course prove a great acquisition to our teams. He followed up his second place in the " Garnett " Cup Championship at Coventry with an easy win in the Hampshire 10 Miles Championship. A few days ago he married, and we wish him and his wife many years of happiness.

A twenty-two-year-old walker of very great promise, Geoff. Gregory, has recently joined us and it may confidently be said that he is destined to win championships and appear in future winning Belgrave teams. Walking as an individual, he was 12th in the " Garnett " Cup Championship and recently won the Tobacco Trades A.A. Championship with ease. A hearty welcome to Belgrave, Geoff.

Alan Sexton, R.A.F.; Peter Baldwin, R.A.F.; and B. L. Chitty, Army Signals; have recently entered the services and wish to be remembered to all members and friends.

Frank Kurton, now a Sapper in 42 Survey Engineer Regt. M.E.L.F., writes from Fayid in the Suez Canal Zone of Egypt and wishes to be remembered to all Belgrave friends and hopes to keep up his running in spite of the heat.

The friends of Max Quackenbos will be pleased to learn that he is now a proud father—a girl. " Quack " (our pre-war Junior Captain) does no competitive running now and marvels at how some of his old friends keep it up. He sends best wishes to all.

We are greatly indebted to one of our members, Mr. R. Plummer, who has recently presented the Club with a handsome silver bowl for perpetual competition. It has been decided to institute a Junior 440 Yards Championship and the trophy has been allocated to this event. Mr. Plummer is Hon. Treasurer of the London Federation of Boys Clubs and has always been interested in the welfare and activities of the young brigade. It is particularly appropriate therefore that this splendid new trophy should be identified with a new Junior Championship, and it is to be hoped that it will be the means of encouraging our younger athletes to a high standard of performances.

Once again our headquarters have been used by a number of sporting bodies, Boy Scouts organisations, etc., for their various championships, and we regard these services as something contributed by Belgrave towards athletics.

Harry Churcher was the recent recipient of a carved wooden shield presented by the Club to mark his world's records for five miles set up in 1948 and 1949. Appropriately the carving shows the Belgrave Badge eclipsing the world, and an engraved silver tablet gives details of the performances.

The shield was carved by an old friend and member, Herbert Bignall, and is certainly a fine piece of craftsmanship.

Peter Martin has accepted an invitation from the Committee to fill the vacancy caused by the resignation of B. L. Chitty on his being called to the Services. We are anxious that the younger members should play their part in the legislation of the Club and carry on the good work in the old tradition.

FINALLY—Remember the date of the six miles walk, APRIL 29th, when we hope to have a large field of UNATTACHED walkers.
