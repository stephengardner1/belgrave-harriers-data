Editorial

YOUR EDITORS

Dear Fellow Belgravians,

It is with a note of sadness that we open the pages of this issue of the Belgravian. Two English gentlemen and true Sportsmen have gone from us and we mourn their passing deeply.

Joe Turner, a Vice President and lifelong friend of our club, a wise counsellor to all participating in athletics, an enthusiastic and extremely efficient official, and our own dear Jimmy Bellchambei who was all that a Belgravian should be and in fact the originator of the word "Belgravian."

Our President, Alf Harley writes an obituary on both our dear departed friends.

Whilst our sorrow is manifest, it must also be borne in mind that we have lost two very valuable officials. Their places in athletics will be hard to fill, but those to whom they pass the torch, by reason of the splendid and unselfish example they both showed, must fill these places, and so on to perpetuity.

Adieu—Joe Turner and Jimmy Bellchamber.

YOUR EDITORS.

Obituary · page 2

J. B. Bellchamber

A.A.H.

February 28th, 1952, was a very sad day indeed for it brought news of the death of Jimmy Bellchamber at the age of 66 years, following a three months' illness which had been bravely borne with typical fortitude. Our sympathetic condolences are extended to Mrs. Bellchamber and her three sons. A number of Jimmy's club mates and friends in Walking and Athletics attended the Requiem Mass and funeral on March 5th, paying their final tribute to one they were proud to have known. To have been honoured by his friendship during his life time, as have so many of us, is something positive we shall remember with pleasure all our lives. We mourn his passing—we honour his memory.

Jimmy came to the Club in 1911/12 as a runner and the late Bob Ricketts quickly noted his abilities and loyalty to the Club and invited him to become our honorary auditor, for finances in those days, although small, needed expert supervision, and Jimmy has told us with a smile on many an occasion of how the balance in hand at the 1912 audit was 11/5d. and of its gradual increase throughout the years (always with Jimmy as the Hon. Auditor—forty years of real service) until the club's assets are now well within measurable reach of £10,000. An accountant by profession, Jimmy's sound knowledge and able guidance was always of the greatest help and a very great debt of gratitude is owed to him.

His outstanding services to the club were recognised by the conferment of Life Membership many years ago, and in 1931/32 he became President. In earlier years he had been a Club Captain and Vice Captain and we have known him in recent years as a first class sprint and walking judge, and absolutely dependable in all he undertook on behalf of the club.

He will always be remembered for his personal influence and leadership in building up the walking side of the club to the grand position it now occupies. In the 1912 era we had a few walkers but the prevailing idea then was that seven miles was just about the limit if a man wished to retain his speed. Jimmy had very different thoughts and set about demonstrating his own ideas and building up that basis of solid team work which is part of the inheritance we owe so largely to him. After a training walk one evening with Bob Ricketts (who in his time was sprinter, middle distance runner, boxer and walker—all with some distinction), from the Flood Street, Chelsea, quarters of the club, it was decided that Jimmy should henceforth remain a walker and that every endeavour should be made to build up a team. This was done to some purpose and the club began to show up well and in a Junior League Competition finished: unplaced, 3rd, 2nd and 1st in the four races held. During the summer of 1914, Jimmy began training for the Brighton Walk, but the outbreak of war in August, 1914, put paid to his ideas—temporarily at all events.

In 1919 Bob Ricketts, Harry Evans, Bill Hawker and Jimmy were quickly together again and were soon reinforced by further strength. In fact Belgrave accounted for every scratch team race held in the Metropolitan area during the season 1919/20 and in the 1919 A.A.A. Championship our 6th man finished 13th or 14th and one of our best, W. J. Hawker, did not start. In 1920 the R.W.A. Junior Championship was won with a team of four post-war members and the foundations laid for the many successes that have since followed. So the Bellchamber influence and example was at work—and so it continued for many years.

In the spring of 1914, Jimmy had been the first Belgrave walker to attain first-class standard (then 3 hrs. 10 mins.) in the R.W.A. 20 miles Championship. In 1920 he was placed third in the A.A.A. 7 miles Championship—the first Belgravian to do so in modern times. In 1921 he was the first Belgravian to beat the present R.W.A. First Class Standard at 20 miles and Captain of the first Club team ever to finish in a 20 miles Championship. He was the first Belgrave Harrier to be placed in the Brighton Walk and the first Club man to beat 9 hours for the journey. He was the first Belgrave Walker to do the double journey and become a Centurion.

All these actual performances have been surpassed by Club members subsequently, but the force of Jimmy's great personal example will always live on. He had to break down the prejudice that racing at 10 miles and over was inimical to track speed; he had to inculcate the ideas of team work, for individual effort was the greatest motivating force at one time. But these things he did and great was the victory and the record books bear testimony to his work through the many championship and Brighton Walk team wins and placings since 1920.

Jimmy was the first winner (in 1923) of the Sussex 10 miles Championship. In September, 1920, he was third in the Brighton Walk and followed up this grand performance in the years that followed in this manner: Sept., 1922—4th; May, 1923—4th; Sept., 1923—5th; Sept., 1924—4th. No wonder that we have built up a great tradition in the Brighton Walks and have recorded more team wins than any other club.

In 1921 Jimmy did not compete in the Brighton Walk, having concentrated on the London to Brighton and back (104 miles) in which he finished second. This was an outstanding performance, for the day journey took place in one of the highest temperatures ever recorded in this country, and by contrast the night had been extremely cold.

Bellchamber as a Walking Judge and as a man commanded the greatest respect and he had officiated at hundreds of races including the Olympic Games, A.A.A. and R.W.A. Championships, Area and County Championships, etc., always competently, conscientiously and with credit to the Club and himself.

Perhaps it was as a moral force—as the personification of the innate good things in life on the spiritual plane that those of us who were close to Jimmy will best remember him. We shall recall his unassuming, dignified, friendly approach combined with intense loyalty, strength of character and an ever present desire to help. And always we shall remember his devotion to Belgrave, his work through forty years to enhance the position and reputation, his dependability, his love for his Club and his fellow members.

Jimmy has delighted many of us with his articles and pen sketches of Club personalities in the Belgravian, and the Belgrave News Letter. In doing so he greatly enriched our knowledge and also revealed himself to us. As a native of Sussex (his name comes from the same derivation as BALCOMBE, and probably indicated Norman ancestry) he was never happier than when he was within its borders, although disgusted with "London by the Sea" and the injury the town had wreaked on some parts of his beloved county. He had read widely and his articles were bespattered with appropriate quotations and with some finely expressed sentiments of his own. This he writes of a stroll to Brighton: "Sunrise and the immaculate hours have the same indescribable charm that was theirs in a younger and fresher world. Right before us Wolstenbury still pushes up an enormous head from out a sea of clinging mist just as he did when Roman Legionaries were encamped upon his brow; whilst away in the west we see the sun's long fingers playing lovingly with the dark tresses of Chanctonbury's curl crowned head before that giant has wakened and pushed aside his pearly coverlet. Eastwards Ditchling Beacon greets us with a face bathed in smiles. Surely there is recognition in his warm Sussex Welcome."

How contemptuous he was of "The sacrifices the modern Moloch demands." Let us re-read his verses:—

THE BYE-PASS Now the motor horn hoots where the nightingale sang, And a petrol pump stands where his low notes out rang From the delicate twigs of his glorified perch, When the soft June morn gleamed on the silvery birch. And a stark straight road runs where thick low leaves amid And emitting faint fragrance, the violets hid Whilst the multiformed beauty of lichened oak boles Is replaced by the sameness of telegraph poles. And the meadow where buttercups mirrored the stars Is now open all night as a garage for cars, Whilst there's jazz for the jaded and tea for the dry In a spic and span jerry-built bungalow by. And the poppies' red flame no more grows on the seas Of the billowing wheat overswept by the breeze. Gone the use and the beauty! whilst ignorant pride Crashes over the land on a juggernaut ride. And the chattering streamlet that winded and strayed Between banks blossom jewelled when springtime displayed Its pale primroses, windflowers and daffodils gay, Through a pipe neath the road is now making its way. Yes, where sometimes was seen like a flash of blue light That the eye could scarce follow, a kingfisher's flight, Blatant road hogs rush past with their poisonous smell, Changing England's fair garden from Eden to hell.

We have indeed lost a great Belgravian. Whilst lying in hospital he told the writer that although a member of the Club for forty years he had never realised until that time everything the Club had meant to him and the number of friends he had within its ranks. Their letters and calls helped him tremendously and he was extremely grateful.

Much more could be written of this great friend of ours and his influence and example will help us in the years that follow. If we can be but loyal to the ideals that were his, we need have no fears for the Club he loved so well, nor for ourselves.

R.I.P.

A.A.H.

J. Turner

We also mourn the passing of Joe Turner, a Vice-President and Honorary Timekeeper for many years past.

Joe's unassuming and unaffected personality had endeared him to us all. His reputation as a first-class timekeeper was known wherever athletics was enjoyed and he had officiated at all the principal track, cross-country and walking championships and at record attempts by the shoal. But delightfully, he also found pleasure in working at the humble school or inter-club meetings. In over sixty years of athletics he had never let anyone down. If Joe said (sometimes twelve months before) that he would come along to a particular meeting, he did so. He was absolutely dependable.

He did not just mix with his fellow officials, but was approachable by everyone, known to everyone. He was a familiar figure at Belgrave Hall and at our track meetings and we are going to miss him tremendously.

We can ill afford to lose such stalwarts as Joe—a grand sportsman and friend, a great worker for Belgrave as well as athletics generally, and a man we've been proud to know for so long.

Report · page 5

F. M. Stone

Following upon the article published in our last issue, our very sincere congratulations are extended to Frank M. Stone (also known as Mark and Rodney) on the recent award of the "George" Medal. He is undoubtedly the bravest Metropolitan policeman ever, and has received (at the hands of three monarchs) the King's Police Medal (the Police V.C.), the British Empire Medal, and now the "George" Medal—all awarded for gallantry; in addition to which he has received the Royal Humane Society's Medal for saving life.

Many years ago Frank was a member of our distance walking teams and in the London to Brighton Walk of 1921 finished 12th and won the open handicap then held in conjunction. He has helped considerably in the matter of raising Club finances in days gone by and his visits to training quarters were always very welcome and of the cheeriest nature. He has always been very versatile in sport with interests in boxing, wrestling (he served on the Council of the A.W.A. for several years), Soccer, Rugby football, etc., etc., whilst Belgrave has always meant a great deal to him.

We are very proud of his achievements and look forward to seeing him at our gatherings as of yore, now that injuries sustained in the incident which led to the recent award have compelled his retirement from the Police Force.

Our Invalids

We are pleased to learn that Oscar Horwood is a little better. We hope the coming fine weather will help him on a bit more. All Belgravians are thinking kindly of him.

Jack Shields, who has been suffering from polio for many weeks, and has lived during that time in an iron lung, is now improving. Many Belgravians have already visited him at Grove Hospital, Tooting, and he will be pleased to see any of his Club mates to help him along his uphill fight towards full recovery. However, Jack is so much improved that he had sent us a message which he would like to be published in the Belgravian, and here it is:—

"I wonder whether you could find space in the Belgravian to put in a word of thanks to all the members who have been so kind to me by visits, letters, messages and enquiries, to say nothing of all the gifts, both individual and those which Tom Carter has brought on behalf of others. I have been greatly heartened by all this kind consideration.

Please thank everyone.

Jack Shields."

Albert Macher, who was recently in hospital, is now well and out again.

Of other people's invalids. We are very happy to report that Charlie Smith, the well-known official and member of Thames Valley Harriers, is progressing favourably in hospital. Derek Pugh, the international quarter-miler, who was also stricken with polio, is very much better. It is interesting to note that Derek has been under the treatment of our own Dr. E. M. McKenzie.

The Belgrave Budget

G. S. DOUBLEDAY

The Finance Sub-Committee, at a recent meeting, made proposals which were subsequently endorsed by the full Club Committee. These proposals are designed to bring our income closer to our expenditure and, in view of general price increases over the past few years, they cannot be regarded as severe.

The charge for programmes at our meetings is to be increased from 3d. to 4d.

Our printed programmes have been very much admired by people outside the Club and it is our policy to continue with them. If every programme could be sold at each meeting it would go a long way towards reducing the loss on programmes which we report each year. Anyone who can help to sell programmes at meetings will be welcome. Now that we are going back to Battersea Park for some of our meetings we think of the days before the war when spectators had to get there early before the programmes were all sold.

The next item will affect active members. As from 1st January the entry fees for Club events for Seniors will be 1s. 6d. for the first event and 1s. for each additional event per meeting. Juniors and Youths will pay 1s. per event as previously. Cross-country and Road Walking events will remain at 1s. Last year's Balance Sheet showed that entry fees were well below the cost of handicap prizes alone, not to mention Championship and Standard medals. I have no doubt that the Section Hon. Secretaries will be exhorting you to give greater support to Club handicaps, so I'll leave it to them.

The next item concerns subscriptions. It is agreed that our income from subscriptions is not big enough to meet our increasing expenditure and it is probable that subscriptions will have to be raised. This will be considered at a future meeting when new rates will be suggested.

Once again I must thank the many who have written to me regarding their subscriptions, particularly those in the Forces. We hope that members in the Forces have been receiving Club literature and are getting opportunities for training. Many have given their demob. dates; these have been noted and we hope to welcome them back to the Club life in due course. Some lucky ones are doing their service locally and we see them often. Others are far afield and send their best wishes to the Club. They can rest assured that we often think of them and wish them the best of luck.

ONE LAST REMINDER—HAVE YOU PAID YOUR SUBS? If not, why not?

G. S. DOUBLEDAY.

Report · page 7

Chelmsford Road Relay Race

This was held on October 27th and we retained our hold on the "Sidney Taylor Memorial Trophy" in no uncertain manner.

Our time of 94 mins. 43 secs. was a new course record and we won quite comfortably from the Aylesford Paper Mills with Essex Beagles in third place.

Chas Walker, although suffering from the after-effects of a cold, ran a good first "leg" which was inside the previous lap record time, held by Bill Lucas, but even so could only hand over in second place to Cook, of Hadleigh A.C.

Archie Bedford, however, soon caught the leader and from then on we were always out in front.

Len Adams kept up the good work and increased our lead and Bill Lucas took us further ahead to hand over to Mike Stearman 68 secs. to the good.

Mike ran steadily and well and lost only a few seconds, despite being chased by Reg Robins of Essex Beagles, an experienced performer.

Len Herbert was on the final relay and he took no chances as Sando of Aylesford, was running the last "leg" for the Paper Mills, who had been having a battle-royal with Essex Beagles.

Incidentally, Jim Peters of Essex Beagles, the A.A.A. Marathon champion set up a new course record of 15 mins. 22 secs., which was 13 seconds inside the old figures set up by Bill Lucas in last year's race.

Our team's individual times were—Walker, 15.33; Bedford, 15.46; Adams, 15.53; Lucas, 15.46; Stearman, 16.10; Herbert, 15.35.

Team result—1, Belgrave H., 94.43; 2, Aylesford P.M., 95.33; 3, Essex Beagles, 96.06; 4, Southend A.C., 99.35; 5, Chelmsford A.C., 100.00; 6, Ilford A.C., 100.46 (15 teams closed in).

South of Thames "Junior" Race

A record entry of 54 teams for the South of Thames "Junior" race held at Farningham on November 10th, and a field of 317 runners faced the starter.

The course was very heavy after recent rain and included quite a bit of plough and in the words of one competitor, it had "everything."

At the end of the first lap Don MacL[illegible]an was our first man with the others quite a long way back and already the Herne Hill lads seemed comfortable winners of the team race.

"Ginger" Newell and Mick Porter came through well on the second lap with Maclean dropping back a bit and Hudson and Mike Stearman were too far back for us to stand a chance of getting placed.

Peter Courtney ran quite well in his first senior race, for although this is known as a Junior event, the word junior applies to ability and not to age.

We had entered five teams as we believe in giving all our members a chance of representing the club in open competition, and as they all closed in, it was evident that our policy was justified.

Sporting columnists seeking sensational copy would have headed their reports of the Garnet Cup Race at Sheffield:—

" The debacle at Sheffield—were the judges too hard?"

SOUTHERN COUNTIES C.C. CHAMPIONSHIPS

These were held at Hadleigh, Essex, on February 23rd, over fairly rough country which included a climb up and around the ruins of Hadleigh Castle.

In the Youths' event our lads got away to a good start with Wright and Goode up in the first half-dozen. Then came tragedy in the fact that they were wrongly directed and by the time they got back on the right course they had lost quite a lot of ground and places. Frank Wright managed to get up with the leaders once again but Geoff Goode found the effort too much for him and was our 4th scoring man. This race is certainly a " hoodoo " for Geoff and Micky Pyer must have been disappointed at our 4th place in the team race, but we hope for better luck next year.

The senior race came next and at the end of the first lap of the country it looked as if Aylesford Paper Mills would retain their team title, as they had three men in the first dozen runners. Our lads were running well together but seemed too far back although Vern Blowfield was going great guns and Eddie Short was also well up. On the second lap we saw Charlie Walker, Eddie Short, Jack Brown and Archie Bedford running well together with Bill Lucas, our captain, acting as the whipper-in.

At the finish we saw Vern Blowfield, who had truly made a come-back and had run himself out in the progress, being placed 11th, then we had four in the 20's and although Bill Lucas had dropped about ten places, we still had 10 points to spare over the holders.

The rest of our team had also run well with special mention for Hudson and Len Bishop and it seemed strange to see Len Herbert back so far, but he has not done a great deal of racing this season. John Bromley had damaged a foot in an Army race a few days previously but pluckily kept going to the end in order that we could win the " Odling-Smee " Cup, which is awarded to the first Surrey club to finish its complete team of 12 men.

We thus regained the " Frank Wynne " Challenge Cup which is awarded to the winners of the senior race, and so repeated our previous wins in 1934, 1946, 1948 and 1949.

The Junior race was last on the " card " and as we had expected Ray Tooby was well up with the field but felt the effects of a Service race in mid-week and so could only finish in 7th place. Mike Stearman was next home followed by Doug Briggs and Don Maclean, and don't think it was the latter's type of country.

(The Editors are happy to grant this Section complete recognition by giving it a regular page in the Club Magazine)

The Long Distance Road Running Section, by reason of its new constitution finds itself now not exactly the " Cinderella " of Belgrave Harriers. It appears that this new status has given the lads an enthusiasm and impetus which will no doubt be felt in our Club life.

At the time of going to press we shall only have been a going concern for a few weeks but nevertheless we can report our first race in the Mitcham 15. DID YOU KNOW THAT—

G. H. Powell (George) is the Club's oldest, active and perhaps finest ever Field Events man.

He joined the " Bels " in 1920 and immediately took up running. He tells me he ran in the Boxing Day Handicap in that year which was won by one of our Past Presidents Ted Gordon. George was nowhere. He soon turned his mind to Field Events—Long Jump and Hop, Step and Jump—and must have been one of the Club's very few Field Events men in those days. He eventually took up Javelin Throwing which proved his best event and this has been his speciality through the years. He has won Surrey and Southern and Inter-Counties Championships but to George's chagrin never a Club Javelin Championship. His record in these events and others is to be marvelled at and the following list speaks for itself:—

1929-30-31-33 Winner Club " Harry Wright " Shield for Field Events in the days before individual Championships were instituted. 1935-36 Represented A.A.A. v. Oxford. 1931 Represented A.A.A. v. Northern Counties. 1936 Represented A.A.A. v. Metropolitan Counties. 1927-28-31-34-35 Represented Surrey in Inter-Counties Championships. 1931 Reserve in Javelin for Great Britain v. Italy. Other Awards—A.A.A. Standards, 6; Southern Standards, 9; Surrey Standards, 5; Club Standards, 7.

George's best performance with the Javelin was in 1934 when at a meeting at Ponders End he threw 183ft. 1½ins. This bettered the then British record and in fact he broke it three times during the afternoon. Unfortunately for him that record could never be claimed as there were insufficient qualified judges officiating. His best performances other than that were:—

A.A.A., 169ft. 4½ins.; Inter-Counties, 172ft. 10ins.; Southern Counties, 175ft. 4¾ins.; Surrey County, 165ft. 3½ins.

Apart from all these performances George has represented the Club in all the most important Trophy events and in Club matches. He is a true Belgravian and whilst he specialised in the Javelin he also competed in the Long Jump, Hop, Step and Jump, Hurdles, Discus and Shot. George's Club spirit was such that if at any match we were short in any of those events you only had to mention it and he would have a " bash "—just for the Club.

Even these days when he is over 45 years of age he can be relied upon to turn out in almost any event and put up a good show. In fact he was placed in the Club Javelin Championship only last year.

He is known all over the South where Field Events men gather as a great sportsman and gentleman and many young athletes who have eventually made their mark have welcomed his good advice and encouragement.

George's advice to youngsters is train, try, try still harder and be determined and you'll succeed.

It is, to men like George, we hope all budding Field Events men will look for inspiration and as an example of a true " Belgravian " and be determined that they will aspire to reach the heights he has, not only for themselves but for the Club.

COMPETITORS' NUMBER STEWARD

TOM MORRELL.

All active and some not so active members will remember me as the chap who has taken your hard-earned bobs for handicap and championship events throughout the last few summers; who listens sympathetically to your moans and groans about the shortness of your mark, or you don't like this or that prize, or most important of all—please can I have a run, as I am not on the programme.

Well, boys, here's a shock for you. On and from May 3rd, 1952, entrance fees for all track and field events, handicap and championship, will be 1s. 6d. for the first event and 1s. for each additional event for seniors. This measure has been adopted, firstly to keep up the high value of prizes given for handicaps and to offset the increasing rise in prices. It must be remembered that prizes are subject to considerable purchase tax.

Secondly, re the shortness of your mark. I can only suggest that you run a little faster or a little more often, with perhaps a little more training.

The third item is prizes. The difficult task of buying these is ably carried out by Eric Bishop who spends hours of his time trying to get a selection to suit everyone. So please, if you manage the impossible and get placed in a handicap, remember Eric cannot foresee who will win the prizes or what the lucky ones would like for a prize.

Running, when not on the programme, was a growing menace of all the track events last summer. If there is no improvement this summer, the committee will have to take stern action to stamp it out. To those in the habit of entering on the day, I ask you to remember the extra work placed on the officials concerned; members of the public who buy our programmes finding that half the starters are not officially entered, and lastly competitors who enter in the proper manner being unplaced by the late entrants. I myself took second place to a late entry on three occasions last summer. So please send or hand your name in at least seven days before the race, either to Ernie Duffett or to me on training nights.

Lastly, a word about pins and numbers. These are an expensive item, therefore you will help me and the Club if you will kindly return them to me in good condition, after the race.

Many thanks in advance for your future help in these matters.

TOM MORRELL.

WITHOUT FEAR OR FAVOUR

FRANK SIMMONS.

If this article seems too much to the point, I make no apologies. It is written against or about no person in particular, but I would be false if I did not say that which is in my mind.

This 1951-52 Cross Country Season, as voiced by Bill Lucas, should be Belgrave's, indeed his slogan " Belgrave for the National " is the best I have heard for a long time and indeed we have the chaps to bring the bacon home, but it is not to the Cross Country boys or the walkers, but the TRACK MEN to which this article is directed.

We can make much better showing on the track than in the past if all really pull their weight. There is too much leaving it to someone else and consequently we lose matches we should have won.

There is ample talent in the Club and I cannot pretend to understand why we do not do better. Perhaps there are too many sitting in the stands or watching elsewhere when their presence on the field of competition would help not only themselves but their Club mates. If you are playing rugger or soccer on Saturdays and have Sunday mornings free, come along and join in the happy bunch of lads at Battersea Park, especially you field events men.

We used to have a very strong field events section, indeed it was those people who used to win our matches for us. Let's get together and make it strong again, so that we can challenge the best once more.

As a regular at Tooting and Battersea I have noticed much talent. If they got together and trained hard they would go a long way to strengthening their part of the Club. I have also been told about talent by numerous other people, but I never see them—what are they afraid of? Hard work! This phrase applies to all, for it is only by hard work and still more hard work that we can obtain our objective. No first-class athlete gets to the top the easy way but only continued effort to make the grade.

As for the Junior sprinters—I want those Surrey Relay titles again this year of 1952. We are going to do even better. We can and I honestly think we can win not only individual honours but team honours in the Southern and A.A.A. Junior Relays.

To the Senior sprinters, it's about time you got together and won the Surrey County Senior Relay (4 x 110 yards). You could also win the Surrey County Medley Relay with the help of the Middle distance men we now have. I believe that the Senior sprinters of both Surrey and Middlesex can win a place in the Southern and A.A.A. 4 x 110 Relays and indeed win them both, but again it means HARD WORK.

Now what about it? Make whichever team you pick, Belgrave's first Senior A.A.A. 4 x 110 yards winning team; you can if you try. But remember the Juniors will be out to beat your performances, not necessarily in times but in team and individual honours and none will be prouder than they themselves if you do better.

To Junior Field Event men, come and give the Junior Track men your fullest support and make our Junior Section the strongest in the Club's history. Turn out regularly for training, whatever the weather and under the expert guidance of Franz Stampfl there is not the shadow of a doubt that you will improve to your own amazement and satisfaction. Lose that awful sense of apathy and you will go a long way to enjoying your athletics.

FRANK SIMMONS.
