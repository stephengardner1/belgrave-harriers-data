Maureen Noel – Fast Lady!

Relatively new to the sport, Maureen Noel (pictured left) can already point to a National Race Walking title over 50km – and a 3:29 marathon. We have the feeling that there is a lot more to come. John Hall tells us more:-

Maureen won the combined women’s Southern and National 50 kilometre walking championships at Earls Colne in Essex on Sunday 4th September in hot conditions. It was her debut at the distance and, considering the heat and the fact that she suffered bad blistering to her feet in the closing stages, her time of 6 hours 2 minutes and 53 seconds was very respectable. And considering she had done very little training beforehand her performance augurs very well for future efforts over the distance.

Maureen has been in Belgrave colours for about two years. She was “discovered” three years ago when she competed in the Defra (Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs) Sports Day. Without any training she did every track event from 100 metres to 3,000 metres and among the trophies she picked up was one for the second highest points score for a woman.

Maureen said she had always wanted to be an athlete but hadn’t known how to get involved. Waiting to your late 30’s to get started may be a bit extreme, but better late than never….

Among the events she did that day in June 2002 was the 2,000 metres walk. Maureen had never tried race walking before but her performance was good enough to prompt John Hall to invite her to join him for training. He entered her for her second race, the 3,000 metres walk in the Civil Service track and field championships at Wigan 7 weeks later, and the rest, as they say….. Maureen finished second in 20 minutes 3.7 seconds and has progressed since then to get her time down to 17:31.

However, short distances aren’t Maureen’s forte. The people who regularly beat her at 3,000 and 5,000 metres walk find her powering past them in the later stages of races from about 10k upwards. So she has won the Middlesex 10 mile title this year and 6 days later won the Southern 20k. And her 10k track time is faster than doubling her best 5k time!

Running still holds attractions for Maureen. She continues to run everything from 100 metres upwards at the annual Defra sports and at the Civil Service veterans championships and can’t resist a couple of runs at the main CS championships – after all a 3,000 metres walk doesn’t take much out of her so why not use some surplus energy…

Seeing the London Marathon, Maureen declared an interest in running it. First John Hall trained with her for the Luton Marathon, which incorporated the Civil Service championship, in December 2004. John ran with Maureen and kept her from going too fast in the first half of the race. At about 15 miles, as he ran out of steam, he encouraged Maureen to run on ahead. She obliged by finishing 13 minutes ahead, in 3:38. Not bad on modest training. The London Marathon followed, with even less running training but an improved time (3:29).

What Maureen needs now is some consistent training and proper coaching and we may really see what she can achieve.

Maureen NoelJohn HallJeremy Hemming

Pipped by Morpeth again

Road Running

Auckland Castle 10km inc. AAA & CAU Inter-County Champs., 21 August 2005.

The long haul to the North East for the Auckland Castle and AAA 10k, closely co-operating with the Surrey team for the Inter-County race, was another enjoyable experience. Once again the men were just pipped for the AAA team race and it was silver again - our fourth consecutive year as medallists. The Belles were also into the hardware, gaining a surprising set of bronze awards. And with both our teams heavily involved in the scoring for the Inter-County titles, there were yet more medals as the Surrey men placed 3rd and the women 2nd.

We like winning, of course, but one of the most pleasing thing these days is that we ride our bad luck and still make a great contribution to the race. Birhan Dagne was unable to stay the course, coming out at 4k, David Anderson is continually troubled by his Achilles tendon problem and was another dnf (can’t say we ever remember that happening before) while Mark Miles was unable to start due to a tendon problem of his own. But for these misfortunes it could have been double team win.

The men's team race was so tight that had it been calculated on accumulated times, as many races are these days, we would have won - exactly the same situation as with the AAA 5k a month or so earlier. Of course some other time this type of scoring could work to our advantage, but looking at the women’s team results one can see that the team places were calculated on actual race positions - including men, guests, overseas competitors, etc. Where so many categories are included within the one event, accumulated times have got to be the fairest way of doing things.

Team Manager in the medals

Surrey County AA Road Relays, Wimbledon Park, 10 September 2005.

This year’s event was switched to the nearby bowls pavilion because Wimbledon Park athletic track was closed for refurbishment; as a result the course was a little longer than normal.

After a long run of silver medals in the Surrey Relay our men’s team had the chance to go for a hat-trick of wins, having won in 2003 and 2004 – but it was back to the runners-up slot once more as alternative events took precedence in our runners’ diaries.

Lee Greatorex is aiming to tackle the longer distances and his extra mileage seems to be giving him a boost over the shorter road runs as well. His fourth on the opening leg was converted to second place on leg two by the Skipper, who interrupted his support of the England cricket team by cycling from the Oval Cricket Ground to Wimbledon to do his bit – and then cycling back again for the after tea session. Only the non-scoring Thames team was ahead at this point and they were caught by Knut Hegvold on stage three, but South London’s Jason Simpson also came through and with a strong trio to follow, it was left to our own Messrs Ellacott, Humphrey and Browne to hold on to second spot as SLH gained a stranglehold on the race.

With M40s, M50s W35s and Senior Women racing at the same time as the men but over just four stages it can be very difficult to work out what is going on. Heidi Cayzer gave us a solid start and then Rosie Powell moved up two places to fourth. Team Manager Catherine Eastham put her clipboard and team sheet aside to keep the Belles in the hunt and with Jules surging through on the last stage we came home fourth – and third in the Surrey Championship as Epsom’s outstanding Emily Nelson had run twice in her club’s team, thus ruling them ineligible.

Drug rule changes

Sport’s incessant war against drugs took a tentative pace forward when the IAAF voted to step up the suspension of convicted athletes from 2-to-4 years. Most genuine athletes would like it see it raised to ‘for life’. If it was ‘for life’ human rights lawyers representing the star athletes so banned would immediately be citing ‘restraint of trade’. That’s life nowadays. However the IAAF must still be commended for doubling the ban. And the most lenient sport for drug takers? That’s American baseball, where those caught rattling with steroids are confined to their dug-outs for precisely 4 weeks!

From Essex Walker

British League Performances in 2005

Time heals, so they say. And whilst reflecting on the track season just passed, and in particular on our failure to hold on at the top of the British Athletics League, the first stirrings of an urge to regain our title are becoming very much stronger. It will be a difficult task and we will most certainly need some huge commitment from our athletes. But in the meantime, let’s take stock of the guys that earned the points in 2005.

Bill Laws tells us: Generally we need between 55 and 57 athletes to provide a league winning effort over four matches. This year we only managed to get 50 to the starting line and that proved insufficient to secure a ninth consecutive Division One victory.

On the face of it competition from other meetings had depleted our teams but it could be argued that this affected all teams so that was not the main problem. A closer analysis of the 57 athletes who turned out last season showed that 23 did not make it this year. So we blooded 16 new members and relied heavily on our older hands.

William Sharman claimed top spot on his own this year as only he, Mick Jones and new member Adrian Phillips made all four matches. There will be some serious revisions to the all time points scores table as many of our big points scorers continued to rack up impressive contributions to the team’s efforts.

Tim Benjamin - 6th on UK all-time list

Tim Benjamin is glad his summer season has finally come to an end despite the success he has enjoyed over 400metres.

Benjamin was runner-up to Tyree Washington in a thrilling one-lap encounter in Monaco and his time capped an outstanding couple of months.

“I’m tired and really looking forward to taking a break,” said Benjamin when it was suggested he is running so well it might be a good idea to take in another couple of races.

American Washington, a former world indoor champion, lunged at the finishing line to earn a winner's purse of £16,312 when it looked likely Benjamin would pick up a first major global title. With four men, including Olympic and world champion Jeremy Wariner in contention for the top prize, Washington set a season’s best to beat Benjamin by 0.05 seconds in their knife-edge encounter.

The pair and Chris Brown were virtually inseparable until Washington clinched it as Benjamin, who clocked 44.56secs, (a Belgrave record) held off the Bahamian by 0.08secs.

Wariner would have been with them until knocked out of his stride pattern when he and Benjamin, in the lane outside him, had a contact of hands halfway up the final straight.

Benjamin lowering his personal best for the fifth time in the last two months said: “I thought I had it won and it cost me 10K - it was so close. Judging by that I can run 0.25secs quicker,” which would put him within sight of fellow Welshman Iwan Thomas’ British record.

Benjamin feels he can match the European gold medal Thomas won seven years ago when Gothenburg hosts the championship next season. He said: “Now I am running so fast, I just hope I can continue to do so next season.”

Paul Freary's jinx strikes again!

Having recovered from the trapped nerve in his neck - after months of agony and no training - Paul Freary gradually got into reasonable shape only for a calf muscle to twang in his first race back. Gradually he got over that problem and became fit enough to take part in The Great Russian Run - Vladivostok to St. Petersburg.

The Russian run went really well with his section of 86 miles being covered in six days. So it was back to the UK to spend the next three weeks getting ready for the SEAA 6-Stage. But that jinx stepped in again. On Sunday 4 September he was about to run in the Rufford 10km but while leaning through the sliding door of his van, just before the race, he was hit by a passing car.

Paul went to Chorley Hospital where it was decided that nothing was broken but he ended up with some almighty bruising from his hips to his heels, with tyre marks down the backs of his legs.

We think we can safely say that he was actually very lucky that it wasn’t more serious. Anyway, his last three weeks before the SEAA race began with a spell of inactivity while he recovered. Paul does still intend to race at Rushmoor Arena so we wish him a speedy recovery.

Report · page 6

Double gold for Harry in World Youth Games

Harry Aikines-Aryeetey – the Sutton & District AC man who also races for Belgrave Harriers – became the first sprinter in the six-year history of the IAAF/Maroc Telecom World Youth Championships ever to win both the 100 and 200 metres gold medals.

The 16-year-old, having won the shorter race title on Thursday 14 July, powered to another magnificent victory over 200m in his final on Sunday night.

Aikines-Aryeetey turned the tables on Cuba’s Jorge Valcarcel, who was given the verdict in their semi-final although both had finished with the same time.

In the final, the Sutton/Belgrave sprinter showed he was the better man, controlling the race from start to finish and winning in a world leading time for the year of 20.91seconds.

His pace saw him easily overthrow the challenge of Valcarcel and Matteo Galvan of Italy, although both rivals ran personal best times of 21.08sec and 21.14sec.

“I just can’t believe it,” said Aikines-Aryeetey who after raising his hand with a number one finger salute, spent a couple of minutes flat-out on his back after his outstanding performance.

“I just can't believe it, I was lying on my back on the floor before it really dawned I was world champion yet again.”

The win saw him become the second British champion over the distance, following in the footsteps of 1999 winner Tim Benjamin who is now carving out an international career over 400 metres.

“I've no idea,” said Aikines-Aryeetey when asked where he found the energy to run the fastest half lap of his life after six demanding races in five days.

He added: “Yesterday I was a bit tired but felt a lot better today. I ran a quick bend and I didn't really see anyone.

“My coach had told me hit the first 120 hard which I did. Then I saw the the Cuban alongside and just drove even harder.

“It’s just amazing. I had so much lactic, I knew I was going to do something special and I just can’t believe it that I ran 20.91 seconds.”

Aikines-Aryeetey, who beat 21 seconds for the first time in his career, has been so focused on his preparations he was unaware that no-one had ever before achieved the demanding sprint double.

“I suppose I’ve made history,” said last year’s Commonwealth Youth Games runner-up. “But yes, I’ve got to be happy with two medals – who’d have thought it?”

From The Belgravian – 25 Years Ago – 1980

Carl Lawton took over from Bill Merryman as President of Belgrave Harriers … Bill Laws edited The Belgravian while Alan Mead was Track & Field Secretary. The club magazine cost around £300 per issue to produce in those days (it was to rise to nearly ten times that by the time it had became a glossy A4 publication – but in it’s current downloadable format it is virtually free).

In August Tom Carter celebrated 50 years as a Belgravian. The magazine reported, “ There are few men who have obtained international honours and given a lifetime to encouraging the endeavours of others.” A surprise party was held at the Hall and pride of place was given to a fine cake decorated with a faithful replica of the Belgrave’s badge.

Derek Jones completed his first year as Secretary of the Women’s Section – the team placed 5th in Division III (South) of the Southern League.

Ernie Obeng (now an employee of the IAAF in Monaco) kept the name of Belgrave Harriers on sports commentator’s lips as he reeled off a succession of 100 metre sprint wins at stadiums all over the world. Although denied the chance of competing at Moscow (in the Olympic Games) due to organisational problems in his home country of Ghana, he must have been considered a potential medallist over the short sprint. For not only did he beat Cuba’s Silvio Leonard, subsequently the silver medallist, just a few weeks before the Games, he also defeated Allan Wells, the Olympic Champion, shortly after Moscow. His best time of 10.21 was a club record.

Junior John Gladwin topped the club rankings at 800 metres (1:48.7) and 1500m (3:52.9) and fellow under 20 Stuart Paton’s name was found at the top of the 3000 metres list with 8:34.0.

Belgrave placed 15th in the South of England 12-Stage Road Relay held at Wimbledon in conjunction with The Belgrave Haig Relay. Highlight of the race was the devastating run by Steve Ovett who took Brighton & Hove from 7th to 1st and smashed the short stage record.
