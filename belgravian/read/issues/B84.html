The Hon. Secretary's Commentary.

As we go to press comes wonderful news of the winning of the English Cross-country Championship at Manchester. A week earlier our walkers had won the R.W.A. Junior Championship, also at Manchester; and we congratulate both our runners and walkers upon their Championship successes in the North. Justice will be done to these races in our next issue. Meanwhile, well done!

* * * * *

Heartiest congratulations to Ted Stimpson and Arthur Penny upon being selected for the English Cross-country team to participate in the International event at Cardiff. Our best wishes go with them

* * * * *

Since our Annual General Meeting we have elected many new members. To R. G. Griffin, R. E. Jeal, H. N. Little, K. J. Winton, H. J. Dickinson, C. H. Dennis, W. M. Butt, J. F. Appleby, B. C. Leeden, J. F. Harding, C. H. Lloyd, A. H. Lomas, J. R. Gibb, J. McDonald, W. D. Buckley, W. E. Budden, P. W. Edwards, A. W. Edwards, L. D. Harris, C. G. Herridge, S. D. Richards, R. E. Duffett, H. B. Halls, K. Moe, J. S. Rowell and A. P. Stevenson we extend a very cordial welcome, and trust they will be with us for years to come. Some of the foregoing have already shown they have ability, and good fellowship, and by now are well established Belgrave Harriers. We would single out for a special welcome, Knut Moe, a Norwegian visitor, who will be with us only for a few months.

* * * * *

Congratulations to Harry Churcher upon continuing his amazing sequence of successes in Open Walks. Influenza prevented him from competing in the Valentine's Open Walk, or undoubtedly Harry would have scored another win and completed two full seasons without defeat in Open road walks.

* * * * *

Our spirited attempt to regain the "Southern" Cross-country title did not succeed but we had the satisfaction of taking third place and finishing within nine points of the winners, despite the fact that we were not at full strength. Tom Carter and Ted Stimpson gave a splendid display of running and should appear in England's team later in the year. A word of praise also to our Captain, Bert Footer, who travelled to the race as a reserve, but when circumstances forced him to turn out, called upon his store of experience and determination with such good effect that he was our fourth man to score, in 25th position

* * * * *

Our walking teams have managed to win scratch team race after race, and we hope to have similar success in the Championship events. Our all-round strength during the last two seasons must be without parallel, and if present enthusiasm and ability continue, we should have many further years of success in store.

* * * * *

We have a new Field Events Captain in Charlie Jones, who will receive capable assistance from L. Jackson, W. Sutton, H. M. Quackenbos, R. E. Jeal, S. Jones and others. With a new season about to begin, it is hoped that all members will co-operate with T. C. Jones and help him to build up a section from which champions and A.A.A. standard men will flow, and that they will continue to represent the Club with distinction and in keeping with the reputation the Club has earned in other branches of our sport.

* * * * *

A new Track Captain in Frank Webb will also require the help and consideration of all members. At the present moment our Summer programme is being planned, and it is likely to be a very heavy one. All track men are asked to support the Club handicaps. New members are invited, and requested to make themselves known to the Club officials and their fellow-members. Any member invited to represent the Club and unable to do so MUST notify the Captain in good time. It is a duty he owes himself as well as the Club.

These points should be borne in mind by every Club-member. If every member resolves that he will not fail in these respects, we may expect the progress that the efforts of the Committee and the Club's hard workers deserve.

* * * * *

We were pleased that the Club's nomination to the Southern Committee of the A.A.A.—Mr. F. G. Thomson—was again duly elected, finishing seventh in the list of twenty successful candidates on this occasion. We are sure Mr. Thomson's efforts are appreciated at Crown Court Chambers, as is his work on behalf of the Club.

* * * * *

The Club is represented on other governing Athletic bodies as follows :— Road Walking Assn.—F. G. Thomson (Past President), A. A. Harley (Committee). Southern Counties C.C. Assn.—H. Parker (Committee). South of the Thames C.C. Assn.—W. H. Hare (Past President), H. Parker (Past President), E. A. Duffett (Committee). Surrey County A.A.A.—F. G. Thomson (Vice-President), H. Parker (Committee), J. G. Coleman (Committee). Middlesex County A.A.A. (as members of Cadogan A.C.)—A. A. Harley (Vice-President and Vice-Chairman), F. R. Webb (Committee).

* * * * *

The Club Track programme is rapidly taking shape. The dates allocated to us by the L.C.C. appear elsewhere.

The Inter-Club relays v. Epsom H., Mitcham A.C. and Croydon H. will take place on May 10th, and the "Doris Shield" Competition v. Ashcombe St. George's A.C. and Thames Valley H. on June 7th. The Club 3 miles Track championship will probably take place on July 31st.

We have a very full programme of inter-club fixtures to fulfil on other grounds, and a series of Division II Southern Amateur Athletic League track races and Division I Field Events in which to participate. Additionally, we shall be represented in all the principal track competitions, such as the "Reading Gazette Trophy" competition, the "Waddilove Bowl" Competition at Birmingham, the "Kinnaird Trophy" Competition, and many other events of importance.

It is hoped to issue the Club Summer fixture list towards the end of April.

Members are particularly requested to note the date of the Ponders End Fête and Gala—July 15th—as at this meeting last year we won two trophies—for the Mile Relay and Three Miles Team Race—and we shall try and defend these with our strongest teams against all comers.

* * * * *

A suggestion has been made by Surrey Walking Club that we should meet them at cricket on one evening during the Summer. The Hon. Secretary would be glad to hear from any members who are interested.

* * * * *

The National Fitness Organiser of the Amateur Athletic Association, Mr. C. F. R. Hilton, has arranged for an athletic camping site to be open in Devon from July 3rd until the end of August. The site is situated 8 miles from Exeter and 5 miles from Dawlish and transport will be provided free from these towns to the Camp. The charge, including board and accommodation, will not exceed £1 per head per week. There will be a staff in attendance to do all cooking, etc., but those attending will be expected to perform some duties in connection with the maintenance of the Camp. A qualified coaching staff will be in attendance. Members interested should get in touch with the Hon. Secretary.

Column · page 6

This And That.

The Clerk of the Weather has not been any too kind towards our fixture list. By giving us a White Christmas the following races had to be cancelled : Match v. Ealing H. and Metropolitan Police, the Boxing Day Handicaps, and the Croydon-Godstone and back Walk. The latter, as I understand, will be held later on. As regards the match on Christmas Eve, some of the Ealing and Police runners enjoyed a social run with our own lads; the only regret being that they had to soil the virgin snow with their footprints. I'm sure that they will always remember this run whenever they hear that well-known verse of "Good King Wenceslas."

* * * * *

To the cries of "You ought to go back to running, your Hon. Editor was challenged to place on record the fact that he was "pulled" in the Club "Junior" Championship. Having also been guilty of walking in a running race (Poly Marathon) my perversity would appear to be complete. I take comfort, however, in the knowledge that I must be "pulled" another five times before I can even think of calling myself a walker.

* * * * *

The appearance in the local press (re our Dance on November 26th), of a question asking whether we needed exercise, might be interpreted to indicate the inadequacy of our training, or that we had taken a leaf from the book of the local "Keep Fit Campaign." To this comment on our activities might be added the fact that many members (don't ask me why !) walked many miles home in the small hours of the morning. At a slightly larger hour they met again. What for? Oh, just for a training stroll!

Harry Churcher gained further honours in winning the London Business Houses Road Walking Championship (7 miles). Eddie also gained distinction in this race by being the first man home from the West End Business Houses, thereby earning the crown which once belonged to Percy Wright.

* * * * *

I have recently had the pleasure of reading the official organs of other Clubs. One was from one of the oldest, "The L.A.C. News." In the three numbers which I perused were detailed reports of their activities, interspersed with articles by such well-known athletes as F. A. R. Hunter, J. E. Lovelock, B. F. McCabe, J. V. Powell and P. D. Ward; whilst there was an article by D. H. A. Christie-Murray on walking.

The other was from one of the younger Clubs, "The Southgate Harrier." This gazette only came into existence last year, but it has already attained an excellent standard, and a high degree of enthusiasm supplies the undercurrent to the reports of the sterling performances by their members in matches and club races. I am sure that all readers of the "Belgravian" will wish for its continued success.

Both these magazines make very good reading, and I am sure that their readers look forward to each issue.

* * * * *

In the Inter-Counties Cross Country Championship, held at Harpenden (Herts.) on January 21st, C. T. Carter and A. W. Penny scored in the winning Surrey team. D. Patience and H. W. Shields helped Middlesex to be runners-up, whilst E. S. Stimpson also appeared in the Middlesex team, but was not in the scoring.

* * * * *

The new date for the Surrey W.C. Croydon-Godstone and back walk, postponed from Boxing Day, is April 22nd. This, unfortunately, coincides with our own twenty miles road-walking championship, so that Belgrave vests will be absent from the Eastbourne road on that day.

* * * * *

One can quite truthfully say of Harry Churcher that "he takes the cake," for he was awarded a cake by the Editor of the J. Lyons & Co. Sports Sheet. This prize is awarded to the man (or woman) who has the most meritorious sporting performance of the week to their credit. Harry's award was in respect of his win in the Ashcombe-St. George's open race. That a first-class walking performance was thus recognised will be a source of gratification to all interested in athletics; as only one award is made each week, and all sports and games are taken into consideration before the recipient is selected.

7 Miles C.C. Championship.

By H. W. Shields.

10th December, 1938.

The long-sought cure for the alleged slow starts of our teams in championships can be said to have been found.

Whether it was design or accident has not yet been discovered, but the struggle of Belgrave's manhood for supemacy in the race to a camera some 200 yards down the course could only be described as on a par with the Gold Rush.

The office of photographer to the teams should carefully be looked into!

Settling down to serious work after this very fast start, Pat Parker forged ahead with Tom Carter, Dan Patience and several others in close attendance. This order was maintained until the hills were reached, when Tom Carter went strongly to the fore, and at the end of the first lap had established a useful lead, with Dan Patience, Charlie Smart and Pat Parker following.

This order was maintained to the finish, with the exception of Arthur Penny (who must have done fastest time in the second lap) who beat Parker for 4th place.

Congratulations to J. R. Shotter, who closely won the handicap held in conjunction with the championship, from L. Penny. Both of these, with careful training, should prove very useful acquisitions to our future championship teams. Micky Walker obtained 3rd place in the handicap and will, no doubt, get much encouragement from his fine run.

Several of our younger members gave sterling accounts of themselves : Beard, Rispin, Shrubb and others, who will prove their worth in later races.

Junior Match v. Blackheath and Old Croydonians H.

By E. A. Duffett.

On Saturday, December 31st, we had the pleasure of entertaining the Junior members of the Blackheath H. and Old Croydonians H. It was the first time we had met these clubs in a cross-country match and I hope they enjoyed their visit to Belgrave Hall.

A field of just under 30 runners faced our President, who acted as starter, but owing to the Old Croydonians being rather short of men, it was decided to score 5 aside.

After the usual fast start our boys soon forged to the front, and when they came to the Kingsmere and turned towards the Windmill and home, it was seen that E. H. Herbert was leading, with W. E. Beard next, followed by B. Rowe, who is a newcomer to our ranks.

Leading a small bunch of black vests was M. W. Jenkins, of Blackheath, and he displayed fine judgment in moving up to his opponents, so that by the time the runners had passed the Windmill, Herbert only led him by a mere 10 yards. Beard and Rowe were next, but our other men seemed rather a long way back.

Jenkins soon had the measure of our lad and eventually ran home an easy winner in quite good time for this course.

Herbert was a good second with Beard close behind, and then came Rowe, who had quite surprised us by his form in his first club race.

As, however, both the Blackheath H. and Old Croydonians wear black vests, what had seemed at first a most formidable bunch of opponents just behind our boys, turned out to consist of three "Heathens" and two "Old Boys."

Thus scoring five aside, we came out narrow winners by the margin of 6 points from Blackheath.

We sincerely hope that next season we shall renew our sporting rivalry over the country with our opponents on this occasion.

Report · page 11

Surrey County Senior Championship.

By T. C. Jones.

The Surrey County Championship was run over the Ranelagh Harriers' course at Petersham this year—a slightly hilly course.

Our team was not so strong as in former years, owing to the absence of Footer and Cohen, but we had hopes of at least repeating our performance of last year in finishing second to Mitcham.

The draw for positions was not too favourable to us and our boys did not get away too well, Kemp being the only one in the first dozen after about a mile.

When the runners were next seen, at about half distance, Carter was in the first three, together with Brown (Mitcham) and Hines (Surrey), with Penny (7th), Kemp (25th), Lucas (27th), Herbert (28th) and Mick Walker (30th) completing our scoring six. Mitcham seemed to be leading Surrey by a few points with ourselves third, and it looked as if we would have to put in a lot of running in the second lap to gain the points required to put us close up to Mitcham and Surrey.

About a quarter of a mile from the finish Brown (Mitcham) led, followed by Close (Surrey) about 40 yards behind, who was closely followed by Carter, and so the first three stayed until the finish. A quick check-up of the scores showed that although we had gained a number of points in the last lap, both Mitcham and Surrey had gained points and kept well in front of us. Mitcham eventually ran out winners, to add to their success of last year, but only by a few points from Surrey, with ourselves filling third place.

Congratulations to Tom Carter on his excellent showing and to all the rest of the team, and further congratulations to Carter and Penny on being selected to represent the County in the Inter Counties Race.

The Middlesex Cross Country Championship.

In this event our members who possess qualifications for this County ran for Cadogan A.C. Although they were without the assistance of J. C. Smart, A. E. Taylor and H. G. Verney, they scored a convincing win over Thames Valley Harriers and Polytechnic Harriers, who were much fancied by the newspaper prophets to win easily.

Ted Stimpson gave a first-class performance to finish second individual. The rest of our team packed very well—D. Patience was 4th, H. W. Shields 7th, A. Allum 14th, R. Tobin 15th and F. R. Webb 17th. The next Cadogan man home was A. Smith, who gave one of the best performances of his career to finish 58th. Although just missing inclusion in the scoring six, Bert can claim satisfaction in the knowledge that had his performance been required for the team, they would still have won. Another feature of our win was the fine running, on a very heavy course, of two veterans, A. Allum and F. R. Webb. J. Daly was Cadogan's only other representative in this race and, no doubt, he was as pleased with carrying the colours of a winning team as though he were one of the scorers! He finished 96th. The positions given of our scoring men are their scoring positions, and not their actual positions.

The Surrey Junior Cross-Country Championship.

Our Juniors scored a most convincing win in this race, which was held from our own headquarters. They had the advantage of knowing the country, but the margin of their win outweighs any suggestion of "Horses for Courses."

With the exception of F. S. Bradnum, who unfortunately had to drop out, they showed good packing and team work, as can be seen by glancing at their finishing positions. It was hard luck indeed that Bradnum was unable to give his usual sound performance. The fact that they excelled themselves in a Championship race shows that they have been training wisely.

The day was rather muggy, and a large crowd of young runners started out on their tour of Wimbledon Common. Our lads soon found themselves in useful positions and our hopes were fairly bright when they left for the open country.

E. H. Herbert led our lads home, finishing in fourth position, thus confirming the good form which he has been showing. Lionel Penny proved his consistency by finishing eighth, one place ahead of another promising runner, W. E. Beard. Len Marchant, 15th, completed the scoring four. To these four lads we give our heartiest congratulations on their well-earned honours. Our non-scoring men, R. Boyden 18th, J. R. Shotter 23rd, N. C. Shrubb 24th, have every reason to be pleased with themselves; for having the non-scoring men well placed adds relish to our win. R. V. Reynolds ran as an individual and finished 36th.

Our Juniors can well be proud of themselves for doing their bit in upholding the Club's prestige. The Club, in turn, is proud of them and hopes to see them graduate to further honours.

"A Fight to the Death" or "Through Mud to Glory" or "The 10 Mile Championship."

By Ubiquitor.

On Saturday, 28th January, the Club 10 Miles Championship resulted in Arthur Penny retaining his title after one of the best struggles that we have seen in a race famous for its keen struggles. The field was almost the smallest for several years, there being notable absentees, including Bert Footer.

As usual, they started over the circuit of the field at a cracking pace, all in a bunch with Ted Stimpson, Penny and Harry Shields leading, but by the time they were back at the start (1 mile) there were three distinct packs with six in the fast, eight in the medium and I lost count of the slow. But half a mile later across the golf links they had to "paddle" a small lake (to the amusement of the spectators, but not the runners) and here Arthur Penny, with a determined expression, moved away from the others with Tom Carter hard on his heels. These two set a fast pace through the woods, and up the hockey field they had a lead of 25 yards from Stimpson, Shields and Dan. The sixth of the original fast pack, Jerman, was back another 50 yards with Tobin, Arthur Allum, and Murdock, on his heels. But the rest were already well spread out, breathing hard, some looking distressed, and it was here that I saw Jimmy Kemp collecting rude answers by standing on one side and telling everyone: "Only another 7½ miles!"

Up to the pond Penny eased off a bit and seemed to be having a bad time and Carter took up the running, but he was unable to get away, and at half distance he led by a yard from Penny, with Stimpson 100 yards back, Dan another 50, and Harry tagging on behind. There was a gap of 150 yards before Allum came along just ahead of Jerman, Mick Walker and Alf Shaw, with Murdock and Tobin just behind. At this point I was pleased to notice the fine style and high position of "Champ" Smith.

The waterlogged course was taking toll, and several dropped out here (ya yeller!), but Tom and Arthur went on relentlessly fighting each other across the golf course and its lake (very funny!) and they went down through the "forest" at a terrible pace, Tom trying desperately to get a lead. The South of Thames Senior, Royal Military College, Sandhurst.

By "Ubiquitor."

10th February, 1939.

R. I. P.

When I first saw our team I thought that it looked quite promising, but when I found that Jerman had hurt his eyes (fortunately only temporarily) and could not run, that Kemp had a gammy hip, that Tommy Morrell was convalescent and that E. Herbert was not fit, I did not think it quite so good! Bels. were well drawn for once and Kemp made a clean start and the large field went away over the first lap round the very charming College Field (1 mile), and as they went by we had quite a few lying well up: Murdock 12, Tobin 20, Kemp 26, and E. Herbert 30, with brother Herbert not far away.

Three-quarters of a mile later, when the runners disappeared into the wood, the position had not greatly changed, for Murdock was 13th, Tobin 18th, Kemp 26th, and although E. Herbert had dropped to 65th, Stride was running really well and had come up to 40th. After Herbert there was quite a patch of claret and gold. Johnson and L. Herbert were together 74 and 75, and another group of Messrs. Boyden, Smith and Rispin 84—86, while Mitchell at 112 was just two places ahead of Tommy Morrell.

The course on the big lap was hilly, rather sandy, and loose in places, but quite dry, and the leaders began to go away from the rest of the field. I am afraid that some of our leaders lost a few places here, and half-way round the lap Kemp was our first man in 22nd, Tobin 23, Murdock 43, and then the rest of the team all within striking distance of one another. It was here that there was a steepish hill (ask the runners!) which I believe caught Kemp's hip rather badly, for he began to lose ground, but Murdock passed both Tobin and Kemp and made up several places.

At the end of the first big lap the positions were: Murdock 19, Tobin 24, Kemp 32, Stride 46, E. (Young) Herbert going steadily at 71st, then a little pack having a friendly fight, Johnson 80, L. Herbert 81, Boyden 85, Champ Smith 86, and Rispin 87, with Mitchell still just ahead of Morrell a little further back.

There was little alteration in positions over the second lap in the country, except for the steady dropping of Kemp, who was caught and passed by Stride at the top of the hill, but I did notice that the junior members of the team were running very steadily, and show promise of doing well, with a little more time and experience. Coming back on to the sports field, with only three-quarters of a mile to go, Murdock was 18th, Tobin 19th, Stride had run through to 37th, but looked rather tired, while Kemp was back at 54 and the brothers Herbert, L. and E., were 72 and 75. Our friendly pack all seemed to have decided that they had run together far enough, but as they all tried to get away from one another at the same time they were not very successful. Moral: start earlier next time!

We were not in danger of being one of the first three teams, which was fortunate, as we lost several useful places over that last lap, as can be seen from the positions below. We did not even get the "12" shield for the first club to finish twelve men, usually a strong point with us, so it was perhaps a little disappointing, but I am sure that had we had a complete and fit team we should have had much more to sing about.

The Belgrave Open Walk.

26th November, 1938.

By knocking 43 seconds off the course record (held by V. W. G. Stone, Poly), Harry Churcher treated a large crowd of onlookers to an excellent display of fast walking and his fellow-competitors to a clean pair of heels.

At a signal from our President, 176 walkers sped up Lauriston Road. The judges soon got to work, for Colin Sutton was disqualified when he had only covered about a quarter of a mile. After a small lap of the Common, Harry was already in front, and Belgrave vests were already prominent. At the half-way stage it became evident that, barring accidents, Belgrave were due for a lion's share of the prizes; for our men who were prominent at the start maintained their positions.

Harry eventually won from E. A. Staker (Highgate H.) by the comfortable margin of 1 min. 30 secs. Joe Coleman gave us his usual sound performance to fill third place, whilst Percy Wright walked hard all the way to finish fifth. The performances of the rest of our lads can best be shown by looking at the team results.

In the scratch team results a preliminary word of explanation is necessary. Our A and C teams (captain's and vice-captain's) were chosen from our best men, and, on paper, there appeared little to choose between them. The chances of the A team were weakened by the disqualifications of Sutton and Treacher. This caused them to have to go into the forties for their fourth man—Stan Goold, who, by the way, is performing well this season. Thus handicapped, they were beaten by the C team, but they were worthy runners-up. To finish two scratch teams first and second in an open race (22 teams taking part) and having accomplished this with two men ruled out, speaks volumes for our present strength. Our B team were only a few points behind the A team, and occupied fifth posiition; that is, only two other clubs could beat our third team.

Looking at the handicap section, it was no great surprise to find that our B team had returned the lowest aggregate time. The individual handicap prize went to the Highgate Centurion, A. D. McSweeney.

Charlie Churcher won the special prize awarded to the first Belgrave Harrier not gaining any other award. The special prize for Police competitors was won by the man who won it last year—R. C. Aylen.

This account would be incomplete without recording our most sincere thanks to the following: Messrs. T. Askew, C. E. Churcher, F. H. Elson and E. The Valentine's S.C. Open Ten Miles Walk.

This race was held on February 18th, in the Goodmayes neighbourhood, and attracted a large entry, including 22 Belgravians. This is the only ten miles open handicap of the season, and we had high hopes of maintaining our sequence of scratch team race wins in open events.

The news that Harry Churcher, hero of a dozen or more successive Open race wins, was prevented from turning out owing to a bout of influenza was disappointing, and the fact that Percy Wright was recovering from similar trouble, seemed to make scratch team prospects more open than has been the case in recent races. Both Metropolitan Walking Club and Surrey Walking Club seemed to be at full strength and we anticipated a keen struggle with both these old-standing rivals.

The brothers Coleman and E. A. Staker led the field at the start, until Len found the pace a little too warm and dropped back. Mile after mile Joe Coleman and Highgate's E. A. Staker kept shoulder to shoulder in the lead, until about six miles or more, when D. Christie Murray, of Surrey W.C., came through the field with a determined and extraordinarily well-judged turn of speed. Staker attempted to go after him, but later had the mortification of slipping back to eighth position. Meanwhile, Harold Whitlock had been gaining places and eventually beat our Joe by a small margin.

An attempt to total the team scores at six miles (approx.) showed that we were scoring 35 points to the 36 of the Metropolitan W.C.! Whitlock, of the Met., appeared likely to gain places and it will be realized that excitement reigned. Our boys were apprised of the facts and immediately responded. Joe Coleman was determined to finish well up. Colin Sutton set about gaining places, as did also our Captain, Charlie Churcher. Len Coleman, fourth "A" team man, also moved up, and Eddie Heather, of the "B" team, and Bert Clayton, fifth and non-scoring "A" team man, rendered yeoman service. They were told to overtake the Metropolitan men and right well they set about their task.

The position improved and at eight miles approximately we were leading with 30 points to the 42 of Metropolitan W.C., whilst Surrey Walking Club were also improving their position. Still, our walkers gained places, and their determination was grand to see, and a tribute to their loyalty to their Club and team-mates. At the finish we were comparatively easy winners, and whilst the efforts of our scoring four were splendidly maintained and worthy of the highest praise, they will be eager to join in the tribute to Clayton and Heather. Unable to score in the team themselves, these two did marvellous work in pushing the other clubs back; and displayed team spirit at its very highest level. May both receive the reward merited in the "Junior" Championship, which follows a fortnight later!

While our six aforementioned men were finishing in the first twelve places, our other walkers were also striving hard and with some effect. Messrs. Roberts, Doubleday, Clark and Heather, of the "B" team, walked well and comprised the second handicap team. Eddie Heather's forward walking, his best to date, earned third individual handicap prize.

All things considered, we can look back with a deal of pleasure on this event. We regretted the absence of Harry Churcher from our team, but it was pleasing to find that we had others in plenty to give of their best and make up for his absence.

With The Walkers.

So we have won the R.W.A. "Junior" Championship once again. A full account must follow in our next issue, but we heartily congratulate the team responsible for our seventh win in this series of races. No other club can approach this record of success, the next best being the four wins of Surrey Walking Club.

As we won last year's race (with a record score) and this team was no longer eligible, we were set the task of finding four new scoring men. This we succeeded in doing, and two successive wins is of the greatest credit to all concerned.

Next year we shall be out to put up a "best-ever" performance and score another win. It must be admitted that this presents many difficulties We have some very fine young walkers coming along but they will probably not be sufficiently matured in time for next March. So we shall, in all probability, rely upon this year's candidates for places in our team, plus the new members that we hope will be forthcoming during the next few months.

We trust that all members will keep a keen look-out for new talent, and we can assure all who join the ranks of the walkers a cordial welcome, good times, and a guaranteed measure of success if they are prepared to persevere.

* * * * *

Now for the "Senior," to be held on May 13th from the Police quarters at Hendon. As the title-holders, we shall use all our endeavours to hold on to the Championship. It would be a wonderful feat to win once again all three "R.W.A." titles in one season, and one which is within our capabilities.

* * * * *

We hope to have all last year's men again representing us, supplemented by some of those who have now left the "Junior" ranks behind for ever.

The Club Championship at 20 miles, with sealed handicap in conjunction, will be held at Wimbledon on April 22nd. The Metropolitan Police W.C. and Surrey A.C. will also be visitors. It is unfortunate that the postponed "Godstone" Walk is to be held on the same day, but our S.W.C. friends realise that we have to carry through with our arrangements.

* * * * *

The following Saturday we expect to travel eastwards for the Colchester to Ipswich Walk, won by Colin Sutton last year.

* * * * *

The R.W.A. 50 kilos Championship is to be held from De Bohun's Farm, Southgate, on July 22nd. This is another title to be defended and we have every confidence in our men.

With the Olympic Games to follow in 1940, we shall be looking to this Championship event to give us some guide as to England's representatives the following year.

* * * * *

Our Inter-club scratch Walks will be held at Battersea Park Track on Wednesday, May 31st, when we expect to receive the usual very fine support of clubs in the Metropolitan area.

* * * * *

Ten Years Back.

The Championship season of 1929 provided us with a great success. It was certainly a proud moment for W. H. Hare, the first Belgravian to be President of the South of the Thames Association; and all other Club members present, when the trophies were awarded. Our winning team consisted of W. S. Mitchell (4), A. W. Penny (7), A. Allum (13), W. G. Webb (16), P. Khlopin (23), A. Robertshaw (26), giving us a total of 89 points. Jones (87), W. L. Bayliss (116), and S. Drake (144).

Helsingfors, 1940.

The next Olympiad is due to be held in Finland next year, and those athletes who entertain hopes of inclusion in the British team are already planning their training.

What are Belgrave's chances of having some of its members in the team?

On current form, and with no unlucky breaks, we have many potentialities, and this brief analysis of our own members is intended to give a few ideas as to who may be honoured with selection for the team.

In the sprints, S. C. Botwright's name stands out; he also does a very good "quarter." Our other sprinters, judging by last season's results, are on the up-grade, and if some can clip off a few tenths of a second, they will be attracting the selectors' eyes.

H. E. Pack has already gained representative honours in the 440 yards, although his times are apt to be dimmed by the performances of A. G. K. Brown and W. Roberts. Our man, however, has beaten 50 seconds on several occasions, recording times which would have won an A.A.A. championship in many years, and if only Eddie could find a sustained finishing effort I am sure we have a world-beater in our ranks.

In the 880 yards, A. A. Bird is already inside two minutes, and with a little more age and experience will be doing better.

Another man to watch over these distances is S. Jarlett, who last year showed great improvement.

We must not, of course, forget T. H. Scrimshaw, who in his day was one of the leading half-milers in the country. Whether Tommy can recover his old form remains to be seen, but I am sure we all wish him the very best of luck.

In the 1500 metres, L. W. Herbert and W. E. Lucas are our best, and although neither has yet beaten 4.20 for the Mile, they cannot be overlooked. Lucas gained inclusion in last year's A.A.A. Mile final, while Herbert also gained an A.A.A. standard by beating 4.25. Both of these runners are yet young, and we look forward to still better performances from them both during the coming track season.

We have to turn to our cross country men for the 5,000 and 10,000 metres (3 and 6 miles roughly). For the shorter race, C. T. Carter, J. Parker, J. C. Smart and L. J. Cohen can all give good acccounts of themselves, and if only Pat would get down to some serious training, I am sure he could make the team.

For the longer race A. W. Penny, an ex-Empire Games 6 miles champion, is still a strong candidate, and although just failing to make the team at the last Olympic Games, we wish him better luck for 1940.

D. Patience, S. T. Ring and E. S. Stimpson are three more who do well at these distances, and the latter, an ex-Southern Junior C.C. Champion, is particularly promising and should have a good season.

Both having well beaten three hours, A. F. Mitchell and F. A. Walker are well in the running for honours in that classic race—the Marathon; whilst if H. E. Footer should train for the distance—as is probable—he might well win this event for Gt. Britain.

For the Steeplechase (3,000 metres) we naturally think of J. Ginty, who was Belgrave's only representative in 1936, but whether Jim's duties will permit of him giving time to the necessary training is rather doubtful. Two others who shine at this branch of athletics are C. T. Carter and A. R. Shaw, and both have good performances to their credit.

As regards the Hurdle races, it must be confessed that our chances are extremely remote, the ordinary clubs seem to be rather overshadowed by the Universities and Services in these events, due in the main to lack of training facilities.

At the last Olympiad, Great Britain sent very few Field Events competitors, as the performances by Britishers are lamentably below the standards of most other countries. Whether they adopt this procedure again remains to be seen, although we are glad to see that the ruling body have taken steps to encourage this side of athletics among the clubs. Naturally our own club performers are below International standards, but I think that our men deserve attention. That our Field Events section is one of the strongest is evidenced by our performances in the S.A.A.L. Field Events League.

In the Shot and Discus, S. Jones, W. H. J. Sutton and L. J. Worrad form a powerful trio who gain us many points in Club matches.

In the Javelin, E. H. Finneron, C. A. Melchior, G. H. Powell and Ron Jeal are a useful quartet, whilst our Club champion, H. M. Quackenbos, with more strength and experience, will throw further.

For the High and Pole Jumps, our Northern member, J. Dodd, who won the A.A.A. Pole Vault title in 1937, would already have gained International honours but for the fact that he is barred by tht International rules.

F. J. Skelley is certainly a bright hope for the Long Jump, he has already cleared 22ft. 8in. and will undoubtedly profit by maturer years.

Our Field Events men have been training under F. Stampfl during the Winter, and their performance next Summer will be watched with hope and interest by all.

Coming to the Walkers, it is noted that a 10,000 metres walk is planned. For this we have many members who give good accounts of themselves over seven miles. No doubt there are some names which may catch the selectors' eyes which have not been mentioned.

These are the members who, by their efforts and enthusiasm will better their past performances. Who are they to be?

Who's Best ?

H. M. Quackenbos

Some interesting results emerge from a study of the Scoring Table for Track and Field Events as adopted by the I.A.A.F. Congress at Stockholm in 1934. This table, in addition to giving a means of scoring for Decathlon competitions, satisfies the curiosity of those who would like to know, for example, if a hundred in "evens" is better than a quarter in 49 seconds. Looking up the former, we find that it scores 872 points whilst the latter obtains 954 points, so that the quarter is better. A scale of points is given for all metric running distances, except the steeplechase and marathon; and for all the Field Events, including the standing jumps. The points range from 1360 down to 1 by intervals of one point. Opposite each number the corresponding performances, in the different events, are listed. The compilers have catered for everyone, from the superman of many years hence, who will run 800m. in 1 min. 41.5 sec., to score 1360 points, to the champion of the kindergarten, who toddles round the metric half mile in 3 min. 17.6 sec., thus getting one point.

Arranging the world's records in order of merit, we find that the Field Events occupy most of the leading positions with Jack Torrance's shot putt of 57ft. 1in. topping the list and scoring 1202 points. If it has been adopted as a world's record, Owen's 20.3 sec. for the 200 metres is the best pure running performance. The middle and long distance records are in the lower half of the list. Wooderson's 800 metres record is worth 1109 points as against 1088 due to Lovelock's 1500 metres in Berlin. Rather surprisingly the "worst" record is the 10,000 metres recently put up by Maki, which scores 1075 points.

Coming nearer home, to A.A.A. standards, we discover, as expected, that the running events score consistently higher points (about 840 on the average) than the Field Events, which average 670 points. The influence of cross-country running is shewn by the fact that the most difficult standard to obtain is the three miles (14m. 50s.) which, converted to 5,000 metres, scores 884 points compared with the easiest, the discus (120ft.), which obtains 613 points. In fact, excepting the high and long jumps, the man who wins an A.A.A. standard in a Field Event does not put up quite so good a performance as he who attains Middlesex county standard in any running event.

It must be realized that this table is true only if the performances are done under standard conditions (say ideal conditions) and makes no allowance for wind, heavy or loose track, etc.

To compare one running performance with another or a Field Event with another Field Event, the table can be used with confidence, but the comparison of a track record with a field record does not give such good results. From the above facts it appears that in the region of World's records the running events do not score enough points while in the part of the table corresponding to A.A.A. standards, the Field Events, even allowing for their low rating in England, should be given more credit.

The Christmas Social.

This was held on Boxing Day, and some 90 odd members and friends sat down to a hearty tea. Then came a bout of dancing, a little breathing-space followed, during which some of our dramatically-inclined members gave us their version of "Little Nell." The following gentlemen formed the cast—let their names speak for themselves!

F. A. Walker—the Father—complete with beard (and how it shook when he laughed!), Wellington boots and other clothes which looked as though he was just off on a Polar expedition!

A. A. Bird—Little Nell (a little fast, perhaps, but she does a 1.59 "half"). She wore black shoes and stockings, a pale yellow shirt which hid a sausage balloon. Evidently the "Pidge" did not wish to give the impression that she lacked maternal affection.

C. T. Carter—the Policeman, complete with coat and helmet, trundling a toy tricycle around—and who can blame him for deserting his running legs in these mechanical days? Besides, his mount added to the dignity and poise of authority.

P. G. Wright—the Villain. Percy undoubtedly knew his villains well, for he was rigged up in evening dress, collar and bow, and spats. He tried to disguise himself by hiding behind a moustache, and by smoking a cigar (real) completed the picture of wanton dissipation.

Suffice it to say that, notwithstanding a few "divertissements" such as the Lambeth Walk, their acting was powerful enough to send the audience into hisses and boos ("booze?").

A band was engaged and Bill Sutton operated a radio amplifier. Even this did not render Bill free from the sudden (but well-calculated) embraces of one of the ladies, who, after careful manœuvering, caught him unawares. A frame-up is strongly suspected.

After the interval, when cries of "Where's the M.C.?" (Les Bennett) had died down, Bert Footer, dressed up as a jockey and mounted on his bucking steed, Syd Jarlett, gave us a quick Rodeo act. They were faithfully followed by Micky Walker in the rôle of stable-boy.

Thus the party amused itself until it broke up at 11-30. Everybody voted the party a success, and it seems to have found a place in our Social calendar. Although not run for profit, a balance on the right side resulted.

Finally, mention must be made of the good work of our Social Committee who, working under our popular Hon. Social Secretary, Tommy Morrell, were responsible for a most enjoyable evening. To them we all express our warmest thanks and gratitude.

The Lighter Side of the Club.

Unfortunately, the success of our first Dance at Zeeta House was not maintained at the second. In an effort to prevent overcrowding our Social Sub-committee did not use their high-pressure salesmanship in disposing of the tickets. As a result the Dance showed a loss, many regular Club members not appearing on February 11th. Now, come, boys! Do not leave it to outsiders to make these dances successes.

I'm sure everyone present had an enjoyable time, including our Cross-country Captain who, between dances, made repeated dives to the Bar.

Looking through some old issues of the "Belgravian," I see mention of a Club record made at a social on November 22nd, 1930. The report is very interesting. Firstly, that 140 sat down to tea on this occasion and secondly, that we had two vocalists, both members of the Club (Tom Cotton and Billy Williams) who sang old favourites with great success.

Now, the purpose of our Social, on March 25th, is to break this very old record, also to discover talent, which I am sure still exists among the many members of the Club. In the old days we had such artists as Bill Williams, T. Cotton, "Castor and Oil," the Nonesuch Girls and the Belgrave Sisters, to mention only a few, who were always willing to oblige for the enjoyment of those present.

The chance is yours! Now see what you can do to brighten our Socials in the future. You will receive full support from the Social Sub-committee. Do not be afraid to make any suggestion if you think that it will make our socials sucesses!

Marathon Notes.

During the last two seasons we have entered teams for this type of race. That these teams have always been in the first three shows that this little band is already attaining the high standards set by our other teams. By the time that these notes appear our marathon runners will be starting training on the road.

For those who do not fancy the full 26 miles, there is the Finchley Harriers open 20 miles race, with a team race held in conjunction. The Surrey County A.A. will probably be holding a similar race. Standard medals are awarded in each race. In the Finchley race it is 2 hours 15 mins.; in last year's Surrey race it was calculated on the winner's time. What it will be this year remains to be seen.

Later on comes the Poly's classic race from Windsor to London (Chiswick). Last year three Standards were awarded, viz., 3 hours, 3 hours 15 mins. and 3 hours 30 mins. This gives the more moderate performers a chance to gain a medal. Even if one fails to gain a Standard and finishes the course, there is the satisfaction of having done the distance, and the urge to train on to a better performance.

Experience counts a great deal, and a certain amount of experiment is needed to adapt one's pace and style to suit the longer distance. In this connection the writings of Arthur Newton can be studied with advantage.

Well, if these notes have attracted any potential "pavement pounders," and if further information is desired, the following will be at Wimbledon on Saturdays until May: F. G. Ford, A. S. Lindsey, A. F. Mitchell, F. A. Walker and F. G. White. Any member who would like to join the pack for one lap (eight miles) is cordially invited to do so.

In conclusion, I might add that much useful advice can be obtained from our old friend George Still, who was a first-class marathon runner during the early part of this century.

Forthcoming Fixtures.

E. A. Duffett

The Cross Country season is now drawing to a close and our thoughts are turning towards the London to Brighton Relay Race.

On March 25th at Wimbledon we hold our Club 5 miles Road race, with the usual sealed handicap in conjunction. I hope we shall see a good field out for this race, and there is bound to be a rare old tussle for the leading places, as our "Brighton" team is selected after this race.

The day of the Relay is Easter Saturday, April 8th, and the start will be from Mitcham, as last year.

Our rivals from Mitcham will be out for blood, as another victory this year will see them equal our record feat of three wins in succession.

I expect the Birchfield boys to put up their usual good show, but if Mitcham are to be beaten then it is up to the "Bels." to do the trick.

Our team should be as good as last year, when although only finishing third, I think it is generally agreed that but for our bad luck on the second stage, and seeing the way our lads ran on the subsequent stages (especially Harry Verney on the third and Pat Parker over the final leg), given ordinary luck this time we shall be right there.

There is always the chance of a runner breaking down, but for it to happen again this year is not to be thought of, and so I can see no reason why we cannot this time turn the tables on our conquerors.

A few words about the coming Track season may be of interest to our members, and as usual the bulk of our meetings will be held at Battersea Park Track, which I hope the L.C.C. will put into much better condition than last season.

I agree that this track is probably the most used of any London one, but a little foresight on the Parks Committee's part could help this "old dust-heap" regain a little of its former fine running surface.

Meetings are held there every day of the week and hundreds of school children use the ground, but surely if the track was closed from 5 p.m. to 6-30 p.m., and some attention given to it, the surface would not be as it is at present.

However, I suppose we must hope for the best and so I give a list of our fixtures to be held during the coming season :—

We have again arranged to hold the Club Championships at Tooting, as this venue proved popular with our members last season.

The fact of it being rather late was due to no fault of ours, as this was the only date that the L.C.C. could offer us. We had asked for a date in June, but were unable to obtain one, and so had to accept August 5th, which, no doubt, some members will recall was always the day fixed for the Club Championships in the past. This is the Saturday before August Bank Holiday, and I hope that our members will take note of the date, and, if possible, arrange their Summer holidays, if any, accordingly.

I have also been able to ascertain some of the other "Open" dates, and so that members can make a note of these, I have asked our Editor to allow me to append the following list of meetings :—

In addition to the above, we hope to arrange several Inter-Club matches both for Seniors and Juniors; these will be given on our Summer fixture card, which will be issued when our fixtures are completed.

Thus it will be seen that our members will be kept busy during the Summer, and if any new members are desirous of any further information I shall be pleased to assist them.
