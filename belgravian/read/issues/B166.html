EDITORIAL

The congratulations of all Belgravians go out to our General Honorary Secretary who,last month, was elected to the Presidency of the Southern Counties Amateur Athletic Association. A.A.Harley is the first Belgravian to hold this prestigious office and all will agree that "Mr Belgrave", as he is sometimes called, is the fitting person for such a "first". A prodigious worker for the club and the associations to which we are affilliated, he is a highly respected figure in the world of athletics and it is with a sense of real pride that we welcome his Presidency. The road he has travelled since his election to Belgrave membership in 1926 has been long, with many turnings. His record during that journey suggests that he has not yet reached it's end. In that 42 years he filled a multitude of offices including the Presidencies of the R.W.A., and the Middlesex County A.A.A. He is a member of the British Olympic Council, the A.A.A. General Committee and, for many years, Chairman and Treasurer of the Association of London Athletic Clubs. To list his achievements within our own club is too much. Suffice to say that he has held almost every office, including the Presidency (3 years); and as Honorary Editor, he produced no less than 59 editions of "The Belgravian". But 1968 is a landmark for Alf Harley and Belgrave Harriers in more than one respect. September marks the 30th anniversary of his election to the General Honorary Secretaryship of this club. Apart from a break during the war years and his Presidency, he has held the reins of our administration continuously. It is no coincidence that the late Jack Crump should refer to Belgrave as one of Britain's "largest, most successful and most affluent athletic organisations". Some have even applied this to Alf Harley himself! On his election to the Presidency of the S.C.A.A.A. we offer our congratulations; and as the 30th anniversary of his election to the Club Secretaryship approaches we tender our heartfelt thanks.

On another page you will find the 1968 track and field standards (page 42) Alongside the three AAA marks for each event are shown our own club standards, where they exist. It does not require a careful study for two points to become immediately apparent. Firstly, our standards need reviewing again - particularly the field events - and secondly, standard medals are unobtainable in all Youth and most Junior events. This latter point exists because our standard awards are linked to Club Championships only. When the AAA changed to the graded meeting rule should we not have followed suit? Is it right that our runners can pick up standard medals in a variety of events yet our pole-vaulters not have an opportunity? Look again at the list. Is it a picture of encouragement to the Youth, Junior or cinderella-event athlete? It would not be amiss for the Club to study the possibility of establishing Club standards for every recognised AAA event and investigating the question of where and when such marks may be obtained. Furthermore, our standards should bear some regular relationship with those of the AAA which reflect National progress - a kind of grade IV standard. Such a relationship should produce a minimum standard. Where our strength warrants it there is no reason why the mark should not be made superior to the AAA grade III, as in the 20 miles mark at present. But never disproportionately inferior. The subject could do with careful study before 1969. Members views would be welcomed by the Club officers and your members of Committee.

Alf HarleyJack Crump

HON SEC'S NOTES

A.A.H.

Our felicitations to Chris Steer and his wife on their recent marriage; and our very good wishes to them on their new life in Canada. Tom Pritchard, a member for over forty years, has been invited to fill the vacancy on Committee, as he headed the list of unsuccessful candidates at the Annual General Meeting last September.

Incidentally this year's A.G.M. will be held on Wednesday September 18th at Belgrave Hall. Please note in your diaries.

Our representations to the G.L.C. some months ago have borne fruit and the Pole Vault facilities at Battersea Park Track have been much improved. We are grateful for this attention.

More recently we have asked the G.L.C. to consider removing the incinerator plant in the north east corner of the Park. There are occasions when the wind blows the noxious smoke and fumes across the track and tennis courts to the detriment of the health and pleasure of those using these facilities, and of the public generally. It seems absurd to develop the park over many years as a recreational centre, and as one of London's lungs, where people may enjoy reasonably fresh air and the many amenities provided; and then permit toxic choking smoke to poison and distress those who visit the park. Our desire for clean air is very real, and competitors in our events inhaling deeply for that extra oxygen to get them to the tape first, or to put that extra effort in the field events, need the purist air available, uncontaminated by smoke and fumes. Lets hope G.L.C. will ensure that they get it !

With our large number of Boy members attending the track regularly for their planned training; we are concerned at the increase in charges for the use of the Dressing Room to 1/-. There may possibly be justification for the increase in charges as applied to adult members; but our Boys with their three or four visits a week to the track plus other expenses involved, cannot be expected to pay a shilling at each visit. We are asking the G.L.C. whether they can extend a concession to those still attending school and now await an answer to our urgent enquiries.

From the poorer to the richer club members! As the president of the Southern Counties AAA, I should like to call attention to the "200 Competition" promoted by that body. Membership involves a payment of 5/- per week (paid monthly by Bankers order) of which approximately 2/- goes to help athletics and 3/- goes back in substantial prizes to you and for your friends in the competition. You may be lucky enough to win a monthly prize (our Hon. Treasurer has just received £25) or one of the special six-monthly prizes of £500 which are payable when the competition is fully subscribed with 200 members. You have the satisfaction of knowing you are helping our sport in the South; and are one of only 200 with a chance of three cash prizes each month, and two super prizes at six monthly intervals. I shall be very glad to see that full details reach you if you would let me have your name - there are still a few vacancies to fill.

We are greatly indebted to Dr. J. TADYAN who has recently presented us with a "FRANK SIMMONS" Cup, which has been allocated to a Boys High Jump Championship.

The A.A.A. is organising an Athletics Dance at the Lyceum Ballroom on Friday Sept. 27th. Tickets to members of AAA Clubs are 12/6 each (normal price £1-1-0) which reduces to 10/- for orders of ten or more tickets. It is expected that HARRY SECOMBE will make the draw for the winning ticket in the competition which is being promoted by the A.A.A, with a Car as first prize and other generous prizes in plenty.

Members will remember that in our last issue we made reference to a 7 miles road run likely to be promoted by us on behalf of the Borough of Wandsworth on Sept. 7th. Unfortunately the restriction on expenditure which is being rigorously applied may now mean that the event and the proposed Wandsworth Show will not now take place this year.

Do you want something ---- for almost nothing?

Of course you do. And you joined BELGRAVE to get it. What do you get? Well, you get four editions of this gazette for a start, and if you don't think it's worth it how about contributing something yourself? Then there's BELGRAVE HALL with its endless hot water (almost) it's tea and cakes, the welcome smiles of Wynn Shepherd, Mrs Savage and the other numerous lady helpers. And, what's more, the building was bought with our own hard saved money. Then there's the 40 odd Club Championship events with their trophies and sets of medals that are fought for each year. One mustn't forget the numerous handicap events either, which produce attractive prizes for those capable of beating the Hon . Handicappers. Then there's the handbook, fixture cards and, of course, those that plan it all. There are our famous socials which are worth twice what you pay. And behind it all are those 30-odd members of General Committee beavering away on your behalf to provide the competition and facilities an athlete needs---to give Belgravians a service second to none. In the midst of all this activity stands the man with the heavily-worn purse strings,the Hon. Treasurer. He bears no small burden and he has ulcers to prove it. All for you. That's the "SOMETHING", now for the "ALMOST NOTHING"-----it's a 40/- subscription. Yes, believe it or not, a mere £2 PER YEAR---just 9d per week. Because it is such a ridiculously small sum, some of you have naturally forgotten to pay it. Don't let us have to send you an unpleasant reminder that you are 9 months overdue---just tie a knot in your tie this instant to remind you to send it to Dave Shepherd whose address is on the Editorial page. As compensation for the torture you have inflicted upon him, why not send him next year's sub as well. If you are truly repentant you could include a donation also. The supreme sacrifice, of course, would be to offer to take over his job--- but then you'd never stand it! SUBSCRIPTIONS ARE 9 MONTHS OVERDUE.

ROAD RELAY ROUND-UP

TWO FIRSTS - TWO SECONDS - TWO THIRDS

Our best for many many years. That must be the undisputed verdict on a Spring road relay season that saw our team do battle on six occasions and return home with awards every time. Agreed we chalked up three wins in 1966 and 1967, but our performances were erratic overall and don't really match those of 1968. The peak of this brief but high pressured season was undoubtedly the National Relay at Leicester.where Coventry Godiva, aided and abetted by our 'own' Mel Thorpe, ran out deserved winners. The arrival at Godiva of Colin Kirkham and the revival of Kilby and Heatley were too much for the opposition, and although our team ran well it was evident early on that it was all in an effort to get second place. That Derby should edge us out by a hair just 11 seconds broad may seem rough justice, but with international Dominic Keily on the side lines no one begrudged them their 'silver'. Watching this Derby team on the short legs made one realise just how old our fellows are by comparison. Including our reserve (Dabbs), five of our thirteen runners are over 30 years of age---headed by Bill Kerr, 36 next month. On June 1st the average age of the team was 28 years-4 months. Unfortunately, there is nothing on the horizon to suggest it will be under 30 in the 1970 event. It will be up to our three under-24's Bicourt, Major and Donnison to share an ever increasing degree of responsibility in our future teams, especially with the loss of Peter Whewell to South Africa, Gerry Williams to Venezuela, Chris Steer to Canada and Geoff North to retirement (again). Such thoughts were far from one's mind when the first relay of the season(Southampton to Bournemouth) arrived on Sunday March 10th---just 8 days after the National Cross-Country. With a long winter behind them, the smell of Spring in the air and the prospect of fast running on good surfaces, runners always seem to find a new lease of life when the road relays come round. Unfortunately they let their fervour carry them away in this opening event---to the extent of two minutes off course!! Only the wily Hyman, of the first seven, trod the correct route and, as described in the March 'BELGRAVIAN', Portsmouth were the official winners. The lads had nearly 3 weeks to forget their Southampton luck---or lack of it---before they descended upon Thames Valley Harriers at Cranford, determined to wrest this Trophy from Polytechnic. Rivalry for the National Relay team showed itself straight away when Bicourt (B) beat Major (A) on the first stage run in, to finish 4th----a grand start. Back in 27th place Dabbs (C) and Black (D) were having a similar tussle, the former scraping home by a one second margin. With 65 starters, and four Belgrave teams in 28, we looked pretty strong. Trevor Hart followed on with his usual reliable run to take us to the front; Bill Dance held this with a slower time and then Geoff North had the galling experience of recording 3rd fastest time of the day, only to see his 8 seconds lead turned into a 6 seconds deficit by Bob Holt of Hercules/Wimbledon. No change on the final stage meant another second place for the record. This was frustrating. But the team had no intention of spending a week licking their wounds. Within 24 hours they were gunning for action at Reading, where fresh sextets from Bristol, Mitcham and the home club were eagerly awaiting us. Their eagerness soon changed to disillusionment as our lads hit the front right from the start, set a course record and chalked up the three fastest times of the day. Only a dozen of the 150 runners broke 17 mins and five of those were Belgravians. Our tails were now really up and when we visited Brighton 6 days later for the Front Relay there was never any doubt that we would retain the trophy we won last year. Our 'A' team was made up of the six long-stage probables for the 'National' team and the 'B' team of contenders for the short stages. A below form run by Chris Steer brought the 'B' team home on the first stage in a lowly 30th place. The interesting feature of the race was how far they could recover. Jim Rimmer ( a man who could soon make our 'A' team if he works hard at it) picked up 4 places and the remaining four worked their way up to a praiseworthy 11th. With Donnison and Dance running they would almost certainly have finished 4th. The score now read two seconds, two firsts. We now, disappointingly, had to face our two thirds. The first of these was at Blackpool,where the event favoured Bolton no end. With only four stages to run, and Hill and Freary in terrific form, it soon became a race for second and North Staffs got us by 17 seconds. The two Bolton stars were each 39 seconds faster than the North brothers---truly fantastic. But we were not deterred, for the event served as no guide to the 12 man National event the following week. What happened the following week at Leicester is now well known to all and can be read in detail on another page. As a team, we ran well. One or two have run better, but no one ran badly and it is on this latter score that they deserved their 3rd place---after all, a very high position for what is a National final. Furthermore, it is 4 places higher than last year. Yes, the 1968 Spring season was definitely one of our best ever---will 1969 be even better?.

WIMBLEDON CUP for O'HARA

Club 5¾ Miles Road Championship - March 16th.

With the North brothers out of the way for this title race, at least the engraver was going to get a bit of variety ! With Thresher also absent O'Hara was the favourite - just. Our President, Ted Stimpson, sent the 45 starters scurrying up Lauriston Road and as they swirled round the corner one caught brief glimpses of "flash"Dance, "T.K.", "I'm only doing 60 miles a week" Newell, "Carnaby" Steer, "Photos" Linstead, Brian Gorman - or was it Hamilton, "What do you think?" himself, the Bishop of Brighton, Supermac and the whole motley crowd were past, already eating up the first lap of the "top". On the second small lap a group of ten detached itself from the field and, with Hart forcing the pace they headed out for Copse Hill. Things were now hotting up and at the bottom of Copse, Hart, O'Hara, Bicourt and Major had broken away from Bill Dance with Bill Kerr and John Dear close up and the rest strung out in single file. On hitting the hills Laurie made his break. Adrian was the first to tail off and then the order settled down to O'Hara, Bicourt, Hart. For the last 1¼ miles no more than ten seconds covered the three but for all the striving their positions remained unchanged. John Dear had his best ever run in this event and Lionel Mann was only 2 seconds outside his best. A dramatic finish was enacted between Ron Linstead and Jim Rimmer with "no one shall pass me" Linstead getting the verdict by the thickness of his number card and being incommunicado for the next five minutes. Brian Gorman produced a most encouraging run and fooled the handicapper by a 45 seconds margin - he deserved his first prize; make the most of it Brian, our handicapper doesn't forget treatment of this sort ! Second and third prizes went to Peter Blair-Fish, who is showing steady improvement, and George Piddington - obviously the sea air agrees with him.

Bronze in National Relay DERBY SNATCH SILVER

LEICESTER SPORTS CENTRE - April 27th

If Coventry hadn't spoiled it all by being out in front almost all the way this would have been the most exciting relay for many years. As it was, the field were racing for the silver medals and for 4¼ hours Derby and Belgrave fought a see-saw battle that was in doubt to the very end, when the holders of the Northern title squeezed home by the narrow margin of 11 secs. Throughout that 4¼ hours the two teams were never further apart than 48 secs. With interest centred on this dual struggle, it came as a surprise after Stage 8 to find that Godiva were suddenly vulnerable - Derby having got to within 21 seconds of them. Any raising of additional hopes were soon put down by Dick Taylor on the next stage who for the second year stamped his authority on this contest. The venue was an improvement on last year, the weather a little kinder (it was at least warmer) and the Belgrave performances free of disaster. Yes, there were some disappointments, but one cannot expect 12 men to hit peak on the same day. Our all-round solid performance was worthy of praise, and praise it got, particularly from that vociferous bank of supporters who, loyally shuttling from long to short stages and replenishing their "flasks" en-route, cheered on our runners at those vital moments. The long lap was 5 miles - 1492 years in length and the short one 2 miles - 1502 yards. The change-overs were effected on the new running track.

STAGE 1. John Bicourt (28:23 - 12th fastest) A late start, by five minutes, permitted Manchester D.L.C. to just make the line but Blackpool had to run a substitute while they awaited the bulk of their team - they remained in last place throughout. John dashed to the front, ostentatiously asked for guidance out of the grounds, and disappeared up Saffron Lane shoulder to shoulder with Mike Fuller - Fowler, Haslam, Francis and Kirkham in hot pursuit. At Wigston village (3 miles), with the fast run home ahead of them, the first dozen were closely grouped with John just maintaining contact. The fast pace gradually reduced the numbers until only 4 remained to fight it out round the track. John held his form well and, although 12th, was only 36 seconds behind the leader. Coventry ominously, were second, Derby 5th, Manchester 14th and Portsmouth (Meadows) a disastrous 21st.

STAGE 2. Tony Fairclough (14:49 - 12th fastest) Dogged by injury for several weeks Tony was not yet back to full fitness. Neverthless, he ran his heart out as usual taking two places but losing three on the track when he had nothing left. We were now 13th, 64 secs down on Coventry and 44 secs. behind Derby.

STAGE 3. Geoff North (27:33 - 2nd fastest) With the opposition nicely strung out ahead Geoff raced through the field, catching the Derby runner (Calladine) after only 3 miles and only just failing to beat Perry of Birchfield for second place. His invaluable effort left Derby trailing by 25 seconds, but Coventry's lead had been increased to 1:28 due to a scintillating run by Adcocks (27:09). Manchester were 11th, Tipton 15th and Portsmouth 21st and way out of the reckoning.

STAGE 4. Roger Donnison (14:55 - 10th fastest) Roger will not need to be reminded of Birchfield's colours. After nearly 3 miles of desperate struggle he cut their lead from 8 seconds to 4 seconds! He was also faster than the Coventry runner (Godfrey) but had to give way to another of Derby's short-leg stars (Hinton) who took them back up to 2nd. 35 seconds behind Roger was ex-Belgravian Hugh Barrow (14:45) running for Sale. 12 seconds now covered Derby, Birchfield and Bels with Coventry 52 seconds ahead.

STAGE 5. Laurie O'Hara (28:10 - 5th fastest) A ding-dong battle was now developing between Derby and Bels, and Laurie had tough opposition in Leeming. However, our man was still riding high after his excellent win in the Wycombe '5' road race and gradually he pulled back the Derby man's 90 yard lead, yard by yard, in a workmanlike manner. With the Birchfield man well dropped, it must have been a shock for Laurie at the 4 miles mark to see Mike Freary of Bolton surge past and go on to clock a fantastic 26:33;the only man to beat 27 minutes on the day. With the stadium close at hand Laurie caught Leeming and his finishing sprint took him 2 seconds clear. His effort of 28:10 was only 5 seconds away from being 3rd fastest, behind Freary and Tipton's Jim Wright, and was just one second better than Basil Heatley, out again up front for Coventry.

STAGE 6. Bill Dance (14:29 - 2nd fastest) With the Derby/Belgrave dual now 3-2 to our rivals, Bill set out to hold our lead and make it 3-3 at the halfway mark. Eley of Derby soon took back the two seconds leeway and, to the horror of our supporters gradually went ahead. Bill, wearing his most determind look - complete with the closed eyes treatment - restricted the lead to 10 yards until, on entering the stadium, he cut loose like a quarter miler to hand over.six seconds ahead. What an entertainer ! "Just a flash runner" said Brenda Dance. Trevor Hart said nothing - he was going to need his breath.

STAGE 7. Trevor Hart (28:19 - 7th fastest) Trevor found himself in hot company. Brian Kilby 52 seconds ahead, Simmons (the Derby No.1) on his heels and another three of the day's top runners, Rushmer, Holt and Jackson chasing hard. Simmons had no easy task in getting ahead of the bearded Hart and, when he did go past, Trevor fought every yard of the way to keep the margin down. To be sandwiched between the two fastest men of the stage was a real test of courage and he came through with flying colours - Simmons held to 27 seconds and Rushmer kept off by 13 seconds. 86 seconds now covered the first four teams.

STAGE 8. John Dear (14:51 - 8th fastest) For several years John has been destined to serve as a regular reserve in our teams, so it was good to see him gaining his place this time as a first choice. His selection was justified and must have been all the sweeter to him when he took 4 seconds off our old friend Mel Thorpe who was busy maintaining Coventry's front position. While the Coventry/Belgrave gap remained at just over a minute, Derby (Peake) had moved up to within 21 secs of Godiva - closer than anyone had managed to get since Stage 2.

STAGE 9. John Thresher (28:00 - 4th fastest) Although not as dramatic as his run of last year, Dick Taylor raced away to wrap it up for Coventry with a lead of 1:48. John's task was to catch Eccles of Derby and put us back in the race for second place. He did not disappoint and, with a final flourish on the track, gave our next man a two-second lead. Manchester (Gregory) and Hercules/Wimbledon (R.Holt) had closed up to within 30 seconds. At this point our supporters felt more confident of receiving second place than at anytime. Belgrave's strong finish of Major, Gerry North & Kerr looked certain to have the mastery of Derby's Domleo, Bullivant and Taylor. If only Gerry North could be given a lead.

STAGE 10. Adrian Major (14:54 - 7th fastest) Adrian's recent form had been such to justify consideration for a long stage. Although a few days short of his 21st birthday he had already attained his athletic majority with 78th in the National cross-country championships and, as he set off hotly pursued by Domleo, there was an air of confidence in the Belgrave camp. Fourteen minutes later it had changed to shocked surprise when a red and white vest was the first to appear, and became dismay as the next 20 seconds seemed like hours before Adrian's appearance. Although "not my day" he was still able to chalk up 7th fastest time, which indicated that Derby had played quite a big part in the struggle.

STAGE 11. Gerry North (27:54 - 3rd fastest) Not for some time had Gerry looked so nervous before a race. During his warm up he was thinking in terms of holding a lead over Bullivant, and North in full flight takes some catching. Faced with the task instead, of pulling back 150 yards he fairly streaked after his quarry and, within 2 miles, had caught the Derby international. Bullivant latched on to Gerry and, although the Belgrave man had edged away by 6 yards at Wigston, that early effort up hill and against wind took it's toll. When they turned into the fast second half a revived Bullivant, in his best track style and with the wind behind him, surged back to the front again going away to make up all but one second of his original lead. Gerry fought on as only Gerry can, but it was not his day either, for even if he had held Bullivant his time would still have been less than he had hoped for.

STAGE 12. Bill Kerr (14:38 - 2nd fastest) Bill was his usual unruffled self at the takeover and, as he left the stadium in that misleadingly nonchalant style, many thought that Taylor of Derby would consolidate their lead. But their worries were not yet over. 36 year old Kerr, the oldest member of the team, was out to show everyone that the old zip was still there and the cause not yet lost. Only the shortness of the distance prevented him from becoming the day's hero for, after making little impression over the first 1½ miles, he hauled back 6 of the 17 seconds deficit in the last mile and the men from Derbyshire didn't relax until their man actually reached the track - just 80 yards ahead. So, after 4¼ hours of running, the two teams finished only 11 seconds apart. Coventry were 2 mins ahead and Manchester 2 minutes behind. And so Godiva scored a National double, taking both cross-country and relay titles, but no one else bettered our combined placings of 4th and 3rd respectively so until we renew our assault next season we give best only to Coventry Godiva - a great team.

SPOTLIGHT ON ... JOHN THRESHER

The place is Motspur Park Track, the date 24th June 1967 and the race about to start is the S.C.A.A.A. three miles championship. As the runners stoop forward and peer at their reflections in the flooded track the starter raises his arm and sends them splattering round the first bend in a shower of liquified cinders. As the lap count down progresses so the leading group diminishes in size, until there is only the holder Gibson, the favourite Johnstone, the Holt twins and two Belgravians. "Two laps to go" is the cry and Johnstone breaks for home. Gibson goes with him followed by Bob Holt and a mud bespattered runner whose spectacles look more like sun-glasses. The crowd cheers, and the "blind" man surges forward as though suddenly equipped with an outboard motor, races past the surprised leaders in the straight and scores what looks to be a ridiculously easy win. Next day millions of sleepy citizens opened their Sunday papers to see the happy, muddy face of JOHN THRESHER smiling at them. Some thought, no doubt, that they had more to laugh at than him; but then they didn't know of the events that led up to his first major championship success. John Thresher was born at Merton on 16th December 1939. When at school, he disliked playing rugby; the only alternative was cross-country and he enjoyed it probably because he was fairly good at it. In the next road to John there lived a certain Raymond Braine of Belgrave Harriers who had no idea of the service he was rendering the Club each time he pounded his way up Erroll Gardens sporting his brightly lettered track-suit top. A few brief words of introduction and 17 year old John Thresher became a Belgravian in October 1957. No-one noticed. But they began to a few weeks later when, over Herne Hill Harriers course at Addington, he carried our colours for the first time. "Young Thresher showed delightful form in winning the Youths race" read THE BELGRAVIAN of that time, and John well remembers how encouraged he was at receiving such early recognition. His first track season produced a 4:29 mile and a 9:53 two miles, yet three years later his mile had dropped only 9 seconds and his 9:53 still stood as a personal best. There were many who thought that if progress was not forthcoming in the formative years 18 - 21 there was little prospect of much following. They hadn't reckoned with John Thresher. They were also not aware of the priority he gave to his studies and work as an articled accountant. His training continued to be remarkably light, but regular - "I owed a great deal to Arthur Penny during this period for his continual encouragement and the holding together of our Youths team". Came the track season of 1961 and studies for his Chartered Accountancy examinations. He reflects "While existing on one five miles training run every Wednesday and the occasional Saturday race, I distinctly remember running a 1:59 half in the Club Champs and realising I was wasting my time. I decided to train harder there and then". Before the season was out he ran 1:55 on three occasions. The writing was on the wall. Illness that Autumn (6 weeks) put him back a long way but only made him more determined than ever and he resolved to do a regular weekly mileage of 40. From December 1961 onwards John could be seen in the heart of all our training packs. Repetition climbs up the horse-rides in the darkness of Tuesday evenings chasing the unseen figures of Robinson, Miller, Shippen and Thompson; out on the dimly lit roads of Wimbledon on Thursdays, gasping behind Linstead, Dabbs, Gebbett & Co. as they did the "seven hills"; sometimes on the "Warren 9" with Bird, Walker, Baulch & Co. And gradually it came. The results were soon apparent when in the South of Thames Senior at Maidenhead in 1962 Thresher finished 26th helping our team to second place medals. The hard winter's work reaped dividends that track season and in one fell swoop he chopped his times to 4:09.4 and 9:04, and tasted his first Championship success by annexing the L.C.C. mile in 4:12. These rewards induced him to adopt a policy of increasing his training work-load each year. The times came down. 1963 - 1:53.0/4:07.5/8:48.4 (and a 2nd place in the London v Munich 1500 metres). 1964 - 1:52.4/4:02.1 (and runner up in the County mile). 1965 - 4:02.7/13:54.0 (and a great win in Milan over 1500 metrs for London, in 3:46.7). In 1966 John Thresher reached the final of the A.A.A. mile - "I finished 7th in 4:04.6, having run the last lap in 56 seconds and been left standing! I resolved to run 3 miles in 1967". Some had expected him to make this decision a season earlier but, nonetheless, all were agreed it was a right one. An 8:42.2 two miles soon endorsed this and before the season was through he had his first B.A.A.B. trip to Vienna where, with Dick Taylor of Coventry Godiva, he ran against Ron Clarke and Lazlo Mecser over 5,000 metres where he clocked a personal best 14:05. Shortly afterwards he finished second to the great Michel Jazy over 3,000 metres at La Baule. Our subject had not been idle during the intervening winters. "I have never been very fond of cross-country due to an old ankle injury - and especially over 9 miles" but he was still a regular member of our winter teams, and the 1966/7 winter would have to be his best ever if he was to become a successful 12 lap exponent. It was. Second in the Surrey Co., 16th in the Inter-Counties and 50th in the National ("I was disappointed not to have had a good one") and John had a good foundation for his season of three miling. He did not realise that it would bring him his finest victory and his most disappointing defeat. After a second place in the Surrey Co. (13:40.4) and a promising 4th in the Inter-Counties at the White City (13:40.8) the way was paved for that exciting battle in the muddied cinders of Motspur Park. ATHLETICS WEEKLY wrote "John Thresher, although handicapped by mud plastering his glasses ran the fastest race of his career - - - - - his time of 13:35.8 might have been 10 seconds faster on a dry track". And so he was Southern Champion - feeling on top of the world. "I felt very confident during my warm up and as I stood on the line I looked around and thought 'I can beat this lot'. The pace felt so slow, I had to keep holding in, checking all the while and jockeying for position. When Johnston burst away two laps from home I had to school myself to wait even then. I felt great on that last lap and could see just enough to keep my feet. Now for the AAA's". He was soon to find out that his rivals had not been standing still during his progress, and at the Championships on July 15th he finished 13th in a best ever 13:27.2. Despite the time his disappointment was greater than ever before. It was obvious then, as it is now, that the three hot favourites for representation in the 5,000 metres at Mexico were McCafferty, Taylor and Rushmer. John has resolved to harry this trio at every opportunity throughout 1968 and, should something go wrong for them, be right at hand as the logical replacement. It will require a lot of hard early-season racing to make his mark and the ability to hold his form - a challenge he is well fitted to meet. What of the future? Well, he hopes to go on competing until he can no longer make Belgrave road-relay teams, and there is always the possibility of turning to coaching or administration. For the present he prefers to be 'spoon-fed' and concentrate on his running - an attitude, he thinks, that many officials fail to understand. He admires Gerry North immensely for his wholehearted approach and has a high regard for Bill Kerr - "A beautifully relaxed performer whom I tried to copy". Of Belgrave? "Well it is the best club anyone could belong to, but it could be even better". Last year John Thresher had a taste of the rarefied atmosphere at Font Romeu in the Pyrenees - he is "hooked" on it. McCafferty, Taylor & Rushmer had better beware; he has an insatiable desire for the Mexico City flavour !

TRACK AND FIELD - THE TIMES-THEY ARE A-CHANGING.

Tony Brooks; Bill Couzens

TONY BROOKS WRITES ON TRACK:

This year, Belgrave Harriers will be one of the twelve clubs who will help re-write British club-athletics history. On July 21st we compete in the first match of the 'B' league against Sale Harriers, T.V.H., Birchfield, Notts A.C. and L.C.A.T. A.C., all of which are clubs of no mean ability. How will Belgrave make out? Will we emerge victorious, or as an also ran? This question can be best answered by the athletes themselves, who they will be will depend largely upon the results of the coming trophy and club meetings, and those who prove themselves to be true Belgravians will be the ones who will be selected to compete in these meetings. Let us then look at the events and the people who will be fighting for the places in the teams. 100/200 mtrs. For the past couple of years, the Vivian/Mitchell/Martin combine has dominated these events, but now that John Mitchell has had an unlucky start to the season due to glandular fever Jim Vivian and Chris Martin had both better look out for the fast improving Steve White, who has recorded times over the longer sprint that Jim and Chris have yet to beat this year. 400 mtrs. In relation to the other clubs, this is the event where we are at our weakest. With Chris Martin being the only one to have recorded a respectable time, our outlook is gloomy. However, if Jim Vivian were to concentrate on quarter miling he could well find himself amongst the top in the country. 800 mtrs. Although having been weak in this event for the past few years, it looks as if in the foreseeable future this could be one of our stronger points. John Thresher is capable of holding his own against other club half milers, but as he specialises in longer events, it looks as if Tom Carter's school will produce Belgrave's future half milers. With Tony Brooks and Alan Mead both under two minutes after only a few outings, Pete Hillier and Lionell Mann close behind them and Bill Curtin (still a youth doing 2:03.0) the prospects look fair. 1500 mtrs. On paper it looks like John Thresher, but look out for Bill Kerr who has improved tremendously over the mile. Also Geoff North who if encouraged enough could give both of these a run for their money. Others to watch include Laurie O'Hara and Adrian Major. 3000 mtrs. Geoff North, Gerry North, Laurie O'Hara, Bill Kerr, Bill Dance, Tony Fairclough, Trevor Hart. Any one of these is capable of emerging as Belgrave's hope for a winner in this event. 5000 mtrs. Roughly the same field again with the added threat of Thresher who has said that he is good enough to take them all on over this distance. 3000 mtrs/stpchse. In this event we are lucky to be able to pick from any one of five men. John Bicourt looks favourite, but just how far behind him John Dear, Bill Dance and Dick Piotrowski are when it comes to selection remains to be seen. Alan Black could prove the suprise in this event once he has regained fitness, as his time last year placed him second behind Bicourt. This then, at the beginning of the season is how Belgrave shapes up. Who is selected remains to be seen, but whoever it is will certainly have to prove himself against strong competition.

BILL COUZENS WRITES ON FIELD.

With prospects for the National league looking pretty good on the track, let me outline our prospects in the field events. High Jump. We have never had it so good in this event with Arnold Bent (Junior) already clearing 6'1½" this season Barry Townsend 5'10", Denzil Winsborrow 5'8" and Shola Ipaye 5'8" we could well have both A and B strings over 6 feet. CLUB CHAMPIONSHIPS

HURLINGHAM PARK - MAY 25th

The outstanding performers at this year's championships were Steve White, Chris Martin and Nick Head. The first two chalked up sprint trebles in the Junior & Senior grades respectively, and the latter repeated his "pinta" treble of last year shot/discus/javelin. Once again performances were not outstanding, but in a very homely affair of this sort there is not the 'needle' of outside competition. This, coupled with the early date and an afternoon of almost continous drizzle was not conducive to the exceptional. Nevertheless, some exciting contests were seen and the boys played a major part in the entertainment. Their half mile was fascinating. Fifteen lads, their ages ranging from 9 - 14 yrs and with height differences up to 1'6", made the event seem a most unequal contest. This was not the case though when they got their legs moving, and many a good little'un beat a good big'un; best of all, they all enjoyed it - who notices the rain anyway? Richard Langley was a convincing winner and a boy worth watching. Nine-year-old Ian Gillings, not content with a stylish run in the 'half', went pot hunting in the interval and scooped a prize in the children's race; after a flyer too! The weather was a bit tough on the field events competitors. The greasy arena, wet implements and the low temperature didn't give them much good cheer and most were well below their season bests. Arnold Bent repeated his Sen/Jnr high jump double of last year and Bob Couillault clinched a useful triple/long jump double. Carl Lawton had a stylish win in the 2 miles walk after a fast start by Brian Ward that suggested an upset was due. Several of our leading walkers were unable to be present due to a clash of dates with the Leicester '20'. Date clashes also accounted for the absence of our Middlesex County six-milers, Trevor Hart and Laurie O'Hara; whereas illness kept out Geoff North and Jim Vivian who, between them, would have been leading contenders for five titles. One of the most exciting contests of the afternoon was between two runners who didn't even meet; Chris Martin and Steve White. Each time Steve chalked up a win in one of the junior sprints, Chris followed suit in the Senior - but he couldn't match the junior times and Steve won the three mythical contests by margins of about 4 yards (100), 5 yards (220) and 3 yards (440). John Thresher took the 880 and mile in a canter; waiting for the bell in both before making his move. Marathoner Lionel Mann shook up the two-lap merchants with the fastest finishing burst of the day and snatched the bronze medal. Bill Kerr towed the mile field through the first three laps and Thresher was the only one with him at the bell. Bill was in his most stylish form and looks well set for some good 3's this season. Last year's steeplechase result was reversed when John Dear just held off the Dance finish to score a popular win. (Our 8:52.0 chaser, John Bicourt, opted for the mile, as did our sub 9:20 man Alan Black.) Perhaps some of the massive mile field would surprise themselves if they tackled this event next year. Many moderate runners have shown a remarkable aptitude "over the sticks". While the boys high jump went on into the evening like an Olympic pole-vault contest, the President and his Lady, with the able assistance of Frank Webb, went about the task of disposing of the 21 trophies and the multitude of medals. The recorders were there to the bitter end as, indeed, was Bob Taylor who organised it all with such success. The only black-spot, perhaps, was the lack of times for the also-rans. The importance of times for these fellows cannot be overstressed and if it requires special arrangements in future then they should be considered for next year.

Surrey Championships

JUNE 5th,6th & 8th---Motspur Park

Belgravians made quite a mark in the 1968 Champioships, held on the fast London University track. The weather threatened continously but never quite carried it out and conditions were generally good. Star of the Championships was undoubtedly Steve White who pulled off an excellent treble in the Junior 100, 220 and long jump events. His 22'-4" in the latter was only 1" inferior to that of the senior winner, international John Howell. Just to show that we were not a one man outfit Arnold Bent turned on the heat with a Junior high jump win of 6'-1" and, to keep Steve company, finished runner-up in the long-jump just 7" behind his club mate. Both Steve and Arnold are 1st year juniors, so what of next year! Our Junior sprint champion was absolutely untouchable in the 100 and although run closer in the furlong was always in control. In the Senior sprints Jim Vivian resumed his duels with Tymms (Surrey AC) and had Chris Martin to keep him company in both finals. Jim could not reproduce the excellent start he gained in his 100 heat and trailed Tymms throughout by a frustrating yard. Chris chased hard for fifth. The furlong saw almost a repeat performance with the Surrey AC man's margin stretched from a tenth to a fifth of a second.

Photo by: R. Linstead Tymms wins the 100 from VIVIAN with MARTIN fifth

The Hamilton twins reached the finals of the Junior 440 and 880, John doing a personal best 53.5 in the quarter and Bob 2:06.3 in the half, just ahead of T. Day (2:06.6). Another Junior to reach the finals was D.B. Rees who finished 5th in the short sprint. With Steve White and Arnold Bent having collected four golds and a silver between them our supporters were really cock-a-hoop, for young L.J. Sullivan had already scooped the Youths long jump on the Wednesday evening with an excellent 20'-6½". Sullivan is a promising athlete of whom we should hear a lot more. It was unfortunate to see that other outstanding Youth, Bill Curtin, standing on the side lines with a leg injury. We could have seen him add to our medal tally in the Youths 880. Alan Mead stood in for Tony Brooks in the final of the Senior 440 as fastest non-qualifier. Although last, he ran a personal best 51.9 and was given more publicity than the winner Cohen. Charles Elliott announced that Alan had been on duty at the Trooping the Colour all morning and had just made the final---such is fame! The star event of these Championships is the 3 miles. Surrey must have the greatest depth of any county at this distance. There were 39 entries and of the 16 that went through to the final the slowest qualifier did 14:13.2! Poor Tony Fairclough found himself in the wrong heat and his 14:12.8 was not good enough to qualify, yet it was not many years ago that this time was sufficient to win. After his second place in the inter-counties 3 on the previous Monday John Thresher was favourite to win and it was rumoured that Mick Gowan (HHH) would tow him through a fast '2' in an effort to gain the Olympic qualifying time of 13:23.0. It was apparent after one mile (4:29.6) that there was no such effort and with the Holts alternating the lead and John tucked in about 4th, the 2 miles came up in 9:05.4. With two laps to go, and Dave Holt leading into the first bend, the pace seemed to falter momentarily and in an instant brother Bob stepped smartly round the leader and hugging the kerb as he sped round the bend opened up 10 yards before John had stepped clear and gathered himself for pursuit. It was a brilliant piece of tactical running by the slower finisher and try as he did John could make no impression over those final two circuits which Holt covered in 2:09. Derek Fernee, our second claim member, was an excellent third, 20 seconds inside his previous best. Next claret and gold vest to hit the finish was that old faithful Gerry North who had still not run off the effects of his inter-counties '20' 5 days earlier yet was able to turn in a useful 13:45.4 for 6th place. John Bicourt found the going too hard after his recent heavy programme and dropped out. Adrian Major was also unable to re-charge his batteries after his qualifying run and had to be content with 12th---though 14:09.0 was not exactly hanging about for a 21 years old! As the 3-milers recuperated, it was announced that the first 10 had run faster than the winner of their rival County, Middlesex; the winner being none other than our own Bill Kerr. And so the 1968 Championships ended with much promise for the future. It is always an interesting well-organised occasion and there's always the annual reunion in the Pavilion bar afterwards to add a bit of spice. More of you ought to come next year. HARRY SHIELDS

R.W.A. 20 KM Championship, Woodlands School, Broad Lane, Coventry. May 11th. HARRY SHIELDS WRITES:-

What walkers ! What a record ! Ignoring wins in Junior events London - Brighton and other recognised classic walking fixtures, which many clubs would be only too pleased to notch, from my records this was our 32nd R.W.A. team win in R.W.A. Championships. No wonder our President, whom we were very pleased to see, was struck silent and had a look of awe and respect mixing with these mighty men who win National Championships more or less as a matter of course. These are not glamour men seeking the tinsel praise of the crowd, but sound dedicated craftsmen hardened by weather and from many hours on the road and track, able to endure when others fail. It is no sport for the faint hearted, but the material on which the country has come to rely on occasions such as the Olympic Games & European Championships. In this Belgrave has a proud record to maintain. From the starter's gun it was evident our men were out for the kill, led by Ray Middleton in his quest for Olympic honours. How superb and exciting it was. 20 KM (12½ miles) is the shorter of the Olympic walking distances and hardly Ray's speciality. This did not deter him from thrashing many of the fancied sprint walkers to lead our team home, and to be named among the six probabilities for the Olympics. He will be included in those to receive specialised acclimatisation training for the more strenuous 50 KM which is his proven distance. I know our thoughts and best wishes are with him during the anxious months ahead. This was a comparatively new team and an indication of the enthusiasm engendered by Pat Duncan and Ray Hall as Captain and Secretary is seen by the number who travelled to Coventry, enabling 15 to start in the race and leaving a good number of supporters and the usual officials. The Championship was ours from the start, with Len Duquemin, Bob Barnes and John Moullin completing the team, closely supported by Gerry Cameron. The need for this support was seen at 15 KM when that bane of distant walkers, cramp, struck John Moullin and Gerry walking like the champion that he must eventually be,advanced from the forties to 28th team position and maintained our position. With everyone giving of his best it is difficult to single out individual performances but it says much for the future to see Carl Lawton and Brian Ward, both only 20, in such forward positions. A word of praise for Steve Black for his perseverance. I am sure this will bear fruit and encourage others.

Feature · page 34

More Reminiscences

EDMUND P. DONOVAN

EDMUND P. DONOVAN emigrated to the USA just after World War 1. He had been a Belgravian for some years and was also a member of Prices AC, at whose ground we held our first championship meeting in 1922. He is resident in New York and has for some time been President of the St. Georges Club---a well known club for New Yorkers of English nationality. He was responsible for many of the arrangements when Her Majesty the Queen visited New York in 1957, and was subsequently awarded the O.B.E. The following notes were penned during a brief visit to London last year; and with them go his best wishes to all Belgravians, "the present members, veterans like myself and all those who are continuing to carry forward the Claret and Gold to still greater success".

I think it was the summer of 1911 that I first knew of the Belgrave Harriers. It came about more or less by accident, as such things often do, and was the result of some good natured kidding. As was the custom then, and perhaps still is, new and junior employees to a firm were fair game for practical jokes of all kinds. In my case, as a result of speaking out of turn regarding my powers as a runner at school, a match was cooked up between a fellow worker, also an easy mark, and myself for a half mile race to be held on Clapham Common which happened to also be used on occasions by a few of the Belgrave Harriers. A silver medal was donated by a former athlete. I would not mention the time of the race even if I remembered; neither do I remember what eventually happened to the medal which I won. I do remember the two brothers who introduced me to the Bels though, their name if memory serves was Blow. My first recollection was the opening of the Winter season at the Spencer Arms Lower Richmond Road, Putney, almost opposite the Cricketers Headquarters of our then traditional rivals Ashcombe Athletic Club. The Spencer Arms was being run at that time by the former British Professional Sculling champion Tom Sullivan, who left England for Germany about 1913 to coach the Berlin Rowing Club and was interned in Germany during the 1st World War. The cross country course in those days was across Putney Common, Barnes Common, up Sheen Lane and into Richmond Park. Another feature of the Bels of those days for a short time, was a Boxing Section in the Club and a working agreement with the Bellevue Cycling Club, this faded out after a time however. The cross country champion at that time was Sergeant Carroll of the Irish Guards, who informed me on noticing I had a blistered toe after a training spin that I should cut my toe nails, a fact I passed on many times later during Army Service. Lew Causer then celebrating his 20th season at cross country was leader and coach of the novice pack and a fine coach he was,ably assisted at times by George Tyrer. About this time George still ran with the club occasionally, although I think only as second claim, his original club being the Highgate Harriers. Later during the war he was engaged full time on war work in the City but kept fit by walking to and from business every day,about 10 to 15 miles round trip. The next winter quarters I remember were at the Boileau Arms at the Barnes end of Hammersmith Bridge this did not last long as there was more road work than cross country to the course. It was then that the Club moved to St. Saviours Parish Hall, Raynes Park. These quarters were used until the outbreak of the 1914 - 18 war. Among the members joining during the years just preceeding the war was Jimmy Belchamber who after a season or two became an outstanding member of the long distance walking team in the London to Brighton Walk. An amusing sidelight in connection with Raynes Park quarters was the special issue of railway tickets, from Clapham Junction to Raynes Park, by Battersea Borough Council for cemetery visitors. A surprising number of Belgrave Harriers seemed to have relatives interned from Battersea! While winter quarters at Raynes Park sufficed for Saturday training spins, inter club races and championships, it did not solve the problem of mid-week training. This was solved by using the Rising Sun in Tite Street Chelsea and running through Belgravia into Hyde Park and along Rotten Row; this still entailed considerable road work. The problem of finding adequate country headquarters was always a problem even over fifty years ago, and must be even more of a headache today. It was, I think, the season of 1913-14 that the club got placed in the South of Thames Junior C.C. Championships for the first time in many years. While visiting Battersea Park and Hurlingham recently my thoughts went back to pre 1st war days and the problems facing the committee of arrangements for club track meets. Once or twice a meeting was held at Stamford Bridge, but more often in the interest of economy Battersea Park was the venue, this meant several of us getting to the park an hour or two before the meet to mark the course and starts. Dressing facilities were provided by the use of the old cricket dressing room, showers or any other kind of baths were unheard of. A great number of people often talk of the good old days, perhaps they were for some but for most athletes they were ones of considerable inconvenience and sometimes financial hardship. However, what we lacked in ability was more than made up in the good fellowship on the part of all the members of the club.

The Easter Holiday - Halls Green, Training Camp.

Reg Hopkins

Reg Hopkins reports:

This was the first of what I hope will become a regular feature for our younger members. Being the first, however, we naturally had teething troubles. A late start going was most disappointing and the cancellation of a proposed road relay on the homeward trip due to expected Bank holiday traffic conditions, that didn't materialise, was most unfortunate. And all, of course missed Mum's cooking ! Knowing now the possibilities of this place, and by planning our programme rather better, and making our own travelling arrangements we can eliminate those points that didn't favour us and spend an even better weekend next time. All 14 Boys certainly did enjoy themselves. The weather, although not warm, did stay dry and allow us to spend most of the day out and about in the country. We spent one whole day canoeing on the river Medway (very tough on the arms - good for the throwers) and this was great fun for all, only one boy(who shall remain nameless) fell in. Having been told that rock climbing could be dangerous we gave that a miss, but we did have an enjoyable afternoon hill climbing. Ascending a steep hill with loose soil and very few handholds took quite a lot of strength and determination and was pretty hard on the seat of one's pants coming down. Among other activities we enjoyed were high jump and javelin contests. We also had weight training sessions every day and these proved most popular. I think, however, that the most popular pastime was throwing Nigel Bacon in the swimming pool ! We played some good games of football too (we have quite a good team - how about a match you seniors?) The funniest occurance throughout the weekend, apart from Nigel being chased "Starkers" through the countryside, was being confronted by the bigest Great Dane we have ever seen, during a cross country run. All put on a brave front and tried to frighten it away but the look of terror on our faces was hilarious. As we had planned, we showed some movie films, including Belgrave's own classic; this the boys loved and it certainly increased their pride in being "Bels Boys". Tom Carter and Tony Brooks paid us a visit on the Saturday. It was a great pleasure to all when they arrived just in time to give lifts back to the camp; we had been on the return march of an 8 mile cross country ramble and still had some way to go. All in all it was a good weekend and I am already planning the next one. To conclude, I would very much like to pass on some comments from various people outside the club regarding the behaviour of our lads. For example, "what a friendly and cheerful crowd they all were" and "how willing to share the chores that had to be done". Well done lads, we are proud of you. Those who went. JOHN KNIFTON, one of our leading walkers a few years back, has been in the U.S.A now for three years--- and he is still in the heel and toe game. Not just, but to the tune of a 92:46 20 Kilos! The occasion was the annual Nat Osk Memorial 20 Kilos in New York's Central Park and, of the 52 competitors, John finished third behind two top American walkers, Dave Romansky (90:04) and Ray Somers (91:35). John, who works at the Texaco Oil Company's Research Centre, writes "I am presently training five times a week but have changed my schedule. I no longer bother with strolling, instead I've reverted to strip training spins of 15-20 miles twice a week. This type of training gives, I feel, increased strength without jeopardising speed". A foot injury in the week preceding the American National 25 Kilos race upset his preparation, but he still finished 10th in 2:05:32 over a measured course (Ron Laird, the U.S.A.'s No.1 was 3rd). By the birth of his son, Matthew, last December John made former Belgravian Editor BEN KNIFTON a proud grandfather.

Harry mentions that for many years he has been a "hanger-on" to that band of voluntary workers who organise our top athletic meetings, and he wonders "how the gaps will be bridged between the professional administration at the top, club organisation and the body of people at present prepared to turn out in all weathers, using their extensive knowledge without recompense". Stop Press! KINNAIRD TROPHY RESULT

Belgrave can feel well pleased with this result. Polytechnic invite the ten clubs they consider to be the strongest in the land and this was our first year back in. Birchfield, Achilles and Poly were always reckoned to be out of reach but the points score shows that not much extra was needed to get in amongst these teams. After all, if Derek Boosey and Arnold Bent had been available our points score would very likely have been boosted to 36 or 37---by such margins are these trophies won or lost. Full strength teams are absolutely vital for the forthcoming National League matches. John Thresher had one of the easiest wins of the afternoon, taking the 1500 metres by a 3 seconds margin in 3:46.2---equivalent to a 4min-4sec mile. The heat was oppressive and Bill Kerr, running at the worst time of all, showed magnificent fighting qualities in the 5000 metres. He finished 4th in 14:36.2, 14.2 seconds behind P. Stewart (Birchfield). What team spirit by Laurie O'Hara! Having done his day's training, and all set for an afternoon's spectating, he was roped in for the 5,000 metres and finished 9th of the 17 runners. Nick Head did season's bests in Javelin and Shot, as did Tony Brooks in his heat of the 800 metres. Jim Vivian finished 5th in the 200 metres final and John Dear 4th in steeplechase (9:51.6).
