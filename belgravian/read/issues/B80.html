Editorial · page 3

Our very heartiest congratulations have been earned by Harry Churcher on his sequence of five wins in Open Seven Miles Walks this season. The manner of the accomplishment and the ease with which Harry has won have been features of the season, and called forth praise from all directions. This succession of triumphs is probably a record in the annals of Walking, and we hope to see our man win the A.A.A. 7 Miles Championship, and next season repeat his very fine winning sequence in open road events.

* * * * *

Our teams also have been very successful and five scratch team wins have been recorded in the five 7 Miles Open events. These wins have been very emphatic and have always been well backed by others who could still have made sure of a team win, even if those in front had faltered. A glance at the Walking results included in this issue will show how complete has been our monopoly of the early placings in the open events, and also indicate the considerable improvement made by some of the younger walkers and newcomers. We trust the forthcoming championships will prove equally successful for our teams!

* * * * *

The Championship season for both runners and walkers is about to start, and we confidently expect all our supporters to assist by turning up at the various events. Incidentally, a helpful comment is appreciated much more by a competitor than all the alleged humour you have to offer. An idea of position, number of club team men in front, etc., may do something towards winning the race.

In our last issue we announced that a fund had been formed to provide a "W. A. Rice" Memorial Trophy. Some of Bill's friends promptly expressed their desire to be associated with this; and others, no doubt, will wish to do so before the fund is closed. No subscription list will be circulated, but those wishing to identify themselves with the plan are invited to get into touch with A. A. Harley within the next few weeks. Subscriptions have ranged between 1/- and £2 - 2 - 0, and we have no doubt that many who came under the influence of Bill's personality, appreciated his work for the Club, and enjoyed his friendship, will associate themselves with the idea before the fund is finally closed.

* * * * *

We were very glad to welcome our French visitors to Wimbledon on December 5th; also the Mayor and Mayoress of Wimbledon, who accorded the visitors a civic welcome.

Our thanks are also due to Frank Elson, who presented the Club with copies of an excellent souvenir programme, which realised something towards the expenses. We should also be lacking in gratitude if we did not thank our Social Hon. Secretary for the special attention he gave to the planning of a very fine meal and Social after the race; and L. J. Coleman for his invaluable services as Interpreter throughout the visit of our French friends.

* * * * *

See you at the "Southern" at Ascot?—the "Junior" at Hampstead Garden Suburb?—the "National" at Reading?

And also don't forget the Senior R.W.A. 20 miles Championship at Birmingham on May 14th!

* * * * *

Club ties, vests, badges, etc., may be had from the Hon. Treasurer, whose address appears on page 1.

Notice · page 4

The Annual Dinner-Dance.

Our Annual Dinner-Dance will take place at Gatti's Restaurant, William IV Street, Strand, on Saturday, May 7th.

Most unfortunately, the "News of the World" have, at the last minute almost, changed the date of the London—Brighton Relay from that originally fixed to May 7th—the date of our Dinner-Dance. In reply to our letter of protest at this late change of date, the organisers have replied regretting the inconvenience caused and saying that it is now impossible to alter their revised date. The letter concludes by saying that it would add greatly to our function if we could win the Cup earlier in the day and have it on the table in the evening!

An attempt has been made to fix an alternative date for our Dinner-Dance but, of course, to arrange another Saturday date at this late stage has been found impossible.

Accordingly, we intend to carry through our arrangements for May 7th, and have no doubt that every Club member will loyally support the Club Function. It is hoped to make some arrangements to enable members of our team to be back in London in time for the Dinner and Dance.

It is most unfortunate that we should have been placed in this dilemma so late in the season, and we look to every Club member to make our Annual Dinner-Dance the success it deserves to be.

Report · page 6

Race versus Cambridge University H. & H., S.L.H., and Aldershot Command A.C.

At Wimbledon, on November 6th, we entertained a team from Cambridge University, also members of the South London H., and Aldershot Command H.

The spectators witnessed a very fine race and over our rather light course the two Cambridge "Blues," E. C. Weir and W. M. F. Hudson, ran right away from our lads to finish an easy first and second.

Arthur Penny ran well to gain third place, with Bert Footer and Dan Patience next, followed by Les Cohen and Tom Carter.

Thus it will be seen our boys packed extremely well in the team race and we won fairly comfortably from Cambridge, with S.L.H. beating the Army lads for third place.

It was the first time that any of these clubs had visited our quarters at Wimbledon and we trust they enjoyed their run with us, and look forward to renewing associations next season.

In conjunction with the race we held a club handicap and this was won by Dan Patience. Much to everyone's surprise the Handicapper gave him 2 mins. start and Dan certainly made the best use of it.

There was a tie for second place, this being between H. G. Verney (1.45) and Rod Patience (2.45). Both of these ran extremely well in the scratch race and well deserved their handicap awards.

It was a pleasure also to see Sgt. Cotterell, the ex-Southern champion and English International, turning out for the Aldershot boys and they should gain much useful advice from such an experienced performer.

The South of the Thames "Junior Race."

F. R. Webb

BY F. R. WEBB.

Saturday, 13th November. Whether it was the date, or lack of voices with that familiar cry of "Up the Bels" was responsible for our team's failure to get placed in the above race I cannot say; but the support they did have must have been the poorest ever. However, the few that did go witnessed a most exciting race.

The course, which was one of three laps consisting mainly of grassland.

Harry Parker, one of the Club's officials, being President of the South of the Thames Association, had the honour of starting the race. It was also hoped that he would present the Shield to our Club as the winning team, but it did not materialise.

Our boys got away fairly well and the field, having covered about half a mile of level going, came to a stretch of plough. After getting over this, they then continued out into the country, and thence back to the starting field, which was preceded by a slight rise, which upset those who had made too fast a start.

The first to appear was a bunch of about six runners. A little further back came Tobin in 12th position, followed by Lucas 19th, Morrell 22nd, and Murdoch 36th. Our next man was round about the 50 mark. On their next appearance they had covered about 3 miles, and Roberts, of Mitcham, was leading by a few yards from Isted, of Westerham ,and Shirley, of Ashcombe.

Our boys had failed to improve their positions. In fact, they had lost some! Tobin was still our first man, and had lost a few places, as had Lucas and Morrell. Murdoch had gained, so had Clements and Stride. These two were running very strongly and we still hoped there might be a chance of a place. Herbert and Selby were definitely below their usual form, running in 102nd and 113th positions. Johnson, who was in the "B" team, was going very strongly in the 80th position.

Again the runners entered the starting field and commenced the last lap; but no claret and gold vests could be seen in the first twenty places. So came the end, with Clements running strongly over the last lap to finsh 18th. Tobin (24th), Murdock (25th) and Lucas (26th) completed our team, which was placed 5th.

Eastleigh Town S.C., who owe much to our old friend Tommy Green, were the winners. As previously mentioned, our "A" team finished fifth with 93 points. Our "B" team scored 410 points, and "C" team 663.

There were 270 starters, 254 of whom finished the course.

Report · page 8

Inter-Club Race with Mitcham A.C.

This fixture took the place of the usual four-cornered social run with Croydon H., Epsom H. and Mitcham A.C. It has been decided to hold one fixture each season, this to take place in turn from each club's quarters.

Owing to Mitcham's quarters being on the small side, we held this year's race from "Belgrave Hall"; and a good crowd was seen out, although Epsom and Croydon were unable to field sufficiently large numbers to count in the team race.

So we had a race with the Mitcham boys and, scoring 20 aside, came out on top by 364 pts. to 456 pts.

First man home was Arthur Penny; followed by S. O. A. Palmer and R. Thorogood, both of Mitcham.

Then came Tom Carter, Les Cohen and Harry Verney, of the "Bels"; followed by L. Scott, of Mitcham, with that old war-horse Bert Footer next.

After that, positions were more or less equally divided between the two clubs, although our boys seemed to pack in small groups and this undoubtedly helped when the final scores were worked out.

Whether we can take this as a good omen as regards the later "big" races remains to be seen, as in fairness to Mitcham we must say that they did not have their full complement of Welshmen running for them in this race, and there is no doubt that the influx of their "Hospital" boys makes them a much more formidable proposition.

Report · page 9

The Club 7 Miles C.C. Championship.

On December 11th we held the Club 7 Miles Championship for the "Blackstaffe" Shield, with the usual sealed handicap in conjunction.

This year, however, marked a very special occasion, none other than the first visit of a foreign club to compete against our boys on their own soil.

At the visitors' request the course was one of 6 miles, but the Committee decided that it should still rule as the Club Championship, and so this year's winner of the "Blackstaffe" Shield had less distance to run than usual.

Our visitors were the runners of the L'Union Sportive Metropolitaine, Paris. They sent 7 men and so it was decided to score 6 men aside in the team race.

The course was the usual one of 2 laps, but, as explained, it was shortened slightly by cutting off a piece out in the country, so that from a spectator's point of view the runners were seen at the usual points of vantage.

On the first lap the Frenchmen broke away in a body to go in front, but Pat Parker soon strode ahead only to give way to Arthur Penny and when they came round the first time, Arthur was well in front and running strongly. Dan Patience had come up to Pat Parker, and then came Les Cohen and Bert Footer followed by Tom Carter and then the Frenchmen, running more or less in a solid bunch.

When they came in sight again at the Windmill, Arthur Penny was still leading, with Dan Patience a comfortable second, and Pat Parker third. Les Cohen and Bert Footer were still close together, but four of the "Metro" boys had come up to Carter's shoulder, and the leader of them appeared quite fresh.

In the run home the positions remained unchanged but Tom Carter fought well, and although having to give way to two of his rivals he managed to fight off the others and finished just in front of three more "Metro" lads.

Thus although the Frenchmen packed very well it was too far back to give any chance of success in the team race.

With the first five men home our position was unassailable, and our captain, Tom Carter, completed our scoring team of six men which we had in the first eight positions.

We are sure, however, that our very good friends from Paris learnt a wrinkle or two which should stand them in good stead in future races.

The winner of the sealed handicap was a very promising young runner, R. E. Stride, Jnr., and on his running in the scratch race, he well deserved his victory. J. C. Smart, who is a first-claim member of the Orion Harriers, finished second on handicap; while third place was gained by Dick Michael, who is better known as a track runner.

With a Tea and Social Evening after the run we thus ended what was quite an event in the history of our Club, and hope that in future years we may hold events of a similar nature, which do much to cement the good feeling and fellowship between athletes of different countries.

The Surrey County C.C. Championship.

The most important feature of this year's Championship was that, after many years in our possession, the title passed into another club's keeping—and Mitcham A.C. are the present holders. This was Mitcham's first win and the manner of their winning calls for the heartiest congratulations. We were sixteen points behind, and then came a huge gap and Thames Hare and Hounds in third position with over double our number of points.

Our team was not disgraced, and our performance of placing twelve men in the first 53 scoring positions was very satisfying.

Middlesex Cross Country Championship.

By T. J. Flower.

It was a long trek to "Folly Farm," New Barnet, but well worth the trip, as our "boys" regained the "Daily Telegraph" Cup, after a very keen and exciting race .

We were all feeling fairly confident before the race, and although Alf Taylor had to drop out early in the race through leg trouble, it seemed to add zest to the others. The spectators saw quite a lot of the race, and with very little effort on our part we were able to see the run at five different stages. The race started in a mad dash and after first 700 yards had been covered, Cpl. Hier and J. Bain led the field. They went out of sight into the woods at this point and we waited patiently for their return. Cpl. Hier still led with Bain, and Mason about 15 yards behind. 50 yards further away came D. Patience (4th) and he appeared to be running very strongly. Then they came thick and fast, but to my surprise Tobin was our second man in the 7th position. Verney was 11th, R. Patience 12th, Smart 23rd, J. Flower 28th, Shields 33rd—very satisfactory for the half-way stage. 85 points for 6—with another close up—was very cheering indeed, and they kept these position round the field and so out again into the woods.

On entering, the field for the last 700 yards of the race, F. L. Mason, of T.V.H, held a lead of about 12 yards and running in beautiful style and very fast, gradually drew away from J. Bain, last year's champion, who ran a very gallant race, game to the very end. Unfortunately for him he met a man at the top of his form, who finished not only very strongly but comparatively fresh—a worthy "Champion of Middlesex."

After this excitement, I concentrated on the Team Race. D. Patience, after a ding-dong battle over the last 700 yards, forced his way to a 10 yards lead over C. W. Griffiths (an ex-champion of Middlesex) and so finished in the 4th position. Very gratifying to myself, waiting to collect the discs! Then came a T.V.H. man (7th), closely followed by Verney (8th) and Tobin (9th). Then two more "Valley" men, 12th and 16th, D. Patience for the "Cads" very close up, 17th, then J. E. Flower 22nd, followed by yet another "Valley" man, 23rd; and what a relief, Shields and Smart, 25th and 26th respectively. A rapid addition of points gave us 84.

The T.V.H. scorer, standing beside me, said, "Our boys scored 96, how did you do, Cadogan?"

Feeling as proud as if I had actually scored in the team, I replied, "84 and I think we've won it!"

"Well done! and congratulations to your lads," was his reply—very typical of the sporting crowd they are!

There was sympathy for Smart, who after running a very plucky race, just missed the winning team by one second. To him I say, "Better luck next time! it's your turn next!"

A. Patience surprised the natives by his storming finish. He was told (wrongly) that he was 6th man and he put in a tremendous effort, gaining 15 or more places over the last 700 yards. A. Smith put up his best performance in this race.

And so for home, feeling quite content in spite of the rain and a 20 minutes' walk to the station.

The International Cross-Country Race at la Croix de Berny, Paris, on 16th January, 1938.

G. Webb and H. Parker as Club Officials.

Unfortunately, a very rough crossing awaited the party that left London on Friday, 15th January, and on their arrival in Paris on the Saturday morning all were glad to take a few hours' rest. After an early lunch the party made a rather extensive tour of Paris in a coach kindly provided by the French Club; and later adjourned to their Hotel for tea, and to discuss Bert Footer's programme for their evening's entertainment. It was quite evident that Mr. Footer knew his Paris well and did not require the services of an interpreter who had been placed at the party's disposal.

The President and Mrs. Bayliss, accompanied by Mr. Togna, joined the company early in the evening and soon we were to hear the voices of our President, Tom Carter, Bert Footer and Arthur Penny coming over the air from the Radio City Broadcasting Station in Paris, telling the French folk why the Belgrave Harriers were in their gay city.

Soon after breakfast on Sunday we were speeding on our way to the Headquarters of the Union Sportive Metropolitaine at le Croix de Berny. This famous organisatiaon caters for sport of all kinds and its hundreds of acres of land all set our for sport are indeed wonderful. I doubt if there is anything like it to be seen in this country. The Club House is a huge building and soon we were taking lunch with hundreds of other competitors. 38 teams were to compete in the Internationl Cross Country Race which included the Belgian Champions (Union St-Gilloise) and Belgrave Harriers as the only British entry.

After the parade of the runners the "Off" was given and soon we had the pleasure of seeing Parker leading the field, with the rest of the team, excepting Verney, lying quite handy. Verney, it must be explained, had developed a temperature, and though he persisted in turning out, it was very evident that he was unwell.

At 3 miles Parker was still leading, with Penny and Footer coming through to the front. At this stage it seemed, however, that our boys were further back than they should be and one began to wonder if that awful sea-crossing was affecting their running. At 5 miles Parker had dropped to 5th place, and though Penny and Footer were running strongly, and Carter and Cohen striving hard, it could easily be seen that we could not win. The team of Algerians (U.S. Belfortaine) was running wonderfully well, and had 3 men in the first four.

Our boys ran very pluckily, however, and were beaten by the narrow margin of 5 points for second place. In the last lap of the race, Penny came up to pass Parker and finished first man for us in the 7th position. Parker finished 9th, Footer 11th, Carter 23rd, Cohen 27th, Tobin 32nd, Patience 34th, and Verney continued gamely to finish 117th.

The Club Boxing Day Meeting.

At Battersea Park on Boxing Day morning we held our annual track meeting and, being blessed with fine weather, we had a good crowd and, what is more important, good entries.

The winner looks a very promising sprinter and, provided he does not overdo matters too early in the season, he should prove a very useful addition to our sprint team.

The 3 Miles walking handicap provided a rather poor field (that is, numerically) and "Ginger" Coleman had no difficulty in winning from Reg. Elson, with a promising young walker in A. E. Elliott, third. Joe Coleman came under the ban of the judges when well placed, while Percy Wright found his Christmas festivities too much for him and retired. We are indebted to Mr. Askew for so kindly presenting a consolation prize in the fourth walker, the only pity being that there were not more members of the walking section there to battle for it. It must, of course, be borne in mind that several walkers were at Croydon, contesting the annual 18¾ miles Boxing Day Walk.

As usual, at this meeting, the most eagerly awaited event was the 2 miles run and a large field turned out for this. George Still was on the limit mark, while Arthur Penny was virtual scratch with 10 yards.

George kept in front for several laps but the field gradually overhauled him and with a lap to go things looked very interesting.

Arthur Penny had come through in great style but still had a good leeway to make up on the leaders. Dick Tobin was in front with A. Harper hanging on his shoulder and A. H. Orton coming up very fast in third place . Down the straight Tobin increased his lead and Orton could not quite catch Harper, while "Champ" Smith had run well to get fourth position. Arthur Penny finished very strongly and Bert Shaw just pipped him for fifth place, which, considering how close he was to the winner, made Arthur doing a very good performance.

In conjunction with the racing events we had a Field Event match with the Metropolitan Police A.C., there being three events and scoring for the first six places in each.

Winding Up The Country Season.

So far this season, results have not been as we hoped they would, but with the real Championship tests now coming along, we hope that our lads will prove their supremacy over the country.

Actually our runners, both Juniors and Seniors, have performed very well; and in the three Surrey County races we have recorded a treble of second places which to some clubs would be very pleasing.

Also our members with Middlesex qualifications scored a win in their County race, while running for their second-claim club.

The next championship in which we compete will be the South of Thames "Senior" race to be held at Cobham. The course there is rather a heavy one, and not likely to suit our boys, while owing to the numerous and, might I say, tedious barring clauses, we are debarred from fielding what can be termed a representative team.

We come then to the Southern Counties Championships (both Senior and Junior) which will be held at Ascot. Shades of "Brown Jack"! What is the world coming to? Alas! the modern trend is to make cross-country running easier in every way. They even have artificial obstacles, and quite possibly they will one year stage a race indoors at Wembley!

Seriously, though, there is quite a lot to be said for such a venue as Ascot. The dressing accommodation, with the consequent after-the-race facilities, should be A.1, and I am all in favour of anything which is to the benefit of the competing athlete.

The spectators should also have a good view of the proceedings, and the "gate" will also benefit the Association, which possibly is the reason for this hankering after race-courses as venues.

In the Junior race I am afraid our team will be rather weak, as R. E. Stride and Bill Lucas, who were our first two home in our club Junior championship, are now both over age. It should, however, provide useful experience to the rest of our Juniors, and we wish them the best of luck.

As to the Senior race, we must expect hot opposition from Mitcham A.C. and Surrey A.C. At first glance, Mitcham would appear favourites, but Surrey should not be disregarded, and they usually put up a good performance in this race.

With our best team in the field we should, however, give them all a rare old battle and we will leave it by saying "may the best team win."

On March 12th the E.C.C.U. hold the "National" championship and this year the race will be staged at Coley Park, Reading.

We can be assured that the local committee will make arrangements for everybody, and I hope our Club supporters will travel to Reading on the 12th to see what is, without doubt, the finest cross-country race of the Season.

Special buses will run from the station to Coley Park, and the course is one which gives the spectators a constant view of the runners.

Last year we lost the title by 4 points, the holders, Birchfield Harriers, having a rare fright before they found victory was theirs once again. This year, we hope the tide will turn, and that we shall regain the trophy which we held in 1935.

Our teams have, in recent years, always seemed to give of their best in this race; and we hope that Tom Carter, in his first season as Captain, will have the honour of leading them to victory.

I am sure all our lads will give him their loyal support. With such names as Penny, Footer, Ginty, Parker, D. Patience, Carter and Cohen to think of, it will be seen that we have every chance of success to bring the title where it has so seldom been in late years, namely the South.

On March 19th will be held the "Round Ilford" Road Relay, and we hope to enter teams, as in past years, and so give all our members a chance of representing the club in "open" team competition.

The country season is now at on end and we start road training with a view to the London—Brighton Relay race.

On April 9th we had decided to hold our usual 5 miles Road Race with sealed handicap in conjunction, but whether this date will be changed in view of the alteration of "Brighton" date, will have to be advised to members later. In any case I hope all our members will support this fixture at Wimbledon, for as mentioned, there is the handicap to go for, and I shall be glad to receive entries in good time before the date fixed for this race.

We now come to what is for us a rather unfortunate happening and that is the fact that the "News of the World," after fixing April 30th for their annual "classic," have at this late date suddenly altered the date to May 7th.

It is understood that it is owing to clashing with the annual Stock Exchange walk to Brighton, although why this question was not gone into when the date was first fixed is to be wondered at.

As our members are aware, May 7th is the date arranged for our Club Dinner and naturally we cannot expect to have half the club at "Gatti's" and half at Brighton.

The matter will be discussed by our Committee and what will happen remains to be seen. It would be a thousand pities if we did not compete in this year's race, after being winners for three consecutive years—a feat not previously accomplished in the history of this event. We lost last year by a bare few yards to Mitcham and had looked forward to reversing this result. Our absence from the race might be misconstrued by some, and it is to be hoped that we shall be able to arrange to compete, although very galling, and absolutely lacking in consideration.

A Message From Paris.

My Dear Sir,

I send you herewith the "proof" of an article that I am having inserted in the next number of our magazine, the "Metro Sportif." It is an article concerning our meeting with the Belgrave Harriers, and I would thank you very much if you would be kind enough to check me up on the details that I have mentioned. I hope you will excuse me for troubling you and meanwhile I beg you to accept my sincere thanks and my most cordial wishes, etc., etc.

WITH OUR FRIENDS AT WIMBLEDON. THE BELGRAVE HARRIERS.

One will read further on, under the heading of "Cross Country," an article describing the visit of our team to England on the 10, 11, 12 December, where they were the guests of the Belgrave Harriers, who, you will remember, were our guests last season at La Croix de Berny. It is not the time or place to criticise the result of our match, which left no illusions, but rather to analyse and see what useful purpose has been gained by our visit, and a reception that will certainly go down to posterity in the annals of our Cross Country section.

To give you some idea of how formidable were our opponents, I am going to give you some details. First of all, let us remember that the Belgrave Harriers specialise in athletics, walking and, above all, cross country running. Founded in 1887, they, along with the Birchfield Harriers, are reckoned among the top flight of clubs in England. They were cross country champions of England in 1935, winners of the London to Brighton relay race for 1934, 35, 36, winners of the Surrey County Championship from 1925 to 1937, and this year further added to their laurels by capturing our cross-country championship at La Croix de Berny in 1937.

Their Patron is Sir Samuel Hoare, former Secretary of State at the Foreign Office, and former First Lord of the Admiralty, now Home Secretary, who combines this presidency with that of the Lawn Tennis Association of Great Britain.

Their active President and Hon. Secy. is our excellent friend, Mr. W. L. Bayliss, who has never failed to put at our disposal his kindly services.

The Belgrave Harriers have more than five hundred active members. Their training ground is situated at Wimbledon, a south-western suburb of London which has a population of 65,000 inhabitants, and which is world-famous on account of its famous tennis stadium. Wimbledon, situated at only 15 kilometres from the heart of London, is served by the Southern Railway and also an underground line. In close proximity there are the "commons" (Putney, Richmond, etc.), fine sandy stretches, sprinkled with woods, and many gradients, which lends itself perfectly to the art and training of cross-country running, and other sports among them being the noble one of "Equitation."

Well, here we are with them at Wimbledon. After a wet and unpromising morning a complete change came about mid-day, and the afternoon proved to be fine and cold. Here, on the premises which the Belgrave Harriers have just acquired and which they intend to demolish in order to build a model club house, is a big hall, comfortably heated. On the floor a badminton court is marked out. At the moment this hall also serves as a cloak room and leading off the hall are several baths, simple in style, but comfortable and above all, warm; whilst a little further on one discovers the pantry, where all sorts of good things were prepared for the dinner that we all enjoyed in common with the Belgrave Harriers, and its many friends.

The Club "Junior" Open Walk Championship.

On December 11th we had hoped to welcome a team of French walkers to an inter-club event, to be decided with all club members competing and with the Club Junior Championship for the "Horwood" Cup, in conjunction. Unfortunately, as the day approached, our French friends intimated that they would not be able to send a complete team, but that Cambrai, a well-known walker on the other side of the Channel, would represent them.

Cambrai quickly went to the front when the Mayor of Wimbledon started the race, in company with our fastest men. He soon found the pace too fast, however, and eventually finished in sixth position. This does not represent his true form, for he has shown some remarkable pace for an hour, and up to 50 kilometres, in Paris. We understood, however, that he had competed in a long-distance race the previous week, and no doubt this occasioned some loss of speed, if a time of 57 min. 25 secs. over our seven miles course deserves this description.

The actual winner, Harry Churcher, won reasonably easily from Percy Wright, who made a good show.

Then came the "Juniors," battling out the Championship—probably one of the finest fields seen out in this race. After a most strenuous struggle, Colin Sutton gained a narrow win over Joe Coleman. Bert Clayton, our Eastleigh member, took the third medal in his first appearance in the Club Championship.

Form throughout was most satisfactory. Len Coleman would probably have given a better performance had he not devoted the previous evening and the Saturday morning to the care of the French visitors, to whom he acted as interpreter.

It was pleasing to see a comparative newcomer in "Jock" Ogilvie take the handicap first, with G. S. Doubleday, another newcomer to walking, in third position. Second prize went to Reg Elson, an old-timer showing a welcome return to form.

The Ashcombe-St. George's A.C. Open Walk.

Held from Sheen Schools over a seven miles course into Richmond Park on January 2nd, our boys showed their liking for familiar roads by recording a truly amazing performance. Recent form has made us expect some good walking from our teams, but to finish the first five men home in a race of this nature is deserving of a welter of superlatives. Harry Churcher scored his usual win in comfortable fashion, and Len and Joe Coleman finished together, equal second. To say that Joe walked wonderfully well is to invite rhapsodic language to describe brother Len's performance and we will content ourselves with reminding our readers that fifteen months previously Len Coleman won a Club novice race! Captain Charlie Churcher finished in fourth position, and Percy Wright was fifth. Actually there is no scratch team race in this particular event (only a set of prizes for fastest team not gaining handicap awards) or Percy would have found himself in the unique position of finishing fifth in an open race, and yet not scoring in his Club team.

Reg. Elson confirmed his recent good form by finishing 10th, and here we would like to record our appreciation of the good nature and splendid sportsmanship of one of our second-claim members, W. E. Ives. Elson was awaiting the return of his shoes from the repairer, who had undertaken to have them at the dressing room in time for the race. Unfortunately, he failed in this, and Reg Elson was ready, all but for shoes. Ives, himself stripped ready to race, hearing of the position, insisted upon the faster man using his shoes, with happy results. We are all grateful to Ives for his self-sacrificing action.

In the handicap team race our "B" team was second. This team consisted of Doubleday, Ogilvie, Roberts and True, the two first-named taking their first team prizes in Club teams. Our "A" team was placed third; and so we may be considered to have had an excellent Saturday afternoon out.

With The Walkers.

At this time of the year, all with any interest in walking turn their thoughts towards the "Junior" Championship, to be held this year from Hampstead Garden Suburb, on March 5th. It is not our usual custom to laud our prospects to the skies; but this year we should be false to ourselves if we did not recognise that we have an outstanding team to represent us. The form of past weeks indicates that we have a team which may well set up a new "low-points" record and better the scores of Surrey Walking Club (1929, 22 points), and our score of 1933 (26 points)—the two best yet recorded.

Certainly it is true to say that practically every other club expects us to win the Championship once again, and our boys will do their utmost not to disappoint them! All being well, our first team will consist of A. H. Bridger, J. G. Coleman, L. J. Coleman, R. F. Elson, E. C. Heather, S. C. Roberts, W. H. Snell, C. F. Sutton. All are certain to show a high standard of performance, and we have hopes of supplying the individual champion.

We shall also have a second team in the field and we can be sure that they will also make a good show.

For the benefit of those who do not fully understand the term "Junior" as applied to this race, it is as well to point out that this has nothing to do with age. A member of a winning team; winners of A.A.A. first-class and R.W.A. first-class standard medals; and members of placed teams in the "Senior" 20 Miles Championship (together with one or two other qualifications), debar a walker from competing in the "Junior" Championship.

One can always depend upon a good-class field, despite the various barring clauses; and it is the ambition of every walker to obtain his "Junior" gold medal.

We hope that our team will receive plenty of support. The ten-mile course is of three laps, and presents plenty of opportunity to see the race. The journey is easily made by Underground to Golders Green station, thence a 1d. bus fare going towards Finchley, to Willifield Way. The dressing rooms will be at the Metropolitan W.C. Headquarters at the Hampstead Garden Suburb Club.

We expect to see many Belgrave members there to give an encouraging word to our walkers; and, we trust, to see and help them record the Club's sixth win in this Championship, which carries with it the "Garnet" Cup.

* * * * *

And meanwhile, what of the Senior 20 Miles Championship to be held on May 14th at Birmingham? This is still the most important event of the Walking calendar, and we hope to succeed in our efforts to take this Championship once again. In recent years the title has eluded us, although we have always been in the placings and on occasions lost the Championship by one and two points only.

In 1938 we should like to make the Championship ours again, and we trust all our walkers will consider how they can help to this end. We have the nucleus of a wonderfully good team and with conscientious training and the determined endeavour to do well, nothing can stop us.

Up to the time of writing, our season's walking successes have been many, and it would be a very fitting conclusion to the Winter season if we were able to regain the "Senior" Championship.

* * * * *

It grows more and more obvious year by year that the Boxing Day Godstone-and-back walk of 18¾ miles is misplaced in the present calendar of walking events. It carries, of course, a large amount of traditional interest and as such always commands a fair entry; but it is very doubtful whether many of the entrants train assiduously and show to the best advantage. In fact, a walker intending to follow the season through, from October's 7 miles races to May's 20 miles championship, would be ill-advised to make a special preparation for the Boxing Day race.

In the last race held we entered the largest number of any club (with the possible exception of the promoters) and for the first few miles seemed likely winners of the scratch team race. The disqualification of C. E. Churcher, and gradual loss of places by A. H. Bridger as lack of distance training began to tell, dispelled our chances, despite the splendid "second-half" of Jack Bidgood, who eventually finished third. The winner, D. Christie-Murray (S.W.C.) was well away from the others at the finish and recorded 2 hrs. 38 min. 17 sec. for the distance. Our men finished as follows :—

Sutton was with the leaders at the half-disance but lost ground later; and one of the pleasing features was the performance of G. S. Doubleday in his first season.

With The Juniors.

H. M. Q.

After an absence of several years we entered, once again, for the Surrey County Youths' Race, held from Belgrave Hall on December 4th. Our boys, many of whom were racing over the country for the first time, were undaunted by the snow and sleet and finished a creditable second.

RESULT.

On December 18th, the biggest field in recent years turned out for the Club Junior (under 21) Championship. The course was altered slightly to cut out the brook but Stride's time of 29 mins. 15 secs. is, nevertheless, of the same excellent standard set by Stimpson and Clements in previous years. Stride became the holder of the "Savage Shield" with something to spare over W. E. Lucas, but there was some close racing for the minor positions. Beer and Edwards ran very well but Selby was right off form.

RESULT.

In the Surrey County Junior at Shirley, Mitcham ousted us from our position as holders and hopes that Lucas would bag the individual title were dispelled by the heavy cold which he had. However, our scoring four packed within half-a-minute, enabling us to take second place. Our non-scorers did well in their first attempt at a junior title and should improve considerably with the experience gained.

RESULT.

Our chances in the Southern Junior are not as bright as they have been in the last two or three years, because Lucas and Stride are both too old. However, Beer, Herbert, Edwards, Selby, Shrubb and Boyden should provide a scoring four capable of being placed. The majority of our other juniors are still under nineteen and with the promise they shew may well provide the club with winning teams in years to come.

Sprinters—A Call to Action!

L. G. Bennett

I should like to take this opportunity of thanking all the sprinters who, last season, so ably assisted the Club and, incidentally, whose performances gave me a great deal of encouragement in my first year as a Track Vice-Captain.

Last season was the most successful for several seasons, as far as the sprinters were concerned. The performances were good and will improve a great deal with correct training and encouragement.

The team spirit was a revelation to me. I felt I could always rely on the sprinters turning out. This trust was proved by the section being, so to speak, self-supporting. I think I am correct when I say that last season was the first for many years when the Club has not had to call on members from other sections of the Club to gain points for us in the Inter-Club Relay Shield. I write these next few words without a blush. The sprinters laid the foundation of a record number of points win to retain the Shield. These races proved to me that the right spirit is in all of you. Reserves were quite prepared to travel with the team; and in this respect I would like to thank Frank Simmonds for his good sportsmanship.

Enough said about last season. Let us now start seriously training for an even more strenuous track season. The next two months are those in which we can gain a solid foundation in the shape of a fit body.

Study your exercises and remember that concentration on the benefit of a particular exercise is the keynote to happy training.

Stomach, leg, arm and chest exercises should be started, and adhered to morning and night, increasing in intensity as the body is strengthened.

I am looking forward to meeting you all again, and March 5th, 1938, has been chosen as the first Saturday of track training for 1938 at Battersea Park, at 3 p.m. I appeal to you all, Seniors and Juniors, to support me on this date, as during training I wish to discuss with you our plans for 1938. Results last year proved that there is real talent in this section of the Club. Messrs. Botwright, Blunden, Pritchard, Skelley and others will all improve with experience. We (the Belgrave Harriers) would also like to see some of the older members back in training, such as Messrs. Bone, Mewes, Kealey and Co.

The Club has many promising juniors also. As I have already mentioned, Juniors will be very welcome on March 5th, and I can assure them I will do all I can to put them on the right road. Come along on March 5th and ask as many questions as you like.

One last word. Get your new shoes NOW, or have your present ones repaired so that they are comfortable for training.

Here's wishing you the best-ever season for 1938!

Ten Years Back.

Let's glance at our "Belgravian" of ten years ago and see what was happening at the close of 1927, and in the early days of 1928. Our Junior Walking Championship for the "Horwood" Cup, was won by J. G. Scammell, with F. H. Elson and S. L. King in the minor positions; and on the same day the 7 miles Cross-country Championship was won by Arthur Clark from E. A. Duffett and W. A. Rice. This latter race was held in conjunction with an inter-club event v. S. George's Services S.C. 34 finished the course, and the placed men in the handicap were F. G. Ford, A. Traquair and A. A. Harley.

Duffett was placed third in the Surrey County Cross-country Championship, running as an individual; and our scoring team on that day was H. E. Footer (6), A. D. Pyer (7), A. E. Smith (14), H. S. Cook (20), W. L. Bayliss (23), J. D. Andrews (26).

The 10 miles C.C. Championship was contested by 21 starters and provided A. Clark with another win. Duffett and Pyer were second and third; with W. G. Webb (fourth in the scratch race) the sealed handicap winner. T. W. Green, A. L. Fletcher, R. D. McMullen were first three in the 10 miles Walk; the handicap first prize going to J. E. Field.

The following paragraph, abstracted from our Gazette of ten years ago, gives some idea of the improvement since made. It reads: "At long last! Twenty-four years ago we gained third place in the South of the Thames Championship, and this year the performance has been repeated." A. Robertshaw, H. E. Footer, P. Khlopin, W. G. Webb, A. D. Pyer and W. L. Bayliss formed the team. In the "Southern" we had hopes of being placed for the first time, but the best we could do was tenth with 422 points! The race, held at Shenfield, was won by H. W. Payne; and our scoring team men were H. E. Footer (33), W. G. Webb (37), P. Khlopin (38), A. D. Pyer (56), W. S. Mitchell (125), A. Clark (133).

In the "National," held at Leamington in a snowstorm, our position was 17th of the 27 teams. Bill Webb was our first man home in 63rd position.

In 1928, the R.W.A. Junior Championship was held at Wembley and there were 238 starters. Sheffield United Harriers were the winners; and our team was third, the scoring team being Scammell (12th), F. H. Elson (17th), J. E. Field (22nd) and Carne (24th).

These were the happenings that stirred us ten years ago!
