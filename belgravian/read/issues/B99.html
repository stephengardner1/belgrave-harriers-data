EDITORIAL

The first post-war Winter season was a memorable one for the Belgrave Harriers. Our cross-country runners won every open championship. In November we had the first two teams home in the South of the Thames "Junior," and the first three individuals. This in itself was a remarkable feat as all of our pre-war "cracks" cannot run in this race owing to the barring clauses. In the New Year we won the Surrey County Title, whilst our Middlesex members, running for Cadogan A.C., took the Middlesex title. This was not regarded as very extraordinary as it had been done on various occasions before the war. The South of the Thames "Senior" Race resulted in another win for us. Here again, a large number of our best men are "barred." The Southern Counties' Cross-Country Championship, 7½ miles instead of the usual 10, was held from Belgrave Hall. Our Captain, Tom Carter, gained a well-earned and popular victory. With David Main third and four others in the first 20 positions, we gained a 99 points' margin over the second team. In fact, our team of 12 was home before any other team had finished its six scoring men! Then to the much-awaited English Championship, of which we were the proud holders. On March 9th our lads travelled to Leamington Spa and had another battle royal with our famous Midland rivals, Birchfield Harriers. Our team finished in the first 24 positions, and after a breath-taking adding up of the scores we were acclaimed winners with a ten-points' margin. We are indeed truly the Champion Club.

Of individuals, Tom Carter deserves our thanks and praise for the successful training and nursing of his team, also, of course, for his own brilliant running. It was a cause for general regret that Tom did not get another International vest. Tom had had a cold the week before the race, which no doubt left its effect. Len Herbert, by finishing seventh, was awarded an England vest, and we congratulate Len on adding his name to the distinguished company of Belgravians so honoured in the past. It is of interest to recall them: A. Allum, H. E. Footer (3), A. W. Penny (3), C. T. Carter, J. Ginty and E. S. Stimpson. Pat Parker qualified for an England vest, but chose to run for Ireland David Main has also entered the ranks of the Internationals, as he ran for Ireland. David Main and Geoff. Pearson scored in all of our winning teams, whilst Tommy Clinton and Dick Tobin graduated from the "B" team in the South of the Thames "Junior" to the winning team in the National.

The Juniors have also done very well. Derrick Burfitt has again been in a class of his own, winning all of his races with the grand ease of a champion. The best team effort was undoubtedly the gaining of third place in the English Youth's event, held at Leamington Spa on the same day as the "National ." Our lads have done well in the various Junior and Youth events, and it is pleasing to know that we have such good material for future Senior teams. Alf. Taylor sportingly took charge of them as A. W. Jannaway has been indisposed. Alf. has taken out packs of Juniors regularly, and their success is no doubt due to his training.

The walkers too have had a very good record for the season, Jack Rutland, after his transfer to town and subsequent demobilisation, has been in the front rank, winning most of the Open walks, including our own promotion. To defeat regularly the best walkers in the South is worthy of high commendation, and we look forward to Jack adding to his own and to the Club's laurels. Harry Churcher has forced his way to the fore,, and we hope to see him resist all challengers for his A.A.A. titles. Our popular captain, Charlie Churcher, has also been finding some of his pre-war form. With those returned from war service and those who kept things going during the war, we should be able to field strong teams at all distances. We lost our R.W.A. "Junior" title, which was not unexpected, as we had to build up a new team, having won this race on the last two occasions and many others.

A large number of our members are now back in civilian life, and many others are expected home shortly. The Juniors are still being called-up when their time comes, and others who were deferred during the war have also been re-directed into the Services. We are glad to say that many of them will be able to turn out for us, duties permitting. One case in point is our Track Captain, Jack Frost, now with the R.A.F. Jack is stationed near London, and hopes to turn up regularly.

We are greatly indebted to Ernie Duffett for the Notes on the various championships, an inclusion which we hope will interest both old and new members.

Tom Carter, Joe Coleman and Dick Savage are on the Committee of the Surrey County A.A.A. Frank Webb is on the Middlesex County A.A.A. Committee representing Cadogan A.C., Alf. Harley as a Past President, is also on this Committee, and is also Chairman R.W.A. Southern Committee, Syd. Roberts is Vice-Chairman of this Committee.

Eddie Jury has been elected a Vice-President of the Hampshire County A.A. Eddie has also been active in Road Walking events down Portsmouth way, and has several creditable performances to his name.

Charlie Jones and Harry Shields are on the Committee of the S.A.A.L. Ernie Duffett is Hon. Sec. of the S.W. section of the S.A.A.L.

Frank Thomson has resigned from the Southern Committee of the A.A.A., where he had represented the Belgrave Harriers for a good many years. Frank has been a devoted worker to the cause of athletics, and he was untiring in his efforts for the welfare of the Belgrave Harriers and athletics in general. It can be truly said of Frank that his interests in athletics were complete. Not only was he a worker for Track and Cross-Country running, but he was a zealous worker in connection with the S.A.A.L. Field Events contests, and was their first Vice-President. He achieved the honour of President of the R.W.A. Frank has also been a very wise counsellor to the Club, and still is, and was our President during the early war years. We extend to Frank our warmest thanks for his great services, and trust that he will enjoy the best of health for many years to come.

Our President, Dick Savage, has had his name added to the R.W.A. Judge's panel. We are sorry to learn that Dick had a bad attack of influenza, we hope that the rest at a convalescent home has brought him a complete recovery.

We were pleased to see two old friends of the Club at the recent Club Championships. Albert Macher started the runners in the race for the "Macher" Shield, and Dick Murphy started the walkers in the "Murphy" Shield race. Both were very pleased to see the lads in action again.

It is not generally understood how important is the rôle of the pointsmen in a cross-country race. They do not get any medals or congratulations, and, according to one Club official who had walked out to Kingsmere and back and returned with wet feet and tongue hanging out, the cups of tea are usually all gone by the time that they get back. The success of the Southern Counties' Championship was largely due to the band of enthusiastic clubmen who turned out and shivered at their points of duty. We must not overlook them, the Club values their services and keenness greatly. Amongst those who have given valuable assistance at Wimbledon are Frank Webb, Norman Dewberry, Bill Merryman, George Clay, Dicky Dumbrill, Charlie Jones and George Chantler.

A new monthly periodical is now issued entitled "Athletics." This is being sponsored by our old R.A.F. friend, Jimmy Green, of Gillingham fame. Subscription is 7/- per annum direct from P. W. Green, Little Grigsby, High Dewar Road, Rainham, Kent, or it can be got from your newsagent. There has been a great need for a journal of this sort, and "Athletics" admirably fills it. We hope that it has come to stay, and we wish Jimmy the Best of Luck for the continued success of his venture.

Our old friend Wally Jewell has been elected a Vice-President of the Southern Committee of the A.A.A.

Surrey W.C. announce that the first post-war London to Brighton walk will take place on September 14th.

Belgrave Hall is open every Saturday afternoon during the summer. Training nights are Tuesday and Thursday at Tooting Bec Track, also Sunday mornings. The University of London Track at Motspur Park is open for training on weekdays until 9 p.m., and Sundays 9 a.m. to 12.30 p.m., 6d. per day, 10/6 per season. Which brings us to the question of club trainers. The Club invites volunteers for this job, and it is hoped that some of the older members will be able to help. George Clay is again carrying on with this work at Malden, and will be pleased to hear from members who find it more convenient to use Motspur Park. His address is 5 Windsor Avenue, New Malden, Surrey. Our parachuting friend, Dicky Dumbrill, who has also been with the A.P.T.C., has volunteered to lend a hand with the sprinters at Tooting. Charlie Cook and Bob Jessop will, no doubt, be helping again. More are needed, if the Club is to maintain its place in the athletic world we must have keen and competent coaches. There is no doubt that training in packs or schools brings out the best in everybody, as witnessed by the performances of our cross-country runners and road walkers. The Hon. Secretary will be most pleased to hear from any member willing to offer his services.

Congratulations to our Hon. Walking Secretary upon the success of the Club Open Walk. This was well supported, and thanks to the generosity of those members who donated to the prize fund, Syd Roberts was able to report a small profit on the event. Those who gave to the fund were: A. C. Beales, J. B. Belchamber, F. H. Elson, H. Evans, A. Harley (senior), Mrs. A. Harley, A. A. Harley, C. E. Churcher, R. Taylor, and Mrs. Churcher, who presented here usual novice prize.

In the International Cross-Country Race at Ayr on March 23rd, Len Herbert ran well to finish 21st, and was fifth man home for England. Congratulations Len, also to David Main, who ran for Ireland, and to our Poly. member, Ron. Hughes, who also scored in the England team.

We are pleased to acknowledge congratulations on our "National" win from: Birchfield H., Polytechnic H., South London H., Mitcham A.C., Walton A.C., Dartford H., W. J. Western, Charlie Hill, Joe Turner, J. Brown, J. Harding and Norman Graves. L. N. Richardson, Hon. Secretary of the E.C.C.U., in a letter to Ernie Duffett, writes: "It was a great day for everyone, and one never to be forgotten for the Belgrave Harriers."

The Club has been most pleased to accept a cup for the walkers from Tommy Green, and a trophy for the Field Events' Section from George Powell.

The Supporters' Club has again been well supported, and once again we have to record our great thanks and appreciation to Les. Cohen for the work he does in connection with the scheme.

Members will read elsewhere that Percy Wright has gone to college for a year on a teaching course. This has meant that he has been unable to produce the B.N.L. Fortunately Alf. Harley has stepped into the breach, and with the assistance of Harry Rouse and Charlie Churcher, the B.N.L. still appears every month.

I understand that Derrick Burfitt has been seen on the films in N.S.W. I wonder if our old member, B. C. Button, who was the N.S.W. Sports Commentator, had anything to do with this.

America has heard of Derrick Burfitt and it is quite certain that they are destined to hear much more yet of this most promising junior of ours. Maybe one day they'll have the opportunity of seeing him in action in another "Mile of the Century"—with the four-minute mile achieved! We quote from "The Amateur Athlete," the official publication of the A.A.U. of the United States:—

"A promising British miler is Derrick Burfitt, a 17½-year-old youngster, who recently ran a 4m. 26.2s. mile. This time was three seconds faster than Wooderson's schoolboy record, and compares favourably with the schoolboy times of American stars, such as Cunningham's 4m. 24.7s., MacMitchell's 4m. 21.1s., Dodds' 4m. 28.4s., Lash's 4m. 30.5s., and Zamperini's 4m. 21.2s.

Obituary · page 6

We learn with deep regret from Mr. Beer that his son, Jim Beer, died from gangrenous appendicitis at Chittagong (India). It was a sad blow as Jim was due for an early leave. Jim was a keen cross-country and long-distance runner. We extend our sympathies to Mr. Beer, who was a keen follower of his son's activities, and known to many members.

We are also deeply sorry to learn from Brian Driscoll that his brother Pat was missing in Normandy in June, 1944, and is now presumed killed.

NEWS OF MEMBERS

Dick Michael is now a Lt.-Commander on H.M.S. "Myrmidon," where he is the Commanding Officer. Heartiest Congratulations, Mike. He has a had a busy time on convoy duties, but hopes that he may be able to visit us when on leave. He may be staying on in the Navy for a few years. Sends the Club "Best Wishes" for the coming season.

Len Rollins has also been promoted to Lt.-Commander, and has been in command of a camp of 700 at Ceylon.

Bill Sutton is now a Corporal. Congratulations, Bill. We hope to be seeing you back in England this Summer, showing us that the Mediterranean hasn't affected your throwing.

Frank Abberton writes to us from Sydney, Australia, where he has been enjoying some surf-riding.

Jack Shotter is now at Singapore, having come from Ceylon. He is pleased to read of the Club's successes and sends his best wishes to all. He keeps fit by playing hockey, and against the A.T.S., too! The most dangerous game he has ever played. He also does some swimming, and writes in admiration of the sea views.

Norman Graves has been running with the French club A. S. Aulnay, who presented him with a medal for his services to the club. He ran in the French Athletic Federation Invitation Race in the Bois de Boulogne. He was the only "foreigner" in the race and got a tremendous reception from the thousands of spectators. The race was won by Raphael Pujazon, the first post-war International C.-C. Champion. At this meeting there were five races, all run off in three hours, they were for Ladies, Youths, Juniors, Seniors, and Champions, Norman competing in the last category.

Dicky Dumbrill turned up at Belgrave Hall for the opening race of the cross-country season complete with medal ribbons of the Croix de Guerre emblazoned with star and oak leaves. He volunteered as an "umbrella man," and was with the S.A.S. He had dropped behind enemy lines in France and worked with the Maquis. He had also been dropped in Holland during enemy occupation, and had been behind enemy lines in other theatres of the war.

Bill Hewitt, serving with the R.A.M.C. in Italy, recently won a 5,000 metres race for the Army at Udine. The R.A.F. and New Zealand Forces were also taking part. Bill's time was 16.34.

Dick Towndrow was second to the International runner, R. V. Draper, in the B.A.O.R. C.-C. Championship. Another Belgravian, who is also an International, Ted Stimpson, was third.

Bill Nankeville met with a lorry accident in Germany and broke a few ribs, so he was brought back to England for a short while.

Reg. Billett, one of our new members, has been called up, and is serving with the Royal Signals.

Dave Bayley has returned from Ireland and is now with the A.P.T.C. at Oswestry, Shropshire.

Sergeant Bolton won a Battalion five miles' C.-C. race; he is with the East Surreys.

Alan Bishop is now with the R.A.F. at Lossiemouth, whilst his brother, E. M., is with the Fleet Air Arm.

Peter Vine, we hear, is in Palestine for a few months on business. He is a Post Office Engineer.

Joe Scammell (Junior) is now in the Army.

Percy Wright has come out of the Army, after six years of it, and has gone to a Teachers' Training College. His course is to last for a year, during which time Percy will get little time for participation in walking events, but we all know that if an opportunity presents itself, he will soon get his shoes out. Meanwhile he has our Best Wishes for Success in his new venture, and we look forward to his return to full participation, both as a competitor and as a worker behind the scenes.

Fred Rickards has been invalided from the Police and is now living and working at Slough. We are all sorry indeed to learn that Fred has left the Police and we trust that his new job and surroundings will benefit him. We hope that he will be able to visit us occasionally, and we look forward to seeing his genial person.

Syd. Warboys writes from Wallasey, Cheshire, and keeps fit by evening walks by the sea and week-ends in North Wales.

Alby Bird, who has been out of touch for a while, writes from Bolton, where he is now living, having been de-mobbed. He hopes to resume his running. He is married and has a baby boy, who already shows signs of some of his father's speed. Alby, or the "Pidge," was a very good midde-distance man, having won the Club Half-Mile Championship, and at his best could beat two minutes.

Arthur Cook is now de-mobbed and hopes to be busy with the megaphone at the forthcoming track meetings.

Harry Thaxter is not so busy in his pub. at Diss, Norfolk, now that the Americans have left, and hopes to pay us a visit. We are pleased to say that Harry is feeling much better. How many remember Harry's great effort in the last R.W.A. 20 Miles before the war, when he came through over the last two miles to be our fourth scoring man and gain a winning team medal?

Arthur Beaumont has been doing all sorts of things in Italy. He has been on bricklaying courses and gunnery, and on the lighter side has done some ski-ing. Hopes to be out by June. It is now confirmed that he is married. Congratulations and Best Wishes to you both.

Charlie Redgrave was suddenly whisked off to Ceylon. We hope that he didn't forget to take his banjo. Expects to be de-mobbed shortly.

Ted Elliott, who will be remembered as one of our most promising Junior walkers before the war, writes from the C.M.F. to say that he is doing daily strolls in preparation for his return home. Here is a walker to watch once he finds his feet. When he joined us in his 'teens he developed a fine style, with added years and fitness from life in the Services, he should soon be shaking them up.

Tony Whincup is on an office job in India, and keeps fit by running back to camp (about two miles) instead of riding in a lorry. How many other Belgravians have saved the Army's petrol at the expense of their leather!

Jim Heathfield, with the B.A.O.R., met Sid. Jones in Germany, and they had a long chat together. No two guesses as to what they talked about!

Len Camin is with S.E.A.C. and is now a Lance-Corporal, congratulations. Went to a sports meeting at Rangoon, and arrived just too late to run in the Mile. Len, by the way, is a quarter-miler. The meeting took place in sweltering heat, and the Three Miles was won by an Indian in 15.55.8, which in view of the conditions must be considered good going.

Stan Botwright expects to be de-mobbed in May. It will be interesting to see how Stan fares with our new sprinters, and we all wish him the best of Luck in defending his three Club Championship Titles (100, 220 and 440 yards).

Jack Frost is now in the R.A.F. and has been stationed in Norfolk, but hopes to turn out for us during the coming Track Season. If Jack can reach the form which he showed when he won the Club 300 yards' Handicap from scratch last September in 33.8 secs., he will take some beating.

Jack Brown is to be congratulated on gaining a stripe. He is at Catterick with the Royal Signals, and hopes to compete in the Army Track Championships this year.

Allan Dunn is with Combined Operations at Troon, and hopes to have a few runs on the beach to keep fit.

Don. Adie is with the Royal Engineers at Aldershot.

Albert Boddy has finished his initial training and has been moved up to Derby.

Bill Jones hopes to be de-mobbed in June.

David Main was called up early in the year and is now serving with the Navy.

Basil Walden has been called up and kept fit during the earlier part of the season with Streatham Rugby Club. He writes in great praise of the help given him by Syd. Jarlett.

Alfred Hanson is another who keeps fit during the Winter by playing football. He plays soccer with Merton F.C.

David Renel has been on the injured list with a broken arm.

George Chantler, who will be remembered as one of our Junior Champions of nine or ten years ago, has resumed running again. George has been in and out of hospital during the war years, having had operations and many months in bed. He has followed the Club's fortunes and is happy to be out with us again. We congratulate George on his courage and cheerfulness during his long periods of illness, and trust that his recovery is complete, and that he will enjoy many more runs with the lads.

Peter Harding, our Walton A.C. friend, is now out of hospital, and getting about with the aid of sticks. Peter thanks his many friends in Belgrave for their good wishes and sympathies. We trust that he will continue to progress and that we shall soon be seeing him walking without sticks.

Oscar Horwood's many friends send him their deepest sympathies on the recent death of Mrs. Horwood.

Harry Hare, one of our bygone stalwarts, has been very ill in hospital for some considerable time. His condition is still causing some anxiety, but, we sincerely hope that Harry's health will improve and that a speedy return to normal will follow. It is interesting to recall that Harry has been President, Hon. Secretary, Hon. Treasurer, Hon. Editor and Hon. Handicapper. He has figured in three R.W.A. winning teams: "Junior" 1922, and "Senior" 1924-5, and has held the Club 20 miles title.

Laurie Jackson has recently become a proud father, and we have great pleasure in announcing the election to the Club of Master Roger Jackson.

Bob Boyden, who has been absent for some time, has recently had an addition to his family, another boy.

Johnny Johnson, our "guest" member from Sheffield, was another who turned up on October 6th. It speaks well for his enthusiasm, as apparently he only docked at Tilbury early in the morning, and had come practically straight along to Belgrave Hall to give us his greetings personally.

BOXING DAY MEETING

The Club's annual Boxing Day track meeting, which in the past was always a high-light of our Winter programme, was this year held at Tooting Bec track, and quite a large crowd was present to witness the events. The field for the 3 miles walk was small, but Jack Rutland walked in fine style to overtake all those in front of him, and won easing up in the last lap. Charlie Speechley was a gallant second, with W. Young third. Jack very sportingly waived his right to a prize in order that the second and third men should be rewarded for their efforts.

THE "NATIONAL"

The English Cross Country Union held their first post-war championship this season. As holders of the title, which we gained when the race was last held in 1939, it was extremely gratifying to again win the championship in 1946.

As a matter of interest to some of our newer members, a review of our past performances in this race is given below.

In 1926 we find the club represented by two individual entries, Bert Footer, who finished 42nd, and Jimmy Flatt, who was 136th.

Bert Footer was a lone individual in 1927, and did well to finish in the 56th position.

Then in 1928 we had a team entered and they finished in 17th place, the scoring men being: W. G. Webb 63, H. E. Footer 79, A. D. Pyer 95, W. L. Bayliss 141, A. E. Smith 149, A. G. Jones 184. Also finished was F. G. Ford 250.

In 1929, although we entered a team, only four men completed the course, their positions being: W. G. Webb 87, A. W. Penny 120, W. L. Bayliss 122, F. G. Ford 197.

Then in 1930 the club made a definite step forward by being placed 6th in the championship and in being the second Southern club to finish. Bert Footer finished 20th and Syd Warboys 42nd, other members of the team were: A. Allum 47, W. G. Webb 65, F. R. Webb 74, E. A. Duffett 81.

In 1931 the club, although not doing so well as in the previous year, had a decided triumph in providing a member of the English team for the International race. Penny 46, H. E. Footer 67, W. G. Webb 83, J. E. Flower 93. Also finished, E. A. Duffett 113, C. T. Carter 165.

ENGLISH C.C. CHAMPIONSHIP

The English Cross Country Union held their 59th annual championship at Leamington Spa on March 9th. This race is more popularly known as the "National," and is the "blue riband" event of the cross-country world. Many famous clubs have won this championship with our Midland friends, the Birchfield Harriers predominating. They had an unbroken run of success from 1928 to 1934 which we broke by winning in 1935, and although they regained the title in 1936 and 1937, since that date it has been in the keeping of the South, represented by Mitcham A.C., and ourselves, a state of affairs which we hope will continue for many years to come.

This year's race had attracted an entry of 26 clubs, together with the usual host of individual entries which are allowed, and the competitors came from the North, South and Midlands. It was a three-lap course, on the heavy side, and first time round there was a small bunch of runners leading the field, and then came Len Herbert just behind them. We were wondering what had happened to Tom Carter, but he soon came along and although not running in his usual form, he was quite well up considering the class of the field. Dave Main was with Tom and Geoff. Pearson, Tom Clinton and Dick Tobin were all going strong. Dan Patience was our seventh man and it was obvious that his spell as a prisoner of war had taken toll of his running strength. Round they came for the second time with Bobby Reid of Birchfield hanging on to Jack Holden's shoulder, and Len Herbert had kept his position and looked a certainty for International honours. The first nine Englishmen home in this race are automatically selected to represent England, a method of selection with which some people disagree. Tom Carter and Dave Main were running stride for stride and then came three Birchfield men and on totting up the points with a lap to go we found that we led Birchfield by 106 to 107. On the last lap, however, our lads came through in fine style and with Len Herbert finishing in 7th position, although for team purposes he scored 5, then Dave Main with Tom Carter just behind, and Geoff. Pearson and Tom Clinton, our new Belgrave "twins," in 21 and 22 scoring positions, our hopes were running high. Yes, it was Dick Tobin, and with No. 24 disc, a quick tally, and our total was 96. We asked the Birchfield scorer his score and the answer was 106, whoopee, we had won the title once again. All our nine runners are to be congratulated on their fine show, and although commiserating with Tom Carter over missing his colours, it was particularly pleasing to have Len Herbert gain his place in the English team.

WITH THE JUNIORS

The cross-country season just ended, has from the point of view of our Junior Section, been one of the most successful in the history of the club. Thanks are due to Alf Taylor for taking over the coaching of the juniors, seeing that Bert Jannaway was indisposed. Under Alf's guidance the juniors rapidly became a fighting force in team events and led by Derrick Burfitt, who has rightly been described as the best junior runner in the country, they did very well in open events.

In December we had an Inter-Club match with the Sutton and Cheam Harriers, in which John Weames was first man home, followed by A. H. Bruce.

The Club Junior Championship was won by Derrick Burfitt, but the rest of the field, although a long way behind, had a battle royal of their own, and so made the race interesting.

In the County Junior races, the Middlesex event was won by Derrick Burfitt, and in the Surrey races, our single individual runner in the under 21 event, John Brown, finished 5th. Our team for the under 18 race did well to finish second, and A. H. Bruce was 4th, Len Bishop 5th and L. A. Adams 8th.

Then came the Southern Junior at Cheam on March 2nd, and Derrick Burfitt once again demonstrated his worth by beating a good-class field of youngsters. Incidentally, Derrick has an unbeaten record in junior events throughout the season, and it will certainly be interesting to see what success he has when he decides to tackle the senior races. Our second man home, was a newcomer to the club, E. A. Short. Eddie ran a fine race to finish 13th in his first open race, and Bishop and Adams also ran well. Our team was placed 5th, which was quite creditable seeing that we were without the help of John Weames and J. W. Brown and that A. H. Bruce had to retire early in the race with a sprained ankle.

The E.C.C.U. promoted a 3 miles Youths' Championship, which was held at Leamington Spa, and was over one lap of the senior course. There was an entry of 28 teams, representing clubs from all over the country, and a large field faced the starter. When they came in sight of the finish, a youngster from Derby was well in front and won in impressive style. Then four more runners and a Belgrave vest, it was Eddie Short, who had run a most brilliant race to finish 6th. John Weames came along in 15th position and then A. H. Bruce in 19th place, and our team was completed by L. A. Adams 34th. We did not know how our total of 74 points compared with the other clubs but on enquiry found that we were third, only 9 points behind the winners and 2 short of second position. Considering the class of the field and that clubs like South London, Birchfield, R.A.F. Halton, had finished behind us, we were indeed more than pleased with our lads' efforts.

On March 23rd we entered a team in an open race promoted by the Sutton and Cheam Harriers, and Derrick Burfitt led the field home, followed by A. H. Bruce with John Weames third, and so won the team race with the lowest possible score of 6 points.

It is truly said that the juniors of to-day are the seniors of to-morrow, and with the present talent in our ranks, I am confident that they will keep the club well to the fore for many seasons to come.

WITH THE WALKERS

Having come to the end of our first post-war season, the club can congratulate itself on having done much towards a return to its pre-war strength and successes

Starting the season considerably under strength, the club supported every open walk with the exception of the Surrey W.C. Croydon to Godstone, which was held the same day as our Club 20 Miles' Championship, resulting in the following score in scratch team events: Won 5, 2nd 4, 3rd 1. This in itself is evidence of the rapidly returning strength of our teams. Our star performer for the season has undoubtedly been Jack Rutland who, after starting the season with 4th place in the Highgate Open, rapidly improved and subsequently took individual honours in seven consecutive opens. Although it is quite evident we have sufficient talent in the club to be able to win opens, we are at the moment relying on about six walkers capable of scoring in a scratch team. All of these except Bob McMullen were with us before the war and obviously won't last indefinitely, so that if we are to maintain the successes we have had during the past road season, fresh talent must be forthcoming. We trust therefore that all members will keep a keen look-out for the youngsters we require. We can assure all who join the ranks of the walkers a cordial welcome and a guaranteed measure of success if they are prepared to persevere. We already have quite a few new members, including J. Willett, R. Taylor and J. McBride, who have not yet had time to show what they can really do, but under the tuition of Charlie Churcher and Joe Coleman, we can be sure that no effort will be spared to bring them along. Perhaps the most promising of our new members is Jack Willett, who has a style very similar to that of Jack Rutland, when he started. Still under twenty, we hope to see him on the track this season and with proper training, develop him into a future champion.

Our open, held this year for the last time in March, proved not only a grand re-union, but a great success for the club. The entry of 137 was the largest of the season up to that time and the race resulted in a double scratch and handicap win, Syd Spencer gaining a well-deserved success in the individual handicap. In addition to this, Bob Readman was second in the handicap and F. Herring (2nd claim) 3rd. John Willett took the novices' prize, presented by Mrs. Churcher. Harry Churcher unfortunately met with the disapproval of the judges, but Bob Readman, walking an excellent race, provided the necessary support to Jack Rutland and Bob McMullen. Our thanks are due to the very generous donors to the prize fund, which enabled the race to be run at a small profit.

It has been possible once again to introduce our Club Championships. All the races were well supported and resulted in the titles being evenly distributed. Bob McMullen retained his Junior title, this being the only Club Championship held during the war, Jack Rutland annexed the ten miles' title after a battle with Harry Churcher, and Colin Sutton regained his twenty miles' title, which he last held in 1938.

The National Junior did not provide us with very great success, the weather being partly responsible for the finishing position, which was 6th. Bob McMullen walked well to finish 4th. Harry Rouse and Joe Wilson, badly affected by the blizzard-like weather, dropped well back, making Sid Spencer, 34th, our second man home. By this time next year, however, we hope to have a much stronger junior team with which we shall no doubt make our presence felt.

In the County races we scored an easy win in the Surrey event. Len Fletcher's welcome return to the road after a long lapse, resulted in him winning a County medal, our other three scoring members filling the first three individual placings. In the Middlesex event (Cadogan A.C.), Jack Rutland had rather a bad time and finished second to the Police Champion, C. Morris. Frank Elson offended when the Club were in a good position and with his exit from the race we did not get placed.

The coming summer months provide plenty of long-distance racing, in which we hope to make a good show. If the Regent's Park 15 may be taken as an omen, our prospects appear quite good with Jack Rutland, Charlie Churcher, Colin Sutton and Bob Readman, backed up by Bert Clayton, Sid Spencer and Joe Wilson and we look forward to the possibility of the Club being represented in the European Games 50 Kilometres.

Tommy Green is promoting the Eastleigh Victory 50 Kilos again this year and we hope to send a much stronger team than last year. The Club is indebted to Tommy Green for yet another trophy which has not yet been allocated to an event.

A very busy season is now ahead of us, including a re-introduction of the Inter-Club competition on the track over 1, 2, 3 and 4 miles.

It is proposed to start next year on the road by entering separate teams in all opens and in order to do this, it will be necessary for all members to send their entries to the Hon. Sec. so as to reach him not later than the Saturday before the race.

As a final note, the Hon. Secretary, Track and Road Captains are anxious to do all in their power to assist newcomers to the Club and put them on their way to success. All enquiries and entries should be addressed to S. C. Roberts, 35 Perrymead Street, Fulham, S.W.6 (Tel. Renown 4738).

THE TRACK SEASON

In this, the first post-war track season, we are looking forward to the return to competition of many old friends of the pre-war era. It is to be hoped that the many old-time "open" meetings will once again be held, and your Committee have applied to the L.C.C. for permission to hold several club meetings at the Tooting Common track.

The Southern Amateur Athletic League is running in four sections, with meetings for the South-West area, in which we are competing, being held at Tooting. This time, in addition to the senior events, there are a few races open to Juniors who are under 19 on May 1st, 1946.

The club are holding the usual club championships, and as in pre-war years, we have fixed Saturday, August 3rd for club championship day. In addition to the placed awards, standards will be given in each event, and it is to be hoped that as many members as possible will enter to try to qualify for one of these. The Three Miles Run will be held at an evening meeting earlier in the season and here again standard awards will be made.

Two of the club meetings have been given over to the holding of handicap races, open to all members, and I trust there will be good entries for all the events. Those wishing to enter should send in their name, together with the entry fee (1/- each event) at least 7 days before the date of the meeting, to the Section Hon. Secretary.

The usual Inter-Club matches will also be held, and any member who wishes to bring his togs along to the meeting, whether at home or away, will be welcomed. This especially applies to any of our Service members who may happen to be home on leave at the date of the meeting.

Evening meetings usually start at 7 p.m., and Saturday afternoon about 3 p.m. Club training nights at Tooting are on Tuesday and Thursday, and the track is also open on Sunday mornings.

We shall again be competing in the Kinnaird Trophy, also the Waddilove Trophy meeting at Perry Barr, and have been invited to Southampton to compete for the Reynolds Garrett Trophy. It is doubtful whether the Doris Shield and the Inter-Club Relay Shield will be competed for this season, as some of the competing clubs are still very much under strength to make such a competition possible.

The usual County, Southern and A.A.A. Championships are being held and entry should be made at least 14 days before the date of meetings to the respective Secretaries on the form provided by these associations.

It will be seen that there is plenty of competition for all members, whether senior or junior, and anyone requiring further information, I shall be only too pleased to assist them.

Entries and enquiries to be sent to:— E. A. Duffett, Running Hon. Sec., 57 Harcourt Road, Thornton Heath, Surrey.

E. A. Duffett
