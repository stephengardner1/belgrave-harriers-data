Editorial

Your EDITORS

Dear Fellow Belgravians,

We humbly present to you the Belgravian issue for November, 1949. In doing so we first of all give thanks to your past Editor, Harry Parker. Many onerous duties have come Harry's way during his career with Belgrave, extending over many years and all he has tackled with great enthusiasm and with remarkable achievements. He is not the kind of person to relinquish his commitments without very good reason, but now he finds his official duties in the service of athletics at least one too many, and he has been obliged with very great regret to hand the editorship over to new co-editors. As we all know Harry has certainly plenty on his plate and always has had and now that he is President of the E.C.C.U., he finds it hard to carry on as Editor. In congratulating him on his great honour, we also thank him for his great work to the club.

As for ourselves, your new co-editors, we follow in the wake of Harry Hare, Ben Knifton, Alf Harley, Gordon Doubleday, Freddie Bradnum and Harry Parker.

We are fully conscious of the great work and standards built up and maintained by our predecessors and we assure you of our best endeavours to keep up this standard.

We have several ideas for the possible improvement of our magazine which we hope you will all approve but, as with this issue we are endeavouring to bring all reports up-to-date, pressure of space prevents us introducing new ideas on this occasion. We do intend, however, to issue another Belgravian at a very early date and to include these ideas, chief among which will be a "Suggestion page," and we ask those of you who have any suggestions, to let us have them in as brief a letter as possible, but let us get this issue among you first and we hope you will find the next one and subsequent issues interesting, entertaining and topical.

Best wishes, Your EDITORS.

Annual General Meeting—October 8th, 1949

The attendance at this meeting was not as large as was hoped nor as large as we have the right to expect. However, a very representative gathering of somewhere about 80-90 members assembled to elect your new officials and committee and to define the club policy for the year 1949-50. Once again our popular President, Bill Webb, was elected to the premier office of the club and this was received with unanimous acclamation. Secretary Alf Harley's report struck a very optimistic note in spite of one or two reverses during the past season. In reading his report we had a moment or two of thought for the passing of three of our club members: dear old Charlie Cook, Tom Sullivan and Rowland Rowbottom, which we all regret. The Hon. Treasurer's report showed us that although we were spending much more money than we received in ordinary receipts and subscriptions, we were able to balance our club budget through the efforts of the Supporters' Club. The Building Fund is in an extremely healthy state and a word of appreciation was given to Les Cohen and his supporters. Our Treasurer, Syd King, has done a wonderful job in carrying on after that great treasurer, Bill Webb. We all thank him for his work and congratulate him on his achievements. We were sorry and considerably concerned, however, when he warned us that this would be his last year of office. Under the capable chairmanship of Bill Webb the meeting progressed. Your officers and committee were duly elected and during the ensuing phase under the heading of "Other Business," the future policy of the club was discussed in a most rational and democratic manner. One left the meeting convinced that the management of the club was again in most capable hands and with a feeling of confidence for the season 1949-50.

The "Brighton" Relay held April 2nd, 1949

RUNNING NOTES

Starting from Mitcham, our runner on the first stage to Purley was Derrick Burfitt. He did well to finish 5th in 30 mins. 6 secs. On the next stage to Merstham, Ted Stimpson held his place and his time was 27.25. Then Frank Abberton tackled the up-hill stage through Redhill and ran a fine race to take us up into 2nd place with a time of 23.9. On the next stage, Jack Brown took us in front and running well clocked 28.7. Len Herbert went off on the 5th stage which had been slightly altered. Jimmy Wood, our Poly. member, caught and passed him but Len managed to hang on to second place with a time of 25.29 and handed over to Albert Frost who did well in putting up the fastest time for that leg which is now only just over 2 miles and his time of 10.51 kept us in second place. Vern Blowfield went off on the long stage to Handcross and soon passed his man to put us in the lead once again. His time was 31.25. Len Marchant was our next but he lost the lead to Green of Reading and clocked 21.40 for his stage. Eddie Short went off on the up-hill climb to Patcham. He passed the leader and in clocking 21.34 did the second fastest time and gave us a lead of 11 secs. over our nearest rivals. Bull Lucas, running the last stage, as usual, did not give his opponents any chance of getting on his shoulder and although Green of Birchfield took his team into second place, Bill finished an easy winner and moreover did second fastest time of 17.49 for his leg.

The order for finishing was 1, Belgrave, 3 hrs. 57 mins. 35 secs; 2, Birchfield, 3.57.52; 3, Tipton H., 3.58.35.

This makes our 6th win in this classic road race, a feat which no other club has performed and moreover, is our second series of three wins in a row as we won in 1934-5-6 and then again in 1947-8-9.

Surrey County Championships

The Senior and Junior Championships, with one or two exceptions were held at Motspur Park on June 4th when the club did quite well and the result of the club's coaching methods was clearly seen.

Alan Sexton performed a double in winning the 100 and 220 yards and in the latter event he set up a new county record of 22.7 secs. Another club member set up a new county record when John Grey, in the Junior Discus, recorded a throw of 137 ft. 3 ins.

The 3 miles championship was held at Tooting on July 6th and Len Herbert set up a new county record in the fast time of 14 mins. 38.6 secs. Bill Lucas unfortunately had to retire with leg trouble, otherwise we may have had the first two home. We are glad to see Len Herbert getting back to form as he had a disappointing season over the country and we hope that he will keep it up and lead our lads in their effort to regain our lost title of National Champions.

FIELD EVENTS REVIEW

by TERRY BATEMAN

Although the year has brought a slight improvement in the standard of field events it has been due to outstanding individual efforts rather than to improved general performances. However, our juniors show great promise and we must see they are given every encouragement.

One of the outstanding and commendable performances of the season was the 440 yards hurdles run by Geoff Jarvis in 56.7 secs. which gave him third place in the A.A.A. Championships. This was followed by another good performance in his winning the R.A.F. Championship and in being chosen as reserve for England v. France match for the same event. Previously Geoff had concentrated on the high hurdles and the change to 440 yards has obviously been most successful.

Another outstanding performance was that of John Grey's discus throw of 137 ft. 3 ins. which won him the Surrey County Junior Championship and broke the existing record by about 18 ft. He also gained a standard medal in the A.A.A. Junior Championships. Our weakest events have been the high and long jumps. Conditions were not improved when mid-way through the season we lost the services of Steve Gibson, one of our most promising high jumpers who went into hospital for an operation on an old war wound. We take this opportunity of wishing him a speedy recovery and hope to see him out at the winter training sessions

John Sinton a junior who has given good support to our senior matches is to be congratulated on his 5 ft. 6 ins. jump in the Police match at Imber Court. Both the juniors, Bob Cooper and Jim Purdy, have been jumping well and show promise. We were not too successful in either the Svard or Kinnaird meetings. One of the best performances was that of Harry Delger at the Kinnaird meeting when he threw the junior javelin about 154 feet to gain third place. Bergvalds was the outstanding performer in the club championships. He won the discus and shot, and was third in the javelin. His discus throw of 123 ft. 8½ ins. set up a new club record. Peter Lowe established a new club record in the hop, step and jump, with an effort of 41 ft. 5 ins. Charlie Melchoir retained his javelin title with Les Higgins as runner-up. Our old stalwart, George Powell, has been consistently throwing the javelin over 140 ft. in matches and has been a source of inspiration to younger aspirants. It is intended to resume the field events training periods through the winter months at Tooting on alternate Saturday afternoons.

WALKING NOTES

Looking back over the season which has just finished, we certainly have not disgraced ourselves. Our wins in Open Races in the South have been up to our usual standard and we have been placed in two of the Senior National Championships. That we can do better there is no doubt, especially do we need some more young members and it is hoped that the coming season will see an improvement in this direction.

Continuing our activities, following the National 10, three teams were entered for the Met. W.C. 15 miles at Regent's Park, but alas, before any great distance had been covered, Charlie Churcher was ruled out and our chances of success thereby greatly reduced. This race seems to be very unfortunate for us, as like last year, we suffered more than the usual disqualifications. These included Pat Sapcote and Jack Baker. The unusual distance of the race got the team selectors guessing with the result that two of our “ B ” team men finished before the second “ A ” team man. Outstanding was Bob Readman who held the lead throughout the greater part of the race, but on the last lap he tired rather badly and dropped back to third place. With a team score of 64 points we were very surprised to find that we gained third place.

In the Chichester to Portsmouth race on Easter Saturday, Charlie Churcher repeated his brilliant performance of last year by winning the individual honour. As we did not send a very strong team, it was not surprising that we were unable to get better than third place, 9 points behind the winners, Woodford Green.

The following Saturday we held our own Club 20 Miles' Championship. As usual, being the first 20 of the season, a number of the starters failed to finish the course, but the 14 who completed the distance walked exceptionally well. Veteran Jack Bidgood, after 20 years with the club, succeeded in winning his first Championship after a well-judged race, followed by Pat Sapcote and Charlie Churcher.

And so we came to the National 20 and from the performances of our walkers in the Club 20 it seemed that we had a reasonable chance of success. Unfortunately Bert Webb was unable to make the journey to Manchester and we were without any knowledge of the capabilities of the Sheffield boys over the longer distance. The weather, for Manchester, was extremely good, in fact, no rain fell throughout the day. At the start the two Sheffield lads, Allen and Proctor, made a quick getaway and joined by Lloyd Johnson, set up a good pace. Lloyd however, did not last with them for long and soon began to drop back. Most of our lads walked extremely well and were ably assisted by Harry Churcher, Syd Roberts and Percy Wilson, but despite all their efforts, they were unable to do better than second place, with Charlie Churcher taking third individual honour. Competition for the Nijmegen Shield was very keen. Sheffield being successful, with Woodford Green second and ourselves third.

In a new promotion this year, the Watford 25, we entered a small team and succeeded in gaining third place in both the scratch and handicap sections.

The last National Championship of the season, the 50 Kilos. was held from the Police Sports Club at Chigwell over a very stiff course. The weather, which was very warm, added to the trials of the walkers and of 73 starters, no less than 31 retired without completing the full distance. These included Charlie Churcher, who developed leg trouble, Pat Sapcote, Fred Herring, Jack Wakeley and Don Heath, so that by the end of the race we had just a bare four left, who finished in fourth position, 2 points behind Woodford.

For the Eastleigh A.C. 50 Kilos. on July 16th, Pat Sapcote was unable to make the journey at the last minute, thus depriving us of one of our best men. However, Bert Clayton sprung a great surprise by leading all the way through and eventually winning the individual race, and although the rest of the team walked exceedingly well, they had to be content with second place to Surrey W.C. Jack Bidgood also won a style prize, and Bert Clayton was second in the handicap.

On August 23rd we were invited to field a team in Surrey W.C.'s 6 miles road race at Tooting Bec Common. Despite the fact that the fixture did not appear on our summer programme, 12 of our walkers turned out and after a very interesting race, Surrey just managed to win by 103 points to 107.

So much for the road walks. On the track Harry Churcher, as is now well known, succeeded in lowering his world's record time of last year for the 5 miles. Walking in excellent style, Harry lapped the track time after time in almost identical times, eventually clipping 10.4 secs. off his time at Motspur Park. In addition to this he succeeded in taking the Surrey County 2 miles' title, the Southern Counties 2 miles title, and on a water-logged track at the White City, the 7 miles A.A.A. title. A fortnight later Harry took second place to Borgesson in the A.A.A. 2 miles but revenged his defeat in the London-Gothenburg Match a week later by winning the 7 miles at the White City in 51.2.2, his fastest time for the distance. This race prevented him from defending his Club 2 miles title and in his absence Bob McMullen, walking in very fine style, became this year's club champion.

On June 22nd we had our annual mob match with Surrey W.C. on the track at Tooting, beating them by 89 points to 121, with Joe Scamell (jr.), Brian Shepherd and Mac taking the handicap awards in that order.

A “ B ” team match against the Veterans A.C. at Tooting Bec on July 9th also resulted in a win for us.

Our Annual Inter Club Walks of 1, 2, 3 and 4 miles proved a great success this year, no fewer than 66 walkers competing. The competition proved to be very keen, eventually resulting in a win for Woodford Green with Highgate Harriers second and us third.

A number of our walkers have figured well in Open Handicaps and Veterans' Events, successes being scored by Bert East, Syd James, Charlie Speechley and young Joe Scamell.

As usual our Police members figured well in the Barking to Southend Walk. Jack Bidgood was once again second with Bob Readman, Pat Sapcote and Bert Webb following him home in that order. The winning team, “ B ” Division, was completely Belgrave.

On August 27th we had a 25 miles match with London Vidarians and Surrey Walking Club. This race next year is to be an Open Event and should prove a good training walk for the Brighton. The race resulted in a win for Surrey W.C. with us second.

Notice · page 19

THE LIGHTER SIDE—SOCIAL ACTIVITIES

The Social Hon. Secretary, Dave Martin Shepherd, has asked for more space for social activities. We shall certainly give him all we can for the social side is just as important as any other part of the club. In promoting these social events we are really showing our appreciation to the wives and sweethearts of Belgravians who without support and encouragement from their dear ones would never achieve the great performances they do. Do you stop to think what you owe to them. Who washes your running clothes each week? Who looks after the diet prescribed either by yourselves or well-meaning advisers? Who sits and listens over and over again to stories of walking, running and field events until they could scream? Who sits alone night after night waiting for committee men to return, having resignedly relinquished many hours of their domestic life to officialdom of Belgrave?

Aye, give them a little in return, and we can assure you that nothing pleases them more than to join in our social activities at the club to whom they undeniably give so much. So make a note of the following dates and come along. Saturday, November 19th. Saturday, December 10th.

Dave Martin Shepherd

Report · page 20

ANNUAL DINNER AND DANCE

The Annual Dinner and Dance which was held at the Express Hall, Clapham Junction, on Saturday, October 15th. was again a huge success and the hall was filled to capacity. Despite the extensive enquiries of the Social Hon. Secretary, it was not possible to find a suitable hall which would hold a larger number of club members and it was with much regret that we were obliged to turn down applications for tickets.

Bill Webb once again presided over the gathering and received a great ovation. The theme of Bill's few words in proposing “ The Club,” was the “ Belgrave Spirit,” and in replying to the toast, Secretary Alf Harley gave us some interesting data on the very early days of the club when handicap meetings were held on mid-week nights on the Embankment, lasting as late as eleven o'clock.

During the course of the evening it was announced that Len Herbert had been awarded the Sir Harry Barclay Cup by the Surrey County A.A. for the best performance of 1949.

The dance which followed dinner was very ably conducted by Bert Ellsey and our thanks are due to “ Dave ” for a very splendid evening.

Column · page 20

NEWS FROM MEMBERS

Tony Whincup writes to say that having got over his exam. he is getting down to training.

Tony Davis is soldiering at Horsham but says there's not much doing in the way of athletics.

Jimmy Coates, one of our young walkers, who has recently emigrated to Canada, writes to say he is very impressed by the Canadian juniors but says there's a shortage of cinder tracks.

Frank Thompson was a very welcome visitor at the A.G.M. He tells us that our old friend, Fred Rickards, who married again last year, has started another family and has a daughter. Good luck Fred, when are we going to see you again.

Tommy Green is reported to be “ retiring ” from business and getting down to walking again. What about next year's National, Tommy?

B. Lulham has passed his Coach's exam. for sprints and relays, and is now a A.A.A. Hon. Coach.

Colin Sutton, settling down in Australia, is already winning races and is the district champion for 4½ miles.

Stan Manning, who was ill last year, is now recovering and hopes to get to Wimbledon during the winter.

Jack Rutland informs us that he will soon be out training again with an eye to long distance. Jack already holds an important executive post and he is now studying for further promotion. We all wish him luck.

Syd Roberts and Frank Elson have been appointed R.W.A. judges.

We have just learnt with regret of the serious accident which befel R. Dick, the popular and leading Highgate Harrier walker. We wish to express the club's sympathy with Mr. R. Dick and wish him a speedy and successful recovery.
