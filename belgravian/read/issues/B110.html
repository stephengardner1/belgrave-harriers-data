EDITORIAL

YOUR EDITORS.

Dear Fellow Members,

It has already been said that this year, with the purchase of the freehold of our Hall, another page in the history of the Club has been turned. But in the flush of this new era let us not overlook a page which has recently been turned, one full of the hazards and hard work that made it possible for the first-mentioned page to be turned.

We refer to the page of W. G. Webb, which, in the process of turning, has given us first a Treasurer and then a President.

To many of us who have served long on your committee a great change has now taken place. A familiar face is missing from the head of the table at all our meetings, a face that for over fourteen years took its place on the right of the President and for the last two years has occupied the Presidential chair. How strange not to see him there now. By this we do not suggest he is taking it easy, by no means. Bill has been a really hardworking Treasurer and a popular President. His page may have been turned, but we know you will continue to see the name of W. G. Webb in future pages, and we hope, too, Bill, for many years to come.

As for our new President, Alf Harley, it is difficult to find any page in the Club history over the past 20 years that has not some reference to him and certainly in our recent progress Alf's name figures most prominently.

We know as a President he will be relentless in getting the best out of everybody for the good of the Club, and were we not good clubmen and eager to serve one who has served us so well, we should view the coming year with a certain amount of apprehension, for the amount of work which Alf will certainly undertake and the amount he will expect us to do. That we will serve both him as the President, and the Club as a whole, he has no doubts, and so he may command.

May we hope that his term of office will see the turning of yet more vital and successful pages in our Club history.

And just by the way. This issue will, of course, be the last one of 1950, so we, your Editors, take this opportunity, perhaps rather early, of wishing you all — and that includes all our supporters and friends — A VERY HAPPY CHRISTMAS AND A PROSPEROUS NEW YEAR.

YOUR EDITORS.

W. G. WebbAlf Harley

FROM YOUR PRESIDENT

A. A. HARLEY, President.

May I express my humble thanks to Club members for electing me to the proud position of President of the Belgrave Harriers. It is with pride, and at the same time humility, that I follow in the steps of a distinguished and hard-working line of Belgrave Presidents, every one of whom has served the Club in a variety of ways and honoured the Club in accepting the honour bestowed on him.

I think of the late Bob Ricketts, President when I joined the Club in 1926; and I know his influence and personality to be with us still. Harry Hare succeeded him. Here was a young man who was Hon. Secretary and Treasurer within 12 months of joining the Club, the first Hon. Editor of a printed "Belgravian," the prime move in initiating our Building Fund and a leader and guide in so many club activities, yet still finding time to win a Club championship and score in winning National Championship teams.

Jimmy Bellchamber, an Hon. Auditor since 1912 and a Walking Captain with personality and real leadership, added lustre to the Chair, as did Jock McKay, our Hon. Treasurer for many years and later an auditor; and Ted Gordon, a tower of strength in so many ways since he joined in 1911, particularly in regard to our Building Fund. Oscar Horwood had been Hon. Secretary of the Club in the difficult days when the club's finances were very meagre and Belgrave had only been kept in existence by constant recourse to one's own pocket and hours of toil, often unrewarding. It was fitting, therefore, that he should become President in happier days. Wally Bayliss, as Captain, Assistant Hon. Secretary and Hon. Secretary, served the club supremely well for many years, and of undisputed right became our President.

Harry Evans, bringing walking honours to the Club name as far back as 1909, worthily held the Presidential reins, as has Frank Thompson (elected a Life member in 1897), a former Hon. Secretary and the Club's representative on A.A.A. Committees and the man who put in more attendances at Battersea Park Track, all in the Club's interest, than any other — alive or dead.

We now come to the more recent years, when the office of President has been upheld in its dignity by Dick Savage, Harry Parker, whose 38 years of Club work have most unfortunately been cut short recently, as we all know to our sorrow, and Bill Webb, 14 years an Hon. Treasurer, a former Cross Country Captain, a Trustee and our President for the last two years, to whom we offer our sincere thanks for a job well done and in line with the traditions of the past.

I can only say that I am deeply conscious of all that has gone before; and with your help will try hard not to let my predecessors down. Let us all find in the traditions given us by our former Presidents, which I have tried to outline (most inadequately and in the sketchiest form only) an inspiration to go forward to even greater things than this great Club of ours has yet achieved.

A. A. HARLEY, President.

FROM THE HON. TREASURER

G. S. DOUBLEDAY.

Those who were at the A.G.M. will need no telling of how important the matter of £ s. d. is to the Club. Running a club of our size and scope entails a continuous drain on our finances. A glance at the balance sheet will show how much is spent on items, such as team expenses, printing, postage and prizes. Throughout recent years the Club has maintained its high standard without increasing subscriptions by any large amount and by keeping Club handicap and championship fees at their pre-war level of one shilling. So it is up to everybody to make sure that they are up to date with their subs and to pay all race fees promptly.

How else can you help the club finances? Are you acquainted with the Supporters' Club, which has raised so much money over recent years? Any Club official will be pleased to furnish particulars of the scheme, or Les Cohen, Hon. Secretary of the Supporters' Club, will be pleased to oblige. It is hoped that many more members will be participating in the scheme. No doubt reference is made elsewhere to Club social functions. These deserve fullest support and if you don't get value for your money it will be your own fault.

If we may return to the subject of the A.G.M., my predecessor, Syd King, said that I would fill the bill for treasurer, he did not say "foot the bill," that's your job.

Thank you for reading this and thank you in anticipation.

ANNUAL GENERAL MEETING, OCTOBER 7th, 1950

The writer of these notes was waylaid by the Editor shortly before the meeting was due to begin and asked if he would produce an account of it for the Belgravian. He was too surprised to think of a suitable excuse before the meeting started, hence this contribution.

Bill Webb took the chair and opened proceedings by asking members to stand for a moment to the memory of the late Harry Parker.

Minutes were read and approved and the Hon. Secretary's report, which had been previously circulated to members, was taken as read. Then came a very important item on the agenda, the presentation of the balance sheet by the Hon. Treasurer, Syd King.

The balance sheet this year appeared in a most businesslike form, due, our Treasurer told us, to advice and assistance from some of our accountant members. It was so well made out that further explanation was not really necessary. The present satisfactory state of the funds is chiefly due to the efforts of the Supporters' Club. A gentle (?) reminder was given to those who had forgotten, or failed to pay their subscriptions.

One or two interesting facts emerged from the balance sheet; Belgrave could run a highly successful "Open" walk and make a profit of 2/1 on it. The increased "Standards" had reduced expenditure on medals.

After this came the election of Life Members. Syd King and Syd Roberts were elected. The former was proposed by Ernie Duffett and seconded by Les Cohen, and the latter proposed by Frank Elson and seconded by Gordon Doubleday. As Doub remarked, the only thing that Frank had left him to say was his ability to collect subs from walkers!

The proceedings were interrupted for the President to award the "Belgrave" Cup for the Brighton Walk to A. J. Stirling Wakeley, who had to leave early.

It has been customary for the retiring President to nominate his successor. Bill Webb conformed to custom and nominated Alf Harley to carry the reins for the year 1950-51. For the information of newer members, Bill gave a resumé of Alf's activities as a Belgravian since joining the Club in 1926. We were told, amongst other things, that the financial stability of our magazine in the days when Alf was Editor was ensured on the profits made on the Club ties he sold! We learnt, too, that Alf, before he retired from running (?) was the forerunner of Wint. It is, perhaps, indicative of our new President's nature that he took up walking and left Wint a clear field.

The existing Vice-Presidents were elected en bloc, together with H. W. Elsey, C. Munroe and F. H. Elson.

The question of who should be Club Hon. Secretary for the coming year had already been discussed in committee, and Ernie Duffett rose to propose Alf Harley, who was duly elected. Alf will continue to control the policy, but will be assisted in the more routine matters by the Assistant Hon. Secretary and various Club members.

The retiring Hon. Treasurer proposed G. S. Doubleday to succeed him. Doub was unanimously elected and was seen in action during the tea interval.

Election of other officials followed.

J. B. Bellchamber gave some interesting details of Club finances. His first balance sheet for the year 1911-12 showed a slight deficit, which members made up. He told how Harry Hare, the originator, Tom Cotton and Frank Elson had started what, in more recent years under "Sir Stafford" Cohen (who apparently has a natural affinity for money), has developed into the Supporters' Club.

Tom Carter was re-elected Hon. Social Secretary, after which names of those proposed for elected members of the committee were read out. Ballot papers were completed and handed in and whilst the count was being taken, members adjourned for tea. After tea, the "Tommy Green" cup for the year's outstanding performance by a Club member was presented to Bill Lucas for his fourth place in the A.A.A. three-mile Championship, where he returned the fastest time (14 mins. 1.6 sec.) ever done by a Club member.

The meeting proper was over now and it was only a matter of waiting for votes to be counted. Whilst waiting, and in lighter vein, various questions were asked and answers given. It is encouraging to know that at some future date there will be garages available for members' cars. However, in the immediate present, accommodation for bicycles is causing the committee some concern.

The meeting closed at 6.15 p.m., and I am sure the members present went away from this A.G.M. with a very satisfied feeling in the knowledge that the hall was at least 100 per cent. ours and that the Club had taken another decisive step forward in the last 12 months.

THE CLUB DINNER

Inspr. G. Malkin

An impression by one of our guests, Inspr. G. Malkin, Hon. Secretary of the M.P.A.A.

Whoever decided that the Belgrave Club Dinner should be held on Friday, 13th October, was obviously not superstitious. As events proved, their optimism was well founded, for the evening was a great success.

When I arrived at the Corner House, Coventry Street, Piccadilly, I was immediately struck by the large gathering, far more than I had ever seen at a club dinner. Squeezing my way through the crowd near the bar, I noticed many well-known figures in the athletic world, too numerous to mention individually. There was, however, something rather strange about them, until I realised that I was seeing them behind cocktail glasses instead of the more usual pint pots.

The speeches were entertaining and just the right length. The standard was set by the first speaker, Alf Harley, in proposing the toast of the "Club," and the response by H. Shields. Other speakers, Syd Roberts, who proposed the toast of the "Guests," followed by Ernie Clynes and the new President of the R.W.A., E. Kennell, all paid tribute to the ladies, rather diplomatically, I thought. Evidently with an eye to the future.

Dancing which followed was to the music of Les Reeves and his band, with the M.C., H. Ellsey, doing his job most admirably.

I believe that it is an axiom of the entertainment world that the customer should go away wishing the show had been a little longer. Judging by this, the whole function was one of the best, for when it was time to go, I think we all wished it had lasted another half hour.

Altogether a very pleasant evening, which I shall remember for a very long time.

I very nearly forgot, the meal was excellent, too.

G.J.M.

To add to the above report, it was unanimously voted a grand night by those attending; but we were disappointed that so many chose to stay away.

To those who enjoyed this delightful evening we say "You lucky people." To those who didn't, either through superstition or apathy, we say "You silly people," and to those who found the financial aspect or other circumstances too pressing, we say "We hope you will come along next year and enjoy yourself with Belgrave Friends, as we did."

MATCH v. CAMBRIDGE HARRIERS

At Charlton Park Track on July 26th there were junior and senior events and these took the form of triangular matches between Blackheath H., Cambridge H. and ourselves.

We did fairly well in the senior match and Lowers won the 100 yards, while Basil Walden won the 220 1st string, with Des Mullen third in the 2nd string race at this distance.

This was a combined meeting with the London Olympiades A.C. and we decided the club Hop, Step and Jump championship together with some club handicap events.

There was a disappointing turn-out for the 100 yards (under 19) race and only four runners faced the starter. The winner was a newcomer to the club, H. Oulton, late of Preston Harriers.

Brian Shepherd won the 2 miles walk with E. Jeffery reaping the reward of his perseverance by being placed second and Sid Jarlett who has now gone over to walking, securing third place.

In the 2 miles run, Bill Lucas ran well off the back mark of 20 yards to go through his field to win in the good time of 9 min. 19.4 secs. Arthur Bruce was second with Jack Brown third.

A. Machell won the Shot Putt handicap and Charlie Jones got away with first place in the Javelin event and it was interesting to see that Tom Underwood secured a couple of placings in these events whereas he does not seem to be having a very good season from the running point of view.

SENIOR MATCH AT DORKING

INTER CLUB TRACK WALKS—TOOTING BEC., JULY 19th

W. ATKINSON.

The popularity of this meeting was clearly emphasised, both by the entries and the support it was given on the night. No fewer than 64 walkers competed in the four races, which were witnessed by a crowd of at least 2,000 people. Five clubs out of the ten entered, fielded a full team of eight walkers and after a close contest between Belgrave, Highgate and Surrey W.C. we came out on top nine points in front of Highgate and eleven in front of Surrey. Woodford Green, last year's winners, were fourth.

Mr. W. Atkinson, one of the judges at the meeting, has sent us the following comments on the evening :—

On July 19th I again had the pleasure of attending and acting as a judge at the Inter-Club Walking Contest promoted by Belgrave Harriers at Tooting Bec Track.

For those interested in walking I consider this one of the best events of the year. At most sports meetings we only see one walking event and that usually takes place late in the programme. Here we have four races, all walking events, and most if not all, the best walkers in the country can be seen. The organisers are to be congratulated on the manner in which the " show " was conducted. The events were well up to time and although it had to be a late evening meeting, we were able to get away bfore dusk. This was quite an improvement on the last time we had been asked to attend this track. We were timed to be there at 3.30 p.m., only to find that the 7 miles walk had started at 3.15 p.m. However, someone made up for this lost quarter of an hour a few days later when we were asked to be at Motspur Park at 3.30 p.m. for a meeting which was not timed to commence until 6.30 p.m. with the walk at 7.45 p.m.

The results of the four races are given below and very little comment can be made on them. The walking was good and most of the results as expected. After a flying start by one of the competitors in the five miles, the race developed into a procession and some of the walkers looked as though they were training for the Brighton. I think McMullen, in the four miles, walked the best I have seen him do. The three and two miles were interesting races and the results remained in doubt until the last lap. Some newcomers to walking were seen and with proper training should develop into good walkers next year. I will only mention one—B. Shepherd, of the Walton Club. I have seen this young man win two races recently and he improves each time he turns out.

I went away pleased to think that no walker had been disqualified but I now learn there was one offender. I always look upon disqualifications in walking as a serious matter and that you are telling the walker he is cheating. At the same time I would like to mention that I am strongly in favour of the English definition., i.e., maintaining contact with the ground. There are many walkers who appear to be running rather than walking until one carefully looks at their feet and the feet are all that should be looked at when judging. Provided the man is maintaining contact, he is complying with the definition, if not, he is running and should be disqualified. There can be no half way between the two.

W. ATKINSON.

DE VIERDAAGSCHE, 1950

(The four days)

Two members of the club, Bert Clayton and Jack Stirling-Wakeley, took part in this year's Nijmegen Marches and we produce some extracts from an article by Bert Clayton published in an Eastleigh newspaper.

Three British teams took part in the Marches this year, two from the R.W.A. and one from the R.A.F., the latter walking in full kit with rifles.

At 5 a.m., on Tuesday, July 25th, the two R.W.A. teams moved off for the first day's journey. Before the 10 miles mark had been reached, I was suffering from blisters rather badly, so Harold Whitlock stopped behind and doctored. By the end of the day we were just beginning to appreciate the difference between racing and marching. We passed all the other teams and finished an hour ahead on each of the four days, because our natural pace was faster, although 11 hours were allowed to cover the distance. The remarkable feature of the whole affair was the terrific enthusiasm shown by spectators all along the routes. At one village we were hailed as " liberators."

The second and third days were a repetition of the first, but the routes being different we saw quite a lot of Holland. At one point we marched along a road, the right side being Germany and the left Holland. At another time we had a good look at the Reichwald Forest, where so many violent tank battles were fought. On the second day we passed by the scene of the Arnhem conflict.

Friday was a grand day and for the English team a most welcome one. Beginning at 5 a.m., the crowds were already then forming and about 6 miles from the finish the roads were packed on both sides with [illegible]00,000 people all armed with bouquets and floral gifts to present to the marchers. Various bands were coming out to escort marchers and the general effect of flowers and music was reminiscent of the " Battle of flowers."

At the conclusion of the marching on Friday the medals were presented and on Friday evening a grand dance was held at the Town Hall. Most of the English team were there, not because the dancing in such a huge crowd was enjoyable, but because the ability or willingness to dance after four days marching was an unofficial test of further endurance.

BRIGHTON WALK, September 9th, 1950

IT WAS A FAMOUS VICTORY!!! Fifteen of our walkers lined up in a field of over 100 to await the first stroke of Big Ben, we being the only club to start two teams. Our only non-starter was Bert Clayton who has een having some foot trouble and without Bert our chances were not quite so rosy. However this did not deter the others who all got going in nice fashion. The weather was pretty bad, with a steady fall of rain, but there was very little wind and it was fairly mild. Jack Bidgood started going up towards the front quite early with Jack Wakeley and Jack Rutland not too far back. Then there followed all in quick succession the remaining five of the “ A ” Team, Don Heath, Hugh McGreechan, “Sir” Harry Shields, Joe Wilson and Dick Frazer. Quick checks on the scoring over the first 20 miles showed that Enfield, last year’s winners, were in a very strong position with Surrey W.C. also well placed. By the time Crawley had been reached, Ruffell of Enfield had retired and Jack Rutland who had been having foot trouble changed his shoes, but in the process fell back quite a good way. Jack Bidgood was still our first man in 9th position with Jack Wakeley walking very strongly in 11th. A little further back, Hugh McGreechan and Harry Shields, both doing their first Brighton, were coming through the field like steam engines. At Peas Pottage, 31 miles, the situation was much about the same and a check of placings showed that Highgate were in the lead with Met. and ourselves second and third. A further check at 40 miles showed that Jack Wakeley was still improving, Jack Bidgood, who although he had lost no positions, was a little slower, and Mac and Harry were still holding their places. The scoring here showed that Highgate were 55 points, Met. 59 and us 60 and it was here that the whips began to put the pressure on. Our 4th man was in 22nd position whilst Highgate and Met. 4th men were 34th and 35th respectively and in the intervening gap were the non-scoring half of our Team, Don Heath, Joe Wilson, Dick Frazer and Jack Rutland, not fully recovered from his earlier trouble. These last four were then given strict instructions to keep the Highgate and Met. men pegged back and right well did they do the job. At the same time Jack Wakeley was putting on the pressure and although Jack Bidgood fell back two places, the other Jack was there to take his place. Coupled with this, Mac and Harry, over the last 12 miles, made up an additional 5 points, whilst the back four maintained their positions, thus preventing Highgate and Met. from lessening their points on the 4th man. McSweeney of Highgate, however, during the last few miles, caught and passed Jack Bidgood, thus knocking four points off their score, but this was more than wiped out by the efforts of our lads. Great was the excitement at the finish of the race, Highgate with three men in were 17 points, Met. with three men in were 24 points whilst Mac and Harry having finished in 16th and 20th positions, our score for the full team was 50 points. Highgate’s 4th man must still be in 34th position for us to win and so we waited for what seemed an eternity as the finishers came in, 28th Stroud of Poly, 29th Hart of the Beagles, 30th Rutland, 31st Frazer (our 8th “ A ” Team man) 32nd Everard, Beagles, if the next man was Longshaw of Highgate it would be a draw, and then, round the corner came Shepherd in the maroon colours of Poly and we knew we had won, but only just, for Highgate filled the next position which gave them 51 points to our 50.

Once again then and for the sixth time (once more than Surrey W.C.) we hold the Brighton Trophy. But great as were the performances of our scoring four, so equally great were those of the other half of the team, on them rested our success or failure and it was their effort which brought success.

Having so far mentioned only the “ A ” Team, let us now take a look back at the slower men. Unfortunately Jack Baker and Gordon Doubleday were compelled to retire owing to foot trouble before the halfway stage was reached and later, at 40 miles, Bill Urry was forced to give up. This still left us a full scoring four in the “ B ” Team and even if they were some way behind, they finished and we are just as proud of them as the winners.

A very good crowd of supporters accompanied the walkers and many Club members and friends were seen on the road including Stan Manning and Jim Chalk. The cyclist attendants deserve a very hearty vote of thanks for their untiring attention to the walkers despite the uncomfortable journey they had in the pouring rain.

A Club handicap, for which prizes were given by Jack Wakeley, was won by H. Jeffery, also doing his first Brighton with an allowance of 1 hour 40 minutes, with Hugh McGreechan, allowance 1 hour, second.

50 GLORIOUS YEARS ON THE BRIGHTON ROAD

A.A.H.

Our history on the Brighton Road in both walks and Relay Runs is a very worthy one and we now give some account of Belgrave’s performances (as far as they are available) in Brighton Walks over the years.

The first London to Brighton Walk took place in 1886—a year before our Club was founded. The next promotion in 1897, organised by Polytechnic Harriers, had at least two Belgrave competitors, Miles, who was sixth, and Poole, 7th, and the third open race over this course was held in 1903 when the now defunct Hairdressers’ A.C. were the promoting body. In this race the then Belgrave Hon. Secretary, H. A. Smith, finished 6th in 9 hr. 33 mins. 8 secs.—a very worthy performance over the rough and dusty road of that period. Further races to Brighton were promoted by the Polytechnic Harriers in 1906, 1909, 1911 and 1913 but our scanty records do not enable any information to be given concerning Belgrave competitors, although it is quite certain the Club was represented. The writer would welcome information from anyone able to fill in these gaps in our records.

In 1914 a young Belgravian named Jimmy Bellchamber determined that the Club’s colours should be prominently carried over the 52 miles of road that year, but the outbreak of war caused a postponement of plans. In 1920, however, Jimmy was down to it again and finished third. Jimmy Bellchamber’s attendant in the Brighton of 1920 had been Bob Ricketts and Jimmy persuaded him that Belgrave could compete with distinction in such events. As a result, in 1921 we had J. H. Allen finishing 3rd, Jimmy Chalk 5th, Bob Ricketts 8th, W. J. Hawker 9th, F. M. Stone 12th, and two others to finish in S. H. Miller and W. Fuller. The race had been won by Pavesi of Italy, but it was Belgrave’s team race with 12 points (3 to score) and our popular Vice-President, Mark Stone, won the open sealed handicap. So Belgrave had really made their mark in Brighton Walks. Bellchamber did not take part in this race as he had competed in the Brighton and back race earlier in the year, finishing 2nd.

In 1922 Jimmy was our first man home in the now annual Brighton Walk in fourth position. The winner was Edgar Horton of Surrey W.C., followed by two Italian entrants, Guisto and Pavesi. Then came Bellchamber, followed by J. H. Allen in 5th position and Bob Ricketts 18th, our team losing on this occasion to Surrey W.C. by three points.

Two Brighton Walks were promoted in 1923 and in these events Bellchamber finished 4th and 5th, so maintaining a very fine sequence of placings which was further continued in 1924 with 4th position. Our gratitude is due to Jimmy for his enthusiasm, persistence and example. He is truly the father of distance walking in our club, and he has had the pleasure of seeing his own fine walking succeeded by that of others, until a great Belgrave tradition has been built up.

In 1923 our late President, Bob Ricketts—himself a more than useful performer—presented the “ Belgrave ” Cup for a Club Long Distance Walking Championship to be held each year in conjunction with the Surrey W.C. THE EUROPEAN CHAMPIONSHIPS 1950

by A. H. Bruce

Having returned from an exciting week at the Heysel Stadium, Brussels, it is good to reflect upon some of the outstanding events during the Games.

The organisation, overburdened with too many officials, functioned reasonably well apart from the Walk in which we were definitely robbed of a title, and of a bad slip in the lap numbering in the 10,000 metres.

The track events proved most encouraging for Great Britain and our team, supported by a very noisy British contingent in the Stadium, rose to the occasion in practically every event.

Highlights were Shenton’s wonderfully improved running in the 200 metres, in which he never looked like being beaten, Derek Pugh’s lion-hearted win in the 400 metres and his equally fine 46.2 flying start quarter in the relay.

For the British contingent in the Stadium, however, the 800 and 1,500 metres were the biggest revelation. After the 800 metre heats the Belgian newspapers said that we only had a chance if it was a slow-run race, to which I must say “ some slow race!”

In the 1,500 metres both our men ran above themselves. Bill Nankeville running especially well as he always does on the big occasion and with a little luck he might have been second.

For the cross-country enthusiast perhaps Frank Aaron’s run in the 10,000 metres was the most interesting. Running in his uusual dogged style he returned a time equivalent to 29.25 for six miles, which is some running!

After reading Geoff Dyson’s reply to his critics with regard to John Disley’s running in the A.A.A. Championships, it seemed strange to see Disley forcing the pace in a much higher class race—with the invitable disastrous result.

The two Hurdle races gave us two very welcome third places with Angus Scott very unlucky in not reaching the final.

The field events were, as expected, the weakest part of our team’s performances, although Paterson’s high-jumping was magnificent and Savidge in the shot, Geoff Elliott in the decathlon and McDonald Clark in the hammer, all performed well.

Our girls showed that they can hold their own with the rest of Europe and only Blankers-Koen and the Russian “ heavy ” brigade prevented us winning more titles than we did.

Probably all these successes were helped in no small measure by the great win of Jack Holden in the Marathon on the first day of the meeting, and it must have acted as a great morale-raiser to the rest of the team, so perhaps we can now look forward to Helsinki with a certain degree of confidence.

As a matter of interest to any statisticians, if all the 5,000 metre finalists had run the last 5,000 metres with Zatopec in his epic 10,000 metre “ gallop,” only three would have beaten him!

SURREY COUNTY ROAD RELAY

At Old Woking on September 23rd once again our team proved too good for the opposition and thus we won the “ Underwood ” Trophy for the third year running.

Ches Walker went off first for us and he beat Stone of Dorking and Martin of Walton A.C. to give Jack Bragginton a useful lead, and his time of 14 mins. 12 secs. beat the previous course record.

Bragginton did 14.38 to further increase our lead and then Len Adams returned 14.48 and when he handed over to Stan Davies the next team was not in sight.

Stan did 15.16 but can feel very satisfied with the thought that he is our only runner to have been in all three of our winning teams.

Archie Bedford was our next runner and he returned a time of 14.26 which beat the old record of 14.30 and then Bill Lucas took over for the final “ leg ” and he did 14.11 which was only beaten by Chataway of Walton, who put up a new record time of 14 mins. 2 secs. for this course of 2.94 miles.

OVER THE COUNTRY

With the cross-country season now upon us we welcome all members to Belgrave Hall and hope they will sample the enjoyment of a run over Wimbledon Common, with a hot bath to follow and a cup of tea and cake to round off the afternoon.

Our Club Championships are being held on the usual dates, a new addition this season being the 3 miles Youths Championship, which is due to be held on January 13th and we hope for a good entry to this race.

Many people have tried to explain the attraction of cross-country running but we think the following remarks from a recent issue of “ Athletic Review ” just about sum it up.

“ Conditions for the competitor are frequently unpleasant, sometimes they are extremely severe. Yet remarkably few fail to turn out whatever the weather or the course may be like, and of those who start nearly all will go through to the bitter end with dogged perseverance or cheerful disregard as the case may be.

Nor do the spectators often find conditions in their favour. But hail, rain or snow, they are there squelching through the mud, facing the rigours of a biting wind, yet withal keeping astonishingly cheerful and maintaining a lively interest in the racing from start to finish. Many of them, probably the majority in fact, are runners of other days. They have been through it all themselves and long association with the sport has provided them with a philosophy which is proof against anything.

No! we do not think we are wrong in our assertion that cross-country running is a sport of true greatness. It receives scarcely any publicity in the press and still less ‘ over the air,’ yet, because of the intense enthusiasm among all concerned, its popularity is growing as a comparison of our present day championship programmes with those of past years will confirm.

Sometimes we are apt to overlook the important part played by our loyal band of officials in the organising of cross-country running as a sport. Far more spare time goes into even ordinary club work than is generally realised. When the staging of big championships is undertaken it will be appreciated what a tremendous amount of work is called for, both before and on the day.

Just think of what it means to stand out in the open for a couple of hours on a cold, and perhaps wet, winter's day, putting down numbers or times with benumbed fingers, or standing out on one of the bleak outposts of the course acting as a marshal. The fact that there can be found men willing to take on such work voluntarily is in itself an indication of the fundamental greatness of this most amateur of all our winter sports.”

Such words as these say far more than I can find to put into words in order to induce our members to sample the “ enjoyment ” of cross-country running and even those who cannot go out for a run will find a Saturday afternoon trip to Wimbledon Common well rewarded.

We are always pleased to receive assistance as regards officials for both running and walking events and can always find a job for you to do.

The competition will terminate with the English National Cross-Country Championships and all clubs will be asked to keep a record of the performances of their outstanding young cross-country runners throughout the winter so as to be in a position to submit them for consideration immediately after the National race.

CLUB SPIRIT

By “ Olympian ”

The cross-country season is once again upon us and some indication of our future strength has already been shown by the results of races held during the past few weeks.

We have probably more runners turning out each Saturday than any other club in the country, as witnessed by the 68 starters in our road handicap on October 14th.

With such numbers there is no reason why those open championships which have eluded us in the past few seasons should not be quickly regained.

If clubs with fewer athletes to choose from can attain the highest team honours, it means that something is lacking in our own club that we should allow them to “ put it across us.”

Your Committee are fully convinced that we have the talent and that it only requires the revival of the keenness and team spirit which won us so many championships before the war, to return us to the “ top of the tree.”

Set your minds on that goal and nothing can prevent the name of Belgrave again being a power in the cross-country world.

It is not enough that a small body of your members should be left to attain this object. Every runner no matter what his present standard, should strive to increase his ability and make as his greatest ambition the membership of at least one of the teams which are eventually to win back those lost honours.

It may not be this year or even next, but as long as some improvement can be shown during this present season then it is a sound foundation for further achievement.

Set out to train more systematically and more frequently and keep before you the object of putting those “ claret and gold ” colours in the front.

Youths, Juniors and Seniors should set as their standards the winning of the respective County, District and National cross-country championships and do all in their power to attain the highest degree of physical fitness during the weeks ahead so that those standards may be achieved.

The road is hard and long and may at times seem hopeless but the pleasure that can be gained by team successes such as these is immeasurable.

Cross-country running calls for team spirit more than any other branch of our sport and the loss of keenness by even one of our members may deprive us of that improvement which we need so much to put us on the road back to the top.

Don't let it be said that you were that one who fell by the wayside. Take every chance possible to train either with the organised packs or by yourself and do not hesitate to approach your captain or vice-captains for advice if you are not sure of your training methods.

Do all the walking you can, you can even save money by walking to and from your work and this money will then assist with your expenses to away matches and you will also be attaining that fitness which is going to help both you and your club.

To those that can no longer be active we say “ give us that moral and vocal support ” without which we cannot press on to victory. Come along and provide that encouragement that you yourself enjoyed in your active days and by working together and showing that “ club spirit ” without which nothing can be attained, Belgrave can succeed—make this your resolution from now onwards.

HERE AND THERE

Jack Stirling Wakeley and Jack Rutland finished 4th and 5th in the Hastings—Brighton Walk on August 19th and Jack Wakeley was 3rd in the handicap.

Joe Scamell won a Veterans' mile handicap at Parliament Hill Fields on August 23rd off 22 yards.

Bert East won the Veterans' A.C. 2 miles Championship and was second in the Erith Open Mile on August 7th.

One of our latest police recruits, J. Tebbutt, introduced by Bob Readman, won the Police 6 miles novices race at Regents Park on September 30th in the very fast time of 53 mins. 31 secs. and should prove a great asset to our “ Garnett ” Cup team this season.

Welcome back to Ted Beesley who has returned to racing after a lapse of twelve months and has his eye on two or three young policemen at Ealing.

Wirrall Athletic Club, Cheshire, write to say that for many years Harry Parker was a Vice-President of their Club.

They do not wish to lose the link of friendship with our Club and so they have great pleasure in electing Tom Carter to be one of their Vice-Presidents. They send a hearty welcome to anyone visiting their district and Norman Graves, one of our second claim members and a member of Wirral A.C. sends personal greetings to all Belgravians. Thank you, Wirrall A.C., for your graciousness and the best of luck.

Harry Churcher was 2nd in an international match London v. Gothenburg.

We are happy to report that Ben Lulham, one of our A.A.A. Coaches is now out of hospital after an operation on a neck muscle and will soon be resuming his activities as Club Coach.

Len Bailey won the Stock Exchange 6 miles handicap at Regents Park.

Len Herbert represented London in the annual match at Gothenburg and ran well to finish 2nd in the 3,000 metres in 8 mins. 30.8 secs. Len also finished 2nd to F. Green of Birchfield in 9 mins. 17.2 secs. in the 2 miles race at the local club's floodlit evening meeting on October 4th at Perry Barr, Birmingham.

Geoff Pearson gained standard award of 3 hrs. 50 mins., his actual time being 3.39.28, in the S.L.H. 30 miles road race.

We congratulate Freddie Bradnum, now a B.B.C. producer in the Midlands, on finishing 12th in the recent Liverpool Marathon, and hope his duties will not prevent us seeing him at Belgrave Hall during the winter season.

At the A.G.M. of the Police Walking Section Syd Roberts relinquished his office as Hon. Secretary and was elected a Vice-Chairman, joining Jack Bidgood who also holds that office.

It is interesting to note that there is a strong Belgrave contingent on the Southern C.C.C.A. new committee, for not only is Les Cohen the hon. secretary with Harry Shields as his assistant, but our club vice-presidents—Joe Turner, Bert Ellsey, Charlie Munro and Ernie Duffett are all members of the general committee.

This is quite a change to a few years ago when Harry Parker was our only representative and shows that we are now taking a strong part in the administrative side of the sport.

Ernie Duffett is also the president-elect of the South of Thames C.C. Association while Les Cohen is on the committee, and Charlie Munro and Bert Ellsey sit as past-presidents when members of the now defunct Ashcombe A.C.

We also continue to be strongly represented on the R.W.A. with Alf Harley as treasurer, and on the Southern Committee by Alf Harley, Syd Roberts, Jack Bidgood and Charlie Churcher.

Brian Rice our young Junior half-miler has now been called-up and sends his best wishes to his many friends in the club.

Percy Wright has now added a daughter to his family.

Fred Rickards sends best wishes to all and says he hopes to be on the road again soon.

In the Civil Service Championships, Jack Brown was second in the 1 mile and Charlie Smart gained a similar position in the 3 miles event. With G. Oliver being second in the 2 miles walk and Bob Jessop winning the Veterans' race our members were well to the fore.

Ray Tooby won the Junior Inter-Banks 1 mile title in the record time of 4 min. 30.4 secs. and we wish him the best of luck now he has been called up for National Service.

In the Triangular International at the White City on August 7th, Des Mullen was placed sixth in the 220 yards and Bill Lucas finished second in the 5,000 metres event.

On August 12 also at the White City, Bill Lucas ran for Great Britain in the match against the U.S.A. and Benelux Countries and after doing most of the leading in the 3 miles race, he eventually finished third to Ashenfelter of U.S.A. with Cosgul of Turkey runner-up. It is a pity that Bill lacks a really fast finish to his races but he is certainly running better now than at any time of his career.

At the same meeting Len Herbert was third to Wilson and Eyre in a special 2 miles race, and Len clocked 9 min. 12.2 secs., which is really fast running.
