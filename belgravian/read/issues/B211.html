Message from the President

Bill Lucas

Message from the President

We are now well into our Centenary Year and after a very successful opening party on New Year's Eve we followed with an equally successful cross country event in very adverse conditions on 17th January. The Belgrave 12 stage relay on 4th April brought commendations from all concerns (except perhaps Hercules Wimbledon) and despite a wet day (a rare occurrence for this event) we can be satisfied that the race was well up to its usual high standard.

My grateful thanks go to the many club members who have worked so hard to ensure that our centenary is one to remember and particularly I would thanks the Centenary Committee, ably led by Clive Shippen, who have formed the working base for our success.

The first match in Division 1 of the National League was held at Portsmouth on 9th May and we finished sixth of the eight teams. This is highly commendable in the face of the best opposition in the country especially as we were short of our best sprinters and a couple of hiccups probably lost us the few points necessary to have finished fourth.

I could only watch the London Marathon on television but was delighted to see a large club banner, a preponderance of new club hats and a very prominent club bag - well done supporters!!! I thought the commentators could have been more liberal with their remarks about our centenary.

(Continued P.4)

Message from the President (cont.)

I must confess to a few disappointments in the running field. We narrowly missed success in the Senior Surrey Cross Country Championship held late in 1986 and were only a few points away from winning the South of the Thames race on our own course on 24th January. Following this we were seventh in our own 12 stage relay which although one place lower than last year was nevertheless a good achievement and qualified us for the National event at Sutton Coldfield. My disappointment arises from our inability to send a representative team fot this race. I realise that our athletes have many commitments but so do the athletes of Gateshead, Birchfield Tipton etc who all fielded teams strong enough to fill the first three places. I can only assume that their runners plan their year to include such events and I would like to see our own athletes, in conjunction with their coaches, planning in similar fashion. Perhaps 1988 could see us as prominent in the Winter programme as we are during the Summer.

We still have many events to come in 1987 including Club Championships on 11th July (a gala day for everyone), the Belgrave "20" on 18th July (incorporating the Southern championship), a river trip on 25th July (a night to be remembered we hope), the club dinner on 14th November which promises to be an extra special evening, the Open Walk on 28th November and the final events of the year incorporating our Boxing Day extravaganza and the closing party on New Year's Eve. Much work goes into the organisation of such promotions - please support them to the full. We look forward to seeing everyone who can possibly attend whether actively, officially or as a passive spectator.

Good wishes to you all for our continuing success.

Bill Lucas President

Bill LucasClive Shippen

RON SEVERN

RON'S MASTERPLAN

Donations:

Some members of the Club would like to make donations to its funds from time to time, but feel than an amount they could afford would, perhaps, be too little to be worthwhile. This is fully understood. At the last A.G.M. I made a suggestion which all members may like to adopt. I put forward the idea that when a member gets his or her bank statement from the bank, they might send me a cheque, made payable to the Club, for the odd pence in the final balance. Thus, if that balance were, say, £118.76 their cheque would be for 76p. A further suggestion on this is that if they felt able to, their cheque could be for the difference between the balance and the nearest multiple of 5. In the example I have given, the cheque would be for the difference between £120 and £118.76 namely, £1.24p. If, however, the balance were to be, say, £116.50p the cheque would be for the difference between that figure and £115, i.e. £1.50.

These may not seem very large amounts in themselves, but I am sure you will appreciate that if enough members did it, the gain to the Club would be quite substantial. It would also, I hope, be painless for members.

I look forward to receiving hundreds of cheques from you. Don't bother with covering letters. Perhaps I can thank you in advance?

grapevine

It was good to see Tom Morrell again at the 12-stage relay on 4th April. Tom was our President back in 1954-55 and had travelled down from Norfolk with his daughter and son-in-law Eric Hall to see the relay. What a day to pick! It rained incessantly, but Tom enjoyed being back in the Belgrave fold again. He was secretary of the Belgrave Hall sub-committee from 1948 to 1970 so was particularly interested in the alterations to our head-quarters. He reckons we have done well to get a quart into a pint pot but will not surprise anyone with the view that our changing rooms and showers need attention. Tom sends his congratulations to all those involved with organising the event and only wishes that he could have been involved too. By the way, Tom is not completely absent from centenary celebrations, his local bowls club is also celebrating its Centenary this year!

Another of our Past-Presidents, Gordon Doubleday (1969-70) was up from Bath to time-keep at the 12-stage relay. Gordon was particularly pleased to see Exeter's fine performance in our race (they were second) because he was a member of Exeter from 1939 to 1942. Gordon discovered on April 4th that Keith Jennings of Exeter was a grandson of Belgrave walker R. "Jock" Martin who was a member of our winning London to Brighton Walk teams before the war and who became a Centurion in 1946 in a 24 hours event at White City. These Belgravians get everywhere!

* * * * *

Some members will recall that two years ago the Club presented a silver salver to Jack Stubbs of SLH to commemmorate 20 years as Chief Timekeeper at our Relay. Well, although he has retired from most officiating, Jack continues to appear as a timekeeper at this promotion. He is now well into his eighties. On race day at Belgrave Hall he caught the smell of liniment from the dressing room and recounted how Paul Khlopin, a Belgravian who came from Russia at the end of the first World War, used to anoint himself with a mixture of olive oil, wintergreen and vinegar. Any left over in his special bottle he would drink! Our older members will remember the stench of pre-war dressing rooms. Perhaps they can recall other peculiar recipes.

* * * * *

The RRC Newsletter recently asked early members to drop the Editior a line. One of the first to reply was Mike Porter who was a member of Belgrave from 1948 to 1953 before moving back to Manchester. He wrote "I have many happy memories of Belgrave. Once a 'Bel' always a 'Bel'. We used to train on Tuesdays and Thursdays from Chelsea Barracks, tearing it up on the Hyde Park horse rides, plus the Saturday Inter-Club races, or training runs over the country; from those days I remember, Tom Carter, Bill Lucas, Bert Footer, Steve Charlton and many others".

Mike has been heavily involved in the veterans movement for some years now.

JACK GOSWELL, Former President, Walking Secretary and now 200 Club Organiser has recently undergone a couple of uncomfortable operations. He is now back on his feet and wish him a quick and full recovery.

At the end of January your President, Gordon Doubleday and Clive Shippen were invited to lunch by DAYE DONOVAN at her London hotel. Although it was a pleasure to meet Daye again the occasion was rather sad because she would normally have been accompanied on her journey from New York by EDMUND, one of our most fervent vice-presidents, who sadly passed away at the end of last year. He would dearly have loved to take part in the centenary celebrations of the club he joined no less than 75 years ago, but it was not to be. As a token of Edmund Donovan's esteem for Belgrave Harriers Daye presented our President with a cheque for about £1,200. It was a gesture that the Club deeply appreciates. We have every hope of seeing Daye amongst our official guests at the Centenary Dinner in November.

* * * *

Within a few months of Edmund's death the Club received a letter from a Lt.Col. J.A. Garnett who was seeking information concerning his uncle J.E. One track mind

Ernest Obeng never ceases to amaze. You would think that what with nearing the end of his research work for a Ph.D at Loughborough University as well as scorching boards and tracks around Europe he would have little time left for other diversions. Not so. In his quieter moments Ernest is a poet of no mean ability. A collection of his work has recently been published by the Exposition Press of Florida under the appropriate title 'One track mind'. Not unexpectedly the subject matter is athletic endeavour with 45 poems illustrated by guess who? Right! If ever you wondered what it was like to sprint at the highest level Ernest's poem 'Just 10' will give you an idea.

To you it's just ten seconds; To me it's been five years. To you it's just a 100 dash; To me it's sweat and tears.

To you it's just the gun then run; To me it's skills, technique. To you it's raw aggression, strength; To me it's power and peak.

To you it's just dive for tape; To me it's body lean. To you it's over far too soon; To me it's long and mean.

To you these men of varied sizes And shapes crouched on one knee Might just as well be saying prayers But who says prayers for me?

"Fastest" Belgravian Injured

"Fastest" Belgravian Injured

About 2 years ago an advert in AW requested the services of top sprinters interested in adventure. The aim was to add some beef to Britain's 4 man Bob teams which were well down the world ranking lists due to lack of "oomph" at the start. Unbeknown to us Belgrave's promising junior Dave Armstrong applied which explains why last year we didn't see much of him. This winter he hit the headlines when the Allied Steel backed British Bobs did so well in the World Champs. He season came to an end however when a cheekbone was badly fractured by flying ice at 90 mph. Dave's story goes like this.

Apparently the French team had just gone down and the brakeman failed to do his job at the finish. The result was a bob hurtling 50ft above the ground which disintegrated on landing seriously injuring all four members. The GB number 2 bob was next down and just before the finish for a reason he still can't explain Dave put his head up to check the line to be sure the brakes went on. At that precise moment ice falling off the rim of the run penetrated under his visor and the million to one accident occurred. Fortunately he is now back in full sprint training and should provide a useful back up to our 4 Internationals this summer. He aims to make the No 1 Bob next winter!

IN MEMORIUM E P DONOVAN OBE

Gordon Doubleday

IN MEMORIUM

E P DONOVAN OBE

We are sorry to learn of the death of Edmund Donovan in late November. He was one of the our oldest members and a Vice-President. He joined the club on 12th August, 1912 when he was a teenager working at Price's Candle Works - a firm with which we had many associations at the time.

He was a C-C runner and in the S of the Thames (Junior) held at Weybridge in November 1912 he finished 171st out of 220, being the third scorer in our "B" team which finished 29th out of 33 teams, the "A" team finishing 11th. Earlier that month the Bels had their annual match against T.V.H and the defunct Ashcombe A.C., the result was T.V.H 36 points, Ashcombe 67 with the Bels 70. His best performance was finishing 8th in a match against St Saviours A.C., he was a little over a minute behind the winner to clinch 3rd place in the sealed handicap.

He was probably better on the track. At our annual evening meeting at Stamford Bridge on 14th July 1914 he was ranked 20th out of 36 entries for the 880 yards Steeplechase Handicap and 49th out of 116 in the One Mile Open Handicap.

Shortly afterwards the First World War broke out and he joined the Army. The Club minutes for 22nd August 1914 stated "J W Macher was elected Auditor in place of E P Donovan who was unable to attend owing to active service". Within four weeks 33 other members were serving, and many, alas, did not return; for many years their names appeared in a Roll of Honour in the Handbook. We think that he may have been wounded. He became one of the first members of the Royal Flying Corps.

Edmund recalls that in those days the Club's HQ was the Spencer Arms in Lower Richmond Road, the course being across Putney Common, Barnes Common up Sheen Lane into Richmond Park. Then, for a short while we were at the Boileau Arms (at the Barnes end of Hammersmith Bridge). Then they moved to St Saviour's Parish Hall, Raynes Park until the outbreak of the First World War. In those days Battersea Borough Council issued special rate tickets from Clapham Junction to Raynes Park for people visiting the Cemetery - a surprising number of members seemed to have relatives buried there!

Mid-week training was from the "Rising Sun", Tite Street, Chelsea, running through Belgravia into Hyde Park and along Rotten Row. Many will remember using the "Rising Sun", Ebury Bridge Road, Pimlico in the thirties, using a similar course, with the walkers going over the bridge, up to Clapham Common, doing a lap and then back again.

After the war he went to the USA where he had a successful career in Insurance. He took no further part in active athletics. For some time he was President of the St George's Club - a well-known club for New York of English Nationality, he was also involved in the St John's Ambulance Brigade. He was responsible for many of the arrangements when H M The Queen visited New York in 1957 and was subsequently awarded the OBE.

On a visit to England in the sixties he got in touch with Alf Harley and has kept in touch ever since and was always pleased to hear of the Club's activities and progress. He was proud of two things, he still held a British Passport and he was a member of Belgrave.

Our sympathies are extended to his wife Daye who shared his enthusiasm for Belgrave, she was also English born (Fulham) having gone to the USA at an early age with her parents.

It is perhaps fitting that in paying tribute to our departed member we have, in this our Centenary year, been able to recall some of the Club's past history.

Gordon Doubleday

I have to thank Clive Shippen who kindly provided much of the above material.

CENTENARY CELEBRATIONS

Clive Shippen

CENTENARY CELEBRATIONS

Clive Shippen reviews the Centenary Celebrations

Already we are halfway through 1987. From seeming ages away Centenary Year suddenly arrived and now we are at the peak of our celebrations. Much work has gone into the planning of the 1987 programme; your Centenary sub-committee first meeting no less than four years ago.

Centenary Year was seen in at Belgrave Hall at the sell-out New Year's Eve Party with "champagne" flowing at midnight. Live music and good food produced a lively party spirit and set the tone for the rest of the year's programme.

Only seventeen days later we were back at Belgrave Hall for the Centenary Celebration cross-country match against the ECCU, Surrey Co., Middlesex Co., Derby, Essex Beagles and QPH. The weather did its utmost to wreck the fixture but despite heavy snow, which prevented Rotherham attending and decimated the Beagles team, the show went on and was a great success. Once again excellent food was provided. A centenary souvenir programme was produced, with a results supplement on the day, and each competitor took home one of our bone-china Centenary Tankards. The promotion cost over £1,000 and the Club is deeply indebted to Past-President Tom Carter for underwriting the cost of this special occasion.

The 22nd Belgrave 12-stage Relay was held on the 4th April. To mark Centenary Year a souvenir programme was produced which included a review of 12-stage relays since the early days of the London-Brighton event sponsored by the News of the World. Further publicity was obtained for the club when Athletics Weekly decided to re-print the article. Once again the weather was appalling and it was a pity that our smart new claret and gold umbrellas had not yet arrived.

We have experienced some difficulty in obtaining some of our mementoes just when we need them. Our total order was massive, as you will see on another page, and clearly not everything could be ordered at once for financial reasons. However, by the time you read this all items will be readily available. Response so far has been very good and several items have had to be re-stocked. Members should place their orders very soon as new stock will not be ordered in the final quarter of 1987.

Club Championships day on 11th July should see a large display of straw boaters, sporting claret and gold ribbon, besides the popular claret base-ball hats and gold tee-shirts. Remember, this is the big re-union day of the year and we appeal to all our distant and long-lost members to join us at Battersea Park. Many members will be arriving at 12.30 with picnic lunches and perhaps we shall have ice-cream and fruit punch laid on to supplement your meals. Club trophies and photographs will be on display and the children's "interval" programme will be re-introduced. We hope that a carnival atmosphere will prevail; and you may well witness the re-enactment of an 1887 sprint race. Make a note in your diaries now.

Only a week later our celebrations will switch back to Wimbledon for our Open '20' road run. This will be the 36th staging of this famous road race which was the brain-child of Arthur Whitehead back in 1952. The Southern Counties AAA have acknowledged our Centenary Year by selecting our promotion for their own 20 mile championship. This will enhance the size and quality of the field and it should be an occasion to remember.

The tempo for July will be kept up by the liveliest social occasion of the year, on Saturday the 26th. Belgrave are hiring the 300-passenger S.S. Naticha for a Thames cruise, leaving Putney Pier at 8.30pm. and returning to the same pier at 12.30am. There will be plenty of food, drink and dancing; but tickets are limited so do not delay.

August provides some well-earned breathing space with time to dwell on the many team committments of our various sections and, of course, the excitement of the World Athletic Championships in Rome. We cannot claim that the World Championships were slipped into 1987 in recognition of our Centenary but it would be nice to see a couple of Belgravians selected in this special year.

With the track and field season complete in September there would seem to be no reason why every able-bodied member should not turn out for our celebration 100 x 1 mile relay at Crystal Palace on the 27th. The race will be incorporated into Blackheath's 50 x 1 mile annual event and Thames Valley Harriers (also 100 years old) will join us in fielding 100 runners. We are so confident of getting a good team together that it is proposed to field a 'B' team too! So whatever your standard, you will get a run on the famous Crystal Palace "tartan" if you turn up on 27th September. This should be an occasion for the claret and gold centenary clothing to dominate the stadium.

Wednesday, October 7th, will be the Club birthday and this will be celebrated at Belgrave Hall by the unveiling of an appropriate memento and the cutting of a 100-candle birthday cake.

Four days later we hope to see the staging of our most ambitious celebration - the London-Brighton Relay. However, at the time of writing sponsorship has not been forthcoming, despite a year or more of intense lobbying, and if we cannot raise the necessary finance (£8,000 to £15,000) this fine event of National interest will not be staged.

The premier social occasion of the year, the Centenary Club Dinner, will be held at the Gloucester Hotel, South Kensington on Saturday 14th November. It will be a very special occasion, with eminent guests joining many of our active athletes on a proud night for Belgrave Harriers. Tickets (£25) are limited to only 250 so orders should be placed before the Club Championships if you are to stand a chance of getting one.

Our last special promotion will be held on 18th November when the oldest of our promotions the Open '7' Walk will be staged at Wimbledon. It is hoped to provide a new course which will enable spectators to have a better view of the race. A souvenir programme will feature a history of the event and we have every intention of making it a very special '7'.

Ray Hall's magnificent win in the Stock Exchange London to Brighton walk earned him a headline of his own in the "News of the World" (back page of course!) & 40 years ago ....

APRIL 1947 "We are now at the end of what can be termed the first full post-war winter season. From the afternoon of the season's opening meeting at Belgrave Hall the atmosphere was set: time seemed to have slipped back and the years of the war became, in many minds, non-existent. As one who "arrived back" that afternoon not having been near Belgrave Hall since 1940, it was a startling joy to see again so many well-remembered faces and to be greeted with such a warmth of welcome. Many things have changed these past seven years and this club has lost some of its dearest and best members, but we are still a club with enthusiasm and unity about us. At present most things are opposed, or unhelpful, towards the pursuit of Athletics. This country, unlike many others, gives no official or material support to Amateur Sports Clubs of any kind. Whether this is for better or for worse is a debateable question, but it is is our heritage that such clubs as ours should be free to control and run their pursuits without any outside interference or aid, therefore what we achieve can verily be called our own. We have much to achieve, both for the benefit of the Athletic world in general and for the glory of this Club. The task of this Club in the future must be to build a strong, ALL ROUND Club."

Surrey County x/c Championships - Report by Colin Pearson

Colin Pearson

Surrey County x/c Championships - Report by Colin Pearson

A good result for the Juniors. We packed in well, but it was not quite good enough to beat Aldershot and Farnham with their scoring four in the first six places. They certainly deserved their fine win. Glyn came through well to finish second. He also looked the stronger at the finish. Graham, Martin and Andy showed the sort of backing which could give them a place medal in the Southern and possibly the National Junior races when Matt and Justin Chaston come into the team. Richard, Simon, Matt and Garry could have got a medal most years previously. Adam and John finished well but probably found the course rather tough. Thanks for your support.

In the Senior Championship John Gladwin got his revenge over Clive Hensby for a couple of years back when Clive beat him in the Junior race. As they went out on the final big lap Clive was about 50 metres up on John but when they came into the playing fields with about 800 metres to go John changed into overdrive and went by the Woking to win by 8 seconds with Bob Treadwell about a half a minute further back. I am sure that Bob and Clive wish that John would stick to 1500 metres track running. Congratulations to John on winning his first Surrey Senior x/c title.

John Wright was our second man home in 8th place and should earn him his first Surrey County Vest. Well done!! It was then a case of its and bits, Andy North, third Bels man home was struggling and we could tell by the look on his face that he was not happy with his own and knowing Andy he will be seeking revenge in the Southern and National. Frank Ward also showed that he intends to remain in the "A" team. We did have some good cheer when we collected our Silver team medals but we were lucky because if Olly or David had dropped out we would not have finished in the frame. They did themselves no good by running with the after effects of flu.

Well done Seniors - and thanks again everybody for your support.

CENTENARY CELEBRATION CROSS-COUNTRY

CENTENARY CELEBRATION CROSS-COUNTRY

17th January 1987 - Wimbledon

Weather conditions had been appalling during the preceding week, with heavy snow in Eastern Counties from Kent to Yorkshire. Rotherham Harriers, who had been particularly keen to take part in this fixture, found most of their team were snow-bound and were unable to travel South. Newham & Essex Beagles had similar problems but managed to find three members well to the west of their territory so at least made an appearance.

Wimbledon Common was not too badly affected and the old revived course down the cemetery horse ride and up the Queensmere Hill presented no special problems.

The ECCU, Surrey and Middlesex County Associations had done us the honour of bringing representative teams to celebrate our Centenary and in addition to the Beagles runners Derby and QPH also fielded teams.

The race itself produced a clear-cut win for Surrey's Bob Treadwell who revelled in ideal conditions for him, leading from gun to tape. However, the ECCU strength soon made itself felt in the chase and they finished 4 of the next 5 runners home. John Gladwin ran carefully to avoid unnecessary injury, particulary on the frozen horse-ride section, but came through strongly to finish 7th. Young Justin Chaston ran particularly well to beat two of the ECCU team and finish 15th leaving a trail of good quality runners behind him.

The team result was predictable of course, but that was not the most important part of the day's proceedings. Fifty runners had commemorated our 100 years of existence and each was awarded a Belgrave Centenary bone-china tankard as a memento of the occasion. Good quality prizes also went to the first three individuals.

Hot chilli-con-carné, french bread, buns and tea were served up after the race, thanks to the stirling efforts of Sue Parrott and Margaret Stroud, whilst Brian Gorman and the bar staff kept the spectators in good fettle with their usual refreshments.

It was a pity that of our six surviving England cross-country internationals only four could be present, weather conditions keeping Len Herbert and Gerry North away. Nevertheless, to join the ever-present Carter, Penny and Stimpson we were delighted to receive Verne Blowfield who was visiting Belgrave Hall again for the first time in 37 years!

Many other guests were present, including the Mayor of Merton and representatives of the various cross-country organisations. In response to our President's welcoming address, words of appreciation were expressed by Barry Wallman (ECCU) and Alan Domleo (Derby) who felt sure that everyone had enjoyed both the racing and the hospitality.

The cost of staging such a fixture, including the commemorative awards and the souvenir programmes, was considerable. The Club was especially fortunate therefore to enjoy the generous sponsorship of TOM CARTER who underwrote the total cost of this memorable occasion.

Indoor Round Up.

Steve Williams

Indoor Round Up. (compiled by Steve Williams)

The outstanding performance this season was John Regis' tremendous Bronze Medal run at the European Championships in Lievin, France in February. His third place came in the fastest ever indoor 200m race and his startling time of 20.54 was just 2/100ths away from the World Record as it stood beforehand. It was also the third time John had improved the UK Record. Continuing a remarkable season, John made the 400m men raise their eyebrows as firstly he slipped in an electrifying 45.6 relay leg against the USA and then set a Commonwealth best over 300m two days later, leaving two good American one lap specialists well beaten.

Our other main man in the media, John Gladwin, made his indoor debut at the end of the season. John adapted readily to the tight bends, despite his 6' 3" frame, with a sub 4 minute mile win against the USA, showing an impressive burst of speed in the last 50 metres, and he followed that up by collecting a UK Indoor 2000m record behind the World 3000m Champion at the Dairy Crest Invitation.

Ernie Obeng continued the fine sequence of 60 metre runs he has set up over the past four years with a 3rd place in the National Indoor Championships and International wins against Hungary and the USA. In Budapest, Ernie shaved one hundredth off his pb to record a fast 6.62 in an invitation run at the International meeting. Life begins at 30 Ernie!

Marcus Adam made up a quartet of Belgravians in the same England team for the match against the USA for his Senior International debut, beating the American 'B' string. Marcus later retained his National Under 20 title in a Championship Best Performance and took the Silver medal over 60m in a pb. Timewise over 200m though Marcus's best was a nippy 21.76 in the Senior AAA's Series when still over a month away from his 19th birthday!

Records Ratified

John Regis's 200metres in 20.54 secs and the England team in the 4 x 400m Relay (John Regis, K Bentham, S Heard, P Harmsworth) time 3mms 7.04 secs at the European Indoor Champs at Lieven (France) have been ratified as UK records.

The Ladies.

H.P.

The Ladies.

After the good summer had by all in 1985 when we were promoted to Division II, I hope that everyone enjoyed the better competition we had in 1986 when Belgrave Ladies finished seventh in their first season in the new League. Well done by all.

However, three sad things to report. First, Tracy Denton (javelin) will be sadly missed in 1987 as she has had to have surgery on her back. I am sure that all will wish her well and a speedy return for 1988. Come on Tracy, 46m +? Good Luck, H.P.

Secondly, we have had to say Bon Voyage to Jo Hilliard who in December 1986 decided that it was time to return home to New Zealand. I think we have all enjoyed her company in her short stay with us and will miss her presence in the team. She wished me to say thanks to you all in the section for making her feel welcome and that she enjoyed very much competing and being part of the Ladies Section. She wishes the Ladies Section every success in 1987 and the future. I am sure with me you will all wish her well and hope that she enjoys her athletics back home. Good Luck, H.P.

Thirdly, another lady we shall miss for four years from the end of September this year will be Frances Guy, who will be going to work in the Middle East. We all wish her good luck in the future and thank her for her support. Also the joy and fun it gave the Ladies Section to see her win the Poly Ladies and Belgrave Marathon Championships. Thanks, Frances - we hope to see you compete for us again on your vacations back in the UK. Again Good Luck, H.P.

As you can see, Ladies, the results for the winter season were very disappointing over the country. First I must say well done to those few who turned out, and thank goodness they did. However, just a few is not enough and very disheartening for those who did. After all, running over the country surely is the basic training and stamina work for all middle distance runners and gives one a break from the long and full track season running on hard tracks. (The legs enjoy the break too). So let's see a few more new faces training at Belgrave Hall on Tuesday and Thursday evenings, Saturday afternoons and Sunday mornings during the winter. As we are lucky enough to have such a facility for goodness sake let's all use it. Next year, maybe, we won't have so many knee and leg problems.

In 1988 I would like to see us go for Division I and I think the time will be ripe for most of you and for the Section. That's what I would like for you and the club to achieve. So Ladies it's all down to you - if you want Division I competition, go out and get it.

Good Luck for 1987 - H.P.

Footnote to all Belgravians:

Harry Porter

Footnote to all Belgravians:

I am very disappointed in the support within the club. In the track season there are but a few, and they are a few, who come along to each and every meeting to help. There are some 800 members in the club and the same small group turn out. You do not have to be qualified to assist or help those people who are, who are now very few and some getting on in years.

I would like at this point to thank those few officials, and the helpers who are mostly parents of the girls, for coming along and helping. One gentleman I would like to thank is our one and only starter Mr Arthur Penny, who is some 80 years of age and still my "man of the match". Thanks Arthur from all of us.

I would like to be able to say the same for many more people next year. So come on all of you that are Bels, we all need your help to keep this the great club that it has always been. Especially as this is our Centenary year.

Harry Porter

WHY NOT BECOME A TECHNICAL OFFICIAL ?

WHY NOT BECOME A TECHNICAL OFFICIAL ?

Our Club is suffering from an acute shortage of Technical Officials. Our Track and Field programme each summer covers young Athletes, Ladies and Men's fixtures in a variety of leagues and other compotitions. In every case we have an obligation to provide Technical Officials.

WHY NOT TRY YOUR HAND AT HELPING IN THIS WAY?.

No previous experience is necessary and you may choose from the pre-season's fixture list those meetings at which you are prepared to assist. All Judges are only to pleased to help newcomers to learn the ropes. The following Categories are used in Track and Field athletics--- TRACK JUDGE, FIELD JUDGE, STARTER, MARKSMAN and TIMEKEEPER

In November each year the Surrey County A.A.A. give an evening's introductory lecture at Wimbledon for newcomers in all these disciplines. If you wish you may take a Grade 4 Test the following week, but it is not necessary. All you need is a copy of the A.A.A. Rule Book, which all officials need to refer to from time to time. Officiating can be a most rewarding and enjoyable part of the Athletics scene

WHY NOT GIVE IT A TRY?.

For all the advice you need Telephone --- CLIVE SHIPPEN [phone number withheld].

Boxing Day, Wimbledon 1986.

Boxing Day, Wimbledon 1986.

This year's Fancy Dress Run was blessed with a nice bright, sunny and mild day and despite the fact that there is no public transport on Boxing Day there was a very good entry and a good following of supporters.

There was a wide range of ideas on what is deemed as Fancy Dress and the photographs above and on the following page of those who caught the camera's eye indicates that there was no lack of originality.

The prize for the best entry, judged by a panel led by Mrs Sheena Lucas, was awarded to Brian Pritchard's Boat Race Four ably coxed by Bill Laws. The photograph does not do justice to the entry - the four oarsmen with their back to front heads, 'dummy' arms and legs to simulate the correct rowing stance was very unique and bearing in mind that the origins of Belgrave Harriers was with the rowing fraternity, it was also very topical.

After the run it was back to Belgrave Hall for what is now the traditional "Biscoe Punch" and lashings of mince pies. Those appearing in the photographs amongst whose disguise can be seen through are:- Johnny Morris, Lionel Mann, Tootsie Gannon, Bob Bridges, Andy Paton, Ron Severn, Jackie Smiter, Geo Flanagan, Ben Cooper, Bill Laws, Olly Foote, Ross Bros, Matt Kinnane and Rin Tin Tin. Our thanks to all those volunteers who always seem to be there behind the scenes preparing the food, doing the washing up etc and often finishing up without a bite because it's all gone. Thank you very much.
