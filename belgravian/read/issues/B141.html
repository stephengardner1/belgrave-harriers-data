EDITORIAL

Dear Fellow Members, Since our last issue the 1950's have drawn to a close and another decade of success and failure, joy and disappointment, optimism and (in the latter stages) pessimism, has been written into the annals of Belgrave history.

The period commenced on a note of exultation, for on Jan. 1st 1950 we were the proud possessors of the Southern Counties, South of Thames Senior and Surrey County cross-country championships, besides being holders of the Surrey County and London to Brighton road relay titles. Five years later we were only strong enough to gain one win, and three places in the corresponding events, and little realised in our disappointment that five years hence this would be a performance worthy of great satisfaction.

Meanwhile our walkers were going from strength to strength and in 1957 took all three major titles; after having recorded successes in one or more of these events in five of the seven previous seasons. A study of the Club Handbook will illustrate the fluctuations in our fortunes more fully, but it is quite apparent that our rate of progress during the last decade was considerably slower than that in the Athletic World at large.

Our main task in the 60's must be to regain our position of 10 years ago. Complacency and self satisfaction at our past are not the weapons with which to fullfil this aim, but determination, will-power, enthusiasm and the ability to realise that we must once again fight really hard to not only gain new honours but do justice to those we already hold.

FINANCIALLY YOURS........

From the Hon. Secretary.

Yes, this great Club of ours is yours to enjoy, and yours to work for with all your might. It is your inheritance, but is also your obligation. It gives you much, it must take back a little from time to time. Financially you have benefitted from the efforts of those that have gone before. It now becomes necessary to show your appreciation by assisting in the present, particularly in the field of finance.

Last year's accounts, as presented at the Annual General Meeting showed that the total cost of maintaining and running the Club was £1,505.14.3. Against this, current subscription income was £332.19.6., so that theratio of expenditure to subscriptions was approximately 4½ to 1. Members were indeed getting super-value for their subscriptions! Fortunately we had other sources of income, apart from subscriptions, which reduced the excess of expenditure over income for the year to £252.0.2.; but obviously we must reverse this trend as quickly as we can for in addition to balancing our books we need further income for expansion and to carry out various progressive schemes now temporarily postponed.

Here are the ways in which you can help the Club financially, and thereby help yourselves.

(a) Pay subscriptions, entry fees and other dues without delay, and without waiting to be asked several times over.

(b) Take a financial share or shares in our Football Scheme as per details enclosed with the "Belgravian". You'll be on a good thing and your Club will benefit.

(c) Become an Agent for our Football Scheme if you can sell 10 or more tickets weekly.

(d) Support with all your efforts our other money-making schemes.

(e) Encourage friends, not interested in active athletics, to become Hon. Members; and persuade members of years ago to take up Honorary membership.

(f) Make a donation to the Club General Funds, or any specific object, when you strike it lucky or your ship(s) come home!

(g) When you make a will remember to include a bequest to the Club.

To conclude, do remember that your financial help to the Club is vital; and that in helping the Club you are helping yourself.

THE ANNUAL DINNER-DANCE

A.A.H.

Members and friends to the number of 160 gathered at Barrington House, Gresham Street, in the City of London, for our annual function and an old cliché came to life in that " a good time was had by all".

The dinner received summary treatment from Belgravian appetites and the essential Toasts were then proposed. Our President gave the Loyal Toast, after which LIONEL WOODCOCK, the Hon. Secretary of the Race Walking Association, a former President of Surrey Walking Club and a member of the Surrey County A.A.A., proposed the toast of "The Club" in glowing terms. Bill Lucas in responding spoke of the glories of the past which had in some measure currently receded from Belgrave; but finished on a note of optimism and confidence in the younger element now developing within the Club.

Our Hon. Secretary proposed 'Our Guests' and spoke of the Club's pleasure in playing host to so many good friends from the world of Athletics- dedicated men who supported the structure of our sport and made things athleticly possible for all of us. In particular he mentioned TOM HUNT, a member of Derby & County A.C. and this years President of the English Cross-Country Union,who had travelled so far to be with us. We were honoured by the presence of HARRY BASSETT, President of the R.W.A., and many other distinguished personages. GUY BASAN, Hon. Secretary of the Association of London Athletic Clubs and also of the oldest club, the L.A.C., responded suitably.

Our immediate Past-President Frank Webb proposed the Toast of his successor in office, Charlie Jones, who made his response in well under first-class standard time. Stan Vickers received from the President's hands the "Tommy Green" Cup, awarded to the Belgrave Harrier accomplishing the best performance in the course of the Club year.

Then the dancing, and for some the chance to reminisce with old friends. It was a pleasure to see Ken O'Kell, a former Club Captain and Champion of the late twenties looking, if anything, rather younger. Former Presidents in John McKay and Ernie Duffett also seemed to have discovered the secret of perpetual youth; and there were many others we were glad to see at an annual function. It must be said there were many we should have liked to have seen who did not find it possible to come along.

Another year we hope all will be there- both young and old, united in support of a first-class function. The date in 1960 will be Saturday November 5th- at the Ardington Rooms, Clapham Junction. Mark your diary right away!.

Our thanks for an excellent evening go to Bert Jordan and his sub-committee; to Syd Roberts on Toastmaster, and Tom Carter the M.C.

MAC NEARLY DOES IT. CHELMSFORD ROAD RELAY-24th OCT.

With one and a half laps of this six lap race to go Don Maclean had snatched third place after running the first half of his leg at a blistering pace. Iron ( V.P.H.) & Ibbettson (Essex B.) were made to look very ordinary as Mac swept past, and it seemed as though Belgrave were assured of third place, with Mal Robinson yet to come. But it was not to be. Iron rallied strongly, Mac faded slightly, George Knight got cracking, and to rub salt into the wounds, White (Woodford) and Harper (Deptford) passed Mal on the run in. From third to seventh in the last 4 miles may have been disappointing but with a difference of only 29 seconds involved the excitement was tremendous.

Fifty- four teams started on the first of the 3¼ mile laps of this race, which is treated by many clubs North of the Thames as their premier road-relay. Eric Gebbett was with the leaders for most of the way and despite a lack of finishing speed was a creditable ninth; giving Charlie Walker a wonderful start with five men ideally placed for "picking off" in the 150 yards ahead. However, no one was more disappointed and apologetic then Chas. for his run this day, for with a time of 16:01 he could do no more than drop us to thirteenth.

John Moore likes a fight- at running that is! and set about the opposition as though he wanted revenge for Charlie. Seven went down for the count, and Jumbo Masters set off in a respectable sixth place. Jumbo had his usual steady run and gained a further position, it was then that Mac had his go!.

Fastest lap of the day- A.Perkins (Ilford A.C.) 14:37.

REDHILL & REIGATE A.C. RELAY- 7th NOV.

The Club entered seven teams for the Senior Relay (6x2 miles), held at Reigate Priory, but out of the forty-eight names submitted only eighteen presented themselves, plus four other keen types who were interested in running. On the first leg we started seven runners (one for each team) in the hope that we could make up the deficiences with late arrivals. By the second leg, however, we were reduced to three teams and after the third we had only two left, who managed to complete the course in 10th and 34th positions.

Our first team,made up of Shippen, Black, Walker, Robinson, Stearman and Moore wandered between thirteenth and ninth before settling in tenth place- a position far below their capabilities. There seemed to be a complete lack of fire in their running and we found minor clubs like Brighton (A & B), Deptford, Hercules, Horsham and Croydon in front. This was an opportunity for a sharpening up run for the South of Thames race the following week, but the chance was thrown away by those who failed to appear.

SOUTH OF THAMES JUNIOR- 14th NOV.

This race was from the Guards Depot, Caterham, and the course consisted of one mile of flat grassland and two laps each of two miles made up of undulating country including a short stretch of heavy plough, which gave every opportunity for the faster men to get well established in the early stages.

The mass start of over three hundred runners was an impressive sight but, as has been the case for a number of years, our boys were pushed back by the weight of numbers and could not take up the forward places necessary to win races. At one and a half miles Charlie Dabbs was 26th with Don Maclean close behind 34th, then came a gap before Alan Black 60th, Clive Shippen 62nd and Mal Robinson 68th, appeared. At three miles there was some improvement. Dabbs had moved up to 20th, Maclean was 33rd and Clive had made many places to 39th, whilst Mal had passed Alan to 60th. A half mile later Dabbs had improved further to 13th but there was little change in the remainder other than Don Maclean, who had fallen back to 40th. During the latter stages, Chas. made even more places and eventually ran out in 10th position. The next man was Clive Shippen followed shortlyafter by Don Maclean and then Alan Black, who had fought back to 49th, giving us a total of 137 points to secure fifth place.

We started five teams of which four closed in. It is interesting to note that last time this event was held at Caterham,in 1953, S.L.H. took the title by one point from our selves. Although we were only 4th and fifth respectively this year, that one point margin still persisted.

TWO RACES IN ONE DAY. LAST MINUTE CAR DASH TO ROCHESTER '5'-21st NOV.

With a star-studded field of over 200 runners warming up for the start of this annual event, five of the Belgrave six-man team were being driven at breakneck speed along the A2 in an effort to beat the starter's gun.

The announcer's final request for Belgrave to declare their team was drowned by the screech of brakes and slamming of doors (or vice-versa!) as the Bels. declared themselves in person. After a rapid change and a further dash to the start, the race was on- just thirty minutes after leaving Dartford.

After the excitement of this 'race' the second was a bit of an anti-climax for the Bels. whose six-man team for the day was Moore, Gebbett, Walker, Hallowell, Davies, and Stearman; with our Captain acting as team manager.

With Stan Eldon setting a furious pace up the long gruelling hill our lads soon felt the effect of an inadequate warm-up, but led by Moore they fought on through the field. After the half-way mark and turn for home, and two miles of down hill running to go, Brian Hallowell made full use of his long stride to improve his position in the nineties while John Moore and Chas Walker were leading him slightly. A mile from home Eric Gebbett caught John, now in the forties, and spurred him on to second efforts-Brian doing the same to Charlie in the sixties. It was here that John Davies, only a little further back, was brought to a standstill by an attack of stomach cramp and could only finish at a trot.

John Moore and Eric Gebbett ran particularly well, especially so when one realises that 13 of the 33 places in front of John were filled by Internationals.

Two things are certain for next year. The Bels. know how not to get to Rochester by road, and they are aware of the inadvisability or two races in one day!

SPOTLIGHT

M.E.R.

CHARLIE WALKER, affectionately known as " What do you think" Charlie, after his habit of enquiring after our chances at all stages of a race, has always been one of our top athletes since joining us in 1948.

In his first year as a senior over the country, running for Surrey he finished 7th in the Inter- Counties. There are not many established stars who have finished so high, let alone their first attempt. It would be true to say that he has performed best over the country, representing Surrey, the Civil Service and Southern Area on numerous occasions. He came to the fore in 1951 when winning the Surrey & L.C.C., followed by his Inter-Counties run, and the South of Thames title. Accepting an invitation for the Festival of Britain Race, where he ran Gordon Pirie to a close finish, meant his withdrawal from the Southern and could unwittingly have cost him an International vest. We sent the same team to the National that finished 2nd in the Southern, so Charlie did not run- and it is largely on the result of this race that the International team is chosen. He followed up his county form by placing second to Pirie the following year, and ran in the Inter-Counties in 1951,52,53, and 57. Previous to this, Charlie had won the Junior '5', in 1949; 1st in the L.C.C., and 2nd to Chataway in the South of Thames Junior in 1950. Those of the track tend to hide his excellent performances thereon. He was only 19 when he figured in his first championship- taking 2nd place in the L.C.C. 1 mile , a title which he won the next year after a thrilling sprintfinish with Bob Taylor. Earlier that year he had gained a A.A.A.'s standard for 3rd in the Southern Mile. After his good country season of '51 Charlie set about the track in no uncertain manner. 5th in the Inter-counties mile, and 3rd when representing London v Gothenburg brought him representative honours for the A.A.A. on four occasions. Trackwise he never again showed this form, although two years later he gained a A.A.A.'s mile standard and in 1956, third in the Sward'2' with a personal best 9:18. Best marks are: 880 1:58.6, 1 mile 4:18.0, 2 mile 9:18 and 3 mile 14:30.4. If he decides to have a full track season who knows what he will do?

He has always been a formidable opponent on the road, Gordon Pirie will testify to this. When Pirie set up new figures for the T.V.H. Road Relay, Charlie stuck so close that only 1 second separated them at the finish. It was the same Pirie who, five years after, broke Charlie's Surrey Road Relay record. A cut finger early in 1953 (he is a carpenter with the M.O.W.) meant a long lay-off from training, but, this could not stop him from running in the 'Brighton'. An injection in the morning enabled him to run and Charlie has to date run 19 consecutive 'Brightons', and taken thirteen medals!

Despite a disappointing early season, and troubled with a foot injury I feel sure that come next April 9th we will see Chas. lining up for one of the Brighton legs, after asking "What do you think?".

BOB BARNES

M.E.R.

After one of our established athletes, we turn to a promising Junior, BOB BARNES:

Bob climaxed his first year of serious racing with first place in the Belgrave Open '7' Handicap- an excellent performance for a Junior! He entered the sport after being persuaded by fellow school-mate and Belgravian John Knifton, to enter the school's mile walk, where he finished second. The next two years were spent winning school cross- country races. Instead of the country team benefitting from this period, John again persuaded him to walk; this time in the Middlesex Junior Mile, where he finished second to Brian Cornick. After this he joined Belgrave. Although, in his first walk for the Bels. he came under the judges' eye and very nearly gave up the sport. Until the beginning of last year exams. kept him from any serious training. Then,being a member of the winning team at Woodford and at Regents Park he followed this with 10th place in the Middlesex '7', in 60:10. During the 1 hour race at Highgate, where he was sixth, he reduced this time to 59:08; quite an improvement in six months. He hopes to get this even nearer the 58 minute mark before the end of the winter season, when he has hopes over 10 miles. In the 1958 National Junior '5' he finished 20th and was in the winning team. He was a little disappointed, therefore, that although he finished 6th the following year we were unable to close in a team, and so relinquished our team title.

Since the Autumn he has trained from Belgrave Hall with Eric Hall, Ted Smith and John Knifton and attributes much of his recent good form to the help they have given him. Given time he has the determination necessary to prove himself one of our best walkers.

A CAPTAIN'S MID-SEASON REVIEW

C.S.

It would seem that the hopes and forecasts expressed in the last Belgravian were over optimistic, taken as a whole. Our hoped for wins in the L.C.C. Champs. and South of Thames Junior Race became disappointing 3rd & 4th places, while our 6th place in the Surrey County Senior must have ranked with our worst ever.

However, rather than being disillusioned, I remain optimistic for the evidence has shown my hopes to be no more than a little premature. "Rome was not built in a day", so however enthusiastic the lads are we must wait awhile before they click: and this should be just in time to greet those most promising runners, Alan Black and Ken Miller who soon move up into the Senior ranks. Their 3rd & 5th places in the Surrey County Junior were indeed good ones.

Our leading performers in the County Championships were Ranjit Bhatia, 1st Oxfordshire; Charlie Dabbs 11th Suurey; Pat Newell 17th Middlesex; and David Jones 17th Hampshire. Ranjit represented Oxfordshire in the Inter-Counties but Chas, although selected to run for Surrey, had to withdraw through knee trouble. Fortunately his lay-off was for no more than five days and he is now running as well as ever. David Jones is fast establishing himself as one of our leading performers and I look forward to his presence on the Brighton road. For one who lives as far distant as Chichester, David is an exceptionally good supporter of club fixtures. I would like to see his spirit prevailing through all Belgrave teams.

Our enthusiastic Vice Captain, Malcolm Robinson, has miraculously recovered from a foot ailment which on initial diagnosis he expected to prevent his ever running again. George Flanagan's recovery from his 2½ year old stomach trouble is very slow, but George is certainly not lacking in courage; many lesser men would have hung up their running shoes by now. Our glorious Devonian, Ken Venton,is beginning to show us the rewards of his hard training, and I anticipate that he and Brian Johnston will be showing us their real form in the coming road relay season.

Most of the lads are eagerly awaiting the return of Jack Brown from Cyprus. His experience will be most welcome to the newer members of our team, and his return to competitive running could well coincide with the commencement of another successful run of Belgrave performances over road and country. The spirit is there and the ability growing, so keep the movement going lads and success must come.

JUNIORS SHINE- SENIORS DULL SURREY X-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP-CHESSINGTON 2ND JAN

W.E.L.

Alan Black, running with fire and determination surprised all but himself with third place in the Surrey Junior Cross-country. The Junior race was run first over a so called five miles, but the winner's time of 17mins 50secs indicated a distance of nearer three miles. The weather was far from perfect, a drizzle having set in afer a morning of continuous rain. The going was very soft and in parts the course was decidedly 'splashy'. It was most encouraging to see four of our five runners well placed in the early stages of the race, and even more encouraging to find Alan Black leading the field at half-way, with Ken Miller almost on his heels in sixth place and John Thresher not far away in nineteenth position. Eric Penny, our fourth scorer, in his first year as a junior was plugging away steadily about fortieth, making headway in the typical "Penny" tradition. The next time the runners were seen was some 600 yards from the finish, where a very muddy gateway had to be negotiated. Alan and Ken were together in fourth and fifth places, but Alan pulling on his reserves sailed past the man in front to take third place, with Ken still fifth. John Thresher was looking very tired but had held on to his place by sheer will power- the final burst proved too much and he had to give way to three men. Eric Penny fighting gallantly was losing a little ground, but with a vocal encouragement of "Father Penny" the chip of the old block won in the end to move up and take thirty-second place. Mike Smithers was our other contestant further down the field. We thought we might have secured third team awards but on the final count in a low scoring race we found our 62 points placed us fourth, behind Walton A.C. (44), S.L.H. (44) and Mitcham (48). What a difference it might have been had Chris Thompson been there, in the form which gained him a high place in 1959.

DABBS FIRST RESERVE.

The story of the Senior race is far different. It was intended that the "elder brothers" should do three laps but a quick decision by the officials before the start added another lap and even then the final time was remarkably slow for a seven mile race. Only Charlie Dabbs ran to form-he was prominent in a strong field throughout and eventually took eleventh place, which secured him the reserve place in the County team for the Inter-Counties at Brighton two weeks later. At three miles Charlie came through in twelfth place but we had a long wait before more 'claret and gold' vests appeared. The first was cross-country captain Clive Shippen in 39th place, with Eric Gebbett nearby 42nd. Then Chas. Walker 50th, Eddie Short 55th and Mal Robinson way back 67th. A further gap followed before Dennis Jones appeared 83rd, John Collier 90th and Mike Stearman 92nd. Don Maclean was having one of his days and was lying 120th and Alan Bishop completed our contingent in 125th place. The second half of the race produced an improvement in positions by the scoring six to the extent of approximately fifty places, but it was not enough to bring us anywhere near the placed teams and we had to be content with sixth. Not a very encouraging state of affairs!

THE "MACHER SHIELD" 10 MILES C.C. CHAMPIONSHIPS-23RD JAN

DABBS MAKES IT A DOUBLE

Charlie Dabbs notched up his second title of the season when he easily won the Club "Macher Shield" from Charlie Walker. A field of 28 lined up for the start and within half a mile Dabbs, Walker, Newell and David Jones were running together, 35 yards clear of the field. Charlie steadily pulled away from the bunch and by half-way had a clear 250 yards to spare from Chas Walker, a distance which he had increased to 660 yards by the finish. The defending champion Pat Newell, who had been troubled with a back ailment for several weeks- it comes on after two or three miles- found the pain too much and for the first time in his athletic career had to retire. Running evenly, David Jones did well to take third place and the handicap award. He had travelled up from his home in Chichester in the morning and within half an hour of the finish was on his way back.

A fast improving Ken Miller won the Junior '5' "Savage Shield". He reversed his Surrey Championship performance(5th to Alan Black's 3rd) by putting Alan a good 300 yds behind him. Ken, Alan, and John Thresher quickly set up a lead and were moving away from the field all the time. Going through the brook Alan fell and Ken was quick to seize the advantage. The 30 yards he gained acted as an incentive for he increased the distance at the finish to have almost a minute in hand.

WE RECEIVE LETTERS:

Black, Blacker & Blackest

The following letter has been received from a firm of solicitors Messrs. Black, Blacker, & Blackest of 1887, Belgravia Side, Lowdown, SHERTS:

"Dear Sirs,

We have been consulted by Mrs Betty Jones who has shown us the issue of a journal entitled 'The Belgravian' dated November, 1959.

This journal bears on its front or title page a photograph or visual image with the caption 'The President'. Our client being the wife of the President of Belgrave Harriers is most concerned that such impression or image should be likened to her husband, in fact she is almost wondering who it is.

Our client has been advised that a photograph or visual image purporting to be a likeness of a named or designated person can be defamatory if not a true likeness or one which tends to bring him or one so close to him into disrepute.

Our client does not wish to make mountains out of molehills but she is desirous of protecting her interests and feels that her position should be appreciated by the Editors of the journal concerned and the Club on whose behalf it was published.

She would be quite content if she were convinced that the said photograph or visual image were likely to fade, but the production and quality of the said journal is such that she does not think this is likely. Therefore she instructs us today that she feels she should be compensated by heavy damages and an apology.

In regard to the latter word and entirely without prejudice to her rights our client might be prepared to consider the matter closed if an apology were made in writing with the concurrence of the author of the photograph or visual image, although she appreciates that a photographer cannot be held entirely responsible for what his camera produces. Perhaps publication of a further photograph, more likeable in the eyes of our client and something in a compensatory nature, which we leave to your imagination would adequately satisfy our client.

We should be obliged to hear from you in the course of your nefarious duties and failing this our client has instructed us to take the matter in our own hands and seek retribution with the utmost means at our disposal.

Yours faithfully, Black, Blacker & Blackest.
