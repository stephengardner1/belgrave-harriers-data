Editorial · page 3

EDITORIAL

The Club's 75th year approaches its end. We now enter the final quarter of our first century with the hope and prospect of reaching heights even greater than the many we have attained during the last 7½ decades.

As an Amateur Club we have a decidedly healthy look and an excellent club spirit. This has become increasingly noticeable over the last year or so and is in no small way due to our younger members, many of whom are taking an active part in the administration of Belgrave. Young faces are appearing on Committee, progressive ideas are being put forward and there has been a marked improvement in our Socials and catering arrangments. Our athletes can feel confident that they have the backing of an Organisation which will enable them to gain maximum results from their training.

Unfortunately, we do not possess any photographs of the Club's very early days but we do reproduce as our frontispiece two pictures kindly loaned to us by Syd King which we think will be of interest. Should any of our older members have any early photographs suitable for publication we shall be delighted to hear from them.

Since our last edition the Mitchell half of the editorship has married the charming lady who typewrites all our material, whilst the Shippen half has moved house. There is, of course, no sinister connection between the two occurrences (!) but we mention them to explain the late appearance of this issue and also to draw your attention to the new editorial address.

We hope that 1963 will see the regular quarterly appearance of 'The Belgravian'. To accomplish this we shall need the co-operation of our contributors and we appeal for all matter to be sent to us immediately it becomes available. Correspondence is welcome from all sources, particularly our distant members.

HON. SEC'S NOTES

A.A.H.

Our Annual report advised the death of our last surviving founder-member (as far as is known) Mr. J.H. Martyn, at the age of 95 years. He has told how the Club was formed after a visit to an Athletics meeting at Kennington Oval, and how he was elected the first Club Captain. The changes that have taken place in the intervening seventy five years are tremendous, both in general terms and in the field of athletics. It seems incredible that the Club cross-country headquarters were once at Battersea Rise; and that our sprinters showed their speed along the Embankment, relatively traffic-free.

☆ ☆ ☆

We also mourn the passing of R.W. (Dick) Best, a Life Member and Vice President of the Club, and donor of our 880 yards Championship Cup. Dick came from the Westminster area which saw the origins of our Club; and between the Wars he was a very popular figure, with a great sense of humour and inherent charm, at hundreds of Meetings in the Home Counties. Dick had a standing order to be entered in all Club meeting running events and cheerfully paid up all his dues at the end of the season. He was a great supporter of the Club in many ways and we shall miss his cheery smile. (Cont'd.)

☆ ☆ ☆

It is proper that we should express our sincere thanks to the Club Officers who were not re-elected or did not seek re-election at the Annual General Meeting, for all their work on our behalf. Particularly, our very genuine appreciation of Sid Roberts' magnificent work must be placed on record For twenty-two years Sid has served us in our Club in one office or another; and since 1952 he has been our highly-efficient Hon. Treasurer. Business reasons compel Sid's retirement from office; and we are indeed grateful for the great service he has rendered in so many different ways over such a long period.

☆ ☆ ☆

Our heartiest congratulations go to Ray Middleton and Ray Hall who were selected to represent Great Britain on the European Championships at Belgrade in the 50 Kilometres Walk. Also to Derek Boosey who well earned his selection for the Triple Jump in the Great Britain v. Finland match.

☆ ☆ ☆

Ron Linstead also merits our congratulations on being selected as one of the four British Long distance runners to represent the Road Runners Club in the Comrades Marathon in South Africa. Despite being out of luck on the day, Ron still managed to be fifth man home in this classic long distance event.

☆ ☆ ☆

Gerry North has been doing great deeds in our name recently and we have every hope that he will lead our cross country and road-relay teams to many many successes. We could not hope to have a finer runner for our Cross country Captain; and if Gerry is given the support that it is in our power to give, Belgrave can be very prominent indeed in all the major events. How about having three men in the International Cross-country team next March?

THE PRESIDENT — D.M.SHEPHERD

A.A.H.

We congratulate ourselves most heartily on having for our President this year one who has developed in the true Belgravian tradition, - one who has not spared himself in service to the Club over many years. This trait of long and devoted service has ever been present in our Presidents, and Dave has certainly helped in a hundred different ways in his thirty years of membership.

Joining us in the early 1930s Dave was to be found at most of our fixtures and in 1934, he was second man home for the Club in our fourth team in the Southern Junior Cross country championship. Studies now claimed his full attention for many years, and although often seen, he was unable to devote the time to training that membership of our teams demanded. He persevered with his studies, showing that determination we have seen in Club affairs, and after years of hard work became an expert in the field of heating and ventilating engineering.

Now began his great work for the Club and he has served us in a variety of ways. His technical knowledge has been of the greatest value to us, he was an excellent Social Hon. Secretary for many years, a member of the Belgrave Hall Sub Committee, and in recent years has carried through admirably the task of Track Hon. Secretary. Life Membership was conferred upon him some years ago and now we honour him as our new President. For good measure, and this is typical of him, he has also become an Hon. Treasurer in succession to Sid Roberts!

Dave would be the first to acknowledge the great help he has received from his wife (the present President of the Surrey County W.A.A.A.) his late Father-in-Law Dick Savage, and Mrs. Savage. Belgrave was ever in the heart of the Savage family; and Dave quickly learned to share their regard for Belgrave and to make his contribution to our Club.

I would particularly acknowledge the great part he played with re-planning the Belgrave Hall when the freehold was acquired; and his attention to detail and the great assistance he afforded when we were planning ahead and battling with the Ministry for building licences. He has certainly deserved well of Belgrave, and his personal qualities and devotion to the Club will ensure that we have a very worthy President indeed.

We shall be proud to serve under his leadership.

A.A.H.

The President's Message

Dave Shepherd

I am most honoured to be elected as your President for this, the 75th year of the Club, and I am looking forward to a most enjoyable year in office.

Our Hon. Secretary has referred to this year as the time for a possible 'break through' and I would very much like to see this achieved. Success is not easy, all must work for it, Athlete, Coach, Official and Spectator, with team spirit and one aim - to be first past the post. I am confident we shall see improved results.

I should like to see all members making full use of the facilities the Club has to offer, it is of course, up to you, and if you are in any doubt you only have to ask any member of the Club committee, I know they will put themselves out to help.

Do not forget that Belgrave is the best Club in Amateur Athletics, that is why I joined, and I know that this was your reason also. Keep the standard up. the part you play in club life is just as important as any other member's. Will you also do your best to support the fixtures arranged by your Section Secretary, especially if you are asked to represent the Club. A lot of work goes into the organising of the meetings and you let yourself and most important, the Club down if you fail to turn out without due notice.

Social functions also need your support. If you have not yet had an evening with your fellow members, make an effort this year, I am sure you will enjoy yourself, and don't forget to bring the wife or girl friend.

Lastly, I do wish you all a most successful and enjoyable season.

Dave Shepherd.

Report · page 6

'75th Year' Dinner

D. E. M.

There was something extra special about this year's Dinner for although it was held in the same place as last year, the Ardington Rooms, Clapham Junction, and although there was the usual array of familiar faces it did mark the 75th anniversary of the Club. Despite it being a bleak rainy evening this does not mean that the spirit of the occasion was at all dampened. This is one Club event when competition is put aside and everybody makes the best of having a good time.

After the Dinner itself we settled back over coffee, wine or in some cases cigars, (Havana?) to listen to the speeches. I think most people will agree that after-dinner Speakers vary in standard considerably and that after a good meal sleep is never far away in any case. But we were fortunate in our choice. A.J.C. Kendall, B.E.M., of the Southern Counties Athletic Association, proposed the toast to the Club and in doing so, drew attention to the enormous amount that Belgrave has contributed to the sport of athletics. We were also praised for our continual efforts in recent years in promoting numerous junior and youth events. It was appropriate that the response on behalf of the Club should be made by our Hon. Sec. unofficial historian, and nigh-on Mr. Belgrave, Alf Harley. Alf mentioned several respected and notable Belgravians present including 82 year old Harry Evans (still without his raincoat), K. O'Kell, A. Foster, and E. Macher and then told us a simple story that in some ways exemplified the true Belgrave spirit. Ted Goode, a member in the 1890s heard of Belgrave, wanted to join, and in order to save the 1/-d. membership fee walked to work from Chelsea to Baker St. each day until he had the necessary sum. It was through keeness and determination such as this that Belgrave has grown into the powerful and successful Club it now is.

Sid Roberts proposed the toast to our Guests and singled out for special tribute the mums, wives and girl-friends present. D. Goodman of Surrey A.A. replied on the Guests' behalf and in his speech described Belgrave as a big-hearted Club and then went on to recall several happy moments he has spent with us and also several of those so-called "Social Runs" that he has enjoyed(?) in our Company. L.W.Woodcock of the Road Walking Association also replied for the guests and in the shortest speech of the evening heartily endorsed Mr. Goodman's opinions of Belgrave Harriers.

Our retiring President Arthur 'Nutty' Penny then proposed a toast to his successor, Dave Martin Shepherd and Mrs. Shepherd. Dave replied and in his own cheeky fashion told us an amusing anecdote of a Club outing to Littlehampton by charabanc some years' ago when it rained 'cats and dogs' all day with the result that they all returned home wet both inside and out! A bouquet was then presented by Mrs. Shepherd to Mrs. Penny, a simple but admirable gesture from one President's wife to another.

Dinner downed and speeches over, tables were cleared away in preparation for dancing. Waltz or rock, tango or twist, everyone had a go and as is usual when things are going with a swing, it was soon 12.00 midnight and unfortunately time to go home. It may be our 75th year but if this Dinner and Dance is anything to go by, there's plenty of life left in the 'Old Dog' yet, and I am sure that all who attended would agree that this was a most enjoyable Club evening and that next year's 'Do' will be another sell-out!

D. E. M.

Track Roundup

D.E.M.

We find ourselves at the end of the Track season and the first league cross-country matches already upon us. What can we say of the recent summer season? Essentially it has been another disappointing year with overall performances not being as bright as we had anticipated. As was to be expected, we are still lamentably short of real class field-events men and sprinters, although some indication was at last given, that youngsters in these branches of the sport are now doing very nicely. If we are to succeed, however, in the fierce competition of trophy matches, then we must be capable of producing a good all round, well balanced team and not rely upon our one and three milers to salvage some of the points.

On the credit side, however, several athletes are worthy of mention. In particular are we proud to see Derek Boosey gaining international recognition and proving himself one of the top three triple jumpers in the country. Whilst on the subject of jumping, it is good to see Dereck Wade improving so consistently and indicating that he'll soon be reaching the 23' plus stage. Derek is a good all round sportsman and as he gains in strength and experience, he should prove himself the best Belgrave long-jumper ever. Let us also pay tribute to John Thresher, yet another of our truly home-grown athletes. John will run anywhere and in anything and his quiet approach conceals an intensely determined and dedicated individual. This year he exceeded all expectations, even, I suspect, his own and it was only fitting that he should carry off two titles in the Club Championships. Dave Cocks must surely be congratulated on his great form this year. His quarter-miling was remarkable for one who can be so good over the longer runs.(What other Brighton relay man can clock inside 50 for a lap?) Two Club records is good going for a season and the way he is moving they probably will not be his last.

Finally, thanks to all those people who lent helping hands throughout the season. Those who held watches, pulled triggers and those who yelled down microphones. Let us hope that the coming winter will see some of the pure track athletes training twice a week instead of once and so make next track season the best for a long time. D.E.M.

MAY 12th - EALING

The undersized track at Drayton Green is invariably in poor condition and consequently the record book shows few performances of note; although one can recall Bob Taylor clocking a 4:16.0 mile there when no more than ten runners in the land had broken 4:10.0. Nonetheless there is always an atmosphere of friendly competition and this is a fixture that is always enjoyable.

The trophy was decided on the aggregate points score of Seniors, Juniors and Boys. The home club appeared to be the only one with any of the latter and this proved instrumental in their winning the contest, for their Seniors were last.

David Cocks was in great form setting a track record for the Junior 880 with 1:59.3 - half a second faster than the Senior winner! Jack Hampshire (2:08.3) backed up well for 3rd place. The Junior mile was also a Belgrave party. Winner Alan Morrison (4:37.2) was followed by Steer (4:39.0) 3rd, Bicourt (4:40.0)-and still a Youth,- 4th, and Redwood, Angus and Satchell making it 6 in 9.

The short sprint was a blow for he-man Blumberg. He was an easy heat winner in 10.3 but in the final, pulled a muscle when excellently placed and hobbled across the line in 3rd position.

Graham Worrell (24.2) and Pat Cornelius (24.8) were 3rd and 4th in the furlong, whilst Dave Mitchell (2:6.2) and F. Paget (2:12.0) took 5th and 7th places in the 880 yds.

John Thresher and John Davies produced fine efforts to beat Barratt of Ealing in the mile clocking 4:24.4 and 4:25.7 respectively. As can be seen already, conditions were certainly not conducive to fast times.

Bill Kerr ran a fine(9:25.0)2 miles to beat O'Gorman and Macey of St. Albans, but unfortunately we were short of a runner for the team race one good man could have swung both the Senior and total scores in our favour. However, we did win the medley relay and the result ended up as follows:

CLUB CHAMPIONSHIPS

MAY 26th - HURLINGHAM.

Inevitably performances generally suffered from the adverse weather conditions - a howling wind down the back straight and menacing overcast skies. We did have, however, certain bright athletic periods and the championships were supported by an unusually large number of athletes which seemed to indicate that there might be a resurgence of that old Belgrave spirit.

We 'kicked off' with the 100 and this title went to Gerry Leroy, fresh from his annual winter lay-off, who had just a little too much pep for our old friend and former international star Alan Sexton. Alan is carrying a little more weight these days but it really is grand to see him on the track again.

Later in the afternoon, Gerry, trying for the sprint double, made a perfect drop finish with inches separating the first four only to find that he had mistaken a post ten yards before the tape as the finish. This let in Graham Worrall and Pat (Nanny-Goat) Cornelius and Mike Smithers to take the medals.

The 'quarter' developed into a two horse race between Worrall and Pete Pepe, whose form over the hurdles has of late been so encouraging. In the last few strides, however, Graham just had the edge and so took his second gold of the afternoon. It was notable that there were no standards gained in this event - shades of that epic final of 1959 when the last man clocked 51.3.

Favourites often come unstuck and tipsters' faces are frequently red as they see their hottest tip floundering behind the leaders. Nobody would have given very long odds on John Thresher and sure enough, he lived up to his form by 'taking' John Davies down the finishing straight. All credit, though, to Dave Bains who took upon his shoulders the unwilling task of pacemaker. Stick to it Dave, one day they might not stay with you, remember Kuts in the 1954 European Games.

John Thresher also annexed the mile title; it must have been about his slowest time of the season but it was the manner of his victory that was so impressive. Don Maclean had an unexpected 'good un' with Laurie O'Hara holding off clive Shippen for third place.

In the Senior Field Events, we had our usual shortage of competitors, but this must in no way detract from the performances of young Derek Wade in the Long Jump. His jumping suggests that as he gains in strength and speed, he may well become another Percy Luxon and start challenging for top honours.

The Junior Championships were,as expected, extremely well supported. Undoubtedly, the star was David Cocks who set up a championship 'best' in the 'quarter' and who also shrugged off all opposition in the 'half' to gain a double. The mile was something of a humdinger with Alan Morrison and Chris Steer battling all the way up the straight with Alan just getting the verdict and John Bicourt coming in well to take third place. No fewer than the first seven were inside standard time against six in the Senior event.

M.F.J.

I review the past season's efforts by the field events section with mixed feelings. THE R.W.A. '20'

Alf Harley

Alf Harley reports:—

We travelled to Birmingham on May 12th feeling that if all went well we could even win this Championship once again. As it happened we gained third place by the margin of one point only; but our strength in depth prevailed once again and we retained the NIJMEGEN Shield for complete teams of eight.

It was a cold uninviting day over the two lap course which was plagued with traffic in parts, but the walkers found it to their liking and a tremendous number finished inside first-class standard time. From the start the holders Sheffield United Harriers swept into the lead with an impressive flurry of red and white hoops, and there they stayed until the last testing three miles revealed their weaknesses. Our lads in the early stages seemed set for a good show. Ray Middleton was walking with good judgment, the two Halls seemed to have settled down to a steady companionable race and Bob Barnes, after rather too fast a start, still looked a reliable fourth man. As the race developed, Middleton came through well picking up a number of places but was unable to break through into the first three. Ray Hall, walking the last few miles as fast as anyone in the race, picked up point after point - truly a Captain's effort. Eric Hall, however, had an off-day and found the last miles long ones indeed, but he kept going and was our third man home. Don Bartliff had taken over fourth team position for us, and confirmed that he has all the promise of championships to come in the years ahead; well backed by Ken Bailey, George Beecham, and Will Belderson. John Keown and J. Morris followed our eighth man in closely, and with Bernard Eglinton and Harry Shields completed our 'B' team. Meanwhile Ken Mathews had recorded an easy win having led from start to finish in his first 20 miles Championship, pursued by Don Thompson the holder.

Before the race our Walking Hon. Secretary and Captain had the extremely difficult task of selecting our team of eight. As Ray Hall said, we would like to "perm eight out of ten" but it must go on record that the nominated eight were, in fact, our first eight to finish - which surprised even our Officers!

In truth, we finished ten men before any other Clubs' eight - a magnificent performance. All this is indicative of good team work, mutual support and good leadership; and without taking anything away from the excellent Captains we have had in the past, we can say that Ray Hall is the best yet, and that his influence and example will be seen in our teams for many years to come.

A.G.M. 1962

D.E.M.

THURSDAY SEPTEMBER 20th.

Like the Club Dinner, the A.G.M. seems to act as a magnet for all true Belgravians. Although the Hall was not filled to capacity, there was in attendance the best part of a hundred faces, young and old, the familiar and the almost forgotten, all intent upon the business in hand, namely the administration of Belgrave affairs.

After the usual pleasantries and, in some cases, reunions, we got down to 'brass tacks! We were introduced, many of us for the first time, to Ken O'Kell, a past President of the Club and a man with a genuine and fond affection for the Belgrave Harriers. Ken was present largely for the purpose of handing over to the Club two honours boards, one recording Past Presidents, the other, International Athletes. These Boards, one of which is already in place, are to grace the walls of our Headquarters giving us a visual record of many notable members. They were duly presented, and their donor made an excellent speech which revealed not only something of his life with Belgrave, but also his pride in being a member of the Club.

Next on the agenda came the presentation of our financial position by our own financial wizard, Sid Roberts. Although we are still keeping our head fairly well above water, it was emphasised that we are becoming increasingly subsidised by football competitions and schemes of similar form . It was indeed largely on account of this that a motion was later to be proposed,and passed,by the meeting to the effect that subscriptions should be raised to 30/-d. in the case of Seniors and to 15/-d. in that of Juniors. Although the voting on this motion was by no means unanimous, it was generally felt that such an increase would in some measure lessen our dependence upon income from outside sources such as football schemes. The Treasurer's report itself was something of a sad affair, inasmuch as it marked the end of some ten years service by Sid Roberts in that somewhat unenviable position. Unfortunately, business ties have called Sid away from office, but we are sure that all Belgravians would agree that a hearty vote of thanks is due to him for the tireless and sincere way in which he has performed his duties.

The greater part of the meeting was taken up with the election of Club officers and whilst there was the usual spate of 'no changes', we must mention certain elections, not out of mere courtesy, but because they are offices which greatly affect us as a Club. First and foremost, of course, Dave Martin Shepherd was elected President (another bald 'un). Dave of course, is a great personality and if he can spread just a little of his own enthusiasm throughout Belgrave, his year of office can hardly fail to be successful As if this role was not sufficient, he was later made Treasurer, so it looks as if we're on our way to a dictatorship! At this juncture, we must also say 'thank you' to Arthur 'Nutty' Penny for the way he has filled the Presidency over the past twelve months. Certainly it can be said that during the past year there has been a stirring of the old 'Bels' spirit, let's hope this really bursts into life under Dave Martin.

Another election of interest was that of Gerry North, National Cross-Country Champion, to captaincy of the Country Section. His qualifications, of course, are insurpassable and during the brief time that he has been with us he has shown that he holds the interests of Belgrave strongly at heart. Certainly if our boys back him up, even make him fight for his place in the teams, then there is no reason why we shouldn't be challenging for championship honours once again.

It is customary during the A.G.M. for Honours Badges to be presented, and this year these were awarded to David Cocks, John Thresher, Gerry North, Derek Boosey and Derek Wade (Junior). Particular mention ought to be made of the fact that Cocks has gained his Senior bedge whilst a Junior and also his Junior Standard whilst only a Youth. At this stage too, the Boys and Youths medal competition awards were made to P. Smith and R. Dommett respectively.

A proposal made by Dave Martin Shepherd and seconded by Pat Cornelius that Arthur Penstone be elected to Life Membership was carried unanimously. This comes as a fitting reward to Arthur who, in addition to his activities in relation to the Road Running section, has also contributed considerably to the maintenance of Belgrave Hall.

After a final vote of thanks to Sid Roberts, voiced by Colin Collis, the meeting was adjourned. It is reported in some quarters, that Dave Martin Shepherd was in fact, last seen buying 'halves' all round.

OLYMPIAN

Was the 13th to be an unlucky day for us or were we to run up to our proper form, secure a place, and so qualify again for the National Relay next April? This was the thought in most of our minds as the runners went to the start in Old Palace Yard, Westminster just before 10 o'clock on a cool Autumnal morning. If confidence was anything to go by we were certain to finish amongst the first three because the spirit of the team was higher than for many a long day and competition for team places had been keen throughout the previous weeks. No-one in their right mind could see Portsmouth being beaten but the second spot was thought generally to be between T.V.H., Woodford and ourselves with S.L.H. and possibly Walton providing opposition. Woodford were without three of their leading runners due to committments on the Continent and we were without the services of Kerr, Linstead and Black all of whom were injured. The race in fact soon resolved itself into a fight between the above-mentioned Clubs and we eventually took third position behind Portsmouth and T.V.H. Walton fought us every inch of the way and this rivalry produced plenty of fireworks on the last few stages.

The race itself was outstanding from our point of view for a number of reasons. Firstly Charlie Walker was performing yet again,in his 25th consecutive Brighton Relay, an achievement of which he can be justly proud and which the Press duly noted with some complimentary comments. Secondly, Gerry North was leading the team for the first of what we hope will be many Brighton races and just how well he did it is indicated by his time of 27:27 on the 9th stage, only 2 secs. outside the record set up by Brian Kilby last April and 68 secs. faster than Bill Kerr's Club record. Then we had John Davies producing the form of which we know he is capable to take the fastest time of the day on the 6th stage. Also Pat Newell returned to the team to record another first-class performance and so lower the Belgrave record for the 10th stage by 13 secs. Pat now holds two Club stage records against Jack Brown's three - this can surely be changed next April Pat!

The story of the race is one that almost excludes the winners Portsmouth for they took the lead on the third stage and from then on it was only a question as to how far ahead they would be at Brighton. In fact, it was something more than a mile, which in some way illustrates the strength of the Club.

Eric Gebbett had been given yet again the task of bringing us near to the front on the first stage and again he did not fail - his 7th position was performed against good opposition and his time was a personal best for this leg. (21:12. 7th)

John Thresher took the second stage and was anxious to redeem himself for his moderate run last April. Starting perhaps a little too fast, he ran nevertheless, very well to take us up to 6th position without losing any ground on the leaders, Woodford,who had entrusted this stage to one of their stalwarts Eric Major.(25:07. 5th)

At Mitcham Jack Brown took over 43 sec behind the leading club on what is probably his favourite leg. How well we remember his grand performances of a few years ago,one of which earned him the record that still stands as a Club best. The encroaching years have slowed Jack down a little but have not dampened his enthusiasm and he put in a steady run which took us up to fifth place although the distanc between us and the leaders, now Portsmouth, had widened.(29:51. 14th)

Charlie Walker made a further place on the run to Merstham, chased all the way by Roly Langridge of S.L.H. and producing a sprint finish with what he himself describes as "a desperate effort". (25:32. 5th fastest)

Next came George Williams the International Walker who only a few months ago was making a great name for himself by helping Great Britain to a win in the Europa Trophy. George has turned to running and may well be the first to achieve National honours at both walking and running. Despite his inexperience of relay running he performed very well. Taking over with Harvey of S.L.H. he was unfortunate to meet a younger man who was running a little above his normal form. George did the only thing possible, he went with him and later had to suffer for a slightly too fast start.(22:33. 10th)

John Davies set off with a gap of 30 secs. between himself and S.L.H. and a deficit of 3½ minutes on the leaders. John ran his best ever Brighton leg and just missed Mal Robinson's Club record by 3 secs.(13:33.1st)

Cyril Forey was making a welcome return to the team on the Gatwick leg. He had the unenviable task of trailing Mick Firth on this windswept stage and it was only towards the end of the leg that Firth's greater experience and fitness took him away from Cyril. (18:48. 5th. fastest)

Barry Sawyer took over in 5th place and he quickly passed his S.L.H. rival and whilst running steadily did not quite manage to produce the same spark that he had shown in recent weeks although it must be conceded that he was very much on his own and so was not hard pressed. At Handcross, Barry handed over 5 mins. behind Pompey but within striking distance of T.V.H. and Walton.(31:27. 10th. fastest)

This was the position that faced Gerry North and how well he tackled it is illustrated by the fact that after 6 miles he had reduced Portsmouth's lead by 10 secs. ( their runner being non other than Martin Hyman), brought us to within 13 secs. of T.V.H. and 4 secs. of Walton. This was the fastest time of the day but unfortunately missed the stage record by a mere 2 secs.(27:27.1st)

On the next leg T.V.H. pulled out their trump card in the form of Mike Wiggs who with a great run knocked off 10 secs. from Jim Peters's stage record set in 1953 and this time in turn was bettered by Bruce Tulloh running in the lead position who ran even 30 secs. faster than Wiggs. Meanwhile our own Pat Newell, not in the least deterred by the class of the opposition, was putting pressure upon Peter Milner of Walton and ended up in third position only 45 secs. behind T.V.H. and 29 secs. ahead of Walton. (19:56. 4th. fastest)

On the penultimate leg we had Laurie O'Hara running in only his second Brighton. He took Dale Hill in admirable fashion and although losing a little ground on the two leading clubs made sure that Walton were not in a position to challenge over the last leg.(21:01. 5th. fastest)

Don Maclean has this year been entrusted with last leg in all the relays in which we have competed and has aquitted himself admirably. This was to be no exception for although he could not catch T.V.H. he certainly gave Walton no chance of 3rd place. As it was Don only missed Jack Brown's stage record by 10 secs. (17:25. 4th. fastest)

Thus we arrived at the Aquarium in 3rd place to ensure that we compete in next April's National Race. We hope that by then we have Bill Kerr Ron Linstead and Alan Black back with us, and also see such people as Miller Moore and our up-and-coming Juniors making competition for those coveted team positions really hot. With that proverbial 'bit of luck' we shall certainly be pushing the mighty Portsmouth and Derby teams very hard next spring. Supporters please book the date now - you are promised an exciting day's sport.

SAWYER WINS '5¾'

September 22nd. - A smaller field than usual faced the Starter, our newly elected President Dave Shepherd. It took only the length of Lauriston Road to separate the"fast men" from the rest of the field and round the first lap of the Common a group comprising Sawyer, Brown, Gebbett, Walker Williams and Fairclough battled for the lead with the steadier types trailing along behind the most prominent of which were Forey, Crooks and Moore. Down Copse Hill Williams dropped away a little but Tony Fairclough keen as mustard to prove himself as good as the Seniors, hung on for grim death. Forey, Crooks and Moore were all making progress behind the forward group and were getting into position to pounce on any of the leaders who showed signs of flagging. Cambridge Road as usual, sorted the sheep from the goats and Barry Sawyer returning once more to the form he showed about a year ago, pulled away from Eric Gebbett and Jack Brown who were renewing the battle they enjoyed in the Poly Relay only a week before. Chas. Walker was showing signs of tiredness at this stage and young Fairclough was finding the hills just a little too much. George Williams who seems to have renounced walking for good, was showing good form and sound pace judgment only to be forced to drop back temporarily thro' an attack of catarrh soon after passing the church. Meanwhile Barry was going further ahead and looked a certain winner with just one lap of the common separating him from victory. Eric and Jack were still locked in deadly combat and Chas was obviously contenting himself with 4th place. George Williams got going again after the "dip" and set off at great pace to recoup his losses.

John Baker excelled himself at the tail end of the field with his fastest run to date over this course which secured him the first position in the handicap. George Piddington also achieved a personal best to take 2nd place and the race winner Barry Sawyer, with an excellent 28:43, faster than is normal for the autumn race, snapped up 3rd prize. Overall the result augured well for the imminent Brighton Relay.

BELGRADE

Ron Langheim

We have all read Hackett, Moor, and Allen on the Championships. We have all thrilled to the films of Messrs. Tulloh, Brightwell and Kilby winning gold medals. Now read the unpublicised story of the exploits of four Belgravians in Belgrade!!

"West London air terminal was the meeting point for the 'Athletics Weekly' party to the European Games. A motley crowd had assembled all keen to set out on a journey which was to provide most of us with our first glimpse of life behind the Iron Curtain. The Belgravians present were Mike Shingles, Fred Paget, 'Glimpy' Jones (Keeping a tight grip on his two main possessions in life, stopwatch and camera) and myself.

After a five hour flight we arrived in Zagreb where we boarded a coach for the trip to Bled, a lake near the Austrian border. Here we joined the party who had made the long train journey. The next stage of the journey was a twelve hour coach trip to Belgrade that soon had all concerned well and truly 'Shattered'. The only person who seemed to be enjoying himself was 'Glimpy' Jones who had armed himself with meat-pies and tablets of all colours. We arrived very tired and just in time to fall into bed - oh what a night! The bed was like iron and we were just nodding off when angry voices could be heard on the staircase - 'Travel 12 hours and find no bed!' In burst Brian Nott of Ranelagh, a room-mate of Glimpy's, struggling up to the top floor with a bed under his arm. Apart from the sleeping arrangements in the hotel, our stay here was most enjoyable and there was even an English-speaking waiter keeping a beady eye open for those who dodged paying for their beer at meal-times.

We travelled on to the Adriatic coast and it was here that we ran in an epic road relay - started on the sea front at seven in the morning by Jimmy Green of A.W., it managed to convince the local inhabitants that the English visitors really were 'bonkers.'

We arrived home with empty pockets although Glimpy managed to bring home some samples of Yugoslave meat-pies, whilst 'keep-fit' enthusiast Fred Paget smuggled home a jar of local honey. All in all, a hectic holiday, but nevertheless a really enjoyable time was had by all concerned."

BELGRAVE OPEN '20'

JULY 21st - This was the 11th annual 'Bels 20' and before all else, credit must be given to Arthur Penstone for the admirable way in which he organised the race. His Worship The Mayor of Wimbledon (Alderman T.H.Metcalfe J.P.) acted as starter and Ernest Neville presented the Surrey County awards as this championship was incorporated into the overall race.

On the first of three laps the leaders were our own Gerry North; Tony Towl and Ted Richardson with other Belgrave athletes such as Gebbett, Dabbs and Linstead also moving well a little lower down the field. At about 11 miles Gerry went out alone - passed 12 miles in 63:30 and was leading in fine style at 15. But at this point, Ron (Best-Bitter!) Linstead was beginning to make his presence felt and at the 15 miles mark he was only some 50 secs. behind North. The strain of leading now began to tell and as the wind on the third lap hit Gerry again, Ron relentlessly closed the gap. At the finish Ron was a clear winner by some 52 seconds and his winning time ranks 2nd to Fred Norris's all time best (1:47:26) whilst Gerry's time is the fourth best ever recorded.

Eric Gebbett ran his usual evenly-paced race to finish seventh with Chas. Dabbs making a most welcome return to something like his old form, eighth. In the team events the Bels. did very well indeed winning both the open and the Surrey County events. In the final analysis, therefore, Belgrave were successful on two scores; their athletes performed excellently and the event was organised in the best Belgrave traditions of hospitality and efficiency.

ROAD RUNNERS CLUB

Road Runners Club was formed in an attempt to channel the abundant enthusiasm that existed, and still does, into an Association uniquely qualified to cater for this branch of the sport as well as to know and foster the interests of the runners themselves who constituted the Club. The Association was actually founded on April 16th 1952 with the object of encouraging running and in particular road running. Membership is confined to amateurs who are members of clubs affiliated to the A.A.As or to the governing body of the country in which the athlete resides. The minimum subscription is 5/-d. per annum payable on April 1st in each year.

A News Letter is published and sent to all members F.O.C. on average 3 times a year. Besides giving detailed results of R.R.C. sponsored races, it gives results of other major long-distance road races held in this country and abroad; articles are often written by runners themselves describing their training and experiences in races.

SOUTH AFRICAN JOURNEY

Ron Linstead

At 7.p.m. on Saturday 19th May, the R.R.C. team of John Smith, Don Turner, Tom Buckingham and myself, boarded a B.O.A.C. Comet 4 which was to take us to Johannesburg in some 18 hours. We were off to compete in the world's longest, hottest and hilliest road run - the 54 miles Durban to Pietermaritzburg 'Comrades Marathon'.

The flight was really thrilling. We had views of the Eiffel Tower, the Alps, the Nile, Mt. Kilimanjaro, the Victoria Falls and the great forests of Equatorial Africa. We stopped en route at Rome, Khartoum, Nairobi and Salisbury. On the plane we fed and drank well, the meals being superb.

We arrived at Jo'burg at 2.p.m. on Sunday and some two hours later John and myself were out training. We found it tough going in the rarified atmosphere, for Jo'burg is at an elevation of 6,000'.

The next day we did a hard 23 miles over a very hilly course. The sun, of course, was shining brightly and Tom Buckingham became severely sunburnt. On the Tuesday we were taken by car to Pietermaritzburg some 300 miles away; this was to be our pre-race H.Q. P'burg is in the beautiful province of Natal, an area rather like the Scottish Highlands except that the sun is always out and there are no lochs - only winding rivers. You can guess what an ideal situation it was for training. In the eight days prior to the race we did one run of 30 miles and six of between ten and 17, with perhaps the occasional stride out. It was sheer joy to run in that hot sun through such lovely scenery. Sometimes we left the roads and went over the dirt and gravel mountain tracks and had superb views. We felt that we were hundreds of miles away from civilization. Occasionally we encountered native kraals and villages where our appearance would cause a sensation. Often barefooted children would run with us for a mile or more, seemingly untroubled by the rocky flinty surfaces. It was all so very peaceful - there was no sound of traffic, no golfers, horseriders, dogs or any of the encumbrances one encounters on Wimbledon Common and in Richmond Park.

In P'burg, we stayed with 'Toppy' Fanver a 40 year old Accountant who was also to take part in the race. In common with many other South Africans he was no one-sport man, for golf, cricket, tennis and carpet bowls were among his interests. Thoughtfully, he kept a fridge stacked with lager. It was great to sit in the bath after a run and have a 'nice cool beer'. We ate well, too well in fact, as I was to learn to my cost. Food is so cheap. A sack of oranges, for example, costs only 31/-d. Spirits and cigarettes are other cheap commodities being only 50% of the price they are here. It must be hard for the South African distance man to keep fit.

The time passed quickly and and 6.a.m. on Wednesday 30th May, we found ourselves lined up with 150 others for the start of the race outside the Durban Post Office. Many of the entrants were just running to finish the gruelling course and so win a Comrades Silver Medal and the right to wear the distinctive blazer and tie. Some were rugby players, canoeists, etc. who were starting because they had strictly unofficial bets on! Bets made, one suspects, when the lads were concerned were full of beer! The ages ranged from 18 to 60; one runner was the blind 60 year old Ian Jardine, for whom two others had unselfishly agreed to act as guides. All competitors had until 5 p.m. to finish - eleven hours. In those who were just running to finish, the true spirit of the aptly named 'Comrades Marathon' was exemplified for they would be out in the cruel sun longer than any of us, helping and encouraging each other.

The start was very fast, with the green vested South African Marathon Club runners Jackie Mekler, Fritz Madel and Frikkie Steyn opening up an immediate lead. Tom Buckingham was the only R.R.C. man to go with them. The first twenty five miles was a steady climb reaching an elevation of 2,500 feet. Just picture it, Copse Hill lasting for almost the length of a marathon! Here briefly is how the race progressed - the distances and altitudes are approximate.

At 31 miles came the gruelling 2 miles long 800 feet high climb up Inchanga. I moved up past Madel and Steyn here. From this point on, Mekler started to tire, and Smith eventually caught him at 44 miles. I took a bashing down this stretch and spent time off the road with stomach trouble (every time the grass rustled I suspected a snake was approaching!) At 45 miles I went by Tom Buckingham and moved into 3rd place and I now felt really hopeful. By 48 miles however, I 'blew up' weakened by stomach pains and cramp around the kidneys. Here Turner went by me, looking for all the world as though he was on a normal Sunday session round the gentle Surrey roads.

At 49 miles came the nastiest hill it has ever been my misfortune to run up. 1½ miles long and 500 feet high, it was the notorious Polly Shorts Cutting, named after the man in charge of the road construction gang. Just picture yourself going up a hill four times as long and twice as steep as Wimbledon Hill, with legs battered by some 49 miles of hard going (about 50,000 strides) and on top of it all a midday sun beating down, the temperature being about 90°F. Macdonald and Footer couldn't have supplied enough superlatives to sum up my feelings. Each stride felt as though it would be my last, I resolved this would be my final road race, wished I had never been a runner and cursed myself for ever getting into the team. How thankful I was to reach the top! I was now feeling half-dead and Buckingham went by me. However, with the nearest South African four miles behind, I now had just to concentrate on finishing. Ahead lay four miles of gentle down gradient, where I was able to recover somewhat. Over this part the crowds lined the roads two or three deep. They were delighted at our success - those of British origin having little love for the Boer descendents. Natal is very pro-British. As I ran , came remarks like "Come on Limey, you've b-----ed up Republic Day!" One woman was heard to say "He looks nice and fresh". Miraculously, I didn't stop to belt her one! Eventually I got there, and did the final 300 yards over the soft grass of the Collegians A.C. ground.

It was a great relief to stop. Someone, whom I would remember in my Will if I had anything to leave, thrust an opened bottle of beer into my hand. It was just great and the contents disappeared in a few seconds. I recovered quickly after that and after a bath and more beer, I felt quite O.K., although some photos I have seen since, show that I looked tremendously gaunt.

John Smith finished first in 5 hours 57 minutes and 5 seconds. Mekler did 6 h. 4 mins 4 secs. Turner, Buckingham and myself recorded 6:7.8; 6:8.26; and 6:10.39 respectively. These times placed us 2nd 4th 5th and 6th on the all-time list, ahead of anything Hayward, Walsh, Newton and Allan had ever done. The sixth man, Charley Chase of J'burg did 6:30.42 - he hadn't been nominated for the S.A. team and spent four days before the race drinking in Durban bars!

There was a great social after the race. Having spent 6 hours in the hot sun chasing my team mates and Mekler it was a welcome change to chase the local talent round a dance floor!

Lack of space, I feel, must forgive me to curtail detail of the last ten days of the trip. The day after the race we went back to Jo'burg and thence to the Krugu National Park Game Reserve for a long weekend. Here we saw the wild animals in their natural surroundings, from the safety of a car. During this trip we covered hundreds of miles over crude roads, passing through breath-taking mountain scenery. We saw tobacco, oranges and sugar cane being grown.

Back in Jo'burg for the last few days we were entertained at many parties and dinners. Usually on such occasions I had to get up and make a speech - indeed, I became fairly proficient for the last one.

Inevitable we were shown down a gold mine, which proved most interesting. Friendly as our miner guides were though, we weren't given any free samples. On Friday 8th June, our stay in South Africa ended and we left Jan Smuts Airport for the long journey home. We were by then very tired but quite contented. Our stay had been a happy one, for the people we met had been friendly, generous and hospitable. It had been a great experience made possible only by the generosity of those who had contributed to the R.R.C. appeal, and here I must thank all those in Belgrave for their donations.

Youngest finisher: 18 yrs old Roy Palframan 62nd in 9:49.55.
