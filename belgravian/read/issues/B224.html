Editorial · page 3

The Games

The Olympic Games. No matter how commercialised they become, or "over the top" with sports that would have been beyond de Coubertain's comprehension, the Games, when each Olympiad rolls around, bring something special to the world of athletics. Apart from the sporting feast beamed into our homes for a couple of weeks, real competition without a pacemaker in sight, there is the exciting prelude for fan and athlete alike - the need to qualify. Impressing selectors, achieving the qualifying standard and fulfilling requirements at the trials are dramas in their own right. The World Championships might be the athletic event for the connoisseur but as far as Joe Public is concerned the Olympic Games are what really count and therefore the urge for athletes to shine at this event is even greater.

Luck and good health allowing, we hope for a strong Belgrave presence in Atlanta's Olympic Stadium in a whole range of events. Our distance runners will have the problem of 34°C temperatures and humidity of well over 90% to face but the sprinters and hurdlers will enjoy the Mondo track which is already acknowledged as being "lightning fast". We won't tempt fate at this stage by naming those likely to gain selection but we wish them all the very best of luck.

Editorial · page 3

Gymnasium project completed ...

Have you been up to the Hall recently and seen the gymnasium. There is probably not another athletic club in the country that has its own gym equipped to such a standard. Several new members have already come our way, attracted specifically by the chance to use the facilities for a mere £25 a year (£15 second-claim), and we wholeheartedly thank those who have kept this particular project on track. To have our own gym has been an aim that we have pursued for the last couple of years and it is good to see the plan draw to a successful conclusion. But this is just one of diverse schemes that we are following. Not everyone sees each project of being of equal in importance and some undertakings do not even gain immediate benefit for our club, being aimed more at assisting the community as a whole with the hope of a dividend being returned to Belgrave Harriers only in the longer term. One thing is sure, however, we have some very dedicated and hardworking officers. Although members of our Committee do not always see eye to eye when it comes to agreeing which road we should take next, it has been said recently that the Club has probably never been in such good shape.

Nevertheless, while we are pleased with the progress we are making, there is no doubt that we could do with a lot more help. On a summer Saturday we might have as many as four track & field teams in action across the country, not to mention walking and road running groups; and there may be a couple of teams in action the following day as well. This is a tremendous drain on our small band of dedicated and enthusiastic officials and team managers. Goodness knows what we would do without them. If you can contribute towards helping to run our Club in any way then please get in touch with the Club Secretary who will be only too pleased to point you in the right direction. We would particularly like to see parents and younger members (exams permitting) getting involved.

Column · page 4

Sidetracks

Clive and Elizabeth Shippen have safely arrived in Sydney, Australia, and are currently living in Dee Why with son Mark. Clive has been running several times a week, mostly in Dee Why Park but sometimes on a circuit of Long Reef that takes in stretches of beautiful coastline. After one session, warming down consisted of an hour of lying on the beach enjoying the sunshine. Tough eh! The Shippens soon met up with Ron Wyld and helped him to celebrate his 60th birthday along with other guests Dave Scott, Joy Gardener (Pete's mother), and Scott Wyld and almost before Clive's feet had touched the floor he was being inveigled into helping to coach pole vault at Homebush Stadium. Apparently Ron drives a claret Datsun Bluebird with yellow number plates! Clive and Elizabeth pass on their regards to all.

Several Belgravians have been in hospital for one thing or another recently. Colin Pearson underwent gall bladder surgery just a couple of weeks ago while Keith Harris, a regular behind the tea bar on washing up duties is in nearby Atkinson Morley Hospital at the time of writing. Arthur Penstone who does so much to keep our Headquarters up to scratch has also been undergoing treatment. Our best wishes to you all and we look forward to hearing of continued good progress.

George Piddington is now more mobile and has enjoyed being visited by various members. He is taken to his local track every Tuesday by his Havant AC colleagues where he helps with coaching.

The 32nd holding of the Darts Championship for the "Denmark Pot" took place at Belgrave Hall on December 2nd and once again Bill Couzens was the victor. That makes it three years on the trot for Bill - a feat he last attained 1963-65.

We are very pleased to welcome several new officials to our ranks. A series of training courses were held at Belgrave Hall during the winter followed by tests and the result was that we now have three newly qualified track judges in Pam Davies, Leslie Earle and Richard Hearn, and two new time keepers in Terry Earle and Phil Mason. Already these good people have lent a hand at our matches and we are more than grateful for their help.

At one point during the Surrey County Championships we noted that on the Track Judges' Stand we had three Belgravians in action while across the other side of the straight three more were holding stop watches. With one or two out on the field as well, and several carrying out administrative tasks we can start to feel pleased that we are contributing a little more to our sport as a Club rather than leave it to a few individuals. But we need so many more (see our Editorial page).

Talking of Timekeepers, it's great to see Ted Moore back in action and looking remarkably well after his stroke. Ted has been timing at several school's matches and has even been up to Battersea a couple of times for our home fixtures. You can't keep a good man down for long.

From The Belgravian

25 Years Ago ... June 1971

BELGRAVE bounced back into form in the Parliament Hill mud this February, with a highly encouraging second place in the Southern Cross Country Championships. The most heartwarming sight of the afternoon for Belgrave supporters was that of the flying figure of Gerry North, who made a thrilling attempt to recapture his title, and had he not been hit by his old enemy 'stitch' might well have done so. As it was, his 4th place just 20 seconds behind the winner gave us a magnificent start and with another three men in the first 34 it was a good day for the Bels. Result: 4, Gerry North; 30, Tony Brien; 32, Lionel Mann; 34, John Bicourt; 42, John Thresher; 79, Tony Fairclough. Team: 1, Reading 137pts; 2, Belgrave 221pts; 3, Hillingdon 225pts.

RWA NATIONAL 10 MILES WALK. In one of the closest races yet Belgrave only clinched the team title in the closing stages. There was never more than a few points in it as our boys battled for the vital places. Wilf Wesch, our reigning champion, was concentrating on staying safely in the race, and avoiding any possible trouble finished a comfortable fifth. Result: 5, Wilf Wesch; 13, Carl Lawton; 16, Allan Callow; 29, Ray Middleton. Team: 1, Belgrave 53pts; 2, Lancashire W 65pts; 3, Southend 68pts.

CARL LAWTON walked brilliantly in the National 20km and clearly deserved his first selection to represent GB in a race abroad - competing over 20 kilos in Bucharest.

TONY BINDA, Peter Carton and Bill Curtin yet again filled the first three places in the final Surrey League. With Mark Clancy (5th) and Paddy Breslin (7th) Belgrave were runaway winners of both match and League title.

and, 50 Years Ago ... June 1946

THE FIRST post-war Winter season was a memorable one for the Belgrave Harriers. Our cross-country runners won every open championship. In November we had the first two teams home in the South of Thames "Junior," and the first three individuals. This in itself was a remarkable feat as all of our pre-war "cracks" cannot run this race owing to the barring clauses. In the New Year we won the Surrey County Title, whilst our Middlesex members, running for Cadogan A.C., took the Middlesex title. This was not regarded as very extraordinary as it had been done on various occasions before the war. The South of Thames "Senior" Race resulted in another win for us. Here again, a large number of our best men are "barred." The Southern Counties Cross-Country Championship, 7 ½ miles instead of the usual 10, was held from Belgrave Hall. Our Captain, Tom Carter, gained a well-earned and popular victory. With David Main third and four others in the first 20 positions, we gained a 99 points' margin over the second team. In fact our team of 12 was home before any other team had finished its six scoring men! Then to the much awaited English Championship, of which we were the proud holders. On March 9th our lads travelled to Leamington Spa and had another battle royal with our famous Midland rivals, Birchfield Harriers. Our team finished in the first 24 positions, and after a breath-taking adding up of the scores we were acclaimed winners with a ten-points' margin. We are indeed truly the Champion Club. Of individuals, Tom Carter deserves our thanks and praise for the successful training and nursing of his team, also, of course, for his own brilliant running. It was a cause for general regret that Tom did not get another International vest. Tom had had a cold the week before the race, which no doubt left its effect. Len Herbert, by finishing seventh, was awarded an England vest, and we congratulate Len on adding his name to the distinguished company of Belgravians so honoured in the past.

Belgrave Dinner Dance

NEXT NOVEMBER - Saturday, 16th, to be precise - sees what newspaper journalists call "the end of an era." That is the date of this year's Belgrave dinner-dance (put the date in your diaries and start saving for it now) and, after thirteen years of organising it, Bill Couzens is taking a rest. He is handing this great event over to Ron Severn who will actually, with Bill's help and guidance, be responsible for this year.

Some of you may not know Ron. He joined Belgrave in 1983 after leaving High Wycombe, where he had been successively Secretary, Chairman and Trustee of Wycombe Phoenix Harriers, as well as organiser for years of the famous "Furniture Five" road race. Soon after joining Belgrave he became Hon. Treasurer, which job he did for five years, and upon giving it up he was elected a Life Member of the Club. He has been away from the innermost affairs of the "Bels" for a while, but is back now, not only for the dinner-dance, but also as a new auditor of the Club, taking over from Ray Conway, another long servant.

Ron says: "Following Bill with the dinner-dance will be almost impossible, he has made such a huge success of it for so long. All that I can say is that I plan no changes in the venue (Preston Cross Hotel) or the format, so I hope everyone can look forward to the same wonderful evening they have always enjoyed. But I need all the help I can get in the sale of tickets, so will everyone please make a special effort this year to buy them, not only to save me many sleepless nights, but also - and this is much more important - to see that Bill and his wife Gwen (who has herself made a great contribution to each year) get a grand 'send-off' and 'thank you'. "

Feature · page 6

Marathon Dave faces his toughest challenge

JUST UNDER a year ago Fleet Street journalist Dave McMillan, a member of Belgrave Harriers for the last 34 years, was delighted to receive a commemorative medal and tee-shirt from the London Marathon organisers congratulating him upon being one of only 41 runners who had completed all 15 London Marathons. Dave's first athletic events had been the sprints back in the '60s but he was always ready to have a go at anything and was regularly to be seen in his teenage years, plugging away at the back of the "Southern" and "National" cross-country fields.

He was so proud to receive that letter from Alan Storey because gradually, over the years, he had got faster and faster in 10ks, half and full marathons until at the age of 47 he had just set his latest pb over the London course with 3:03:05. Club-mates jokingly reckoned that by the time he was 60 he would be down to about 2:30! Dave was determined to carry on until he was the only person left who had completed every single 'London' but sadly fate does not always favour the nice guy.

While out for a Sunday morning 13 miler in the Warwickshire country lanes near his home earlier this year and with just a mile or so to go before that hot shower, he was cruelly struck by a speeding car and received the most horrific injuries. With a leg shattered in four places, broken ribs, punctured lungs, a crushed arm ... his proud record of consecutive marathon runs was quite obviously over.

But you know, runners are such a positive breed and no-one could be more determined than 'Dave Mac'. For a start he was pleased to be alive. The ambulance rushing him to the Radcliffe Infirmary in Oxford had to radio ahead to report that he was likely to be dead on arrival unless special surgery was carried out immediately. Another ambulance with the necessary equipment was dispatched and at a rendezvous in an Oxfordshire country lane an innovative emergency operation was carried out.

A three month minimum predicted stay in hospital was then reduced to a month as Dave oh! so painfully carried out every exercise and more prescribed by the doctors. He had already told the hospital staff that he would be running again and they devised additional 'work-outs' to keep tendons and muscles in tone for that big day in the future.

Alan Mead

Both of these races could provide rich pickings for us next year. Few of our runners currently suffer from the barring clauses that prevail in these races - so let's go out next winter and get barred in a big way!

We never did have the main championship events inked into our calendars but when one looks back one can see that with a little better organisation we could have won the Surrey title and with Matt Yates finishing in the 60s in the National one couldn't help getting excited about what we could achieve if we had enough runners with the same aim.

On to the Southern 12-Stage. Sadly the wheels came off during the last few days but to end up 8th was a fine achievement and surely a herald of what is to come.

Finally the London Marathon gave us the high profile win that we were looking for. Our negotiations bore fruit and as a result we may well be pressing for an amendment to BAF's rules to make them more workable in today's sport.

So now it's all before us. If we really do want to move on, we can. We have a good team running the show and as Team Manager I would like to thank Charlie Dickinson, Gordon Biscoe and Don Anderson for their help but most of all thanks to you the runners led by Captain 'Gerry' Adams and Vice-Captain Roger Alsop. Already I am looking forward to the prospect of next winter's campaign. There's the challenge of completing teams in the major championships, County and National 6-Stage Relays to have a crack at, the defence of what we have won this winter past.

We're in the medals

SOUTH OF THAMES "JUNIOR" CROWHURST, 11 NOV 95

We're in the medals

It was heartwarming for all those involved in cross country to watch a fine second place from our team and an inspired individual 4th from captain Graham Adams.

Overnight rain, the first for several weeks, had softened the woodland paths which together with some very tough hills and more rain during the race made for a demanding course. A narrow downhill start produced the predictable manic rush with the Graffin twins from Tonbridge already heading the fray just ahead of Graham Adams. Then came a bunch of Belgravians, Roger Alsop, Nick Kello, Phil Carstairs, and Said Chettati nicely packed in the low twenties.

The end of first short lap saw Graham maintaining his position in the leading bunch, Roger moving up, Nick dropping back slightly, and the others roughly the same. Futher back Steve Willimont looked unhappy but Tony Binda and Paul Cooper were running well.

At the halfway stage a breakaway group of seven runners were forcing the pace with Graham Adams hanging on at the back. Roger Alsop continued to move up into the low teens and Phil Carstairs, not too well lately, was beginning to look uncomfortable. Graham made a huge effort after the long hill on the final lap picking off three tired bodies in a long run for home. An excellent effort.

Roger showed that he is fast getting fit again after achilles tendon problems and a disappointing track season. He too finished strongly and got up to 11th. Next came Said who had got past Phil Carstairs and ran himself almost to a standstill in finishing 29th, another very good performance.

Although suffering badly, Phil managed to hang on to the end without dropping too many places which was a good job as Belgrave only finished one point ahead of Basingstoke. Further down the field Nick Kello pulled a calf muscle but battled on gamely and cheerfully to the end. Tony Binda did well to finish 94th and we all know he has the potential to become a very good veteran. Paul Cooper too has a great deal of natural ability, he just needs to train harder and drink less!

In all a very good day, well done Belgrave and well done Graham.

League hopes take a dive

REEBOK SURREY CC LEAGUE DIVISION 1 RACE 2 RICHMOND PARK, 18 NOV 95

High hopes and hard work in attempting to round up a strong team counted for nothing. While we went through the list of Belgravians we had hoped to put on the starting line but who had failed to appear for one reason or another, Hounslow were at their strongest and romped to a clear win with 8 men finishing in 28. Luckily for us, but unluckily for the "Irrepressibles," South London H crashed so badly that they dropped from 6th after match 1 to 8th here and as a result we actually moved up in the league!

Sweeney on his tod in 'Surrey'

SURREY COUNTY CROSS COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIPS FRIMLEY, 9 DEC 95

Those who braved the icy conditions at Frimley were treated to a wonderful victory by Chris Sweeney, returning to competition after three years of injuries. Chris was running as a second claim member for Belgrave and was unfortunately unable to score for the team (not because second-claimers are barred but because he only entered at the last minute). The rest of the squad all ran well but just lacked sufficient depth to claim any medals although it is worth noting that had Chris stated his intentions earlier we would have been within a point or two of winning.

Two times around the frozen playing fields and both Chris Sweeney and Graham Adams were well up in the leading pack; Chris looking very relaxed, Graham already working hard. Further back Jim Estall and Roger Alsop were nicely placed in the second group. Then the field climbed up the steep bank into the woods for three tough undulating circuits.

By this time the sun had disappeared and with it any semblence of warmth. Many runners never really warmed up and found the hard rutty conditions very tiring. After the first lap Chris Sweeney had already forged a useful lead and he looked comfortable and confident as he floated over the ground with his economical style. As the race progressed so his lead grew, all the way to the finish. This was really the first indication of the form which later took Chris to a successful Grand Prix series and a Great Britain vest in the World Cross Country Championships.

Graham Adams again led by example and battled around the course to finish in 7th, an excellent result which reflected his strong early winter form. Then Jim Estall, unhappy with the cold, but finishing a fine 14th, again ahead of BVAF National Champion Barry Atwell and also Bob Treadwell. Roger Alsop ran steadily for 27th, then a gap before Mike Nouch and Junior Galley in the late fifties. One more engine room runner here would have given the Belgrave team medals even without Chris Sweeney, but it was not to be and it was thanks to Tony Stone for completing the scoring six back in 185th position.

Leo Coy's lads looked as if they would be on for a set of medals in the Under 17 Men's group but unfortunately Simon Alexander suffered an asthma attack, putting paid to those hopes. Highest placed first-claim Belgravian of the day was Steve Beaver whose 6th place in the Under 15 Boys race must be ranked as his finest run to date.

Darren 2nd at Norfolk

At Caldecott Hall, Norfolk, Darren Mead ran into controversy when recording the same time as the winner in the Norfolk CC Championship. Turning out for his second-claim club as one is perfectly entitled to in County CC Champs., Darren found himself accosted by a Norfolk official who told him that he would not be allowed to run. After a heated discussion (not the best preparation for a race) it was agreed that the official was wrong and that he could indeed represent Thetford. But the day had more twists in store yet. Tucked in behind the leader as the finish was neared, Darren unleashed a sprint for the line and although our man felt he got his nose in front, as did his rival and various other onlookers, the decision went against him and second place in the same time as the winner was all he could claim. We believe that this is the first Norfolk CC title Darren has ever lost.

Reigate Priory Holly Run

REIGATE PRIORY, 17 DEC 95

Belgrave's senior men finished 4th on the day in the last Surrey league match of the season, their best result in years and at last some real encouragement for the team and reward for the huge effort put in by Alan Mead over the winter.

There was already a confident atmosphere before the race with most of the recognised Belgrave distance runners reporting for their numbers. Then Gary Staines strolled up with Linda. This was Gary's second consecutive League race, only a week after his victory in the South of Thames "Senior," a really unexpected bonus for the team. One could immediately sense the positive effect this had on everyone else.

And now we are really beginning to get an engine room. Not just the old reliables, Graham Adams, Jim Estall, Roger Alsop and Phil Carstairs, but add to that list Bruce Barton, the new revelation Dave Beaver the much improved Alex Luce, and don't forget new second claim member Steve Hewes who will hopefully be scoring for us next season. Yes, there is already an air of excitment about next season.

As the runners climbed the long haul up to the downs for the first time, Gary looked very comfortable sitting in the first group. Then the already mentioned Belgrave vests stretched from the early twenties to the fifties with Junior Galley looking relaxed after his Caribbean holiday (perhaps a bit too relaxed) and Said Chettati, struggling hard against the effects of his fast for Ramadin. By the time they reached the steep hill after two miles Gary was controlling the race from the front with Dave Taylor of Herne Hill, not long out of hospital, struggling to keep in contact. Graham Adams, Jim Estall and Roger Alsop were all packing together in the early twenties with Bruce Barton running a sensible race a few places further back, followed by Phil Carstairs, dropping slightly, Dave Beaver and Alex Luce.

London Marathon Team Champions

LONDON MARATHON 21 APR 96

This race must go down as the classiest Club Championship of all time. Paul Evans was expected to produce his usual high standard run while for Gary Staines, it was his second attempt at going all the way, humid conditions making things difficult in his first attempt in Vienna just on 12 months ago. Then there was Jim Estall - as fit as a flea - and a whole bunch of others who may well spring surprises: Marcello Bizio, Stu' Paton, Junior Galley, Mike Kazimierski ... who would close in our team on what we hoped would be a winning occasion? What no-one bargained for was the unseasonably high temperature with the mercury being pushed to 21°C (70°F) by midday and there is no doubt that it had a great effect on the way the race turned out.

Early on a large bunch including all the main players broke away with Paul Evans' blond head bobbing away in the midst. Gary's start was more conservative and by Tower Bridge he was conspicuous in the second wave of international runners with former winner Eamonn Martin already struggling just ahead.

Looking for a time in the mid 2:20s, Jim Estall had set off at 5½ minute mile pace but he soon realised that this was not going to be the day for such a pace and eased off to 5:45s for fear of blowing up. Mike Kazimierski came past him at Rotherhithe so as this pair crossed the river it was 'Kaz' ahead with Jim tucked in not too far behind. Bouncing along, Stuart Paton looked in great shape, accompanied by Marcello and an incognito Matt Yates out for a training spin. Surely no team could match this. Six men through in about 200 places.

The second half was murderous. As the leading bunch was whittled away around the Isle of Dogs Evans continued to look comfortable but Gary's pack seemed to disintigrate. For Stuart the signs of dehydration became evident. He had suffered similarly on a recent long training run so he knew he was in for a bad time. Junior and Jim were also feeling the heat but Mike Kazimierski's run was looking better and better although all of them were now running a long way down on their "hoped for" times.

Well, we all know now that among the front runners Mexico's Dionicio Ceron was the iron man. When he made his break with just over 3 miles to go it was all over for the others as he powered on to his third consecutive victory. Belgium's Vincent Rousseau looked strong along the Embankment while Paul and the young African Kabiga battled it out for third and fourth. A good number of club members had gathered here to cheer on their team and when Gary Staines hove into sight in 9th place they jubilantly proclaimed their excitement at seeing that our second man through in the Club Championship was also the next Briton. What a second half he had run and tired though he was, a wry smile briefly flickered across his face at his Embankment reception.

Now we wanted a third and fourth man through to complete the team. The gaps were huge - far greater than normal - but at last a Belgrave vest was spied ... and it was Kazimierski, some five minutes down on his projected time but running strongly and in the 50s. The trouble was, he was not eligible for team competition until 1st May. Then, drenched from tipping water over himself, Jim Estall came out of the Blackfriar's tunnel, but where now was our legitimate 4th scorer?

It was some twenty minutes before a tired Junior came through followed by the battered remnants of our team. There had been all sorts of drama around 20 miles. Stuart had completely hit the wall and walked for ages between 17 and 19 miles before Dennis Williams of H/W got him going again and it was at this stage that Junior passed him. Marcello had reached 20miles in 1:57 but then took an hour for the last six miles, his groin injury having got to him again. Our chances in the team race seemed to be blown away.

There were plenty more Bels to cheer on. Lionel Mann had an amazing run over quarter of an hour faster than last year while Paul Cooper was thrilled to bits with his effort.

Then late that night came the news that in spite of everything we had indeed won the team race. All the teams had been equally decimated by the warm conditions but our scoring quartet's times added together came to 9:56:02 and we easily had the measure of any other squad. And just think, if Mike Kazimierski had been eligible we would have been home in 9:31:15 - a team time out of this world - not to mention the fact that just after the race Anthony Robertson (125th in 2:36:34) signed up for the Bels!

One of the most interesting points had been Stuart's debut. As a class 1500m runner, his club-mates, in changing-room discussions, had him down as anything between 2:35 and 2:50, so was he disappointed with his run? Well, he'd like to have gone better and as he said, " ... with a 1500 if you run a crap race one week you can always run another one the next weekend - but there's no way I'm doing another marathon for a bit. Still, it's got to me. It's a whole new sport - and I will be having another go."

Race Walking

Since Race Walking was re-introduced to the Young Athletes in the Winter of 1994/95 it has taken off like an express train. In every age group the Club is now having major successes. From Young International down to County Championships the team has proudly represented the Bels. In 1995 we won no less than twentytwo National, Regional and County Medals, six English School Vests, two South of England Vests, two County AA Vests, and already we have been informed that at least three of the team will be representing the South this year, with another two or three on the borderline for selection.

It is impossible to mention every Young Race Walker in this short piece, although they really do deserve it, so if your name has not been mentioned, please accept that everything written applies to you all.

We have travelled far and wide, and if the British League Team think they travel a long way, try this for a year's travel - London (many times), Dublin, Weymouth, Chesterfield, Birmingham (three times), Coventry, Leamington Spa, Brierly Hill, Bexley (four times), Stoke-on-Trent, plus taking part in the Thameside League.

In the 1994/95 season we finished fifth out of twentyfour teams in the RWA Younger Age Groups' Grand Prix Series, and we only competed in half the matches. This year we are expecting to improve and we are fighting for second place with Sheffield RWC and Steyning AC (two of the country's top Race Walking Clubs). The Under 15 Boys won their 1994/95 series and are expected to be in a similar position again when September comes around. The Girls, although small in number, are also improving on their previous year's position.

Race Walking Excites The Club

Steve Whorlow, Snr.

In February we took a near clean sweep of the Kent County AAA Road Walking Championships with five Golds (out of six), followed by four SEAA Road Walking Championships Golds in March, and with numerous Silver and Bronze medals. Feature · page 24

P.W. "Jimmy" Green: A man of many talents

CLIVE SHIPPEN writes:- In 1954, with the aid of several army physical training instructors, I was able to create a track and field team to represent the School of Military Engineering, Chatham. It was built from raw material and required much honing and refinement if it was to succeed at Army Championship level, so competition was essential. With this in mind I contacted the nearby City of Rochester A.C. to see if they could assist in both competition and training.

It was thro' this approach that I first met P.W. "Jimmy" Green. He was a stalwart of the local club and heavily involved in their management and coaching. His response was most supportive and we soon arranged some matches between our teams. I did not realise at the time that he was Editor of Athletics Weekly, then in its 9th year of publication, nor indeed that he had been a member of Belgrave during the War years. Our discussions soon became most animated when he knew I was a Belgravian too.

The Military School team benefitted from his help and went on to reach the Army finals the following year.

I had not expected the Editor of AW to be so actively involved in club athletics. He seemed to know everyone and it was in no small measure due to him that the City of Rochester '5' was a major event in the racing calendar at that time. No mean athlete himself in earlier days, he had achieved much by 1954; yet he was still only half-way through a life-time in athletics.

We kept in touch during the 'fifties and 'sixties via the London Brighton Relay mainly, a passion we both shared, and I sent him race analyses and the occasional article.

He has now reached the head of Belgrave's membership list, and it seems appropriate that the following profile should feature in The Belgravian.

Born on the 20th March 1909, P.W. "Jimmy" Green spent his formative years in the Tolworth/Surbiton area of Surrey. It was after surprising himself by winning an Open schools 100 yards race that he was approached by Billy Dear of Surrey A.C., a well known figure in cross-country running. Dear invited Jimmy and his friend Bert Pryor to visit his home and see his showcase of medals and trophies. The youngster stared at the case in awe, marvelling at the display and signed up on the spot.

When 14 years old he joined the R.A.F. as an apprentice and went to Halton for three years. He continued his sprinting with moderate success in the first year but then took to cross-country running in his second year. He later returned to the Old Malden Fields to pit his new-found fitness against Surrey A.C. runners and found cross-country specialist George Constable on the line. During the five mile race Constable and the front men went off course shortening the race to 3¾ miles. Jimmy found himself just behind the leaders with the finishing straight (100 metres of cinder path) now in sight. In a flash the young newcomer was up on his toes taking them all by surprise with his sprinting ability. It was shock win and Constable did not take it too well; but Jimmy had left his mark with the Surrey AC members who were mostly amused at the upset.

After finishing his R.A.F. apprenticeship he was posted overseas first to Palestine and then Iraq. In his four-year posting he was unbeaten in Services races until his old friend and International Sam Ferris joined him for a second stint in the desert. They had last been together when representing the R.A.F. against Middlesex and the Civil Service.

Jimmy eventually returned to the U.K. and concentrated on half-miling where he enjoyed much success. But he no longer felt the R.A.F. to be the life he needed and he bought himself out. He went into the retail business, owning two shops, but after only a few months of operation World War 2 was declared and Jimmy found himself straight back in the R.A.F.

Posted to R.A.F. Detling he met Charlie Smart who had been similarly posted. They became firm friends and trained regularly at the United Services Ground, Chatham. Additionally they thrashed their way round the Detling Aerodrome perimeter every lunch hour. Jimmy had never known training like it.

Encouraged by Charlie he joined Belgrave who were one of the few clubs to keep going during the war. He was a member for five years, until Surrey A.C. resumed activities in 1946. Charlie persuaded him to step up his track distance to 2 miles, in view of his strong finish in the mile, and he was soon joining Len Herbert, Doug. Main, Les Cohen, Tom Carter and Tom Morrell in the two-mile team races in which they were invincible at that time. Etienne Gailly, the Belgian who so very nearly won the 1948 Olympic Marathon was also one of the Belgrave group.

When peace arrived in 1945 Charlie Smart went to Germany with the Control Commission where he was unbeaten over 5,000/10,000 metres for two years and was the U.K.'s second ranked 6 miler. Before they parted the two friends had discussed Jimmy's conviction that Athletics deserved a magazine of its own. Coverage at that time was restricted to newspaper columns and various sports magazines. Armour Milne and others had brought out modest gazettes but they were unable to survive for long. If it had not been for his posting to Germany Charlie might well have joined his friend in this ambitious enterprise.

Jimmy decided to go it alone. Despite contrary advice from other friends and associates he approached John Langdon Ltd. of Maida Vale and convinced them that it was a viable proposition. Athletics was published for the first time in December 1945. It was a monthly production and remained so for 5 years before it became Athletics Weekly.

Although the work-load on the magazine steadily increased and it gave became more and more successful Jimmy continued to give much of his precious time to coaching; enjoying nurturing youngsters from school and taking them through to senior status.

Amongst his successes were that formidable duo of the 1960's Brian Barrett and Ken Caulder who finished 1st and 2nd in the National Junior Cross-Country Championship and went on to gain senior international vests.

Many older members will remember the remarkable 'Roy' Beckett whom Jimmy converted from miling to 3 miles. He put him through a tough cross-country regime in the winter and was criticised by other coaches of the day for subjecting a track athlete to such work. Beckett went on to win track races at 1 mile, 2 miles and 3 miles in personal bests. His outstanding race, which brought everyone to their feet at White City was the A.A.A. 3 miles event of 1951. He entered the finishing straight shoulder to shoulder with hot favourite Chris Chataway and in one of the most excruciating finishes seen at these Championships won by a mere inch. Jimmy's coaching had been vindicated.

In 1982 Surrey A.C. amalgamated with Belgrave and Jimmy Green was once more a Belgrave Harrier. His continuous membership of the two clubs has now reached 71 years, which heads our current membership list.

In 1987 Athletics Weekly was sold to EMAP Pursuit and in 1994 he stopped coaching at the age of 85 years; four years after receiving the Master Coach Award. His career has been packed with a whole range of experience. He represented the A.A.A. at 880 yards, 1, 2 and 3 miles; ran for the R.A.F., Kent, Cambridgeshire and Bedfordshire; coached athletes to international standard; edited a national magazine; organised meetings and was a Grade I Starter. His son Tim was a promising middle distance runner, until injured in a road accident, and his grandson Clifton is the A.A.A. U15 Javelin title holder who has been likened to a young Steve Backley.

Athletics is certainly in Jimmy's blood, and there is no doubt that a large proportion of it is coloured claret and gold. On April 20th he celebrated his 87th birthday. Belgrave Harriers wish him many many happy returns.

"Jimmy's" career has been packed with a whole range of experience: an athlete representing the A.A.A., R.A.F., Kent, Cambridgeshire and Bedfordshire at one, two and three miles, a Grade I Starter for some 40 years, a Senior Coach from an early age and now Master Coach, organiser of athletic meetings, President of Kent County A.A.A., President of City of Rochester A.C., Medway A.C., and Surrey A.C., sports shop proprietor, innovator in the design of athletic shoes and clothing, breeder and racer of greyhounds, athletics tour organiser, founder of "Athletics Weekly" and Editor for 22 years until it was taken over in 1987.

Ladejo back on track

In 1994 Du'aine Ladejo had a brilliant summer season, winning the European Games 400m Gold Medal and going on to collect further hardware at the Commonwealth Games. It all started that year with a win in European Indoor Championships in Paris. Everything seemed to go wrong the following season but after a miserable 1995 it looks as if the man's back on track, following his 1994 path to glory by collecting the 1996 European Indoor title at Stockholm and becoming the proud owner of four European Gold Medals.

The Stockholm track did not turn out to be a favourite with the athletes but it gave no worries to Du'aine who coasted to a 47.84 win in heat three with Saber of Italy fastest of the round in 47.29. Surely a 46.74 has never looked so easy as it did with his semi-final and barring accidents the final on March 10th looked a formality. It was. With 22.10 at halfway he was already out on his own and in total command. Stretching away for another relaxed run he won in 46.12 - a personal record for indoors - but looking for all the world as if he could have gone to low 45s. Frenchman Hilaire was 2nd in 46.82 with Saber third, 46.86.

Now for the summer season and the Olympics in Atlanta. A certain American by the name of Michael Johnson would have to be everyone's favourite but let's face it, any American who makes the Games is going to be red hot. Then again, Du'aine has to make the British team first and there are some seven other guys in the UK who are currently showing tremendous form and won't be easy to brush aside. But the Belgravian believes he can go all the way to the top and with such confidence anything could happen.

Leo Coy

Bels. 3rd - top British placing ever outside of the UK

Dave Smith got our team off to a solid start in the Hammer. With Team Managers, supporters and team mates watching he caused a few flutters as he had three no-throws! But showing good experience for his age he put everything right in the 5th round with a mighty 68.80 to finish 5th.

Richard Aspden produced a season's best in the High Jump but could only share 7th place in a very high standard competition. Similarly, for Derek Browne, the Triple was probably the strongest event overall and he had to settle for 8th.

At this stage Larios were out in front with Fiamme Oro in second and we were vying with Racing Club for 3rd place.

Matthew Yates ran an excellent 1500m and was just pipped on the line for 2nd while Darren Mead placed 6th with a good run in the 3000m and the sprint boys came through after a slow start to take 4th in the 4x100m relay. At the end of the day we were placed 4th behind Larios, Fiamme Oro and Racing Club.

The team talk arranged for 11pm had to be held outside as our hotel was on fire but we were safe in the hands of Fireman and Captain Phil Goedluck! Nigel Bevan got us off to a great start with a first round effort of 74.36 and this proved to be the best throw of the competition to make Nigel our second individual winner. RCF were placed 7th and we had moved into 3rd!

Olympic Champion Tarasov was waiting to enter the 'Vault as Mike Edwards went out but at least he shared the points equaly with RCF.

Justin Chaston was next up in the 'Chase and he led Lambruschini until 600m out when the Italian kicked away - a great performance by Justin. Darren Mead had a really hard double by holding on to 6th in the 5000m after the previous day's 3k and just failed to catch the slowing RCF man.

With one event to go we were a clear third ahead of Racing Club who now had a battle on their hands to stay fourth with Belarus and Dukla Prague close behind.

Our finest moment came in the 4x400m relay. Tom Lerwill ran a storming leg on first, coming through late and handing over in third place - 48.0 seconds. Jon Ridgeon was next, holding second with a brilliant 46.8 before handing over to Chris Rawlinson who sat in 'til the last bend and then cruised through into the lead - 47.2. Now it was Dave Nolan leading all the way, giving the opposition no chance of catching him as he lengthened his lead down the straight - 46.9. A superb new Club Record of 3:09.30 which ensured third place overall and the best ever British performance in the European Clubs' Cup on Continental soil.

It was a brilliant team effort, not only by those who competed but also by the reserves - Lewis Samuel, Roger Alsop and Leith Marar - the Team Managers - Bill and John - and the supporters. The noise generated while the long relay was in progress out-did every other team.

Well done to Phil Goedluck and his fine team, a performance all the more creditable since the first two clubs are totally professional outfits sponsored by Larios Gin and the Italian Police Service respectively.

The event ended with a banquet for all the teams at the Princess Hotel. The hosts arranged an excellent evening with beer and wine flowing in plenty, an authentic belly dancer and some traditional folk dancing.

Belgrave received further awards: Du'aine got the best track runner award and our relay team won best team award for their 1st and 4th places. Derek Browne finished runner-up in the Euroclubs Belly Dancing competition with some lovely moves! A great end to an excellent weekend and the Belgrave Office will be decorated with still further pieces of silverware.

But the most moving moment was yet to come. Watched by a coachload of his team mates Haroun said goodbye to his Turkish friend ... "Haroun, I love you!" It was a very touching moment as tears flowed from the love-lorn young lady's eyes - and from the eyes of thirty hysterical passengers!

Three of a Kind

Three runners who have recently joined Belgrave Harriers are likely to strengthen our hand considerably when the cross country and road relay season comes round again.

Suffolk based Nigel Powley, previously a member of Colchester Harriers, is sponsored by Mizuno and likes to compete regularly over anything from 5 to 20 miles. It was on a trip to Ewell for the 26.2 Club's 10k that he caught our eye, running "unattached" and placing 2nd in 31:29. According to AW he was looking for a club and had Belgrave in mind. So with a bit of detective work we tracked him down and found ... that he didn't know where AW had got that piece of information from ... he hadn't really been thinking of the Bels. ... but on the other hand why not ... and signed up within the week! Nigel was delighted to don Belgrave kit in his next road race and has every intention of joining our road relay and cross-country squads. His local press already feature him regularly and made a point of noting that he had teamed up with fellow Suffolk man Paul Evans and that Belgrave are the "currently the Manchester United of the athletics world".

The last five months or so have seen Nigel steadily coming back from an achilles tendon problem. The signs are that he is over it now and raring to go. Just take a look through his cv in the accompanying panel and you'll know why we are so excited to have him on board.

Anthony Robertson became a Belgravian just a few weeks ago. He hails from South Africa but his father was born in Ireland and athletically, that is his nationality. Living in Wimbledon now, he has great running country not so far from his doorstep with Wimbledon Common and Richmond Park and he's looking forward to teaming up with other runners to get himself into good shape and really motivated. A word of warning though, he's a 14:40 5,000m man and around 31½ minutes for 10k on the road so don't take any liberties on those 10 mile fartlek sessions.

Anthony had heard about Belgrave from other South African runners who have been over here and now being a local man, is looking forward to using our gymnasium. Ideally Anthony likes to clock up 100 to 110k each week including 15 or 20x400m sessions and recently tried his first marathon - this Spring's London - where he ran 2:36-odd to finish just a little way down on Jim Estall. What an asset this man's going to be.

Now we'd like to introduce you to another runner from across the Irish Sea - Donal O'Riordan. Watching Donal race in some of our opening meetings this year one cannot help but be struck by the fact that he really does look every inch "a runner". But it's not surprising really as his father is Tom O'Riordan, the first Irishman to run inside 14 minutes for 5k back in the 'sixties, a competitor who proudly bore his emerald green singlet in the Tokyo Olympics of 1964 alongside some great names - Jazy, Keino, Clarke. Tom is now a sports writer for the Irish Independant and will be in Atlanta this year to cover the Games.

Donal only really started training at the age of 22 when he went to Loughborough University but within a couple of seasons he has logged up some classy runs over 1500 metres and the mile. He lives in London at the moment and has spent some time training with the Shaftesbury Barnet boys but it's Belgrave for him, influenced to a certain extent by Derek and Dave Kelleher. Donal hasn't really gone in for road racing so far but has some definite aims for the near future. He wants to run cross country in the County and Southern Championships next winter and has his eyes set on our indoor Club Record for 1500 metres - Stuart Paton's 3:46.65 from 1983.

From Belgrave to Bondi

Dave Scott

DAVE SCOTT, BELGRAVIAN AND FORMER BRITISH LEAGUE 800M MAN, HAS SWAPPED LIFE IN LONDON FOR A BUSY ROLE AS ATHLETICS NSW DEVELOPMENT OFFICER

After spending two years as the Development Officer for Athletics New South Wales, the track and field organisation for the largest state in Australia, (in terms of participation levels) I have come to terms with some major differences in the way the sport is conducted here, compared to the way that I was used to things being done when I was part of the English system up to 1993.

To begin with, the system of administration of the sport is vastly different. Our national body, Athletics Australia, runs a highly successful Grand Prix series over here, which showcases domestic elite talent and some overseas stars in competitions in every state in Australia. Stars from overseas who have competed whilst I have been here include Gwen Torrence, Michael Johnson, Mike Powell and Sergei Bubka, as well as Linford Christie and Colin Jackson, who are regular visitors to Sydney to train in the European off season. Athletics Australia also organise the National Championships, overseas teams and major championships trials. In this respect Athletics Australia is similar to the BAF.

When one gets to the state bodies, of which I am part, the situation differs greatly. The national body officially has no jurisdiction over the state organisations, who can (and sometimes do) go their own way in terms of local competition and development. In addition, these state bodies are funded in part by the state governments, but exist as independent entities, as opposed to the council-run system in England. From the development aspect of things, which is my specific area, this independence is valuable since it gives me the freedom to base a development programme on what I, as the "man on the ground" know needs attention rather than having to implement a programme passed down by politicians. Sponsorship dollars, becoming more readily available due to the Sydney Olympics in 2000, allows me to have a fairly ambitious programme, something which I know the money-starved development officers in England would sorely love to have.

Co-operation between the states and national body is vital if progress in the sport is to occur here in Australia, and disharmony between the various bodies in the past has led to a dramatic slide in track and field participation numbers across Australia. From a low of 9,000 registered members nationally in 1990, the sport has recovered to have 15,000 registered in the 1994-95 season. This looks and indeed is pretty pathetic compared to England, but track and field here is a relatively low profile sport, with the media dominated by rugby league, cricket, surf lifesaving (ironman) and swimming. That is where the big money, and consequently the high profile, goes.

The club system is very different too, for various reasons. To begin with, there is no national club competition like the British League or GRE Cup. The country is just too big for a club to compete in other states - Australia is a continent bigger than Europe, and Perth, the city furthest away from Sydney is one time zone and a 6 hour flight away. Makes a British League Division One bus trip to Edinburgh seem like a piece of cake!

The clubs themselves are different too. Although some large clubs do exist, the nature of Australia, and New South Wales in particular, with a small population of 5 million living in an area 10 times the size of France, means that most clubs are very small with perhaps 100 members maximum. This makes even a state league competition based around clubs impossible, as most clubs couldn't field a full team. Also, use of many venues for the competition is impossible as there are only five all weather tracks in New South Wales, two of which are at the new Olympic site in Sydney.

Instead, a competition is held on a weekly basis during the club season (September - January) in Sydney, based around teams comprising clubs from regions of NSW. This system is entirely unsatisfactory, and, along with the dire lack of facilities, is a major factor in the near terminal decline of grass-roots track and field in NSW and Australia as a whole.

Sounds pretty depressing so far doesn't it? Well, I was fairly disgusted with things as I saw them in 1992, but so far I have only outlined the negative parts. There are some major advantages to the sport out here, which properly used, I believe will make Australia a strong international force, which has not been the case since Herb Elliott, Ron Clarke, Ralph Doubell, Alby Thomas and Betty Cuthbert dominated in the fifties and sixties.

First and foremost is the fact that Sydney is hosting the Olympic Games. Aside from boosting my chances of actually getting a seat in the stand at the Games, the Olympics have provided an impetus for all that has been lacking in Australian track and field; profile, media coverage, sponsorship dollars and public appeal. At last the sport is moving in the right direction and all in the administration at the national level and within NSW are determined to capitalise on the opportunities arising.

The aforementioned Grand Prix Series in Australia is a great boost to the sport too. Started in 1989, it is well supported by Australia's elite athletes, and provides much needed media and public exposure for the sport as well as revenue for the state organisations. Earlier this year the IAAF granted the Melbourne Grand Prix "B" status, putting it on a par with any meet held in England. This should help to boost the international profile of Australian track and field. This year I had a heavy involvement in the Sydney Grand Prix, held in February. There were sponsorship proposals to write, negotiations with potential sponsors, and I had to organise ticket offers to schools and think up incentives to get more people through the gate. One of the great features about my job is that I am gaining experience in aspects of sport other than purely development issues, such as sports marketing and event management, which will come in handy for future career moves.

Probably the greatest innovation in Australian athletics in recent years, and a programme working well in NSW in particular, is in the area of junior elite development. On a state level, we have divided our area into six country regions, excluding the Metropolitan area of Sydney. Each of these regions has a Regional Academy of Sport supporting several sports, including track and field. A squad of 20 talented young athletes aged 12-19 are financially supported to attend training days, receive elite coaching and travel to competitions. Some of these regions have smaller "development" squads of younger athletes. These squads are determined through talent identification, testing and thorough analysis of schools competition results.

Each regional programme costs roughly $6,000 (£3,000) to run for a year. In addition, the athletes have to be registered with a local club and compete at certain events. These programmes have the effect of retaining young talent in the sport at a crucial age when many leave to go to other sports, supporting athletes in remote country areas and raising the profile of the sport on a local level. The programme has been highly successful, with the first athletes to enter the programme now beginning to enter the World Junior team and compete well at senior level.

At the next level above this is the NSW Academy of Sport track and field programme, run by a British Olympic bronze medallist and Belgrave adversary, Keith Connor. This programme handles the elite junior and senior athletes from NSW and funds them to compete around the country in the Grand Prix Series, or in organised trips overseas, such as on a tour to the USA to compete at the Penn State Relay Championships. This is really the transition point for our juniors to senior level, and allows that change to be smooth. Few of our elite juniors, once in the system, are lost. In addition to these administration structures, Athletics Australia have introduced a national junior elite programme, called the "Oz Squad". This programme is designed to identify talented juniors from the Under 14, Under 16 and Under 18 age groups from each state and maintain their interest in the sport through newsletters and track and field camps, forming a talent pool from which to draw athletes into the World Junior and eventually, World Championship, Olympic and Commonwealth representation.

NSW currently has 102 athletes in the OZ Squad, of whom 16 are currently qualified to compete at the Sydney World Juniors in 1996. Every athlete in the Oz Squad has to fulfill tough performance standards, and are all linked to high performance coaches and clubs in every state.

As far as I know, this system is unique to Australia, and is modelled roughly on the Eastern European model of the seventies and eighties. It is a very popular and effective programme, and a key element to Australia's future success, in my belief. I am heavily involved with the planning, organisation and administration of the above programmes and feel that other countries should try the same if they are to succeed in track and field in later years. Our model has only been running for three years and already the benefits are being felt through increased registration numbers, better international performances and higher public recognition of the sport.

One of the great paradoxes of Australia is that because the population is so into sports, we have a reduced success at international level. In Australia a wider variety of sports are played per head than anywhere else in the world. Whilst this is great for public health, it results in the limited talent pool being diluted across many sports, hence the reduced success on a world scale. The above programmes retain talent within our sport, and help to boost overall participation in the sport through providing a media profile. In this way track and field is beating off the challenge from other sporting bodies, and has ensured its future. Britain and Europe are just beginning to feel the effects on track and field of "new" sports such as baseball, basketball and American football as they encroach upon the traditional talent resources. It is probable that, unless similar measures are taken to address the problem, then Britain too will begin to lose numbers and success. From what I have seen, the BAF and Belgrave in particular are taking steps to stop the slide.

In addition to my involvement with all of the above programmes, I am also a member of the Organising Committee for the 1996 World Junior Championships in Sydney and the Organising Committee for the 1996 Pacific Schools Games to be held in Perth. I am also the meet organsiser for the NSW All Schools Championships, an event with over 3,000 competitors held over three days, and help organise the NSW State Open Championships (the equivalent of a county championships).

All up, a pretty busy schedule. We have a small full time staff in our office of an Executive Director, a Media Liaison Officer, an Accounts Manager, an Admin. Assistant and myself, so its all hands to the pumps much of the time. Still, I guess this set-up would still make some county organisations jealous!

I've only really touched on the highlights of what I do on an annual basis. There is the usual round of school coaching visits and athlete camps on top of this. One school I coached at this year was in Broken Hill, a mining town 11 hours drive out of Sydney, so that gives you some idea of the area I have to cover! On a personal note, I feel that Australia's potential is yet to be fulfilled, and I am certain that you will see more of our athletes kicking European butt around the tracks in years to come; and not just in the junior ranks, which we are already doing!

News of old Belgravians in and around Sydney: a small community seems to have grown up here. About five minutes drive from me Wayne Larden has set up home with his wife and baby. Wayne is a regular visitor to Belgrave Hall each winter as he passes through to compete at the World Cross Country Champs. Andy Lloyd, an ex-Belgravian and ex-Commonwealth Games 10,000m Champion lives on the other side of town, providing the team boat when the boys want to go out fishing on Sydney harbour! Ron Wyld too has found his way over here and lives a few kilometres north of me. I bumped into Ron a while back in the NSW Academy of Sport weights room, puffing and blowing on a rowing machine whilst Steve Smith and Brendan Reilly stood there laughing at him. (The boys were over here training at the time of writing.) Brandon Reid, former British League 1500m rep. and Surrey County bronze medallist also pays visits to Sydney, mainly to be out-drunk and out-run by Wayne and Co.!
