EDITORIAL

The festive season is now upon us and we would like to wish all Belgravians a Happy Christmas and health and happiness in the coming year, It is apparent that as a club Belgrave will in 1964 enjoy one of her most successful ever seasons. Results during the past year have continued to improve and Dave Martin's year of office has seen a resurgence of the real Belgrave spirit. Gerry North has given many of the boys the right kind of leadership and now in his brother Geoff, Barry Sawyer, Tony Fairclough, John Thresher, Dave Cocks and others we have athletes of international potential. With Bill Lucas in the chair we trust that the wheel will turn full circle for our runners and we will, with the combined help of our flourishing walking section, prove ourselves to be THE Club.

Our congratulations are extended to Sid Roberts on his being elected president of the R.W.A for the ensuing year. The club is honoured indeed that the years of work put in by this staunch Belgravian should receive the recognition they so richly deserve.

THE PRESIDENT -- W.E.LUCAS

A.A.H.

In the early days of 1936, news came from our spies of a young long-striding runner of exceptional promise employed in the Insurance world; and past-President Ernie Duffett (our Running Hon. Sec. for 25 years) made it his business to seek out this young man in his City office and persuade him to become a Belgrave Harrier. This was done to good effect and Bill Lucas became a member in February 1936 - something on which we may still continue to congratulate ourselves all these years later when, by virtue of his athletic and administrative ability and personal integrity and competence, he has been elected our President by unanimous acclaim.

Within a few weeks of joining, Bill ran for us at Sandown Park in the Southern Junior C.C. Championship (incidentally, won by Ted Stimpson) and finished 32nd, which caused our scribe with the Belgravian of the time to write 'Lucas, a newcomer, ran well in his first important race in our colours, and clearly has great potentialities'.

The following December, in the Club 7 miles Championship, Bill Lucas finished 13th out of 34 finishers and took second prize in the sealed handicap. Incidentally, Arthur Penny won the Championship, followed home by Les Cohen. About this time, Bill finished 3rd in the Club Junior 5 miles Championship and scored in a winning Surrey Junior Cross-country team (Wood 1, Herbert 4, Lucas 5, Shepherd 6).

In the spring of 1937, Bill was brought in as a reserve for an Olympic runner Jim Ginty in the London-Brighton Relay and ran a splendid sixth stage to maintain the lead we then held; and which was only lost in the last few yards at Brighton. 1937 also saw Bill take second place to Les Cohen in the Club One Mile Championship, and it was very obvious that our young runner was destined for the highest class of competition. So he continued to improve particularly on track and road. His long striding action was not too well suited to the country and his sympathies were not in that direction, but nevertheless they were very fine performances that were recorded in the years that followed.

Then came the war which was to change the course of so many lives. Sgt.-Pilot W . E. Lucas became the official title of our young man, who later transferred from fighters to bombers and still later became Pilot-Officer and eventually Squadron Leader, a member of the elite Pathfinder Group and a holder of the D.F.C.

Bill was back with the Club in 1946 and quickly found his running. He represented Surrey, won a South of Thames Junior race and figured in a large number of successful teams. In 1948 he finished third in the A.A.A. 3 miles Championship and earned selection for the Olympic 5,000 metres race. In the following year Bill came on to our Committee and we have had the benefit of his services ever since. His running continued with great personal success and exemplory team support until 1955. During those years Bill won Surrey County titles at 1 3 and 6 miles; represented Gt. Britain v. U.S.A. and Benelux Countries, figured in a winning Southern Cross-country Championship team as well as numerous Surrey County Championship teams and victorious road relay teams. Truly a great athlete his career was such that it is not possible ot itemise here in detail.

On this splendid foundation, Bill has continued to build for the Belgrave Harriers and athletics. He has served us well for years as Cross-country Hon. Sec. and as an Hon. Handicapper. He has been of the greatest assistance on our Finance Sub-Committee and at our Social gatherings - he has served Surrey County as Team Manager and his voice has become known to thousands - possibly millions - as the clearest and most competent announcer at White City Meetings.

His great work for the Club will continue as our President; and that Pathfinder instinct is already in operation again leading our teams back to the winning way they knew so well in the past. In his task we know Bill will receive the help and support of Mrs. Lucas and their daughters, as has been amply evident in past years; and we are certain that the Club Year 1963/4 will prove a vintage year indeed.

In this we thank our President and his family in advance at the same time assuring him of our maximum support and loyalty.

The President's Message

Bill Lucas

When new recruits join the Army they are told there is a Field Marshals baton in every private's knapsack. The same applies in Belgrave---every new member has the chance to become the President of the best club in the country, although I must admit that when I first became a member in 1935 this thought was furthest from my mind. It is the greatest honour that Belgrave can give and I am deeply appreciative that I should be considered to hold the office for this year and I would take this opportunity of thanking you all for supporting my nomination.

It is always the hope of all new Presidents(and I differ little from my predecessors) that their year of office should be the most successful in the Club's history.

Fortunately I can see this as a distinct possibility rather than just a pipe dream. We have at the moment the strongest teams on the road and over the country that we have been able to field for many years, the Walkers are still very powerful and our track teams are capable of the highest honours. There is a chance that we can make a clean sweep of the cross country honours in 1964 with wins in County,Area and National Championships at both junior and senior levels; to be followed by a win in the National Road Relay. The walkers can retain the championships they at present hold and are capable of pulling out that little extra effort required to recapture the R.W.A. 20 miles and 50 Kilos events and perhaps even the London to Brighton. None of these honours can be achieved without the backing of every club member either as competitor or supporter. Every time a first team member drops out it throws more work on to the rest of the team and reduces the chances of a win. Success can only be gained if everyone is prepared to pull their weight to the utmost. When you are selected to run or walk there should be no excuse for not turning out---dont forget that team-wins by the club reflect just as much on the individuals who make up those teams, as on the club itself. Those on the fringe of the first teams must always be prepared to step into replace the injured or sick---your turn will come and you must be ready. Those not likely to be selected can be just as helpful by turning up as supporters---vocal help can do much to turn a narrow defeat into complete victory. Much enjoyment can be obtained by travelling with championship teams and becoming an ardent club fan.

The Belgrave spirit is as high now as it has ever been---a modicum of success recently is spurring everyone to greater efforts---keep it up by doing all you can to help your athletes and officials and by supporting all fixtures whether they be at home or away and by joining in with all the activities arranged on your behalf. Make 1963/64 a year to be remembered and the forerunner of many years when the Bels are invincible.

Bill Lucas

A.G.M. 1963

C.S.

Seventy-six members were present at this year's Annual General Meeting, held at Belgrave Hall on 18th September.

An encouraging number of younger members were present, besides the usual 'regulars' who enjoy exercising their right to play an active part in the proceedings. Few brick-bats were thrown but many forceful points of view were forthcoming - it will be a sad day when no dissident voices are heard from the floor of the Hall.

The Hon. Secretary's report had been circulated to all members and was taken as read. The Hon. Treasurer's report was made on Dave Shepherd's behalf by Ken Miller, one of the auditors. His lucid explanation of the balance sheet must surely brand him as the A.J.P. Taylor of Accountancy!

That hardy perennial, 'subscriptions', bloomed once more, but this time on two accounts:- a) the high cost and b) that 200 subscriptions were still due for last year. The first point had, of course, been thrashed out at the A.G.M. which sanctioned the last increase, and on which occasion it was pointed out that one visit a week to an L.C.C. track could cost 26/-d. a year (a subsidised figure as it is). Ted Stimpson suggested that the two points might well be inter-related and that the last increase had made payment difficult for some. When the Treasurer replied that the figure of 200 was little different to last year, various calls were made from the floor suggesting action on what was generally agreed to be a serious matter. Howard Satchell asked if it were not possible to give a six months deadline and strike from the register anyone whose arrears were in excess of this. Our Hon. Sec. replied that this was, in fact, the essence of rule 10 (e), but this was a problem common to most clubs and should not be treated with too heavy a hand. Not all of those in arrears were guilty of willful neglect and it would be preferable to improve still further on means of collection before harsher methods were implemented. Those members concerned would do well to honour their obligations, for this subject is certain to be raised again at the next A.G.M.

From an unpleasant topic the meeting switched to one that gave pleasure to all when Ted Pallant, on behalf of the Committee, proposed that C. W. "Charlie" Walker be elected to Life Membership. Charlie had been a Belgravian for nearly 15 years and throughout the London area, at least, his name was synonomous with Belgrave. Ted then listed the salient points of Walker's athletic career, which included the remarkable feat of 27 consecutive outings in the London/Brighton Relays. In seconding the proposal Clive Shippen said that Charlie, in his 15 years as a competitor, had done for Belgrave as much as any administrator could do. The proposal was carried unanimously. For once Charlie was lost for words but, in his own endearing way, he managed to thank the meeting for the honour they had bestowed upon him.

Following the presentation of a Senior Honours Badge to Barry Sawyer for his excellent run in the Poly Marathon, the election of officers took place. The retiring president, D. M. Shepherd, thanked the Club for its support during the preceding 12 months and, as is customary, introduced the man who was considered to have "emerged" as the new President - W. E. Lucas. After thanking the Club for the great honour, Bill Lucas expressed the hope that his year of office would be the most successful since 1951. He then took the chair and proceeded with the elections.

On getting his usual 'no change' Alf Harley said it was uustomary these days to consider persons in authority to be there to be shot at - for good or bad; this was not the case here! He and Frank Jarvis both reiterated how they would like to see younger men preparing to take the reins. All other appointments were made as listed in the Winter Handbook.

When it was agreed that the Vice-Presidents should be elected 'en-bloc', Ted Stimpson proposed that the name of Bert Mitchell be added. This long-distance runner of earlier days was responsible for much encouragement in this section of the sport and ultimately donated the"A.F. Mitchell" Shield for the Club Marathon Championship. After Ken O'Kell had said a few words on Bert's behalf, our Hon. Sec. seconded the proposal with great pleasure and the motion was carried unanimously.

With little other business to discuss the meeting drew to a close at about 8.30p.m. and those who were able, joined the President in the nearest Saloon Bar to toast his appointment and the Club's success; those who were still able, an hour later, went home.

DABBS WINS '5¾'

Wimbledon---Sept.14th

With the absence of our best three runners of the moment---Gerry North, Kerr and Thresher---it was very encouraging to see such excellent running from the first four finishers. Each one provided a pointer to great things in the weeks ahead. Dabbs (28:28) proved beyond all doubt his return to top class running. O'Hara (28:35) erased all question-marks against his stamina quality, and actually closed up on Dabbs in the final stages. Sawyer (28:45) showed that a 10miles run in the morning did little to affect his amazing strength and improving speed and, what's more, that his enthusiasm was waxing fine. Whilst Newell (28:48) turned in a time that a few years back was giving him a winning margin---this was a Newell who appeared to enjoy his running more now that the burden of "carrying" the team was no longer with him.

A fine turn out of 51 members paraded in Lauriston Road for the start and, of these, 46 finished. Afer one lap of the 'top' had been completed (1¼ miles) Geoff. North held a 2yd advantage over Moore, Sawyer, Linstead, O'Hara, Walker and Dabbs. On the second circuit Ron Linstead had a bad patch and dropped considerably before recovering. The order going out to Copse Hill was then Walker, Dabbs, Sawyer, Newell, O'Hara, North, Angus with Moore now 15 yds in arrears.

Half way up the Cambridge Road drag, Geoff North retired---still feeling the effects of a hard race the previous evening at the White City. Dabbs used this as a signal to pile on the pressure and fairly shot up the hills, stringing out the leading five to a 200 yds stretch by the time the Common was reached again. The order was now Dabbs, O'Hara, Sawyer, and Newell at intervals of 30-40yds with Walker a further 60yds back and Moore, still a solitary figure, an equal distance behind.

Dabbs increased his lead up to the Monument, but O'Hara cut this back with a fast finish; the next four held their places. A fine win for Dabbs.

Other notable performances were by Brian Nott(9th) in his first run as a second-claim member, John Bicourt (11th) still a youth and Mike Coningham(15th) our South African member who was having his first outing at Belgrave.

Some fine handicapping by Bill Lucas gave 1st prize to Reg Hopkins by a mere 2 seconds from Don Newell, and no less than 3 runners tied for 3rd place---O'Hara, Moore and Edwards. On a toss of the coin 3rd prize went to young "Noddy".

DEAR WINS Yacht '3'

WIMBLEDON ---SEPTEMBER 28TH

Twenty-years old John Dear, who has been running for little more than twelve months, illustrated his rapid improvement by winning this year's Yacht '3' from a mark of 2min-10secs.

Dear started in 30th position and went through the field like a dose of salts to win by 10 clear seconds. His actual time of 16:05 was 12th fastest of the day's 59 runners---Bruce's was 17th; an excellent effort by the younger man.

Alan Black and Ron Linstead were back markers but could only fight their way through to 31st and 42nd respectively; despite Ron doing a fine 14:47 for the day's fastest time.

Six of our best runners missed this event to visit Kingston and win the Surrey AC relay.

Great Win in Walton Relay

Portsmouth beaten

The frustration experienced by our successful Poly relay team the previous Saturday, when their main rivals were found to have switched their allegiance from Poly to Blackheath, had been biting deeply during the week. It was, therefore, most satisfying to see the first, second and fourth teams of the Blackheath Relay appearing at Walton to do battle with the winners of the Polytechnic event.

Portsmouth got a grand start, with Shergold winning the first leg 15 secs up on Dabbs, who was 5th. Barry Sawyer continued his good work of the previous weeks and closed to within 5 secs of Portsmouth's Salvat in taking us up to 2nd. Unfortunately, on Stage 4, Dave Cooke of Pompey got ample revenge for his team by taking 39 seconds off Charlie Walker and giving his twin, John Cooke, a useful lead. Gerry North, our captain, then turned in fastest time of the day to bring us to within 3 seconds of Portsmouth at the final change over. It was here that Hyman threw away his team's advantage by misjudging the time of the take over and being caught with his track-suit on.(It was raining at the time). OLYMPIAN

by OLYMPIAN

A great day for Belgrave with a win on the Brighton road for the first time since 1952. "Gerry and the Pacemakers" fulfilled the prediction of the leading Press writers in attaining the major honours in no uncertain manner and so put an end to the run of successes by Portsmouth AC in this race.

It was a bright and warm morning when the runners lined up in Old Palace Yard awaiting the first stroke of ten o'clock which signals the start of the race. Barry Sawyer was our first runner and we had every hope of a good start for Barry had been showing great form during the preceding weeks. Our confidence was well proved for in a very relaxed and easy manner he trailed the leaders over the first three miles and then left them to race ahead up Cedars Road and on to the finish at Clapham Common to give us a lead of 8 seconds with Surrey AC, Portsmouth and Polytechnic in second 3rd and 4th places. Never have I seen Barry run so well.

John Thresher, running on the second leg for the third time, was anxious to beat the bogey which had beset him on previous occasions but again the luck was against him and he was forced to relinquish his lead. A cold earlier in the week had obviously sapped John's energy and he was a very tired young man when he reached Mitcham in seventh place.

Geoff North running for the first time in Belgrave colours on the Brighton road faced a tough task against strong opposition but with a good run he caught Surrey AC and Ealing; although having to give way to "Kipper" Herring of Blackheath. Nevertheless he gained one place and finished at Purley close up to all but the leaders who were now Polytechnic--Hogan having put up a great performance for them with fastest time of the day.

Charlie Dabbs soon showed he was back to his best form and with a well judged effort on Stage 4 he passed Blackheath, Walton, Ponders End and Polytechnic and took us up to second position with a time surpassed only by Lindsell of Portsmouth. At the Jolliffe Arms Merstham we were only twenty seven seconds behind Portsmouth.

Ron Linstead was the second fastest runner on the Redhill stage and although not gaining on Portsmouth he took us well away from the clubs behind and consolidated our second place.

Tony Fairclough, the only junior in our team, was not a bit overawed by the reputation of Dick Jones the Portsmouth miler and set about closing the 31 second gap in no uncertain fashion. Over the first mile he pulled back more than 100 yards and then slowly gained on the Pompey runner to give over to Laurie O'Hara only ten seconds behind. Tony recorded the fastest time of the day and equalled the club record for this stage put up two years ago by Malcolm Robinson--a great run.

Laurie had been showing in training that he was running better than ever before and we soon found that he was in devastating form. Soon after turning on to the Gatwick Airport road he caught the Portsmouth runner and giving him no chance to respond he raced away to reach the Sussex border with fastest stage time under his belt and a lead of forty six seconds. From this point we were to maintain the lead until the finish.

On a number of occasions during the past few years we have seen our runners ahead of Martin Hyman at the beginning of the 8th stage only to be caught and passed over the long leg to Handcross. This time however Gerry North was well in command and completely shattered Hyman and the rest of the runners by being the only runner to beat thirty minutes and by taking us into a lead of almost two minutes. Gerry in this mood is without doubt the best road relay runner in the country and although his time was much slower than his best on this leg, due to strong head winds, his performance was a joy to behold.

Feature · page 16

SPOTLIGHT ON PATRICK NEWELL

D.E.M.

For the past decade, Pat Newell has been one of the mainstays of Belgrave road-relay and cross-country teams. Despite Pat's success and reliability as a team member, however, individual honours have not frequently come his way. He has always been something of an enigma and his career has been, to say the least, controversial. Yet during our recent lean years, the name of Pat Newell has figured consistently and he has, along with other stalwarts like Walker, Linstead, Dabbs, Davies and Maclean, Helped keep the Belgrave flag flying, albeit a little limply.

Like Bob Taylor, Pat was given his first real taste of athletics whilst doing his National Service about 1950. One of his camp-mates at Bicester, Oxon, Doug Briggs, suggested that he should join the Bels. but Pat was rather apprehensive and felt certain that he would be utterly outclassed. Doug was not to be deterred and struck a gentleman's agreement with Pat that if he won the Novices mile that year, he would join. Pat cantered home first in 4:53.0 and kept his side of the bargain.

Pat was an extremely ambitious and enthusiastic young athlete. Unfortunately his enthusiasm for training and hard work often seemed to leave him a little 'drained' and he seldom seemed to realise his undoubted potential when the 'chips' were down. Like many middle distance runners of the fifties, Pat attempted to mould himself on the lines of Emil Zatopek and he set himself some extremely vigorous training schedules and there is little doubt that on his day he was a man very much to be feared. One of his earliest, and perhaps noteworthy, successes was his second place in the South of Thames Junior in 1952. In the early days his favourite events were 5 miles C.C. and 3 miles on the track - as he admits he didn't know any better at that time. Pat would be the first to admit that he is not a 'natural'. In fact his running style is completely contrary to the normal long-striding conception of the athlete. His style is neat and precise, his stride is short, staccato, almost mincing and he has very little knee-lift and drive-off. It is actually a style that is better suited to country and road-running than track and his performances tend to mirror this fact. Pat tends to associate himself with the Kuts and Zatopeks of this world rather than the Elliotts and the Snells. The Europeans are examples of athletes who through hard work have succeeded despite their lack of natural athletic grace and ability whereas the colonials supremely illustrate the natural athlete who has only had to develop this inherent ability.

Pat's first run for Belgrave in a major championship was on the Redhill stage of the National Brighton Relay in 1952 when he clocked the fastest time of the day and in the process brought Belgrave from 10th position to 4th. From then on his appearances in first team colours were to be frequent. His improvement was certainly not rapid but his steady rate of progress was maintained. In 1957 he had his only open Championship win when he annexed the L.C.C. 3 mile championship. In 1958, Middlesex 3, he ran a courageous 2nd to the powerful Frank Seal and the following year in the same event he was beaten into 3rd place by Nat Fisher and Peter Miller. Some consolation, however, was gleaned from the fact that he recorded his fastest time ever of 14:00 for the twelve laps. Nowadays, only some half a decade later, this time is quite mediocre and 13:40 is becoming a good time for a better class club-runner. To Pat's credit we must state that although he never broke through the 14 minute barrier, he ran so consistently that his career average over 3 miles is 14:15. Two things have probably prevented him from ducking under 14 mins and establishing himself as a 13:30 top class performer. He lacks real basic speed and he tends to run with insufficient fire in his belly. Pat has a best half-mile time of 1:59.5 and his fastest mile is only a disappointing 4:17.0. Certainly his physique and running style are not conducive to speed and his racing has always seemed to lack that vital dynamic kick that all great champions have had. Realising this deficiency Pat has attempted to compensate for them by improving and deepening his own physical condition. Many say that on occasions he has gone too far and left much of his form on the training track. For an athlete, he is possibly a little too much of a thinker: " ....the native hue of resolution, Is sickled o'er the pale cast of thought," . Undoubtedly he has many good training sessions under his belt and all the essential fitness is there in depth. Married now to Wendy, sister of our own Mike Shingles, he may settle down mentally as well as physically. He has started back on the long grind and, after overcoming and surmounting a few intermediary Everests, he feels convinced that he can still contribute more to his already long service record. Although we look to be entering a period of success and although these may well be halcyon days for Belgrave, complacency must be avoided and the Newells have a great contribution to make. Individually Pat still has many good races in him and as a member of Belgrave packs and teams, his experience and judgment can be of great benefit to the sparkling, yet raw and inexperienced talent that we trust will provide our National Championship teams of the late '60s.

'OPEN' YOUTHS RELAY -- Oct. 19th.

FINE WIN BY MITCHAM

Belgrave's seventh annual road relay for Youths was held on Oct. 19 at Wimbledon and attracted an entry of 46 teams. Coventry, the holders, who set a course record last year, were not represented, but it is doubtful whether a club in the country could have touched the Mitcham quartet on this occasion. Messrs. Perry, Strudwick, Badgery and Cope not only led from start to finish but they smashed Coventry's course record by no less than 48 seconds.

Bill Lucas set the race in motion on this windy autumnal Saturday and Mitcham, Ilford, Watford, Q.P.H. and Essex Beagles were soon fighting it out at the front. Derek Hurd was on this stage for Belgrave, but has not been running long enough yet to match this class of opposition. He is young enough to be eligible for next year's race so, with 12 months of conscientious training, could well be to the fore next time. On this occasion he finished 31st, 1:20 down on Perry of Mitcham.

From then on Mitcham increased their lead with every step to finish 1½ minutes ahead of the runners-up, Luton United.

Ron Hayes set about the opposition like a wild man on Stage two, carrying his bobbing head of unruly hair past no less than 12 opponents to send John Bicourt off in 19th place. John had the doubtful privilege of starting 2 seconds ahead of Kicks (V.P.H.) who took his team from 20th to 7th with fastest time of the day. Thanks, once again, to Ray Conway the organisation went off extremely well. The able assistance of Arthur Whitehead, of Denis Brickwood and his results team, and of the many officials, was invaluable. The line-up of officials would have done credit to an international meeting.

THE CLUB DINNER

Held on November 16th at the Ardington Rooms, Clapham Junction, this year's occasion was attended by 219 members and friends of Belgrave. Thanks to Ted Pallant and his social committee the evening was a great success.

Our principal guests this year were Mr. L. Golding, A.A.A. team manager and representing the Southern Counties C.C.A., Mr. R. Goodman of the Southern Counties A.A.A., Mr. J. Martell of Surrey County A.A.A., Mr. L. Woodcock of the R.W.A. and Mr. W. Coyne of the London Association of Athletic Clubs. Other guests included Mrs. Savage, and the evergreen Harry Evans. It should be noted of course that our own Sid Roberts, who was also present is the current President of the R.W.A..

Mr. Ron Goodman, who hails from H.H.H., in proposing the toast "The Club" jocularly questioned the use of the definite pronoun, but admitted later that he had actually come round to admiring past runners like Bert Footer and Arthur Penny and,of the present team, was even beginning to like Charlie Walker! Full of praise for our success over Portsmouth on the Brighton Road, he had great pleasure in changing the toast to "Winners of the London Brighton Relay" - a diplomatic move which left his allegiance to Herne Hill untarnished! Jack Goswell, replying for the club, gave an excellent review of recent successes, an assessment of present strength and finished on an optimistic note for the future.

John Thresher's maiden speech was made in a toast to 'Our Guests' He handled this with the same technique and judgment that one associates with his running, and he can feel sure that this was the first of many such appearances. It was good to see that the improving strength of our athletes is reflected in the enthusiasm of our club committee and that our hard-worked Hon. Sec. Alf Harley was able to have an evening off.

Les Golding, and John Martell replied on behalf of the Guests. The latter, who is Editor of the Surrey County Newsletter 'SCAN' hoped that our new President, in his capacity as team manager, would celebrate his year of office by taking the County Cross Country team to victory in the Inter-Counties Championship which will be held on home ground at Epsom in January.

Our immediate Past-President, Dave Shepherd, then proposed a toast to the President. The essence of his 'This is Your Life' speech was similar to that given elsewhere in this gazette. Dave, of course, applied his usual humorous trimmings and did not fail to mention that Bill Lucas was the only man in the President's household; following this with some 'inside' information on Bill's domestic aptitudes!

The President's reply, after a few appropriate comments in Dave Shepherd's direction, was an optimistic appraisal of his hopes and forecasts for the coming year. "There is no reason," said Bill, "why we should not add the National Relay title to our Southern Brighton success. I also hope to see us win the Southern and even the National Cross Country Championships - Junior titles as well. Yes, Coventry, Portsmouth and Derby - Here we come! ". He also considered our strong walking section, "who had kept Belgrave's name to the fore during lean years" well enough equipped to regain the R.W.A. 20 and 50 kilos titles and looked forward to several trophy wins by our track section. He was convinced that 1964 would be a memorable year for both Club and Country and hoped that Belgrave would, once again, supply at least one athlete to the Olympic Games team for Tokyo. Bill Lucas closed his speech with the presentation of a plaque to the retiring President, Dave Shepherd.

Clive Shippen (toastmaster) and Charlie Manning (M.C.) were both 'performing' for the first time and it was not long after speeches that the latter had the dancing in full swing. Showing a remarkable zest for his task, Charlie really hotted up the festivities with his non-stop novelties and threw in everything bar circuit training! It was good to see a larger element of younger members this year and, if 'Noddy' Edwards was anyone to go by, they had a really high time.

So another Dinner came to a close, This annual festive occasion, which brings together those Belgravians who have worn Claret & Gold a full life span apart, and where members from all walks of life join in an evening of true Belgrave flavour, had ended. Yet the spirit of the President's message, of the re-unions and of the festivities was still with the 219 when they arrived home in the early hours of Sunday morning. All except, perhaps, 'Noddy' Edwards!

LONDON - BRIGHTON WALK

14 SEC SEPARATES RAY HALL FROM BRIGHTON VICTORY.

At 7 AM on Saturday 7th September 1963, eight Belgravians lined up with the other starters opposite Big Ben at Westminster for the London to Brighton walk. As Don Thompson (Met WC) the winner for the previous 8 years and the Olympic 50 Kilos Champion 1960, was unable to compete this year owing to injury it was the first time for many years that the probable winner could not be selected at the start. But the Bels were very hopeful that our own Ray Hall,who was in very good form,could pull it off.

As the clock struck seven a group of six walkers including Ray and Ken Bailey (Bels) followed Colin Young (EB) who was second in 1961 and 1962 across Westminster Bridge, for what was to be the most keenly fought Brighton for many years. At 10 miles this group were still together, and the Bels were very nicely placed for team points, but luck was not with us because Don Bartliff had to retire at this point with a hip injury when lying in 10th position. Although John Morris, Bernard Eglington, John Keown and Bob Farley fought hard to make up team places,this was to affect the team result considerably in the end.

When the leading group reached 15 miles at Coulsdon, Ken Bailey went into the lead and opened up a gap of about 100 yards in a brave attempt to hot up the pace and kill off some of the opposition. But Dennis Read (Steyning AC),the winner of the Hastings to Brighton three weeks earlier, soon went after him with Ray Hall close on his heels, and at Redhill( 20 miles reached in 3hrs 2mins 25secs)these two were ahead of the field followed by Dennis Vale (S.W.C.). Read began to put on the pressure at Gatwick and he had Ray struggling to hold on at times. But like the fighter he is, he was still on Read's shoulder at Crawley: 30 miles reached in 4 hrs 39 mins 42 secs. At Peas Pottage it was still neck and neck with Vale (S.W.C.) still 3rd with Ken Bailey giving a good account of himself in 5th place, and the rest of the Bels including George Beecham who had been ill with asthma backing up well for team positions. As the leaders went down Handcross,Read showed his great ability to walk down steep hills and as this is not one of Ray's assets he opened up a gap which had extended to 1 min 6 secs at Bolney 39 miles reached in 6 hrs 1 min 30secs with Ray still 2nd and Vale (SWC) closing in 3rd place. At Hickstead 41 miles Read was 2 mins 15 secs ahead but now the course began to go up hill and this slowed him down somewhat. With great encouragement from Harry Shields on his cycle and from his attendant car Ray began to close the gap and at Dale Hill(46 miles reached in 7 hrs 8mins 57 secs)he was only 51 secs behind.

Now Read,a Sussex man,was in his own territory and heading for home, but still Ray chased him clipping off the seconds until at Preston Park with about two miles to go he was only 19secs behind. Can he do it? That was the question we asked ourselves as we waited on the front at Brighton. But in the end Ray had to give second best to Dennis Read who won in 8 hrs 6 mins 58secs with Ray just 14 secs behind in 8 hrs 7 mins 12 secs a truly magnificent effort and a race that will be remembered by those present for many years. Dennis Vale (SWC) was 3rd in 8 hrs 16 mins 45 secs, Ken Bailey walked a fine race to finish 5th in 8 hrs 37 mins 7 secs. John Morris was 18th in 9 hrs 21 mins 32 secs followed closely by Bernard Eglington 20th in 9 hrs 23 mins 56 secs. This being our scoring four we now had to wait for Surrey W.C.'s fourth man to see if we had won the team race. But he just managed to scrape in and we lost by one point 28-29.

VAUGHAN THOMAS

Vaughan Thomas

Since joining Belgrave, international walker Vaughan Thomas has been, to say the least, a controversial character. This, however, is nothing strange where dedicated men are concerned. Singleness of purpose and supreme self-confidence often lead to misunderstanding and the dedicated are frequently lacking in tact. The result of all this is that team relationships can become strained and explosive situations sometimes arise.

Vaughan Thomas feels that he has been misunderstood in some quarters and in an effort to state his case, has written the following letter to us. SELLING BELGRAVE HARRIERS

Frank Jarvis

In the murkiness of a November evening, a small party of Belgravians met outside a building; grim and forbidding in its outline on a sunny day, but seen through the dismal fog that was descending over South-West London that evening, this edifice seemed to have ' You are not welcome' in its bricks and mortar. It was easy to conjure up memories of school moralists teaching ' right from wrong'. One member of the party examined his inner-self for any dishonesty, but comforted himself with the fact that he had been invited to attend.

A few weeks ago a letter was received from the Secretary of the Sportsmen Club, Wandsworth Prison, with an invitation to the Committee to send a panel of officials to the Prison to discuss Athletics and the Belgrave Harriers. In response to this request it was agreed that such a panel under the direction of the President, Bill Lucas would attend on 27th November. The group made up of Bill Lucas, Jack Bidgood, Frank Jarvis, Ron. Langheim, Brian Nott, Charlie Manning and Chris. Steer and Arthur Penny duly met outside the gates. The Assistant Governor was there to greet the party and after introductions, gave a general picture of the proceedings; that the Sportsmen Club would organise the meeting and having listened would, undoubtedly, ask endless questions, ( apparently, the gentlemen of this prospective audience are avid readers of newspapers, in particular, news relating to athletics).

This preamble being completed, the group stepped forward into the prison and the gates were locked behind them.

It may be of interest to tell of the following - Wandsworth Prison has a residential population of about 1400, none of whom is a first offender. The Sportsmen Club is made up of 25 inmates, it is completely self-contained, no prison official takes part in any of its organisation, the only check made is when the outgoing letter of invitation is censored and countersigned for the benefit of the recipient. The Club is controlled by its Committee of three - Chairman, Treasurer and Secretary. Any ungentlemenly conduct or any behaviour which could bring the Club into disrepute is a serious matter and would cause the member concerned to be expelled from the enjoyments of the club.

There is a number of more than 300 inmates that wish to become members of the Sportsmen Club, so, as the Secretary said, the standard of membership is very high and no misbehaviour occurs.

On the occasions when speakers are invited to attend, each member of the Club is entitled to bring a guest ( another inmate), if this guest causes trouble, his host suffers the penalty.

The number of members is restricted to 25 because of the difficulties that would be met in moving more that number from one part of the building to another. For the visit of the Belgrave Harriers a ' full house ' of 50 had gathered. On entering the room the members stood and hand-clapped a warm welcome. At the end of the room were placed chairs on which were tagged the names of the various members of the panel; the value of this was, of course, that as the speaker stood to expound stories, his name would be known to all.

It had been arranged with the Secretary, that the panel and meeting would be under the Chairmanship of Bill Lucas.

Dinners and Social affairs are, as a rule, the right places to attend if one wishes to gather information about your Club or organisation and its members which is not common knowledge, and this visit to the prison was no exception.

The opening address of the President was packed full of interest, from his opening lines he had the panel and audience sitting tight. His epitome of the affairs of the Belgrave Harriers, from the start of the Club up to the recent Brighton Relay was a masterpiece of delivery and full of information. ( It is respectfully suggested, that Bill Lucas could mark his year of Office, by having this address printed and distributed to all members and future Belgravians for their information and to tell them what type of Club the Belgrave Harriers is and its service to Athletics).

As the summary was being given, the audience was most attentive; no movement of chairs; no coughing, it appeared that the listeners were intent on hearing every word. Gently, Bill steered his words to introduce the first speaker, Frank Jarvis, to talk on the Club

Ron spoke of various ways of training developed over the years, Ron. did mention of a certain house on Wimbledon Common where these training devotees met and reclined in baths full of scented water and using exotic soap, he did say, that it was possible that training was second to the scented bath, which amused the meeting. Arthur was in great form, his anecdotes were legion, in particular, his escapades with Bert Footer together with the full history of the ' syrup of figs' and methods of training that have no relation to training of to-day. The audience were amused, they understood the language, here Arthur produced his British Commonwealth Games Victory plaque and International Badges for the men to examine. Jack Bidgood was the next to speak of his experiences and spoke, in particular, on the merits of Race Walking and Belgrave Harriers great record in this branch of athletics, then on to Brian Nott who spoke on British athletes and their chances in the Olympic Games next year.

It was agreed by all concerned that the evening had been most satisfying and well - worth while. An evening to be remembered.

REIGATE RELAY

Chris Steer

CHRIS STEER REPORTS:-

The popular Priory Relays, organized by Redhill and Reigate A.C. once again attracted a large entry and Belgrave's 'A' team, although unable to repeat last year's performance, were far from disgraced in sixth place.

The first leg was taken by Dave Cocks, fresh(?) from a brilliant track season, who used his speed to good effect and handed over in about tenth place. Jackie Hampshire on the second leg kept in close contact with the teams in front, and Chris Steer moved up five places to sixth on the third leg but, by this time, the leaders were well ahead and, with no stars to call on, the possibility of a 'place' looked somewhat unlikely. Nevertheless, Tony Fairclough ran a good fourth leg to close up on the fifth team (Hercules). Trevor Hart, showing a welcome return to form, and John Davies maintained sixth position quite comfortably. Belgrave 'B' team finished fifteenth, Pat Sadler having a good run with 10m 24s.

Surrey AC retained the team title thanks to storming runs by Dave Gibson and John Snowden on the last two laps.

Once again the time-keeping was suspect and several of the times were shuffled about--in one report Tony Fairclough was credited with third fastest time! I think the times in this report are reasonably accurate but it seems a pity that such a popular event should be marred yet again in such a way.

South of Thames Junior

Reading, Nov. 9th

Belgrave were sixth in this year's race.

The numerous barring clauses left us hard-pushed to find a suitable 'A' team and only five of a possible eight were able to start. Twenty-two runners were there to support our remaining teams, however, so there were plenty of Belgrave vests about.

The race was held over a 4 lap course of 1¼ miles each lap, and after the first lap Chris Steer was setting the pace with the leading group. Hopes of an individual win diminished on lap 2 when Chris faded to 11th, and disappeared the following time round,by which time he was 16th. With John Moore then 20th, Dave Cocks 49th and Jackie Hampshire moving through in the 70's, there was still an outside chance of a team place, but Chris then had a bad bout of "stitch" which sent him back 40 places in the space of a mile, and with our other runners roughly holding their positions we had to be content with 6th place.

John Moore ran well, moving from 25th to 19th over a course a little too short for his liking, and if he,Cocks and Steer show anything like the form expected of them during the next 12 months then they also will be barred from this event next year. So there is plenty of scope you youngsters for gaining some glory!

John Dear(a useful prospect), Mike Shingles and Fred Paget were the only others to show noticeably good form.

DABBS WINS CLUB '7'

SATURDAY 7th December

The Club 7½ championship was held in well-nigh perfect cross-country conditions. Bright sunlight, crisp-cold air and the going not too soft. Gerry North was running on the continent and this left the race wide open. At the start a small bunch , consisting of Dabbs, Moore, Thresher and Fairclough headed the field as the first of the three laps was embarked upon. At the village on the end of the first lap, Dabbs was 30 yards clear of "surprise package" Trevor Hart, with Moore and Fairclough in close attendance. Hart's appearance in 2nd position surprised many people and the sceptics in the crowd doubted that he could maintain his position. Next time round, Dabbs had increased his lead to 80 yards but Hart was still hanging onto the second spot although Moore was now looking dangerous. 'Our Trev' was not the only one surprising the forecasters because Authur Bruce was in a bunch of runners behind the leaders and sharing a most welcome return to form. Half-way round on the final lap, Moore passed Hart but found Dabbs with too much of a lead to be able to dispute the leadership. Chas. Walker in his first run since the 'Brighton' finished a good sixth but Bill Kerr after a hard week's training, gave no indication of how fit he really is.
