THE OCTOBER 1977 BELGRAVIAN

the official gazette of belgrave harriers

Front page: Mark Shippen shoots for 4.20m in the Southern League pole vault at Battersea. Photo by Alan Mead

Mark won the Surrey Schools event in June with 4.00m to break his own Youth and Junior Club Records and then went on to equal this height twice more before the month was out. In July he improved again to 4.15m but then lost the All-England Schools event to a lad who had never beaten him before and who had upped his personal best twice during the competition to keep pace with Mark. They both cleared 4.09m but Mark lost on the countback. In the Schools International our man broke his pole while warming up minutes before the competition began - a nasty experience but one which all vaulters have to face once in a while. Luckily he was uninjured but was forced to sit that one out.

Then, on September 10th, Mark became the first Belgravian to hold all Club Records for an event, from Boys' age group to Seniors', when he cleared 4.20m in an invitation event at Woking won by Britis[illegible] Record holder Brian Hooper. The bar was raised another 20cm after this and Mark failed to go any higher but he was near enough for it to be seen that 4.30m is on the cards.

For those who still fail to think metric, 4.20m is 13 feet 9½ inches

Editorial · page 3

The A.A.A. 4x100 metres relay championship was won in 41.3 by Wolverhampton & Bilston, with Thames Valley 2nd in 41.6 and City of Stoke 3rd in 43.2. Where did Belgrave Harriers place? Surely our team which has clocked 42.3 this season should have been in there fighting for the medals.

There was a very good reason for the Bels non-participation. The powers-that-be who arrange the nation's athletic fixtures seem to think that it is a good idea to hold A.A.A. championship events and Southern League events on the same day!

For some years we have had to put up with our decathletes choosing between supporting their club or going to the A.A.A. Decathlon; now our sprinters face the same problem.

Please, somebody up there at the A.A.A. offices, get our major fixtures sorted out. We know that the fixture list is crowded these days and that it is difficult to fit everything in but there are only six Southern League dates in the calendar and there is some talent outside the National League sides.

You may have noticed from the address shown on the left that the "Belgravian" Editor is now living in Berkshire. This will obviously preclude him from attending Belgrave Hall so frequently to keep abreast of Club news so help is therefore required to keep our magazine going. Ideally a person is needed to take charge of each section's results and reports and to forward them to the Editor at regular intervals. There is no need for these folk to do all the writing - they can farm it out as much as they like - but if one person can take on the responsibility for each area of our activities, it will make our Editor's task of co-ordinating the whole thing so much easier.

Please contact the Editor if you would like to help.

Column · page 4

grapevine

Our oldest member, Charlie Speechley, who will be ninety by the time this is in print, recently fell from a ladder in his garden causing very bad lacerations to his legs and breaking two ribs. He was badly shaken but after a spell in hospital was patched up and competed in this years Veterans' A.C. 100 metres Championship. Charlie doesn't come to many meetings these days but sends his regards to his old friends.

It was good to see Vice-President Jack Bidgood at the Club Championships. He still has a powerful physique that many a thrower would envy. In his day he was a good long-distance walker and the energy he developed walking from London to Brighton or from Barking to Southend (an event he won on one occasion) must have been terrific.

Have you noticed the Autumn 1977 slimline Bob Taylor - he's obviously getting fit for cross country course marking. It appears that the back markers in our races will again have the embarrassment of not being able to hold onto the man collecting the flags.

Steve Williams has had an interesting first track season. The Jubilee Relay saw him miss his take-over, sprint 200 metres to grab the baton and then commence to run his mile whilst divesting himself of most of his tracksuit. In the Club 10km Championship he managed to get involved in running an extra lap, and then turning up to watch a a league match on another occasion, suddenly found himself ushered to the start of the 800 metres despite all his protestations.

Congratulations to Don Bonser who is now married and living in Sutton. We don't know his good lady's name but we send our best wishes.

Gerald Collins is also now married, to Jane, and he has obviously made a good choice as she is not only a jogger herself but reminds him to get out training. Best wishes to them both.

And yet another Belgravian has taken the plunge. John Stow and Pam were married in September with many Belgravians present. It seems confusion was caused by Gordon Biscoe handing out numbers and pins at the reception while Colin Pearson spent half the time looking for the changing rooms!

We were sorry to learn recently of the death of C.A.Wattam. Living in Lincolnshire he competed in a number of "Brightons" around the thirties. A modest performer he was nevertheless enormously proud of the standard medals and certificates which his efforts brought him.

Fund raising is no new conception to us. Our Vice-President Edmund Donovan on a recent visit back home (from New York) recalled how in his day (about the time of World War I) the wife of one of our members used to get some white material and run up neatly tailored shorts to sell for Club funds. Another good lady made home-made toffee to sell for the same cause - it sold very quickly. Two or three years ago Ed. entertained a few of the Club officers to dinner at his club. This year the compliment was repaid at a pub in Battersea. He was delighted to talk over old times and personalities and one thing became very apparent - he knew more about Battersea than his hosts did!

Our first social event at Belgrave Hall since the alterations was a great success. The 'Jaguars' were as good (and loud) as ever and will be back with us again in the New Year. Well done Messrs. Lawton, Satchell, Gorman, Pearson and Couzens who worked up to 1am the previous night getting the Hall ready.

It was good to see the slim and stylish figure of Eric Hall competing in the Club 3,000 metres walking championship. One of our greatest all-round walkers he gained place medals in all the R.W.A. Championships, winning the 20 miles on one occasion. He had similar successes on the track and his efforts earned him two Olympic vests - 1956 (50km) and 1960 (20km).

Once again Mrs. Aplin braved the elements (you always have to for this event) to attend the Club 10,000 metres Track Walking Championship, and once more we have to thank her for donating a handicap prize and presenting the awards.

It is rumoured that the Jubilee bonfire on Epsom Downs consumed two tables from Belgrave Hall. It seems that some of the fixtures and fittings at the Hall were not recognisable from the junk that was thrown out during rebuilding.

It has certainly been a bumper year for Belgrave babies. Judy and Jim Rimmer had twins Owen and Stuart on April 18th - weighing in at seven and eight pounds apiece! Clare Louise made her entrance on June 28th, much to the pleasure of Jean and John Hamilton, and at around this time Jan and Martin Best produced a son, Luke. Latest on the list is Hazel Ellen Mead who arrived on September 14th making Pat the first of our lady members to give birth. Alan Mead says that Hazel is sure to be another Irena Szewinska as she arrived at 4.40 (yds or pm?) feet first. Other births are imminent.

A 2nd and four 4ths in A.A.A.

JULY 23rd 1977 - CRYSTAL PALACE

A 2nd and four 4ths in A.A.A.

Graham Seatter was not only the highest placed Belgravian at the 1977 A.A.A. Championships, he was also the highest placed New Zealander. That's some feat when one considers that those Olympic Medallists and World Record holders John Walker and Dick Quax were competitors. Graham placed 2nd in the 3,000 metres walk in 12:14.9, not too far behind Roger Mills of Ilford who equalled the Championship Best of 12:08.4. Other Belgrave walkers were Carl Lawton, 4th in 12:56.7, and John Hall, 9th in 13:28.4.

John Bicourt qualified for the steeplechase final with 8:46.8. He started at the back of the field in the final but then worked his way up to 4th by the finish - 8:48.4.

In the sprints Ernest Obeng looked really sharp. Running into a 1.9 metre wind (worth one or two tenths) he took his heat in 10.74 ahead of a cruising Clancy Edwards of the U.S.A, 6th fastest man in the World this year at 10.18. Another tight finish in the final had Ernie just out of the medals by 1/100th of a second as he crossed the line 4th in 10.75. Only one U.K. competitor was ahead of him, Tim Bonsor 3rd in 10.74, with the first two positions going to the Americans Edwards, 10.48 and Charlie Wells, 10.65. Behind Ernie were top rated British sprinters Green, Hoyte and Roberts plus Davies of Jamaica.

These performances added to Carl Lawton's fourth place in the 10km walk back in March give Belgrave a highly creditable 2nd and four 4ths for 1977.

Feature · page 7

OUR PRESIDENT John V. Baker

Charlie Manning

by Charlie Manning

I was asked at the 11th hour to write something about John - it will not have the polished professionalism of Alan Mead or the suave confident touch of Clive Shippen (although I think he uses a ghost writer) so I decided on a sort of questionnaire......

Charlie: The beginning's a good place to start, so where were you born? It may appear a silly question but half the Club don't think you were born at all but hatched like a vulture and the other half, giving you the benefit of the doubt, would say Israel. John: New Malden.

C: Let's assume you have a good memory. Can you tell us something of your youth? J: They started a war when I was quite young....I left home at 15 (C: bloody delinquent even then!)....joined NAAFI (Navy,Army,Air Force Institution) and was put in the Navy to be sent abroad.

C: On reading back so far is that OK? J: No! No! Having joined NAAFI before the war and working on the naval side, naturally decided to go to sea. I joined HMS Bramham, a newly commissioned Hunt Class destroyer (under 1,000 tons) on June 13th 1942. Escorted the King and Queen to Ireland, then had a near escape from death when the ship made a sharp manoeuvre and I lost my footing. I skidded across the deck and was saved from going over the side and into the propellors by a pile of netting on the edge. You have to remember there are only stanchions and one wire - no formal railings.

The first notable action was Operation Pedestal on which the whole war hinged - it was the relief of Malta. Of a convoy of 14 merchantmen, only five got through, of which the tanker "Ohio" was the key. The "Ohio" was carrying essential fuel and was under constant attack from submarines, E-boats, mines on parachutes and one Stuka actually crashed on its deck. Three hours sleep in 90 hours and no food for five days - and I weighed more before that than I do now. I was seventeen years old then.

The following month we were in the Artic Circle! Two of my engagements they made films from. Within two months I was on Operation Torch which was the North African invasion. We were hit and made port with the decks under water. Then we took off the dead and injured. One bomb came right through my mess deck!

C: As well as sums, was there anything else that you enjoyed doing? J: Well actually maths was my worst subject at school.

C: What were your ambitions apart from Denis Healey's job? J: Don't think I've ever had any.

C: As a young man, and I'm sure you have been one, what was the general pattern of your life? J: Scouting, cricket, tennis, cycling, camping...and athletics. C: 'ang about - we'll come to that in a minute.

C: Right. Now let's turn our attention to athletics. Can you tell me in just a few words what misguided chance led you into sport? J: The NAAFI had a sports day and I came last in a mile race. They put a cartoon in the local paper. Then they had an athletics section - I was on the committee. We raced against other firms - that's how I met you. As you were no good we let you run. C: I personally have known you to masquerade as a runner but contemporaries of Alf Shrubb have suggested that you are a failed walker too, can you comment? J: I've never done any walking with the Club but I was once detailed to compete in a mile walk for my firm and I came second.

C: We all have a favourite mountain to conquer. Yours would be me - but on a more realistic level is there anything in particular you'd like to achieve? J: Yes. The London to Brighton run. I still intend to do it - providing my leg doesn't give out.

C: Some of our younger members have flattered you by saying you have been Giles' model for 'Chalky'. Would you now make a firm denial? J: Ha! Ha! Ha! - having been in one cartoon......

C: The mafia of Belgrave Harriers have unanimously agreed to your election as President. What's in it for them? J: Ha! Ha! You're not putting that are you?

C: What's in it for the Club. J: Tightening of belts.....

C: What do you hope to do that Belgrave will remember you with despair. J: Increase the subs. - no, I'm not a vindictive man. C: Remember you with pleasure? J: (...a long pause and no answer).

C: You are obviously aware of your role at the Club Dinner. A furious debate is going on about who will be your partner. Gordon Biscoe thinks it will be Racquel Welch - I think it will be Christine Keeler - and Brian Gorman thinks it will be Brian Gorman. You could stop a lot of speculation now by telling us so we can keep Nigel Dempster and William Hickey away. J: Well, there won't be anyone - I'm a lone wolf. C: (Watch it girls - you know how they hunt!) J: I'm always on my own on the top table anyway. Really I haven't made my mind up yet. All members can apply - including Brian Gorman.

C: Are you going to make any statement on your policy? J: It's all in my report as Treasurer.

Well that's it but looking around his flat I see his taste in reading is basically Zane Grey, music is Glen Miller and he exercises with a night storage heater.

WALKING

Carl Lawton

Before we start the general report a short advertisement. This autumn, October 15th, Belgrave will be holding the National Junior, Youth and Boys Championships from our Headquarters. The course will be on the top of the common as this will be easy for spectating. It is hoped therefore that all Belgravians will make every effort to attend. I will in the course of the next month be asking various people for assistance on the day of the races.

Three Nationals to date this year, a 4th and two 3rds, certainly the best for a few years, we could have done better if.... but that is the usual story. We are regretfully having to rely too much on the long serving walkers in the club and we are in desperate need of a larger group of the mid 20's for the distance events, as it is a few years since we finished 8 in either the National 20 miles or 50 kilometres. The question is from where do we build. It is no use waiting until our present Youths and Juniors get older as by then many will have fallen by the wayside indeed as some of last years juniors have done.

Certain events this summer need special mention. The 100 mile relay was well supported by Belgrave although there was the possibility of there being 2 teams starting, this fell through and we only just managed to start one and that was due to Doug Fotheringham stepping in at less than 24 hours notice. Special note of thanks to those who stuck out the long damp, cold and foggy night. John Keown, Bob Whitehead on recording ably assisted by Rosemary and Barbara. Betty Bromley provided a constant supply of tea and Barbara Dunsford stuck it through as judge, one of only two for the event.

One other person who had possibly the hardest job, Ron Greene, well known to our walkers in recent years as Ray Middleton's attendant, was pressganged into the job of team manager and ensured, with difficulty, the flow at 7 to 8 minute intervals of walkers to be ready on the line. Not an envious job but he managed to carry us through. We didn't win but we might have done if....

The girls' section is not exactly quite as it should be. We have had individual winners in the Lily 'B' league but we are not getting full turn out when there are the team races. Maybe we need a new dynamic coach and whipper up for the section. Any suggestions?

Carl Lawton. August 1977.

Ray's Record Collection

G.S.D.

MAY 14th 1977 - STRETFORD - R.W.A. 20KM CHAMPIONSHIP.

Ray's Record Collection

The account of the R.W.A. 10 miles championship concluded by saying that we should soon be in the medals again and this was put to the test on May 14th when the 20 kilometres championship was held over a fast three lap course at Stretford. One of our previous team, Richard Tanner, was missing as he was competing in the Police Barking to Southend walk the following day, this also affected other Clubs.

After five kilos Olly Flynn, Roger Mills and Brian Adams were away battling for Individual honours and they finished in that order. Then came the leading group which contained Carl and Graham, Ray soon appeared but where was our fourth man? John seemed further back than usual, he was taking things easy in case a previous injury flared up. It looked as if there would be no medals this time. At halfway Carl was well up but Graham seemed to be falling away, Ray looked good and John had kept his position. Sheffield looked good for the Team title, but the other positions looked close - a chance for us perhaps.

At 15 kilos. Graham had slipped back but John was going better and was going to gain places. At the finish Carl was fifth (having had two short stops), Graham, having a bad day, fought hard to maintain a good position. Next came Ray who had picked up places including overtaking a vital Coventry man, and John kept up the pressure to close in the team. The points for the placings were very close; Leicester W.C. 84; Belgrave 87; Coventry 88; Brighton 93. So by one point we were in the medals again. A fine performance as only one of the team had a trouble free race, Ray Middleton, who powered his way along to collect his 46th R.W.A. team medal. No other walker can have collected as many so it must be a record collection.

Ray won his first R.W.A. team medal in 1956, he has also won Individual medals, and for the last 13 years he has never been lower than fifth in the 50 kilos. championship. The following break down shows his tally.

18 Gold, 21 Silver, 7 Bronze, Total 46.

Surely a record that will be hard to beat, indeed he may well add a few more. Ray typifies the Belgrave team spirit and we can unashamedly hold him up as a prime example to our younger members. Each of his medals required the support of three others. We have some promising youngsters and if they can have the dedication of Ray and his team mates they too can bring honour to the Bels. and start a collection.

Mexican Fiesta

Obituary · page 17

A. H. 'BERT' EAST

G.S.D.

It was with deep sorrow that we recently learned of the death of Bert Only a few weeks earlier he had been seen in the Park. Born in 1903 he joined us in the twenties and had a virtually unbroken spell as a walker in our colours. Although he never reached the top he was a good Club and Team performer. He had won the Horwood Cup (restricted to those not "barred" from the Garnet Trophy) on a number of occasions. He had scored in placed teams on a number of occasions in the Garnet Race, but the winning team eluded him. Perhaps his best year was 1948 when, in the Inter-Counties Road Walking Championship, held in Hyde Park, he scored in the Surrey team which tied with Middlesex for first place. He kept to the shorter distances and was a great supporter of any race (he was very keen on handicaps) up to ten miles. He was also a member of the Veterans A.C. and was their Walking Vice-Captain.

On the small side, he had a good style and even when well into the veteran's class he maintained fine co-ordination and fluidity. He was essentially a quiet chap but the one thing which got him talking was walking - a sport which he loved and to which he gave much devotion and dedication. He will surely be missed by the "regulars" at Battersea Park and Wimbledon who held him in high esteem.

Not a very appropriate title as far as Belgrave was concerned. The 42nd R.W.A. 50 kilos. Championship was, in a way, a rehearsal for the Lugano Trophy Walks to be held in September. The course, not severe, was twenty laps, but the organisers hadn't arranged the weather - the worst possible for a long event - warm and very humid. This caused the field of 105 starters, including 14 teams, to be whittled down to 48 finishers with only five teams closing in with Belgrave finishing their team of four starters intact.

Unfortunately we were without Carl Lawton who was due for an International in Sweden a few days later, he was however a tower of strength to our four and refreshed and cajoled them into finishing. Ray Middleton, after a good start, was well placed and at about halfway looked as if he would be challenging for a place, but he dropped a few places towards the end (most unusual for him) to finish a creditable seventh. Our next man, John Hall also looked good about half-way, but he too found the going tough and slowed considerably. By now a good many teams had disintegrated and were out of the reckoning. Dave King, with little training, walked his usual steady race but the speed wasn't there. Completing our quartet Bob Picton, who had shadowed Dave for many laps, began to drop away and had a real struggle over the last few laps. In common with other competitors our lads looked greatly relieved as they crossed the line for the last time.

Individually the race was a triumph for Brian Adams (Leicester W.C.) making his debut over the distance on the road. For a long way he was in the lead with Jake Warhurst (Sheffield) but eventually got away, although the Sheffield man was pulling him back towards the end Sheffield won the team title to make a clean sweep of the R.W.A. Senior titles. Boundary H. (Isle of Man) gained unanimous applause upon their great performance in taking second.team medals (with fifth and sixth individual placings to lead them). Leicester W.C. took revenge on us by squeezing us out of third place.

Our four deserve all praise on their courageous and sterling performances. Their fourth place probably makes us second to Sheffield in the overall reckoning for the four Senior Championships. Ray (First Class) and John(Second Class) gained standards.

SPOTLIGHT on JOE MCDONALD

Alan Mead

A late September sun shone down as tired road runners, well down on the race leaders, plodded their way along the tarmac strip that was the A23 - the "Brighton Road". Mile after mile the tortuous procession made its way, the runners becoming increasingly strung out. One weary but tough looking little man found it momentarily too much. He dropped down to a shamble and then to a walk, seemingly at the limit of his endurance. Within a minute or so a car stopped and the driver a woman, enquired to see if she could be of assistance.

"Are you alright. Would you like a lift?" The car door swung invitingly open. "No, no, I'm O.K.", the runner gasped, confirming that he was really far from alright. "How far is it to Brighton?" The shattering reply hit him like a hammer blow: "About 25 miles."

For this Belgravian dropping out of the London to Brighton run was something that just could not be considered and before long Joe McDonald was plodding the old A23 again. He finished. "...Well, I was really ****...but I couldn't let the other lads in the team down could I!"

Mac's running career is full of incident. Almost any Belgrave distance runner can relate an anecdote about Mac, the man with little natural athletic talent who through sheer hard work and tigerish aggression eventually became a Club Champion. Talking to Mac about his running is like facing a wild man. In hot-blooded Anglo-Saxon his rivals are decried and with arms waving and eyes glittering he will spit out threats to that "*****bald headed old ***** Baker" and anyone else who dares to suggest that they will beat him next time out. Now in his latter 50's he still terrorises the scrubbers in our races over all distances from "Yacht 3" to the Club 10 mile cross country race and woe betide any lap scorer who disagrees with Mac's calculations about how many circuits he has left to cover in the 10,000 metre track championship.

Before we examine Mac's story in more detail perhaps we should explain, for the benefit of those few who do not know him, that it is impossible to write about this fiery character in language that is fit for a club magazine. Without that language, however, he just isn't the spitfire we know and cherish. Mac's adjectives are many and varied but the meanings are unimportant. It is only the vehemence with which he utters them that tells how he feels about a subject. He can curse with a frightening violence revealing hate and bitterness - five star words these. The same words used gently underline his affection for a topic - three stars, while four stars show how earnest he feels.

In Mac's parlance, when talking of a rival: "He's an old ***" means one of his pals, a runner he has known and liked for years. Whereas, "He's an old ****" means a very old, one might say ancient, runner indeed. Then we have, "He's an old*****"....someone who beat him in yesterday's road race, or the Hon. Handicapper!

Joe McDonald was born in Kensington on June 2nd 1921 and he has lived and worked in this Royal Borough ever since. As a lad he well remembers hanging about outside the gates of the Stamford Bridge ground in Fulham Road when the A.A.A. Championships were on, waiting for the marathon runners, usually headed by the great Sam Ferris, to sweep into view and then turn into the stadium to finish their 26 miles and 385 yards. "I never dreamed that I would do it one day."

As a schoolboy at Beaufort House Mac took part in the athletics but without a great deal of success until in his last year, as a 15 year old, he did win a couple of medals for the 100 and 220 yards in the school sports.

"Those races were at the Duke of York's track in the Kings Road....funny how in all these years that place keeps cropping up.....Anyway, I was second in the 100 yards and then in the 220 this ***** cut me up and I ended up fourth when I should have been third. The judges **** saw him though and disqualified the ***** so I got the medal after all.

Mac became a member of the Church Lads Brigade and it was as a Church Lad that he first tasted success.

"We used to have this three mile cross country race every year over at Parliament Hill and the first time I ran in it I was doing quite well and was leading. Then when we got near the finish this ***** just pipped me on the line. I said to him, 'You *****. You **** wait till next year! I really trained for the next year's race and when it came up I thought - right no ***** is going to get me this time. I ran flat out from the start. An old harrier used to lead the race to make sure that we didn't go wrong. He says to me, 'slow down, I can't keep up with you' but I was **** if I was going to slow down and let that ***** beat me again. I won by over half a mile."

The Vicar who ran the Church Lads Brigade helped Mac to one of his greatest achievements and a moment which he still recalls with pride.

"I had run well so the Vicar, he was Scotch, a Mr. Watt, he bought me a tea - and I'd thought he was a tight old *** before that. He told me that he would train me to serve at the altar in church. I said I couldn't do it, I mean you know me! Anyway he insisted. So I practised and practised and learned all the words. Then when the time came all my mates were sitting in the church laughing and making fun of me but I did it. I did everything just **** right - lighting the candles, getting all the words right, I showed 'em."

Mac had begun to think about joining a running club. "I nearly tried to join that club over at the 'Dysart' but I thought they might be a **** snooty lot!" Down at the "Duke's" Jimmy Beard had been helping the Church Lads to try and beat five minutes for the mile. Jimmy was a member of Belgrave Harriers so it was natural that our Mac should also become a Belgravian, joining on November 2nd 1938. "Poor old Jimmy, he died when he was only 21. He got pneumonia."

So it was that Mac began to carve his niche in the Club, racing over the courses that are still familiar to us today and occupying more or less the same positions in races then that we expect him to do today. His first event was the seven mile cross country championship at the age of seventeen. Tom Carter won and Mac was 42nd out of 44. Three times in the week he would train with Jimmy and then on Saturdays it was up to Belgrave Hall to run out over the country but somehow it never really seemed to click.

"I always wanted to run marathons but they told me you're too young Mac, there's plenty of time. One day though Dick Tobin says to me, 'Mac, you like going to the pictures don't you, well you're never going to get fit by staying up late. You've got to go to bed early, train harder and give up going out so often.' He was right. It took me about five years to get really fit but he made me, him and Stimpson."

After about two years of hard work Carter said to me, 'We've seen all this training but isn't it about time you raced?' So I ran in the five mile cross country handicap in October 1947. I was in the bath afterwards when I heard them announce my name - I'd won the **** handicap - an eight day clock. I was really pleased."

Two years of hard training had taken Mac way up the field and he had placed 14th of the 53 runners. Now began his golden era. In May '48 he took part in the Chichester to Portsmouth '16' and placed 18th for the 'B' team. In fact the 'A' and 'B' team tied for third place so Captain Bert Footer tossed for the prizes and the 'B' team won. Two week's later Mac completed the Kent '20' and then on 19th July in the quadrangle of Windsor Castle (by kind permission of His Royal Majesty King George the Sixth) he lined up for the Polytechnic Marathon.

Mac's aim in the race was to beat the A.A.A. Standard and at 20miles he was well inside the schedule to do so but as every marathoner knows, those last six miles odd can be hell. Mac slowed and slowed and on eventually reaching the track he felt so bad that he refused point blank to run once round to the finish. The road running captain shouted at him enough to goad him into action and the final line was crossed 3 hours 17 minutes and 1 second after he started.

That first painful marathon was the beginning of a fourteen year period of consecutive "Poly's", Mac's last being in 1961. He is particularly proud of the fact that he never pulled out of one, come blisters....heat....cramp....or anything. The Polytechnic Marathon was always a rather special event. One year Her Majesty Queen Elizabeth, the Queen Mother, chatted to a few of the competitors before the start - one of whom was Mac! "'I bet you don't half have to train hard' she said to me - well, she didn't actually say 'I bet' but you know what I mean."

As Mac's time for the marathon came down over the years, so the A.A.A. Standard was made harder but in 1952, when the race was designated an Olympic trial, our man ran to his fastest ever. Of the 16 Belgrave entrants Mac was our first home in 29th position with 2 hours 46 minutes and 6 seconds - a Club Championship and Honours Badge had come his way at last.

"The Duke of Edinburgh started the race that year and some of the runners went off just before he fired the gun but he made all the ***** come back and line up again."

"Then there was the time the Queen started the race.... she was a princess then. Well, she went to fire the little silver gun they use but it didn't go off. Her dad, the King, he laughed like ****."

It was in 1952 that his club mates tackled him about what was to become the road run of them all. "Well now Mac, you've done your marathon and you did alright but what would you think about running two marathons, one after the other?" Mac's reply to the effect that they were barmy didn't blunt their persuasiveness and before long he had agreed. The first of the Road Runners Club London to Brighton runs took place on August 27th with around 50 starters tackling the 52¼ mile course which then as now started from the clock tower at Westminster and finished at the Brighton Aquarium. Wally Wortley, 10th, led the Bels home with Mac 16th, George Chantler 17th and Arthur Whitehead 21st.

Mac's training was a five times a week affair. Most of it was on the road and it averaged out to between 40 and 50 miles per week, excluding Sunday's four hour walk. Very often the team would run as a pack in the long races, helping one another along and gaining quite a few team prizes in the process. In 1954 Mac got his R.R.C. 2nd class award, a certificate that required a set standard to be attained in a '10', a '15' a '20' and a marathon, all in the same year.

The seasons rolled by in a succession of Wigmore '15s', Belgrave '20s' and S.L.H. '30s' and Mac's stature as a "character" in Belgrave grew. There was that London to Brighton run in 80 degrees heat. "Where's Mac," went up the cry and when they went back along the route looking for him, found him sitting on the grass verge at the roadside. 'I'm **** hungry - got anything to eat?" And then he wolfed the proffered leathery old ham sandwich before setting off once more.

On Arthur Whitehead: "I always used to go off like a maniac in 'fifteens' and always near the end of the race that *** Whitehead used to catch me and say 'What are you hanging about for Mac?' I beat him one time, though, when a couple of ***** louts started shouting at us and Whitehead stopped in the middle of the race to thump them!

More recently there was the episode where Mac and others were accused of running on to the polo field in Richmond Park while a match was in progress; the verbal assault on the intervening Parks Police Woman and the letter of complaint that followed. Or what about that bleak winter's run in the snow two seasons back and the stares of the passers by as Mac, at the head of a scruffy group,ran clad in a pair of womens black tights (seams and all) in an attempt to keep warm.

The long distance career of Joe McDonald slowed down from around 1961 but he has never stopped competing over the shorter Club and Veterans courses. He continues to train in the week at the "Dukes" and on a Saturday and Sunday at Wimbledon. The old speed might not be there in today's sessions but they are none the less ferocious for that.

Yes, Mac's a character alright and for his loyalty to the Club he was made a Vice-President in 1972. Road Running was always his sport but just look around at the Club Track Championships or at a youngsters meeting and he'll be there; measuring the long jumps, putting up the bar in the pole vault, collecting and washing the glasses at a social event. When the ladies are around Mac can hold his tongue. His language might be salty but underneath he's a real gentleman.

Thinking of the members he has known over the years Mac recalls those he has admired. "Tobin....Burfitt....all those who helped me get started....that young bloke who used to run the 880 .... Cocks. Oh! there's loads of them."

And asked what he thinks of Belgrave Harriers: "What! It's my **** life that's what it is!

Alan Mead

THE ACORN PLANTER

Clive Shippen

a tribute to W. H. 'HARRY' HARE 1895 - 1977

On 23rd April 1977, Belgrave's oldest surviving Past President, HARRY HARE, passed away at the age of 82 years.

By leaving us in 1977, the year of our headquarters extension, it was almost as though he had chosen this moment of departure. Almost as though he wanted to see the finalisation of his dreams before moving on to that land from whose bourne no traveller returns.

For it was Harry, perhaps more than anyone, who initiated the sequence of events which led to the recent improvements at Belgrave Hall. He it was who found himself in the hot seat back in 1921 when the Club unexpectedly found itself without a home. He it was who searched and found our present premises, who negotiated the original rental, who founded the Belgrave Building Fund and who guided us along the path which led to the purchase of the leasehold in 1936, the freehold in 1950 and extensions in the fifties and seventies.

Today's membership owes a great deal to the methodical, painstaking and far-seeing work that was put in by our young Secretary/Treasurer of more than 50 years ago.

For fifteen years (1921 - 1935) Harry gave his all for the Club. He was Secretary/Treasurer for 4 years (during which time membership doubled), founder-editor of the Belgravian (2 years), President (2½ years) and Treasurer (3 years). When he relinquished the last office in 1934 the building fund had the handsome sum of £728 to its credit - a great deal of money in those days, which went a considerable way to purchasing the leasehold two years later.

Tragically, it was just as the first part of Harry's dream was about to come true that he suffered a steady falling away in health which resulted in a breakdown and his eventual absence from any further Club activities.

Despite that long absence his thoughts were frequently with Belgrave and no-one was a more avid reader of The Belgravian than Harry. Even during his last years he was well informed on Club activities and personalities and followed with interest the progress of athletes whose fathers had not been born when Harry was at the helm!

But it was those days back in the twenties for which Harry had the fondest memories - when he faced and conquered the biggest problems of all and helped secure the permanence of Belgrave Harriers as a leading athletic club. During those days he was one of a small band of men who worked increasingly to revive the club from the doldrums of the First World War. Amongst his colleagues were other legendary names such as Oscar Horwood, Harry Parker, Bob Ricketts, Frank Thomson, Jock McKay and Wally Bayliss.

Harry joined Belgrave in 1920 as a promising novice walker, progressed rapidly to our scoring teams and had the honour of being our third scoring man in the Club's first National win - the '20' of 1924. Belgrave retained their title in 1925 and Harry once again collected a team winner's medal. Only once did he attempt the onerous London to Brighton race, finishing in the creditable time of 9:10:50.

After less than 12 months in the Club, young Hare decided that he should attend the A.G.M. of 1921, He emerged from the meeting as a bemused, bewitched and very bewildered Secretary/Treasurer, nervously clasping a bundle of books and papers - the soothing words of Oscar Horwood the retiring secretary still in his ears.

Our headquarters in those days was "The Swan" in the Ridgway - or at least the skittle alley and yard at the side. One of the first tasks for the new secretary was to negotiate for the use of the premises for the coming winter season. It was no small shock for Harry to learn from the publican that the accommodation was going to be converted into a saloon lounge and that no facilities would be available for Belgrave.

After two weeks of searching for alternative premises Harry spotted the pub's potman in the Ridgway. He was conversing with the local postman who also happened to be part-time caretaker of St. John's Hall, Denmark Road. The dialogue became a trialogue and within a couple of days Harry was seated in the parlour of the Parish Hall's Treasurer. He emerged triumphant with an agreement to rent the Hall on Saturday afternoons at seven shillings and sixpence (37½p) a time.

On the day of the Opening Run and Walk Harry had to stand in the Ridgway and divert members from The Swan to our new home. One of the early arrivals was the diminutive Harry Parker who sniffed around the building, inside and out, made approving noises and said "Yes, very nice, but where are you going to get the members to fill the place?"

Four years later our membership had doubled, and the scope of the Club's activities had so widened that when Harry relinquished his Secretary/Treasurer post it was necessary to elect two officers to replace him, thus separating the financial from the general secretarial work.

During those years many of our hard working officials felt the need for a club journal. None more so than Harry Hare. Stepping down from the Secretary/Treasurer post enabled him to set about realising another dream. The result was The Belgravian Magazine of April 1925 - a modest 8-page issue which blossomed under successive editors to the quality magazine we know today. In that very first issue, the founding Editor wrote "This issue of The Belgravian has been produced after considerable thought, having regard to the cost incurred and the demands of training. If however this effort in any way achieves the object it is wished to attain, then we shall be happy in the knowledge that the time has been well spent and the cost amply repaid". The magazine has remained an important link between the centre of Club activities and our many distant members.

After 2½ years as our scribe Harry handed over the magazine to Ben Knifton and answered the call of our seriously ill President, Bob Ricketts, to preside over Club affairs. Harry served the Club as President for the remainder of that year and was further chosen in 1927 and 1928 to fill the Club's principal office.

During all of this time he was still training and competing, and although it may seem that he had already given a lifetime of service to Belgrave, he was still only 36 years of age when he took on the Treasurership for the second time in 1931.

Harry always had something positive to offer when he took on a job. This time his brainchild was the Belgrave Building Fund, which still exists. Everyone wanted to see Belgrave own its headquarters and money had already been raised to this end by Xmas Draws and Derby Sweeps. But Harry wanted to see a fund set aside solely for that purpose. With Teddy Gordon's aid, wooden money boxes were made, called Belgrave Bricks, and these were distributed to nearly all members. In announcing the scheme's inauguration Harry Hare said "This scheme will not cost you a penny". The slots in the boxes were to receive half-pennies! The idea was that members would put away every half-penny that came into their pockets. As the bricks were filled they were handed in to swell the fund which became a great success.

At the A.G.M. of that year (1931) Jimmy Bellchamber was elected President and during his address said "All of Harry's work for the Club, whether as an athlete, Secretary, President or Treasurer, has in reality been Club building in the truest sense of the word, and it has consequently borne the imprint of permanence". The membership of today can certainly vouch for that.

Sixteen years later, with the leasehold our own, and the freehold itself but three years away, Jimmy Bellchamber found good cause to continue his theme. "Truly, the Building Fund can now be likened to a flourishing oak tree - a shelter from wind and rain should present storms beset us and above all, both the promise and the material from which an enduring home for the Club will one day be fashioned. That day is surely not far distant, and Harry can be sure that those grateful for the oak tree will be mindful of the acorn from which it sprung and of the enthusiastic spirit that set it there".

For many years, due to crippling arthritis, that enthusiastic spirit was dimmed but never extinguished. Although frustrated at times, he came to terms with his ill-health and was happy to have his wife, Alice,and daughter, Jane, close at hand. Alice remembers him looking through his well-thumbed back numbers of The Belgravians recently. "We asked him if we could help. He said 'No', teased us, smiled thoughtfully and added 'You might read all about Harry Hare one day'.

That day has now arrived.

And when you, dear Belgravian next enter our modernised headquarters pause awhile and think for a moment - of Harry Hare and his contemporaries - the acorn planters.

Clive Shippen.

WITH THE BELLES

Ray Hall

The Summer has passed, if you have not noticed it, but the important aspect is that the Ladies Section have now finished their first full Track Season. We have not won any trophies but we have not been disgraced or outperformed. The Season has been made up of two major competitions, the Womens Southern League Division IV for Seniors and Juniors (11-14 years) and the Lily 'B' League Division III for Fillies (11 and 12 years) and Juniors (13-14 years). These Leagues consist of three matches in each, competing against the same Clubs each time. I personally thought we had overstretched ourselves in tackling these competitions but the results proved otherwise.

In our Division of the Southern League our rivals were Aldershot, Farnham & District 'B' team, Dorchester, Hercules Wimbledon AC, Portsmouth 'B' and Winchester AC. We started this League with a visit to Portsmouth where, not knowing what to expect, we were extremely nervous. The conditions did not help with a strong wind blowing off the sea and heavy rain showers, but at the end of the day we were pleasantly surprised with the result, finishing 3rd behind Aldershot 'B' and Portsmouth 'B' teams, and we travelled back through heavy rain with light and possibly relieved hearts. Our second Southern League match saw us visiting Aldershot where we again finished 3rd, but our match score had increased to 134 pts. as against 103 pts at Portsmouth. For our final match, we visited our local rivals Hercules Wimbledon. This meeting was a great disappointment due to the illness of some of our team and others not turning up. We only managed to finish 6th with 94½ pts which put us 4th overall in our Section and meant the loss of a place in the Promotion Match.

The Lily 'B' League proved to be harder than the Southern League, and our opposing teams were Camberley & District, Dorking St.Pauls AC, Herne Hill and Walton AC. For our first match we visited Redhill track on a very nice sunny day, where Dorking St.Pauls were the hosts. We had some fine performances here, with Walton AC taking first place, Herne Hill second and Belgrave third just in front of Camberley & District. Our second match saw us travelling to Walton on a cold Saturday afternoon - claiming to be a Summers Day - where we again finished 3rd behind Herne Hill (1st) and Walton (2nd). For the final match Belgrave were hosts at Battersea Park where good weather and good competition provided a great afternoon's sport with Walton winning with 352 pts., Herne Hill 314 pts. Belgrave 311 pts. and Dorking 159 pts. This result gave Walton and Herne Hill promotion to Division II, where we wish them luck. We welcome Mitcham AC and Redhill & Reigate who join us in Division III next year.

Late in the Season we were invited to compete in the Melbourne Trophy at Wimbledon, where the Fillies won the 4 x 100m and 4 x 200m relays against good opposition. The full result is not to hand as we go to press.

Many people - Club members and otherwise - have commented on our girls and how smart they look in their outfits. We will without doubt go from strength to strength, and in the Club Championships in July we had 57 entries.

In writing this Summary I have not mentioned any names of athletes or helpers because it has been a team effort and the points gained came evenly from track and field, Juniors and Seniors alike. Many people competed in events other than those they had trained for and many have found new events.

May I say thank you Girls on behalf of the Club for all your good efforts in this our first Track Season.

Ray Hall.

YOUNG ATHLETES

Brian Pritchard

After two years of being involved with the Young Athletes section of Belgrave Harriers I have reluctantly had to stand down through pressure of work but I am very pleased that Leo Coy has been able to step in and take over. I am sure he will do an excellent job.

As the cross country season approaches it is nice to see that we now have enough active Youths to fill teams in our coming fixtures, a thing we have been unable to do with any consistency in the last two years, and this must be good for the Club. In Richard Dorman's appointment as Junior Captain I think Belgrave have made an excellent choice. He is friendly with the boys and although a Youth still has time to take an interest in boys five or six years his junior. Richard always turns out when required to compete for the Club and has an excellent head on his shoulders for such a young man.

I would like to thank all the boys and seniors who have supported me during my term of office and I hope they will give Leo the same support. The hardest part of the Young Athletes Secretary's job in my opinion is the transportation of the athletes to and from fixtures and if any member or parent is prepared to help out in this capacity I am sure Leo would be very grateful. Leo's address is on the inside back page of this magazine if you wish to contact him. I can assure you that you need not be tied any more than you wish to be.

In closing I would like to say that I have enjoyed my time as Young Athletes Secretary and although I have made some mistakes (Editor: "Don't we all") I would hope that I have helped this great Club of ours in some small way.

Brian Pritchard

1976 BRITISH HOGGAR MOUNTAINS AND BILMA SANDS EXPEDITION

George Flanagan

GEORGE FLANAGAN writes of his experiences as leader of the 1976 BRITISH HOGGAR MOUNTAINS AND BILMA SANDS EXPEDITION

The purpose of the expedition was to make a first ever crossing of the Sahara Desert from Lake Chad travelling due North using no local guides but relying solely on our own navigational aids and to explore the remote area of the Hoggar Mountains in the Sahara Desert known as the Tahifet Region and climb in the Atakor Region.

On October 9th, 1976 six people in two specially equipped Land Rovers left Dover travelling through France and Italy to Tunisia and Algeria.

The route planned going south was an easy one through the Sahara Desert via The Tademait Plateau, In Salah, Tamanrasset Agadez reaching Kano in Nigeria where final preparations were made for the formidable task of crossing the Grande Erg De Bilma from Nguigmi in Niger just to the north west of Lake Chad.

To reach this point we had to make a long detour due to the river Komodougan being in flood. In this region just to the north of Abadam we found small villages where many had not seen white people before.

A tremendous amount of preparation had gone into the proposed crossing northwards from Nguigmi. Our choice of two prepared routes was reduced to one due to the activities of the Taubau and the Frolinat Forces infamous for their kidnapping of M. Claustre. They had extended their activities to include a point where our firstly proposed route was due to emerge at the northern side and no one in the party fancied taking their chances with an encounter similar to the one reported several months earlier when the occupants of two Land Rovers were killed for their vehicles.

The route we plumped for went almost due north and had only been previously attempted using local guides to lead the way.

We were crossing using our own system of Dead Reckoning with a Sestrel Compass and using Stars and Planets at night to fix our position ready for the next days navigation. The first three days were fairly easy going in which we made good progress not having to deviate much from a straight line and using a track of sorts for about half the distance. The fourth day took us into the complex dune areas proper and we were constantly changing course, sometimes as much as every few hundred yards.

The system used was for the lead vehicle to find a route through the dune field as near to our approximate direction as was possible to travel, weaving through the dunes and climbing when necessary, using the magnetic compass. The other vehicle would follow and keep an accurate Dead Reckoning Plot on the progress which would be plotted onto the large scale map at the end of the day. This worked very well but the nights would often be late by the time this plus sightings of stars had been taken to obtain the astronomical fixes.

By now we were right in the middle of The Grand Erg which when translated means large sea of sand and this was a perfect description. As far as one could see in all directions there was nothing but a maze of endless rolling dunes. These constantly gave the impression of absolute obstacles but we always eventually managed to weave a way through or climb over when no way seemed possible.

On one day we registered forty miles but only progressed little more than ten in the true direction of our goal. This continued for three days during which we sometimes had to travel away from our goal to avoid particularly difficult ranges of dunes which blocked our way. Driving through such terrain was a new experience. Often it was impossible to tell whether we were going uphill, downhill or on the flat due to having no datum point. Only the engine gave an indication and this often lied due to passing through particularly soft sections of sand. It was necessary to run fast at a dune face to reach the top in one attempt and there was always the risk of not stopping and sliding in a precarious manner down the steep face on the other side. On one occasion a vehicle was driven straight into a dune face which caused it to shed its ladders. It had been just impossible to see it as the horizon and dunes often merged and everything between became a brown matrix with no contrasts from which to pick out any detail. When vehicles became stuck it was a case of hard struggling usually managing to get an alloy ladder below the wheels to drive off only to have to repeat the process a few yards further on in many cases.

It was certainly no difficult task to sleep at night! For three and a half days we lived in another world where all was sand and sky and where the adrenalin would often flow fast. It is perhaps put into perspective by a verse taken from one members diary as follows......

Moonlit dunes that stretch As far as imagination will allow Dunes and sand: valleys of hard "flying" sand as Vehicles sing along the packed Granules. Throw over the wheel and luckily The soft grey 'cement' is missed Yet again. Mind bending Expanses of sand that flow to The earth's curvature. Ridge Dunes with flying 'spray' Coming off the Cornices and Making driving more difficult Down wind where you've Found a col to lurch the Vehicle through. A ridge ahead - scream up To the top and hopefully the drop On the other side isn't too bad. A quick brake, and then slide - Pulses quicken - adrenalin flowing The vehicle dips, nose into Oblivion, Sky disappears over the roof Line - and your gone, down the Slope - second gear - metal and cogs Racing, finishing, bending - foot on the Throttle and you're down the slope And away fighting the steering Wheel as the drive wants to go Its own way. And then a mile away Its another ridge and more hard Work and sweat and tears and swearing And cursing as vehicles refuse to go where the drivers want them. Bilma Sands - an experience to end All. Something to always remember, to Relive, but most difficult to Recount.

Having completed this vehicle section of the expedition we rendezvoused with three climbers back at Tamanrasset from which one vehicle with three non-climbing members returned to U.K. The remaining vehicle with six members drove towards the Tahifet Region, being a very remote point to the south east of Tamanrasset, and drove into the area as far as was possible going cross country with the vehicle. Here we set up vehicle base camp and then trecked in a further 9 miles on foot to set up our advance base camp from where we explored the mountain range. We had to carry all water from a point three miles short of advance camp and it took us four days to stock up the camp with equipment and food.

Having spent time in some of the most beautiful mountains imaginable, we returned to the main Hoggar circuit where, notably, the French and Italians have put up a series of classic climbs. We repeated almost all of these and added three particularly good new routes. Practically all of the fortyone existing routes we did were first time British Ascents.

George FlanaganM. Claustre

GEORGE IS UPSET !

George Piddington

Dear Editor,

I would like to write through the medium of the Belgravian about my feelings and disgust at those who let the side down by not bothering to turn up at the Crystal Palace Jubilee 25 x 1 mile relay on Sunday June 19th.

As you know I ran in the Vets. team and it really hurt me and I was very upset for days afterwards to learn that had we finished a complete team of 25 (I think we only managed 22) we would have been the fastest vets. team in the country beating our nearest rivals by over 8 minutes.

I think the persons concerned know who they are so I will not embarrass them by having their names mentioned, but they can be sure of feeling guilty next time they line up for the start of a club race.

I feel very sorry for the officials in our club who put in a great deal of hard work to get teams together for this great event which can only come once in a lifetime.

It does not require much effort to either phone or drop a line to those concerned to say if they could not make it, so a replacement can be nominated.

I worked as usual in a night club (following a full days work) on the Friday evening arriving home 3.30a.m. Saturday morning. I did a 10 miles training run on the Saturday afternoon and was back in the club during the evening arriving home at 3.30a.m. Sunday morning, to catch a few hours sleep before getting up at 8 o'clock. I then drove up to Crystal Palace (from Hayling Island) very proud of the fact that I was taking part in such an event and wearing the colours of such a great club as the Bels.

One of those who did not turn up had the excuse that he was tired while another was seen to be training that very day elsewhere, not far from Crystal Palace. Another who was supposed to be injured still went out training. Are they ashamed to wear club colours, or even run for the club?

I feel that if a fine of some sort was imposed for everyone that does not notify the officials when they cannot run after having entered for an event they would soon think twice before doing it again. It cost the club £35 to enter the teams for the race.

I think that I have made my point clear and what was to have been an event which I could look back on in future years as a proud participant has now been shattered by a mindless few who also let down 20 odd other runners in their team and the hardworking officials.

George Piddington.

Bill Laws

JUNE 19th 1977 - CRYSTAL PALACE.

SILVER JUBILEE RELAY

Departing from the popular 100 x 1 mile format Blackheath Harriers celebrated Silver Jubilee year by reducing all teams to 25 stages of 1 mile each. The correspondingly large increase in the number of teams meant more runners on the track at any particular time and at Crystal Palace 24 teams took to the tartan in the morning and 20 of the faster teams in the afternoon.

Belgrave entered a Vets. and Under 17 team in the morning and an A & B team in the afternoon. Perhaps the bigger disappointment was in the Vets. team where, due to the non-appearance of 3 vets. doubling up became necessary. The running of two teams at a time put a strain on the handful of Belgrave officials who turned up to help out and it was almost inevitable that something would go wrong. Steve Williams was the unfortunate one - he hadn't stripped off ready to take stage 14 for the A team and his time represents some kind of record for running to the change-over point and doing a striptease while running the first 2 laps.

Gerry Deegan became the fastest ever Belgravian over 3,000 metres at Crystal Palace on May 18th. In the Philips Silver Jubilee Night of Athletics, he placed 7th in 7:59.1. Our sprinters were superb. Ernest Obeng swept away our complicated 100 metres list with a devastating 10.6 seconds and then started our 4 x 100 metres relay squad off to another Belgrave best - 42.3 secs - thus erasing that 1954 A.A.A. winning time from the record books.

With our sprinters faster than any who have competed in claret and gold before, Mark Shippen now set about making himself highest ever Belgravian by tackling 4.21 metres in the pole vault. He had already smashed by 15 centimetres his own junior and youth marks of 4.00 metres to win the event but now some doubt existed as to the height of our senior record. Was it 4.19m.....4.20m perhaps? No one seemed to know for sure so Mark had the bar set at 4.21m. He wasn't too far away and it will not be long before he does beat that senior best of 4.18 metres.

Nice running came from another of our youngsters Dave Bailey. His 400 metres showed just a hint of what this boy could be capable of with regular training.

JULY 30th 1977 - CROYDON - SOUTHERN LEAGUE MATCH 5.

A day to forget

This was the day when everything went wrong for the Bels. Dropped relay batons, runners pulling out in mid race, members turning up to support and finding themselves toeing the starting line - it all happened for our penultimate match. Luckily the clubs placed behind us in the league table were some points down so even with only two match points gained after a couple of hours of trials and tribulations our overall league position remained unchanged - 8th.

The appearance of Glyn Ratcliffe's huge frame gave the Bels something to enthuse over but surprisingly even he, the Army Champion, was beaten in the discus. It just goes to show the standard of competition these days.

At long last Ernest Obeng's unbeaten run came to an end. He took the 100 metres for his 19th consecutive league win but his young North London opponent had pressed him hard and in the longer sprint the decision was reversed.

Bels finish with a flourish

AUGUST 20th 1977 - BATTERSEA PARK - SOUTHERN LEAGUE MATCH 6.

Bels finish with a flourish

"We start as we mean to go on and we finish as well as we start but something always seems to go wrong in the middle of the season." Our Field Events Secretary has never said truer words than these, spoken after our exciting win in the final match.

Battersea Park, not previously known for its fine athletic facilities had never looked better. Brand new equipment was on show everywhere: pole vault stands and landing beds, a hammer/discus cage, new high jump stands, all-weather javelin run-up, a cleaned up shot putt area and even a proper "laps-to-go" sign, all were there for our use thanks to the G.L.C. Parks Dept. (and the regular harrassment of the Parks Dept. by our Committee).

The javelin run-up gave Peter Crosbie a boost as he launched his second longest throw ever and another to take full advantage of the new equipment was Mark Shippen. After winning at 4 metres Mark had the bar set at 4.20m in another attempt to make the Belgrave Senior Club Record his own. He cleared on his third try....only to dislodge the bar with his arm on the way down.

The trackmen, of course, were still competing on the sluggish Battersea cinders but Ernest Obeng does not seem to worry what surface he runs on. He zipped to 10.9 in the 100 metres and with a winning lead of four tenths one could easily understand how he clocked 10.5 during the week on an all-weather track. Jim Evans backed up as reliably as ever in the 'B' sprints.

John Bicourt turned out to give us a 5000m/1500m double and Ian Duncan, fitting into our team very nicely, scored well in the 'B' 1500, but again the most exciting race was the 4 x 400 metres relay. With our team looking for a fourth member, Ernest Obeng was cajoled into running his first ever 400. Jamie Stevenson, suffering from a cold, handed over near the front but soon Ernie was loping along in the rear, coolly taking his pace from the other runners. When they hit the straight he exploded into action and gave Reg Hopkins a slight lead. While Ernie recovered from "rubbery legs" the race see-sawed back and forth until Charlie Gillett again took the claret and gold to the front of the tightly bunched group with only 50 metres left. Ilford's man pipped him on the line to finally win the race, as thrilling as any international.

This team victory leap-frogged us over Blackheath H in the league chart for a final 7th place among the 25 clubs in Division 1.

SEPTEMBER 3rd 1977 - WEST LONDON STADIUM - LONDON A.A. CHAMPIONSHIPS

In the GLC Championships our ace sprinters were again to the fore. Ernie Obeng was a clear winner of the 100 metres in 10.6, two tenths clear of a "blanket-finish" for second place. International Les Hoyte (Thames Valley) was awarded the silver medal with our Jim Evans in 5th place but sharing the same time, 10.8, as Hoyte. It was an equal personal best for Jim.

Ernie handed another defeat to Hoyte in the 200m although this time they were both given the same time of 21.7, whilst 5th place again went to Jim with 22.6.

Jamie Stevenson placed 6th over 800m in 1:58.8 and Belgrave finished up third for the Club Trophy with 12 points to Thames Valley's 32 and Shaftesbury's 23.
