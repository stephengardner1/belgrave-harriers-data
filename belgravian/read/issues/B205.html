Editorial · page 3

HAPPY TIMES

It is possible to say, without fear of contradiction, that the last 6 months have seen the most rapid and significant upswing in the affairs of Belgrave for the last 20 years. Indeed there are some who would rate our progress higher and say that our advances on all fronts have no previous comparison.

So to the facts. Our mens track and field team has gained entry into the British League. Our ladies team has been transformed from a depleted side at the start of the season into a match winning combination at the end. More Club records have been broken than in any previous season by both men and ladies. Our strength is in our youngsters and we have the unique situation of 2 juniors heading the U.K. Mens Junior T and F rankings at the blue ribbon events of the 800 and 1500 m plus a new U.K. 4 x 1 mile Junior record. And to ice the cake Ernie Obeng and Paul Blagg competed at Brisbane in the Commonwealth Games. Our walkers as usual performed at the highest levels.

Towards the end of the season Surrey AC mens section decided to go into liquidation and the majority of the athletes and officials have joined with us to forge a stronger management and athletics teams for the years ahead. They are doubly welcome. And to cap it all Battersea Park Track is to be dug up in December 1982 and will be resplendant with one of the Country's finest all-weather surfaces in time for the next track season.

The Gods are smiling on us. We deserve it.

Ernie ObengPaul Blagg

Welcome — THE "AMALGAMATION" - SURREY AC MEN JOIN BELGRAVE.

Many of you will have seen the controversial correspondence in "Athletics Weekly" about the amalgamation of ex Surrey AC members into Belgrave, their participation in our British League Promotion match and the resulting adverse reaction from a few individuals and teams. Confused? Well these are the facts. Belgrave was approached at the beginning of the T and F season by officials of London AC and Surrey AC who explained that both clubs were experiencing difficulty in finding officials and putting out league teams. Their proposal was to form a new Club with Belgrave. The matter was to be put to E.G.M's of the 3 Clubs but the plan fell at the first hurdle when LAC rejected the idea. At the Surrey AC meeting the ladies voted against joining Belgrave but the men could not raise enough officials and decided to close down and amalgamate with Belgrave subject to a full integration of officials and athletes into Belgrave's affairs. The Surrey men were welcomed into our ranks and already have made a positive impact by strengthening our administrative and athletic activities. The British and Southern Leagues were informed that as from August 31st 1982, Surrey men would join with Belgrave. At about the same time Belgrave learnt that it had qualified in 8th place for the British League qualifying match which was held at Luton on September 4th. Eight of the Surrey men wished to compete and after clearance in writing from the Southern Counties AAA's we ran them and won. Immediately the British League invited us into Div.5, we accepted and then the problems started. Many people could not accept that our actions were legal. The Secretary of the Southern League met with Senior Belgrave Officials and asked us to withdraw in favour of Windsor, S. and Eton. We felt we were in the right and so Windsor - backed by the Southern League - put a motion to the AGM of the British League demanding our withdrawal. Derek Crookes and Bill Laws went along to the AGM on October 30th and argued the Belgrave case. The debate was fair and wide ranging and only 1 club (a south London one) argued strongly in favour of the motion. After a 1½ hour debate a vote was taken. 28 were in favour of Belgrave and 5 against (Haringay, Hillingdon, Herne Hill, Leeds and Met. Police). A compromise solution was then put by the BAL executive and that was to include a 7th team (Windsor) in Div.5 for the next season only. After a card ballot it was agreed to include Windsor by 18-15 and everyone breathed a sigh of relief. To quote Wellington. "It was a damned close run thing". And so in 1983 the eyes of the BAL will be on Belgrave. Its up to you the athletes to show them how good you are. Your Club will give you every possible encouragement and support.

Derek CrookesBill LawsWellington

MONEY MATTERS — 1981/82 Accounts.

With so much demand for financial support for our teams, for coaching, as well as for developments at Belgrave Hall and Battersea Park Track it was with great anticipation that the treasurer's report at our AGM on Sept. 16th was so eagerly awaited.

In masterful style which would have done justice to the AGM of a large Corporation Jim Heathfield took us step by step through the not insignificant sums which spelt out our financial achievements for the year. Subscriptions were up to a new record of £2,779 which when backed by other fund raising monies increased income to £4,356. Expenditure, however, increased to £4,179 so that the current account surplus for the year dropped to £177 from the previous year's £916. The two main items of expenditure were:- Printing, stationery and postage at £1,463 and Team fees and expenses at £1,123. So once again let it be noted that subscriptions in no way meet the running costs of the Club.

The separate Belgrave Hall accounts presented a much rosier picture with total income reaching £12,732 offset by purchases and expenditure totalling £9,111 to give a nett profit of £3,621. Thus the overall Club profit for the year increased to £3,798 which helped to swell our cash in hand for our development programme to a robust £10,439.

This healthy state of affairs is the result of efforts by those seemingly few hard working members who put your Club at the top of their priorities. There is so much to be done in the year ahead that everyone's help will be required. So please - examine what you did for your colleagues last year. If it wasn't a lot resolve to help and support other people's efforts in the year ahead. Remember we have plenty of members only too willing to give advice. The ones we cherish are those who believe in deeds - not words.

Jim Heathfield

GEOFF DAVIS - JOINT PRESIDENT

GEOFF DAVIS was elected Joint President with Derek Crookes at the A.G.M. in September having joined Belgrave following the Marriage with the men from Surrey A.C. where he was President.(For an appreciation of Derek Crookes refer to the Oct. 1981 issue of the BELGRAVIAN ).

Like so many members, Geoff began his athletic career while at school and first hit the headlines in 1948 when he won the English Schools Boy's 220 yard Championship in 22.9 which was then an English Schools' record. Also while he was at school he was the British ATC National champion over both the short sprints in 1948 and 1949. Although living in the heart of Berkshire he joined Polytechnic Harriers and ran for Poly for a number of years. He won no less than 7 Berkshire A.A.A. titles at 100, 220 and 440 yards in the early 1950's and was a member of the Polytechnic 4 x 110 and 4 x 440 relay teams which includeded Macdonald Bailey, Arthur Wint and Brian Shenton, and which won A.A.A. titles. Running on the Poly track at Chiswick he won the SCAAA Junior 220 yards title in 1950 and 4 years later he was third in the Senior event at the same distance. At National level he took 3rd and 4th places in the junior A.A.A. 100 and 220 yards Championship at Birmingham in 1949 and followed this up in 1950 with 3rd in the 220 yards. He moved up to the 440 yard event in 1954, figured well in the National rankings in this event the following year and narrowly missed selection for the 1956 Melbourne Olympics. He represented the A.A.A's the Royal Air Force and Combined Services in representative matches and also competed in France, Holland, Belgium and Germany. When his competitive days were over he turned his attention to the administrative side of athletics and was Secretary of the Insurance A.A. for some years before joining Surrey A.C. in 1970 initially to support his daughter Nicola who won Surrey County honours in both Long Jump and 200m. He was for almost 10 years Secretary of Surrey A.C. and has been Team Manager for both their young athletes and senior sides. He is currently a Vice-President of Surrey A.A.A. and team manager for the Surrey County juniors and youths and a number of Belgravians will already know him through his association with the County. Under his leadership the Surrey Youths have won the annual Inter-Counties event for the last 3 years while the Juniors narrowly failed to complete a double hat-trick this year by a handful of points. He is particularly interested in developing the talents of young athletes and has encouraged many youngsters to develop into good senior athletes. Geoff is a married man with 2 daughters and a son and despite his youthful appearance has 4 grandchildren! In 1981 he qualified as a Track Referee and this year he refereed the Thorn E.M.I. Video International meeting commemorating the B.A.A.B. Jubilee 1932-1982 at Crystal Palace.

In Memorium

Gordon Doubleday

We have recently mourned the deaths of three of our oldest members, their combined membership exceeded 170 years! Each played a part in the affairs and fortunes of the Club, and, in regretting their passing we pay tribute to their memories.

CHARLIE SPEECHLEY. Probably the oldest active athlete in the country he was taking part in Vets sprint handicaps until fairly recently. About two or three years ago he fell from a ladder severely lacerating his legs, but he carried on with his legs in bandages. During his later years his eyesight was beginning to fail but this did not prevent him carrying out his activities. He was 94 years of age and joined us in 1919. He was also a keen swimmer and in 1908 he took part in the diving trials for the Olympic Games.

His athletic hey-day, as a walker, was between the two World Wars and he took part regularly in Club and Open Handicaps and in Team Events. In 1933 he was a member of the team which won the Nijmegen Shield - awarded to the first Club to finish its complete team of 8 in the R.W.A. 20 miles Championship (now 35 kilos).

BERT FOOTER. Without doubt the finest C.C. Captain we ever had is the unanimous opinion of those who knew Bert - indeed some would say that no Club ever had a better one. He was born in 1904 and joined us in 1922 and trained hard and became Club Champion.

His prowess continued and he became County Champion and on one occasion he won the Southern Championship. It became his ambition to lead the Bels to a win in the "National" (E.C.C.U) title. For many years Birchfield H. had monopolized this race, gradually the Bels crept up the placings, third, second and, then on that glorious day in 1935 at Beaconsfield, he led the team to collect the winners medals, this was repeated in 1939 at Manchester.

He gained two International Vests. In 1933 he was fourth home in the England (the winners) colours at Caerleon, and the following year he did the same again at Ayr - fourth man home with England winning again.

In 1939 he set the marathon as his target, he had already made a name for himself at road races up to ten miles. He ran in the Poly Windsor to Chiswick, and, in one of the toughest races of his career he finished third.

With the outbreak of World War II he joined the Marines and got involved in many tight situations. After the war he resumed his running, but others were coming along and he retired. He moved to Ramsgate and ran a small business. He developed arthritis which gradually worsened and eventually his general health deteriorated.

A number of his old C.C. team mates attended his funeral to say "farewell" to a tough little man - a true C.C. type for whom gates, ditches, obstacles and "plough" held no terrors.

ALF TAYLOR. A regular member of our cross country teams of the thirties which figured so prominently in the Southern and National Championships. He was a member of our winning teams in the Southern (1934) and the National (1935) as well as the London to Brighton Relay where he ran the six miles stage from Handcross to Hickstead. He was also a staunch Counties man and represented Middlesex on a number of occasions.

In paying tribute to these three, typical good sportsmen of their era, we record our pleasure at having known them, not only for their athletic achievements but as friendly people whose company and memories we will treasure.

Gordon Doubleday.

BERT FOOTER

Arthur Penny (Nutty)

BERT FOOTER who died on the 23rd. April was a dedicated Belgravian,achieved International Status and was a personality in his own right. A superb Club Captain and a leader of great spirit. That is how I shall remember him.

I shall remember that when I joined Belgrave in 1928 Bert was already there,small in stature but a giant in dedication. He was the driving force behind all we achieved in the 30's, a man who was never beaten until the tape was broken. His successes were too numerous to list here but suffice to say his name was a household name.

He leaves a widow and a daughter who had looked after him for years,for he was crippled with arthritis and had great difficulty in walking. Bert has now passed on. May he rest in peace.

Arthur Penny (Nutty)

WALKING

Carl Lawton

Autumn leaves - that sums up the situation of the past few months. We won the National 10 mile, were 4th in the 20 kms. 2nd in the 35 kms. and 3rd in the 50 kms, but when the longer events came, except for the Dawlish, things were not so good. We were beaten in the Hastings,and did not start a team in the Brighton or National 100 kms. We would have won all Nationals if ..... But with so many fixtures these days, we cannot expect much else.

Congratulations must go to Paul Blagg for his selection for the Commonwealth Games. By the time of reading this it will all be over and whether he succeeded or failed at least he got there which for many of us is a "pipe dream". Also congratulations to John Dunsford for completing the 100 miles at Leicester and thus joining the ranks of Centurions. John began having problems before 50 miles but managed to struggle on.

The fact that we did not start a 100 kms.team means that we drop down in the senior rankings from our 2nd place. Next year I believe we can win the Mc Sweeney again, but unfortunately relies on one thing,co-operation from all club members. We now have a small girls section and the possibility of getting a senior girls side back on the road again. Obviously our failing is the U/20 mens side. Here we fall down on two fronts, lack of numbers and lack of help. Have I said enough!

I have already, via a separate letter, requested that all semi and fully active walkers make an effort to compete regularly during the winter and I should now like to request that the non-active ones step forward and offer their services for the Belgrave 7 as pointsmen, recorders etc. and not have to rely on members from other sections who are already overloaded.

WITH THE LADIES

Derek Jones

It has been a really exciting and extremely rewarding season. At the beginning of the year the whole future of the section looked very bleak indeed. Although we had a good nucleus of Seniors we had only a handful of Juniors. However, the crisis resolved itself with an influx of juniors, all from one school to begin with, (Walsingham) and later in the season from other schools in the Wandsworth and Battersea area - notably from Elliots, Mayfield, Battersea County and Notre Dame. Useful contacts have been forged and there is some very good athletic talent around the area that is, as yet, untapped.

Highlight of the year must be the 3rd placing in the Inter Ladies 200m Final in the Womens A.A.A. Championships by Jackie Harman. Jackie also placed 4th in the 100m Final and set club records in the Heats. Unfortunately, Jackie was unable to compete in any of our Southern League matches this year.

The Senior Relay Squad are now Southern Counties Relay Champions,unfortunately because only Belgrave showed up on the day, but the squad equalled the club record. In the Southern League Div.3 N.E. we finished in 8th place. This was a very good position to attain and makes us hopeful for the future. We hope to be able to get to the 2nd Division in 1983. Our 8th position was achieved without the benefit of a home match. The fact that some of our new youngsters had only the benefit of a months training before being thrown in at the deep end says a lot for their character and it was good to see them all doing their best for Belgrave. Just to emphasise how well everyone has done, Club Records have been equalled or improved on over 20 occasions and personal best performances have been set on numerous occasions (at least 30 in our last 3 Southern League matches.)

Good performances were put up by Wendy Dunsford, who had a steady season with the Shot and Discus. Wendy was 3rd in the Senior Ladies Shot in the Middlesex Championships and 3rd in the Inter Banks Track and Field Championships. In the Javelin, Veronica St. Ange came 3rd in the Senior Ladies Javelin event in the Surrey Championships, and 3rd in the London Boroughs Championships throwing for Croydon.

Good performances throughout the season too, from Jackie Barclay, Earlene Murrell, Dawn McNeil, Glenda Mahoney, and Tina Jordan. When they were available, newcomer Brenda Davis and veteran Pamela Davis gave very good accounts of themselves. Of the other Newcomers who have distinguished themselves Nikki King, Shirley Daure, Maude Okuley, Nana Nyarko have all given good performances and it is a pity that Pam Skeete was not always able to come to meetings.

Turning to the Juniors, I would just like to praise Inoyo Etete, who joined us as a Shot Putter and Discus thrower but who was converted into a very good sprinter. Inoyo won the 200m and Shot in the Wandsworth Schools and won the shot in the London Schools Championships in her age group (by well over a metre) as well as anchoring her school relay team and Wandsworth Relay team.

Notable performances from other Juniors have come from Jessica Palmer, Janice Porter, Lydia Montoute (who in her 3rd run for the club broke the Junior 200m record and also equalled the Junior 100m record and beat the Senior high jump record) Tracey Johnson, Audrey Clovey, Suzanne Jordan, Gail Porter, Sharon Martins, Janet Johnson , Diane Williams Lucia Spinelli and Alex MacLean. All this could not have been possible without help, and I have had some excellent assistance from Frank Simmons, Harry Porter, Joann Warnett and Jackie Barclay. A few more people like those mentioned are needed down at Battersea Park. Come on Belgravians and parents - where are you? Come along and get stuck in and help. Every section needs help. Not just us.

To the Girls I would say this. It is Commonwealth Games Year. In four years time these Games will be in Britain. Work hard and do as you are told and it could be you who will be there. Well done all of you.

Ladies Secretary Derek Jones

Report · page 21

ALL WEATHER TRACK AT BATTERSEA

At last, it really is going to happen. Battersea Park is to have an all-weather track with improved jumping and throwing facilities. And the deadline is not years hence but right now. Indeed, if you pull out your copy of this issue of THE BELGRAVIAN over the Christmas holiday work will have already begun on digging up the running surface. . And so how has this happened so quickly? Well to find out and to put the course of events on record we interviewed our 'Special Projects Man' - Bill Laws - who single-handed has negotiated the deal with the G.L.C.

Talk of developing Battersea Park track has been going on for at least the last 10 years. Hopes were high in 1974 when the London and South East Region of the Sports Council published its forward strategy for developing athletics in London. In that document 14 tracks were highlighted as being in need of special development and some of them would have greater importance as Regional Stadia with good spectator facilities. Battersea Park figured high in the list and we sat back and waited. Year by year developments occured, not at Battersea, but at Copthall, New River, West London, Southwark Park and so on. Year by year Belgrave officials visited the G.L.C. Parks Department to encourage progress and on occasions Parks Officials were guests at our Annual Dinner and Dance. And still nothing happened. It became clear that our big drawback was the G.L.C. itself. All the other tracks were owned by Local Authorities and many of them were prepared to put up substantial sums of money to develop athletics and enrich local community life. Not so with Wandsworth Council - they did not own the track and that was the end of it. It was up to the G.L.C.

Desperate to see some progress Bill Laws was asked by the Belgrave Committee to see whether some way through could be found. Bill was keen to have a go because his pet scheme - The Masters Coaching Scheme - was not firing on all cylinders partly ,due to many of our better ahtletes not wishing to train on the famous 'pudding basin'. The boost of an all-weather surface was obvious. So Bill approached the permanent officers of the G.L.C. once again. After some delving it became clear that a master development plan involving the track did exist. This plan, if implemented, would have cost around £9 million and involved the construction of new football pitches, a multi-sport indoor centre, direct access to Chelsea Bridge Road and the re-siting of the track on an axis 60 degrees from the present one. When pressed for starting dates it was pointed out that total G.L.C. funding was impossible, external private money would be needed and that at the earliest 1987 would be the start date.

Not content with this Bill prepared counter proposals based on upgrading the existing facility for a comparatively modest £350,000. The response from the Bureaucrates? Nothing. OK try again. This time the document was sent to the London and South East Region of the Sports Council. A good welcome from Anthony Walters. How could he help - with money? Great. Next to the Wandsworth Sports Council. Yes they would back it in principle but no money. The crucial breakthrough was to contact one of the influential and financially powerful elected Members Committees of the G.L.C. - The Arts and Recreation Committee - chaired by Tony Banks with Peter Pitt as Vice Chairman. Tony seemed to lead the Arts side with an annual ad-hoc budget of £4 million while Peter was busily trying to attract that sort of money in support of the Sports side. The time was the Brixton riots and suddenly the quality of inner city life was a political hot potato. Peter Pitt was looking for projects. Belgrave had a good one and what's more we had a good record of helping youngsters as a result of the Masters Coaching Scheme.

It was agreed Bill would make a presentation and to further smooth the way he lobbied the two Wandsworth Councillors on the G.L.C. Arts and Recreation Committee. The plan was quickly approved in principle - it was now up to the full time officers to cost it out. Specifications and budget estimates were prepared. The Sports Council would give some financial help. It was all systems go. From this point on liaison with the G.L.C. became first class. Plans were received and circulated within Belgrave and many useful comments and requests made, many of which have been incorporated in the final scheme.

By August 1982 the G.L.C. had written formally announcing its intention to proceed and in the same month we informed both the Southern and British Leagues in the hope we would get home fixtures in 1982. The only possible drawback would be if the quotes to the final specification which was issued in September 1982 exceeded the budget figure.

It was with some relief we learned that by the closing date of mid-October at least 5 tenders had been received at prices below the final estimates. A big sigh of relief all round. Work is to start in mid-December 1982 and is to be completed by May 1983. And Bill's reactions? "I'm glad that's over. It does show what can be done in 1 year when everyone's focussed up. The G.L.C. and the sports Council can be proud of this one." So are we.

THE 1908 MARATHON.

The 1908 London Olympic Marathon is famous for the collapse of Italian Dorando Pietri. He did actually cross the line first but having been "helped" by the officials, who guided him as he staggered over the last metres, he was inevitably disqualified.

However, that marathon also has other claims to fame. The race had been scheduled to be run over 40km (24 miles 1,504 yards) but the distance from the East Terrace of Windsor Castle to the White City Stadium proved to be 26 miles and a further 385 yards were required to reach the Royal Box. This was the distance finally adopted the world over - 42.195kms.

The competitors themselves must have been the best fed and sweetest smelling marathoners of all time. At four points along the route runners were offered rice puddings, raisins, bananas, milk, soda, Oxo - and eau-de-Cologne.

TRACK AND FIELD

LEO COY, Track and Field Secretary

In trying to summarise the 1982 Track and Field season one would need this entire issue of The Belgravian. It proved to be our best season after a number of years in the doldrums. Why did this happen? It was not entirely unexpected as we had aimed to consolidate our southern league position this year and hoped to qualify for the British League in 1983, but we obviously got our predictions wrong!

There were many reasons why the track and field side made such impressive progress and I shall deal with each in turn. Derek Williams and John Aska were elected Track and Field Captains respectively and they fulfilled these roles admirably. Derek gave top class performances every time he competed and John played a vital role in our success all season and especially at Luton, even though he did not always compete himself.

A large number of athletes turned out time after time and gave a very solid base to all our teams. Among them were Mark Shippen - how good to see him increase his club PU record at the end of the season; Steve Samson; Mark L. Bayley - who commuted from Scotland to be at every League match, Pat Lyttle; Snowy - now back in the BL after 10 years; Terry Welsh - our remaining resident Irish Guard;Paul Whitby - who intends to commute from Scotland next season.

YOUTHS AND BOYS

As the results for our maturing Juniors have been reported in the main Track and Field Section the following relates to the achievements of the colts boys and youths.

Ian Horton was unable to devote a great amount of time to the youngsters in the summer and this combined with the fact that we have been unsuccessful in attempts to get into any of the Young Athletes Leagues has meant that all of our colts, boys and youths have had to find competition elsewhere. Letter · page 45

Clive Shippen

Dear Bill, For Belgravian purposes I thought you might like to know that I called on Ben Knifton while I was holidaying at Albufeira, Portugal.

Ben retired to the Algarve with his second wife, Peggy, about 12 years ago and they have an apartment at the highest point overlooking the sea. Now 74 years old he is as fit and active as ever - swimming three or four times a week for all but two months of the year. He also travels quite a bit and a few days after my visit was due to bus it down to Algecires and hop across the Med for a 9 days spell in Morocco!

Ben, of course, was very active in the Bels walking section and frequently second in our championship winning teams. He was also the second editor of The Belgravian, serving his stint between 1927 and 1929.

Seeing Ben also brought me up to date on the other Knifton, John. "Young" John is now 42 years old and still living in Texas where he emigrated back in the mid-sixties after gaining a degree in Chemistry. He since obtained a Ph.D and is now Deputy Director of Research for Texaco and travels worldwide speaking at international symposia. Following several such visits to Europe he was recently elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Chemistry. He has a teenage son - a big strapping fellow who is a fine swimmer and footballer (American rules) - and a daughter.

John will be remembered in Belgrave for his outstanding performances as a Junior and as a National title-holder. We did not see him at his best as a Senior, but the Americans did. Between 1970 and 1980 he won no less than 13 National walking titles between 3 km (track) and 50 km. He was also desparately unlucky to miss the U.S. Olympic team in 1976 when he suffered badly from the heat in the trial - a rare bad performance.

Now a veteran, John is as active as ever and, apart from competing in open walking events, still with some success, he has also turned to running daily. He recently ran 4 marathons in 4 weeks clocking 3.03, 2.57, 2.55 and 2.53, having already tried his hand at the fast stuff with 34.00 for a track 10 km!

Ben and John send their best wishes to all Belgravians and follow their progress with great interest. Sincerely, Clive Shippen.

Report · page 46

SOCIAL — BELGRAVE HALL RUNS DRY !

No, not a case of the bar running dry but almost as bad. Over the last few weeks there has been a substantial increase in the number of people training from the Hall on a Sunday morning. Packs of up to 40 seniors is not an unusual sight. Add to this the fast Junior pack and over 20 of our very young members and it will be appreciated that in the space of over 1 hour around 100 people can descend into the showers. Of course the most enjoyable part of a winter's run is the time in the showers which is generally spent on catching up with the latest gossip!

Well that's all very well but of late there has been a great deal of important matters to discuss and the net result is that our fuel bills have gone up and even more importantly the roof water tank which supplies the whole boiler system sometimes runs dry. When this happens beware!! Bill Couzens-our Hall impresario- may be seen running around in ever decreasing circles uttering strange sounds known only to those who have experienced the traumas of trying to organise anything at Belgrave.

What are the possible solutions? Well we could gag Brian Gorman. We could issue smaller bits of soap but the only suitable solution will be to install a new 3000 gallon tank in the roof. This is a major undertaking bearing in mind the weight involved and it will only be possible when we start work on the development of the Hall. And when is that likely to be? Well, hopefully next year. We understand that as a result of the improvement in our finances the BELGRAVE HALL DEVELOPMENT FUND now stands at over £10,000.
