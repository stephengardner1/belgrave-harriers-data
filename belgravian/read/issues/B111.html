Editorial

YOUR EDITORS

Dear Fellow Belgravians,

We, your Editors, are inclined to give ourselves a little praise. It would appear that our Editorials are not only read, but digested.

We quote briefly from our Editorial of the April, 1950, issue—"And now for a subject that is causing some people concern—our defeats over the country . . . Let us not worry unduly . . . and now that we have been vanquished, shall we say temporarily, let us turn about and endeavour with all the enthusiasm we can muster to regain once again our former premiership . . . No, we are not dead yet, let us say a little unwell. Let us not try to find the cause, but certainly let us find the cure . . . Let us look upon our recent defeats in the nature of a tonic. The cure will be completed by 100 per cent. Club Spirit."

Will you look up the April, 1950, issue and re-read our Editorial? We are sure, by recent results, that it did some good and that the re-reading will do even more good. This season we have won every club match, the Surrey County Championship and South of the Thames Championship—and—in spite of the doubts of many, our lads rose magnificently to the occasion and confounded the wiseacres by being narrowly beaten to second place in the "Southern."

We, your Editors, who are, or rather were, walkers, but yet true Belgravians, will be forgiven if in our ignorance of cross-country running are still enthusiastic about our chances, but we do contend that any club who can win the Surrey County Championship, the South of the Thames and gain second place in the Southern, MUST stand a reasonable chance in the National Championship.

It may be that by the time this is in print the National Championship will have been decided, but we are sure that whether or not our forecast has been proved correct, our lads will have once again shown that they are endeavouring to take us once more to the front in the field of Cross Country Running.

Even so, re-read our quoted Editorial and carry on with a successful cure.

YOUR EDITORS.

P.S.—Had it not been for the unfortunate injury to Steve Charlton during the early part of the race, our forecast as above would have been almost realised. Nevertheless, we commend Steve for his courage and team spirit in finishing the course which enabled us to win the Ottway Cup.

Steve Charlton

In Memoriam

Norman Walker

It is with deepest sorrow that we have to report the death of Norman Walker, who passed away on Saturday, November 25th, at the early age of 24 years. Norman joined us in 1943 and was one of our most promising runners and certainly one of our most popular and likeable members. We found him a quiet and unassuming lad, who enjoyed his running, and he will be sadly missed by his many Belgrave friends. He represented the Club both on the track and over the country. He was a profound student of athletics and could discourse on almost any subject appertaining to our sport. A wreath was sent from the Club, and on Saturday, December 9th, on the day of our Championship, the President, Alf Harley, acquainted all present with the sad news and a reverent silence to the memory of Norman was observed.

We are sorry to report that A. M. Bassett, one of our promising young junior members, has been killed in a flying accident while serving with the R.A.F.

Progress Report

A. A. HARLEY, President

PROGRESS REPORT—By Our President

In the ast issue I referred to the past—now I would like to make comment on the present and the immediate future.

It is good to see the enthusiasm and high morale which has manifested itself this season among our cross country men and has given us excellent wins in so many championships and second place in the Southern Championship, ahead of the holders, Thames Valley Harriers, and a mere six points behind the winners. This, too, without some of our better known men, who were prevented from turning out! Hard luck was ours also in the Southern Youths Championship, in which our own Club Champion, owing to influenza, was unable to reproduce his normal form, which would undoubtedly have given us a team win instead of 6th place of the 34 finishing clubs. However, the results are not all that important—the spirit is good and in the old tradition, and with that excellent spirit all things are possible. It should follow from the Southern Senior Championship result that we should be among the placings in the "National" once again, as we were for a sequence of ten races until two years ago. We sincerely hope so: and on the London-Brighton Relay, although not a championship event, we may expect to be very near the front when Brighton is reached on April 7th.

Among our walkers, also, morale is high and if any club is to beat Sheffield United Harriers in the forthcoming 10 miles National Championship, we feel it is the Belgrave Harriers. We were within an ace of doing so last year, and with a stronger team this year may well bring back a title from Macclesfield on March 17th. Remembering the excellent walking of every Belgrave Walker in the R.W.A. 20 miles Championship last year, we can look forward confidently both to this event, the 50 kilometres in June and the London-Brighton in September. And whilst great deeds are being done at these distances, our track walkers will be showing us that at the shorter distances Belgrave still takes a lot of beating.

Our track runners and field events men will already be at their early training and we look for their loyal support and first-rate performances throughout the season from them. Our programme of Club track championships is one of the finest in the country and will demand the support of all members on the first Saturday in August: and from April until September we shall be looking for the standards which have given us so many Summer International athletes in the past. Loyal and enthusiastic co-operation with our Secretaries, Captains, Vice-Captains and Coaches in the spirit of Belgrave endeavour, which is such a real thing, should enable us to surprise even ourselves! Throughout the summer, too, our road runners will be carrying our colours in various parts of the country, and we hope they may excel themselves in the marathon events.

In general, let us remind ourselves that in the all-round-field of athletics, no club has, as yet, come anywhere near the performances that Belgrave can show. Let us be aware that in our numbers, in the quality of our membership, in our administration, our latent talent and proved abilities, our traditions, we have everything in good measure, that any club can desire. And let us resolve to apply to all these qualities with which we are so richly endowed, Enthusiasm, Determination, Perseverance and Intelligence—all in a spirit of good sportsmanship—and the name of Belgrave will stand even higher than it has done before! The youngest member has something to contribute, the oldest something of value to help us along, let everyone of us do his share.

A. A. HARLEY, President.

Socials—Or Fun And Games

Owing to occasional absences from town on business, Dave Martin Shepherd decided that it was not possible to carry on as Social Secretary at the last A.G.M. Dave carried out a very difficult job, a job which requires a cheerful spirit and smiling face to the customers, but one which has many worries, notably food and disappointments: these disappointments often occasioned by the apathy of those who the Social Secretary caters for and tries to please. Let us say most fervently, "Thank you, Dave," and we are pleased to learn that although not now Social Secretary you are still a pillar of strength to the social side of the Club.

In Tom Carter—our new Secretary pro tem.—we have one of dynamic character, one who gets his own way by his forceful personality, one full of ideas, one not afraid of asking for others' help and one who cares not a fig for the niceties of red tape, but just gets on with it. It may be that his methods are not always exactly au fait, but they certainly do provide the social side of the Club with a very definite social side.

As a result of the remarkable success of our last dinner, Tom arranged a little New Year's Eve function at the same venue for any Belgraves wishing to attend. It was unfortunate that only a few could take advantage of the facilities offered, but those that did certainly have reason to thank Tom for a really grand night, packed full of fun and with a real New Year spirit, and we can safely say that those who did attend impressed the rest of the company that they were members of B-E-L-G-R-A-V-E—Belgrave! Yes, it was a grand night and we hope Tom will arrange a repeat this year and for a larger number.

On Sunday, January 7th, the Club held a Children's Party. I am not quite certain of the exact number, but I should say about 70 members' children had a grand tea from a table daintily arranged with the attractive little things that make children's eyes glow. Whoever were responsible for the tables and the fare thereon are to be thanked and congratulated. After tea many willing helpers cleared up and got the children sitting around for an entertainment by a really clever conjuror, who certainly had a way with the children (of all ages) and who also presented, in my opinion, the grandest Punch and Judy show I have ever witnessed. Many growp-ups were entranced by the expressions on the children's faces during this show. Of course, Smith (Champ) Junior, had to present the "pièce de résistance" by disclosing the procedure at his home when relatives visit on Sundays and caused perhaps the biggest laugh. After the Punch and Judy show every child was given a present by Father Christmas. I don't think I am at liberty to disclose the identity of the said Father Christmas, but he was a very popular one with the children, and he will undoubtedly be asked to grow his whiskers again in time for the next party. Again, whoever chose the children's presents are to be congratulated on the very sensible choice for so many of so many ages. It was also pleasing to see "little" Bertie Footer enjoying himself with his toy motor car on a string. We must, too, thank all those who contributed towards the party, they are assured that their donations were well used.

Finally, on January 27th Tom Carter arranged our last social of the season. This was a Carnival Social and a real carnival it was. A goodly number sat down to tea, but great numbers turned up in the evening. There were streamers and cotton wool balls, hats and balloons and very jolly people, both young and old. In fact, so much did we enjoy ourselves that we asked Tom to extend the dancing to 11.30 p.m., and Tom, knowing so well that the assembly were enjoying themselves, decided to do this. Yes, we seem to be having some good times socially, but let us not forget to thank the many helpers, not only among the members, but also some of their womenfolk, and lastly, and certainly not least, our caretaker, Mrs. Savage, and her family.

After all this, Tom Carter still insists on being known as Social Secretary—pro tem.—or should it be sine die!

Cinema Show—And Straight Talking

F. H. ELSON

Belgrave Hall, February 17th

As a means of attracting the people for whom this "Pep" talk was intended, the film show seemed deemed to failure, but as a means of entertaining some sixty members from different sections of the Club it was definitely a success, and we do thank Charlie Jones for the arrangements.

A film of the 1950 European Games and of the 1950 Poly Marathon was shown and a running commentary was given by Charlie Jones. As I have already said, the show was very entertaining to those present and after listening to the talk afterwards, it became obvious to everybody that the Field Events Section was primarily affected, but on looking round we saw very few of those members present.

In presenting his list of moans, for moans they certainly were, Charlie Jones did not pull his punches. Let us be honest. It is very definite that with the exception of the very few stalwarts the Field Events Section is letting us down. We hate to admit it, but if we face up to it and manage to jar these laggards into a sense of duty to the Club of their choice we shall get better results. Those present were asked for any ideas for the improvement of the Club, but few were forthcoming. Is it because the members are so completely complacent or could it be that everything is perfect? I believe that many members have suggestions, but are a little timid in presenting them in person at a meeting. Let me say that if this is the case, as Editor, I would be very pleased to have them and either publish them or present them to the Committee. Mention was also made of non-replies to postcards sent by officials. Again I will be outright and say that this is not only unprincipled and rude, but also a waste, not only of Club officials' time, but also of Club money, which, in effect, is your property.

The coaches then had a little moan about late or non-appearance of their pupils. This again is a source of annoyance to those gentlemen who are prepared to give their knowledge and time to assist the young Club members and who should show their appreciation of the coach by turning up regularly and early. I was sorely tempted to intervene here, but decided to use my words as a winding-up of this report. As Walking Coach I think I am the only coach without a grouse. At the last coaching school I had a regular and very prompt attendance of nine walkers each evening. Again our Walking Captain and Secretary always receive answers to all postcards and letters. Our lads invariably turn up for all races, whether selected or not, and we are a very happy and chummy crowd. Perhaps it is because they have a very keen sense of duty to their Club and fellow Club members, and in closing I would recommend the Field Events Section to study our methods of clubship, and we shall soon be hearing praise once again for their section.

F. H. ELSON.

Highgate Harriers 7 Miles Open—October 28th

Congratulations are offered to Highgate Harriers on their successful Open Walk, successful not only from the organisation point of view, but also in competition. First man home in record time, first scratch team, first handicap team, is quite a good day's sport. A record entry for opens of 270, a record number of starters, 230, and a record number of finishers, 217, was a magnificent start to the season's Opens. Coleman got away to a very fast start, followed by our Harry Churcher, who, at about 1¼ miles, was disqualified. We feel that Harry was definitely unlucky in being so ruled and with Bert East also suffering disqualification in the last half-mile of the race our team was too depleted to be a danger, and we feel that we did very well to finish third in the scratch team race. Gerald Gregory walked splendidly throughout to finish in the fifth position and will certainly be a power to our teams when he qualifies. Syd James was well up, with McMullen, Bob Readman and Bert Clayton constituted our team. Gerald Warr had a battle with Gordon Waddingham and George Oliver. J. Norton, one of our new members, did very well to finish in the centre of the field and should feel bucked at beating such old campaigners as Joe Wilson and Jack Baker.

Chelmsford Road Relay

Held on November 4th this was the third annual race for the "Sidney Taylor Cup," and our second attempt to win same.

The course was similar to last year and fine weather prevailed throughout the race and conditions were almost perfect.

Impressions of the Belgrave "Open"

By Eric Kennell, President, R.W.A.

By Eric Kennell, President, R.W.A.

If future entries for the "Belgrave 7" approach last Saturday's mammoth total, the local authority will have to seriously consider a road-widening scheme for Lauriston Road. The 192 starters, all eager to get free from the crowd, indulged in some hectic jockeying for position at the outset, and the race did not really settle down until the competitors were over the Common.

At this point, George Coleman had a few yards' advantage over the Belgrave trio, Gregory, Churcher and McMullen—well stimulated by the exhortations of numerous "Bels" supporters. Coleman was forty yards ahead of Churcher at the two miles point, with like distances separating Gregory and McMullen, and then a little further back Barrett (Cambridge), Tunbridge and Brazier (Highgate) leading a biggish group.

The stiff climb to Cottenham Park Road saw Coleman with much the same distance ahead of Harry Churcher, but Gregory was having a rough time and had dropped behind McMullen. Cotton had come up strongly with Barrett and they had passed Tunbridge and Brazier. Leaving Wimbledon Common for the second time, the leading positions were unchanged, but McMullen and Gregory were having a private "ding-dong" of their own some 120 yards in arrear and had also pulled away from the next bunch. At this point, Highgate seemed to have the team race well in hand, with Shaw not far behind Tunbridge and Brazier. The "Bels" were ahead of Woodford, who had three men well up—Cotton, Goodall and Foster—but whose fourth man (then Alf. Pope) was some distance behind.

And so the race proceeded, with Coleman and Churcher walking at much the same pace (they both seemed content with this and neither were all out) and Gregory putting in an effort between 5 and 6½ miles, which actually took him nearer the leaders. The promoting club were putting in some hard work for the team race, and with Shaw "dying" at 5 miles, Highgate were not such clear favourites: the team race took on an exciting aspect, but Dumcan came strongly at the finish to snatch victory for Highgate by two points only.

The leading bunch of walkers did splendidly throughout, but some of the back division were ragged and appeared to be trying too hard before they were really fit. Full marks to the promoters for almost a punctual start, but time did seem to drag whilst we were waiting for the results.

Club 7 Miles C.C. Championship

This was held on the second Saturday in December as is usual, and was over a two-lap course across Wimbledon Common.

Surrey County Senior Race

Held at Coulsdon on January 6th from the S.L.H. quarters, the race was across Farthing Downs, with two laps of the country about a mile from the start.

At the half-distance J. W. Stone, the holder, was leading, but Ches Walker was close behind and, moreover, was running very easily, and it was obvious that unless Stone could get away from his opponent on the second lap of the country he had had it!

Len Herbert and Jack Braughton, both International runners, came in together for fourth place, and then came Les Stokell ninth, with John Bromley, running his first race for us since he became qualified, in tenth position, and we had the spectacle of four Belgraves fighting for the honour to be our sixth scoring member.

We would have won the team race with our second lot of six counters as they totalled 111 points against Surrey A.C. 156 and South London H. 162.

A match v. Met. Police A.A. was held in conjunction with the Championship and 44 competitors started on what was intended to be a 10 miles walk. Unfortunately owing to misunderstanding with the pointsman, the field was wrongly directed towards the end of the race and the distance walked was only about 9½ miles. However, although this does upset club records, it had no effect on either the result of the scratch or handicap race. The first three scratch men had consolidated their respective positions and also the three placed men in the handicap were sufficiently in front of each other, and the rest of the field, to have made any alteration in the result, had they gone on for the extra half mile. It was unfortunate that Geoff. Gregory could not be there as he would have made the race more interesting for the first three places. A. Burns, one of our new men from Eastleigh, was most unfortunate to be disqualified in the first 50 yards. He removed his number and carried on walking and impressed us all with his speed and very fine style. Once again we must thank our Eastleigh section for producing another fine team man, for Burns is bound for championship class in the near future.

To report briefly upon the race, Harry had a fairly comfortable win, with Mac an easy second. Bob Readman, still showing good form, was a popular third, with Captain Charlie fourth. Our Juniors showed up quite satisfactorily and we have great hopes of a win in the coming “Garnet” Cup race. George Checkley, improving fast, deserved his handicap win and Jack Coton made use of a generous mark for second place. Third in the handicap was Ted Smith, who just stopped “Pot-hunter” Shields, who filled the fourth place. Mention must be made of J. Casey, one of our new police members. Although through a misunderstanding he was not entered in the handicap, he nevertheless walked so well that he must have stood a chance in the handicap with a reasonable mark. Perhaps next time he will be rewarded for waiting. In a match versus Met. Police, scoring 16 a side, Belgrave won by 201 points to 327.

R.W.A. “Garnet” Cup Championship, Sudbury, February 3rd

In atrocious weather 152 walkers from all parts of the country faced the starter. Once again Sheffield United Harriers won the race and created a record that will possibly never be beaten. This was their fourth successive victory and also in these four races they have finished three individual winners and a second. As all the winning team and individuals are eliminated from further participation, the performance is almost uncanny and the Sheffield club are to be congratulated on their really remarkable performances.

We do not make excuses for our own failure but bad luck was our predominant opponent. A few days before the race Syd James was bereaved by the death of his father and was unable to compete. Another one of our best men, Jim Tebbutt, was on the sick list. This certainly depleted our team, but our lads although beaten by one point for fourth place, walked most gallantly and gave a fine display of team work. At three miles Geoffrey Gregory was leading in 22.20 and walking in fine style. Ron Kemp was 19th, George Checkley 32nd and Gordon Waddingham 34th. At 5 miles Gregory had a comfortable lead of 100 yards in 38.22 but was ruled out at about 5½ miles. At this point Belgrave were lying 6th but our whips got busy and got Syd Jarlett, George Checkley and Gordon Waddingham together in 19th, 20th and 21st positions, with Hugh McGreechan just behind in 25th place, at 8 miles, which brought our team to 3rd place. Unfortunately Syd Jarlett, who had done such yeoman work, was disqualified at 9 miles. Gordon was having a terrific struggle to hang on to his place and walked a plucky race. Hugh was still battling quite strongly three places behind but with the loss of Syd our team was right out of the picture. Dismayed, but not beaten, our whipper went back to the hero of the race, Jack Wakeley, walking in the 36th position. Learning of our bad luck and listening to the entreaties and bullyings of the said whip, Jack put in a superhuman effort and caught nine men in the last three-quarters of a mile. It was a magnificent example of team spirit and we thank Jack for his effort in bringing our team from 6th position to only one point behind 3rd team. Jack was not a bit pleased with himself, though, when he learned that we only wanted two more points and blamed himself for not catching just one more man. George Checkley is to be congratulated on leading home, in his first year, our gallant team. Ron Kemp is not quite fit and when he is eligible to score for us he will be just one more valuable team man. R. Garwood walked very well and is showing improvement. Abrahams seemed to be walking strongly but expressed distaste for the longer distance. Fred Rolfe also finished and will do better next year if he can keep clear of foot trouble and get in some regular training.

South of Thames C.C. Championship

This year's race was held at Sonning, near Reading, on February 10th, and we scored our fourth victory since the war. Seeing that the winning team of six scorers is barred each time, it will be seen that we have done excellently to score wins in 1946, 1948, 1949 and 1951.

We also had the individual winner in Ches. Walker and he ran in very smooth style throughout and only one other runner got anywhere near him over the very heavy 7½ miles course.

Our team all ran well with Archie Bedford surprising not a few by finishing in 4th place, while John Bromley proved his worth by being placed 8th out of a field of 226 starters.

Jack Bragginton ran his usual dogged race and Len Adams maintained his recent improvement by finishing on Jack's heels.

Eddie Short was our sixth scoring man in 29th position, and seeing that he is now serving in the R.A.F., Eddie can be said to have done very well.

It was a pity that Mick Porter had to retire with shoe trouble as with 11 men in the first 81 positions he could have walked round to finish last and we should still have won the “Colman” Cup, which is awarded to the team with the lowest score for its complete team of 12 men.

Reg Bolam, Eric Herbert, B. L. Hudson, a new member, all did well to finish in the first quarter of the field, while Len and Eric Bishop both ran their usual plucky races to help peg some of the other clubs back.

Southern C.C. Championships

These were held at Trent Park, Cockfosters, on 24th February, and large fields turned out for the Youths, Junior and Senior events.

The Youths' race was first away over a very sticky course, which was mostly grassland but owing to the rain was in a very heavy condition.

It was unfortunate for us that G. J. Goode, our Youths champion, had been in bed during the week with 'flu and he should not really have run but did not want to let the team down.

Harry Greene ran the race of his life to finish in 5th place with Brian Trower 31st, Peter Courtney 40th and H. Cornell 76th. Goode finished 140th and if only he had run in his usual style we should have won the team race.

The Senior event over 10 miles came next and with a field of 431 runners it was a case of every man for himself at the start.

When they came round at the end of the first lap of the country John Bromley was going well in the first dozen and he always kept his place and eventually finished 8th, which was a very fine performance.

Steve Charlton was another to do well and although at one time he had a bad attack of stitch, he kept going in his usual plucky fashion and did not yield any places.

Jack Brown and Eric Herbert also ran well and the latter put up what was easily his best ever performance over the country.

Ted Stimpson and Les Stockell completed our team and it was a pity that either of these two were not a little further up as we lost the team title by the narrow margin of 5 points.

Aylesford Paper Mills thus repeated their win over us of 1947 and here again the margin was 5 points in their favour.

The Junior race was the final event of a very full afternoon's sport which was witnessed by quite a large crowd, and although we have a very large membership except on occasions like the Brighton Relay only a handful of supporters turn up to give our lads a cheer.

I am sure that our senior team would be the first to say how much the help and encouragement of the few Belgrave supporters, who were able to give information on the progress of rival teams, nearly helped them to pull off a surprise win, and maybe with a few more members present at crucial points on the course, they could have closed that gap of 5 points by which the race was lost.

“Ginger” Newell was our first man home in the Junior race and he was closely followed by D. J. Abbott, and they both ran very well indeed.

Gerry Trower, although not liking the mud, also did well and our fourth scoring man was D. G. Maclean, who was farther back than we had hoped for.

Nevertheless, our team gained third place in the team race and we were well pleased with the results of our lads' efforts.

To finish second in one event and third in another was a good sign of our club strength and as explained above, but for illness we may quite well have had a first place to go with the other two placings.

The 1951 Track Season

E. A. DUFFETT.

After a very successful cross country season, we are hoping that our track men will show the same amount of improvement and so make this season one of our best ever.

We make an early start by a match at Ladywell Track on April 28th, and follow this a week later by our first match at Tooting Bec.

On this day we have as visitors the Leicester Colleges of Art and Technology A.C. and their track secretary is none other than Fred Clay, our old member, and the son of George Clay.

South London Harriers are also coming to make this a triangular fixture and although the field event men will be weakened by the fact that the “Sward” Trophy is on the same day, we look forward to a very interesting match.

The following Saturday we have the “Gordon Stewart” Trophy contest at Ealing and we always look forward to meeting old friends at this annual fixture. On the same day there are the Surrey Youths track championship at Tooting Bec.

On May 19th we are at Stompond Lane track for a match with Walton A.C. and on the same day there are the Surrey County Relays, with the Pentathlon and 7 miles walk at Tooting.

On May 23rd we have our second home fixture at Tooting and have arranged a Junior match with the Wallington County School and Wimbledon College, who will have the assistance of some of the Rutlish boys in order to strengthen their team.

On May 26th we have the “Brockman” Trophy meeting at Tooting Bec, and hope we shall have a full turn-out for this fixture and so improve upon our 4th place of last season.

On June 2nd there are the Surrey County Senior and Junior championships at Motspur Park, and we look forward to our members doing as well, if not better, than last year.

Calling All Track Men

F. SIMMONS.

Our season starts early in May and in the past we have been weak in all events simply because training has not started early enough. You, and you alone, can make amends by getting down to training now and being determined to support club fixtures and club handicaps early in the season and by continuing to do so up to the bitter end.

Some of our away matches may be a fair distance to travel to, but this is offset by opponents having to travel to us for home fixtures. It is only common courtesy for us to field a full team when we have an away match and it does our reputation good to turn out a full team when we are away as well as at home. I am not sure that we have enhanced our reputation even in some of our home fixtures in the past. Let's cure that, shall we?

We are a large club and there is ample opportunity for all to compete, for it is only by competing that you can attain high standards. Don't leave it to others, have a go yourself and you will be surprised at the enjoyment you will get in the keen rivalry with your clubmates.

If you cannot get to a fixture, LET YOUR CAPTAIN OR SECRETARY KNOW, he can then contact somebody else and thus ensure that Belgrave do turn out full teams.

Even if you are not competing for the Club, we still want your support and don't forget to bring your kit in case of an emergency.

Once again, get down to it now. Whether you train at Tooting, Motspur Park or Alperton, or wherever you train, ensure that we have our best season ever on the track.

Also, don't forget your county and club championships, also the Southern and A.A.A. championships. I would personally like to see more Belgravians in these. Even if you don't win or get placed, you have at least had a try and shown off Belgrave colours.

The cross country lads have had a good go this year and it is up to all track men to carry on with the same determination to see our great Club back to the front in athletic endeavour and true sportsmanship.

I will end with this old Greek motto: “Posit Feni”—translated, it means “It can be done.”

F. SIMMONS.

Training Comments

By “Olympian”

By “Olympian”

The summer season will soon be upon us and track men should already have begun their training, especially if they have had no background of cross-country running to build up their stamina.

Those that have been training regularly throughout the winter should concentrate mainly on speed work by training and racing below their best distance. There should be no fear that their stamina will desert them and care should be taken to build up speed in easy stages, but as fitness develops more frequent fast runs over varying distances can be undertaken.

Members who appear keen to reach the top seem to fall by the wayside through lack of determination when they are not progressing as rapidly as they had hoped for. Often a change of distance or new training methods will make all the difference.

Talk about your training with others, compare notes with those who have succeeded and do not be afraid to try something contrary to your usual practice. Read as much as you can about all sides of athletics—there are some very good books and publications now available. The A.A.A. have recently issued a booklet on Middle Distance Running and Steeplechasing, which is admirably written and costs only 1/6d.

Leading Continental runners use “Fartlek” training with great success and you, too, can adopt this method, for it is suitable to all athletes. Find out all you can about this form of preparation and you will enjoy your running far more than you do at present. Remember that above all else the secret of success is to enjoy every minute of your training.

I feel sure that too many of our members stick to the opinion that the only way to get fit for running is to run! It is most essential that the body is prepared for the work you expect to get out of it, and unless some form of strengthening is carried out, success will not be fully attained.

Ten minutes each day on well-selected exercises will more than repay you for the time so spent. Don't forget that walking is extremely beneficial and can be most enjoyable. An excellent method of improving physique is for track athletes to take up a field event. This will also relieve the monotony of training and you may find that you have hidden talents, which, with some expert coaching, can be developed both to benefit yourself and also your Club.

Everyone should make it their object to be included in a Belgrave team in such events as the “Kinnaird” and “Waddilove” Trophy meetings. Don't leave it for a few members only to represent the Club in these events. Belgrave can attain a position in summer athletics such as it has attained in the cross-country world, but only with the support of all its active members.

What happens to the majority of our members when they hang up their shoes? Why not volunteer to help as an official or coach and so make use of that invaluable knowledge and experience gained during your active days. The lack of officials at some of our matches has been most noticeable in the past and only makes us appreciate all the more those stalwarts who do turn up, rain or fine, but we are sure they would welcome some help.

Don't forget to advise the Club as soon as you are not available through injury, illness or some other reason, in order that a substitute may be found. Also keep them advised of your progress, so that you may be considered for team selection. Don't think that the Club has no need of your services, as we have matches to suit all branches of the Club and can find a place in one of our teams in order to give you a try-out.

Belgrave has this season regained some of its lost prestige over the country, by training a little harder and more frequently. Let this also apply to the track performers, who can, by a little effort this summer, put Belgrave on the map once again.

Field Events Gossip

T. C. JONES, Field Events Captain.

A new season is approaching, which it is hoped will be a much better one that last year for the Field Events Section. Frankly, last season was most disappointing. The support for the Club matches and the big events such as the “Sward” Trophy and the “Kinnaird” Trophy was not anywhere near what is expected from a club of our size and reputation.

The handicaps put on for your benefit were also poorly supported and the Committee will have to think twice about putting so many on this season.

Ans Bergvalds was undoubtedly our best performer. His Club Championship record of 125 ft. 7 ins. in the Discus, added to which in the Boxing Day handicap he put the Shot 41 ft. 5ins.—the best performance ever by a Club member—were most gratifying. Pete Lowe's new record in the Hop, Step and Jump and Bob Cooper's equalling of the High Jump record were the only other highlights, except for Paul Bower's fine throw in the Surrey Junior Javelin, which he won with a new record of 175 ft. 6½ ins.

All these will be available for the new season. We hope Bergvalds will get better support and look to Arthur Allen and Dennis Childs for this, as we cannot expect Bill Sutton to go on for ever. Murray and Cornell should show improvement in the Shot.

The Javelin was last year one of our weakest events. Charlie Melchior again won the championship, which shows the paucity of good performers. Terry Bateman will, we hope, show considerable improvement, and with Doug Whitehead, now back in “civvy street” aftr getting a R.A.F. Blue at this event, perhaps we shall see some better throwing. A number of others show promise, but must do more hard work to achieve a better standard. George Powell will, we suppose, turn out again and show real Belgrave team spirit. It is amazing how he goes on and on. In the Surrey Championship he threw over 140 ft., which is a worthy performance when one remembers he first competed over 25 years ago.

High Jumpers are few, but after Bob Cooper's 5 ft. 5 ins. we shall expect to see even more improvement. Jack Sinton has even better possibilities if he has recovered from his illness. Steve Gibson will be available again, together with Nick Nickini, who showed promise before going into the Forces.

In the Long Jump and Hop, Step and Jump it looks as if we shall have some better performances than since 1939. Pete Lowe's jumping in the triple jump was good, but he should improve still more and, we hope, reach 43 ft. His long jumping could be improved, and he, with Luxon and Steadman, two newcomers, we look to for jumps of over 21 ft.

We seem to be at last finding a few hurdlers. The standard is not first-class yet, but we have hopes.

What we want is Pole Vaulters—any volunteers?

The Junior Section shows great promise. Alan Law's shot putting should still further improve. Paul Bowers and Harry Delger will be our Javelin throwers.

For the Long Jump we shall have Ian Forrester, Brian Tillot and Nigel Cleeve.

Bob Cooper, who won our first Junior High Jump championship, will be a Senior, but Jim Purdy and one or two others show great promise. Whilst mentioning this championship—support for it was very poor. It must surely be a thing wanted by Juniors, but how can we continue it if we do not get better entries.

We have quite a school of promising hurdlers in Tillot, Hunt, Purdy and Simpson, who should all do well this coming season.

I do want to remind you of all the handicaps we run for Field Events men. These are put on for your benefit and encouragement. The entries, however, received last year were most disappointing. The Committee will seriously have to consider cutting these down if better support is not forthcoming. These handicaps are not put on solely for the benefit of those interested in the particular event, they give opportunities to all to try out a new event, and we should like to see more and MORE entries.

The practise of the handicapper in the past has been to handicap everyone whom he thought would compete in the event. These names have all been printed on the programme and, to give an instance, in one of the Javelin handicaps last year there were over 20 names on the programme and only five turned up. This is a waste of the handicapper's time, and money to the Club for printing costs. The Committee have decided that in future entries must be made otherwise your names will not be printed in the programme. A member whose name is not on the programme will not be allowed to compete.

We expect you all to try to make this coming season a better one for the Club and yourself and so put Belgrave back on the Field Events map.

T. C. JONES, Field Events Captain.

Here and There

“Judge praises Tooting runner,” these were the headlines in a local paper recently, and they referred to the fact that our track captain, Stan Davies, had assisted in apprehending a man who assaulted a young girl on Tooting Bec Common. Stan was warmly congratulated by the chairman of the London Sessions, and we, too, offer our congratulations on a public-spirited action.

C. W. Walker was first man home in the L.C.C. 6 miles cross-country race held at Parliament Hill Fields, and we confidently predict another Belgravian gaining his International vest over the country in the next season or so.

Ray Tooby won the Junior Inter-Banks cross-country title and, although he is in the Forces, he still seems to be running as well as ever.

John Bromley was first man home in the Southern Command c.c. championship, while Ray Tooby also finished close up, and both these members should find a place in the Army team this season.

D. G. Maclean was first man home in the Highgate Inter-Schools race, and so repeated his win of last season, and he has also won several other Inter-Schools races this season over the country.

Eddie Short is now the Officer-in-charge of Athletics at the R.A.F. Halton Camp and his C/O is none other than the old Olympic hurdler, Don Finlay.

D. B. Evison, another of our junior runners, is now living at Thetford, Norfolk, but hopes to be able to turn out for us in any races that we require him for, and he sends his best wishes to all Club members.

George Simpson, who was a member of our placed Youths' team in last year's Southern c.c. championship, is now stationed in Vienna with the British Army there. He has not been able to do much in the running line, but has been on a ski course in the Austrian mountains, which should certainly help his stamina.

“Ginger” Newell, who was our first man home in the recent Southern Junior c.c. race, finished second in his Corps' cross-country championship, and Doug Briggs was tenth in the same race.

D. E. Marshall, our Surrey Junior sprint champion, is now serving in the R.A.F. and is stationed at Hereford, and at the moment is not getting much chance to do any training.

We do not seem to have had any news of A. J. Sinton, who was a very promising high-jumper, and we know he was advised by his doctor to rest from athletics for a while, and trust he is now fit and well again.

Basil Walden sustained a damaged shoulder while playing “rugger” for Streatham recently, and we trust that the injury will not affect his track prospects this summer.

We have quite a “school” of Club members now at London University and these include Reg Bolam, Colin Collis, D. J. Abbott and A. J. Snoad, and recently Bolam was first man home in a match against the Reading and Southampton Universities.

A. J. Snoad also finished 12th in the U.A.U. cross-country championship, which was quite a good performance, and we look forward to further successes by this young man.

Seen in the crowd at a football match at the Tooting and Mitcham ground was our old Club member, Alec Robertshaw, and we feel that any of our members, whether active or non-active, would be better occupied by an afternoon at Wimbledon, rather than by watching their local football team.

Received too late for our last issue, we now have pleasure in offering congratulations to Victor Meek on gaining third place in the Cornwall County 100 yards championship. It's a far cry from London, but it's an honour we are proud to record.

Donald Harvey is now serving with His Majesty's Forces and all Belgravians wish him well.

John Norton, one of our new walking members in the Regular Army, has recently been drafted to Malaya and sends his best wishes to all.

A letter received from Tony Whincup congratulates Gordon Doubleday on being elected as Treasurer in the august ranks of Belgrave officials. Tony has been unable, owing to stomach trouble, to do any running in the Army. He hopes to return to London and resume running with the Belgrave lads. Expects Belgrave to be at the front in the “National” this year. Sends best wishes to all.

Pte. Joe (Splinter) Tranter sends greetings from Malaya and curses the powers that he was unable to be with us during the 1950 track season owing to extension of period of service. He intends to be with us in 1951 and hopes his nick-name has not been attached to anyone else, as he feels it may be hard work making another name for himself.

On December 29th, to wind up his good year, Harry Churcher won the Business Houses 7 miles championship. In the same race our members, J. Coton and M. Abrahams, stepped out of the novice class by winning the first and second novice's medals.

Bob Readman continued his long run of Police successes by winning the 7 miles Inter-District championship on January 6th. He followed this up on February 22nd by winning the Police 10 miles championship in the second fastest time ever recorded, 80 mins. 11 secs., only 52 secs. outside Jim Morris's record.

At a recent film show on walking it was most satisfying that Harry Churcher emerged with flying colours. In every film he was most definitely walking fair.
