Heading for promotion

Track & Field

Match Two, Coventry, 2 July 2006

Belgrave’s women’s team provided nine ‘A’ string wins at Coventry in the second UKWAL match of 2006 and stretched their league win streak to five matches. Now two league points clear of Marshall Milton Keynes AC, and with the final match on home territory at Sutton Arena, there was every chance that the Belles would enter Division Two next season as champions of Division Three.

The early field events set the tone for the day as Belinda Heil made a welcome return to the team in the hammer and Sarah Wellstead produced her first win of the afternoon - the long jump. The pole vault saw Janine Whitlock enter the competition at 4.15m and this height she cleared at her second attempt, equalling her own division record, before going on to unsuccessfully try at 4.31 – which would have been 11cm higher than the UKWAL record.

By now the track events were well under way where we opened with a win thanks to Hannah Stares in the one lap hurdles. Claire Robinson was totally untroubled in the 800m and from there on the Belles gradually opened up a winning lead in the match.

Warming up for the shot by turning her arm in the discus, Eva Massey’s single valid effort was good enough for second and when her speciality event came around, every one of her throws was good enough to win the competition. Now without a coach, Eva is experimenting with the rotational shot technique.

A double win came in the 200m where Michelle Carey had a 5 metre advantage in the A race but Jennifer Culley’s B victory was very much closer, with the first two recording the same time. And soon another maximum-pointer came up as Sarah Wellstead returned to the horizontal jumps pit to take the triple jump with Michelle Nestor’s effort in the B competition being good enough to beat every other athlete.

The final individual event on the track saw Michaela Hutchison make her UKWAL debut and a pretty auspicious one it was too. She tracked City of Norwich’s Ruth Senior until there was just over half a lap to go and then launched a powerful finish that left Belgrave supporters very impressed; and amidst this excitement, with the field strung out in her wake, it became apparent that Caroline Shanklyn had picked up the B win as well.

All in all, a good afternoon’s work.

Going up!

Match Three, Sutton Arena, 6 August 2006

Athletics doesn’t get any busier than this. A men’s British League match on the Saturday was followed by a home UK Women’s match on the Sunday and everyone was pressed into action to help the meeting run well.

There was no doubt that the Belles were going to gain promotion but it would be nice to go up as Division Three Champions and maintain an unbeaten record. However, Marshall Milton Keynes were equally determined to put a few good finishing touches to their season – and in the end they took the match by just four points which resulted in them closing, but not quite catching our women in the race for the league title.

Congratulations all round then – especially to team managers Cynthia James and Bob Bridges. But just take a look at the league standings below and then consider the make-up of division two for 2007! To continue the meteoric rise of the Belles is going to take some doing.

Joachim Wolf says goodbye

News

Joachim Wolf says goodbye

It’s time for me to do a Sven-Göran, ie leave England. As you may have noticed I have been quite absent from the race scene lately. The reason is my imminent move back to Stockholm, Sweden. After almost eight years in the UK the time has come to go back home, and believe me moving country with the whole family (selling house, buying house, moving job, changing car, packing etc) is a full-time job in itself. I have been training well though and I expect to run a good race in a 90k relay back in Sweden. The course goes over the famous 90k Cross Country Skiing course and several hundred teams are running this. I was part in the winning team in 1998 just before departing for the UK so obviously it would be great to return to Sweden with a win, just to add to my incredible relay wins as part of a superior Belgrave team, (Milton Keynes etc).

I have also entered the Lidingöloppet, a massive cross-country over 30k in Stockholm (I was “just” 10 mins behind the great Paul Evans when he smashed the course record in 1994 beating off several top Kenyans, so you can imagine how impressed I was when I found out that I would hand over to Mr Evans at that relay in Milton Keynes.)

I will also run a couple of Half Marathons and hopefully another marathon in the late autumn, and I’m also planning a busy Cross-Country Skiing race season between Dec-March, this will replace the unforgettable cross-country league races with Belgrave, although the best way to describe Cross-Country racing over long distances is like running a muddy Parliament Hill with slippers and a backpack.

Finally I would like to thank Belgrave and in particular Alan and Pat for giving me some great running memories during my time at Belgrave Harriers, and I would be delighted to run the odd race for Belgrave whenever I come over to the UK for the occasional visit. And if things go my way I’ll be back for the London Marathon … year after year ...

Just to mention some of the Highlights...

• Handing over the baton to Paul Evans in the 12-stage at Milton Keynes when we won a great victory. • Running past Rob McHarg in the last 30metres in an 800m Open race in Watford. • Running the Flora London Marathon encouraged by thousands of people shouting “Go Belgrave” at me. • A third place in London Half Marathon at Silverstone. • Running 1:58 for 800m just a day after a Half Marathon Race

Awesome Gauson

Racing in the 42nd annual Antwerp Gala in Belgium on Saturday 26 August, in perfect conditions and with a large and enthusiastic crowd three and four deep lining the track, Kris Gauson was among three British teenagers who narrowly missed being picked for the recent IAAF World Junior Championships in Beijing but were recompensed by selection for this meeting.

Kris beat the World Juniors 1500m qualifying time and out-kicked seniors James Thie and Chris Moss. The young Scot covered the last lap of the A race in 57 seconds to clock 3:43.10 – a PB by 1.99sec – and snatch third place with Thie fourth and Moss fifth.

He also narrowly missed the Belgrave U20 record set by Stuart Paton at this very meeting 24 years ago - 3:42.86 - but Kris has time on his side as he's still a junior in 2007!

Feature · page 4

What was – and what might have been

Eric Hall

One item, among the many records held by the Club, is of great interest to the walking fraternity in particular. A small exercise book titled “Walking Records 1929” was compiled by Harry Churcher one of our greatest short distance men in the ’thirties and ’forties. The title was prophetic as Harry was indeed to become a holder of many records including two world records set almost 20 years after he made that inscription.

The entries give, we believe, an almost complete record of all Harry’s races until the last years of his long career. The first entry, on 26th October 1929, is a 7 mile event at Harrow. He notes that, “I was unplaced. My first road walk.” He recorded his handicap allowance and stuck two photographs of the event on the page. These also are of great interest. Harry started out as a member of London Vidarians and was soon winning prizes, the first of which was a handicap silver medal plus six spoons and forks. At the end of November he raced in the Belgrave Open ‘7’ and notes, “I came over very ill one mile from home”. He was certainly not the last to feel that way on that course!

After nearly two years Harry joined Belgrave. Having a very light frame his style often attracted the attention of the judges and for some events the entry in the exercise book is “Pulled”. Two things show the difference between athletics in the 1930s and that of today. Firstly, Harry notes on occasions, that he did not race when he intended to due to “Working”. Secondly there were back then, and for many years after the war, local village meetings on grass tracks with handicap events for all.

The newspaper cuttings are revealing as, for example, on December 17th 1932 - Harry won the Club’s Horwood Cup and Arthur Penny won the 7 mile cross country title. Arthur beat Tom Carter quite easily while Harry got the better of Charlie Speechley. Charlie was racing in 1919 and was to continue to do so into the 1960s. There is not room to cover all the entries but one point of interest is that at the back of the first book Harry has stuck the receipts for his Club subscriptions!

A second book follows the same format but with photographs of Harry in major events. A newspaper report of the Belgrave Open on 25th November 1939, won by Harry with brother Charlie second, notes that “Churcher is undoubtedly on top of his form at present”. The entry, on the very next page, is headed January 12th 1946! As with so many sportsmen at that time there were other things which needed attending to before they were able to resume their sport. What might have happened if the 1940 and 1944 Olympic Games had taken place is a matter of conjecture but it is safe to say that Harry, and no doubt some of his Club mates, would have had a part to play if the war had not intervened.

Tantalisingly, the last entry is for the 25th June 1947 and one is left wondering if a third book existed. If it did it would have recorded that Harry won further titles and represented Britain in the 1948 Olympic Games where he finished 5th in the 10,000 metres walk. A very modest shy man, he usually trained alone and rarely, if ever, referred to his achievements. My abiding memory of him is a speech he made when presented, by the Club, with an award recognising his two world records. In his view champions were only as good as the opposition made them and without other competitors there could not be champions and it was those who won little who should be given awards. Harry died in 1972 at the young age of 61. To appreciate something of his standing in athletics, take a look at the selection of his medals displayed in the Club Committee Room.

Eric Hall

… and now some web stats

And while we are providing answers to questions – here’s another one. Someone wondered how many people actually download our electronic newsletter – The Belgravian.

Well, we know how many visits we receive at our website (a visit being classed as access from one computer in one day – in other words if you come back more than once from the same computer in any one day it doesn’t register more than one visit). Usually we get around 300 visits per day and after a big event such as a British League match or national road relay it can hit 600. On average over the last year we have received around 11,000 visits per month.

So, back to the question. We can’t tell how many download and actually print The Belgravian. We do get a regular 800-900 people per month who visit to view one or other of our newsletters and when a new publication arrives it receives around 200 individual visits within its first month.
