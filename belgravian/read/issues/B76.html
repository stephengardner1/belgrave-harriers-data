Column · page 3

THIS AND THAT.

Heartiest congratulations to our Hon. Secretary, W. G. Webb, upon the occasion of his marriage. We wish Mr. and Mrs. Webb many years of happily married life.

* * * * *

We have received good wishes from our former member now in Australia, B. C. Button, for his old friends, and the Club, in 1937. These, we heartily reciprocate, and trust that this year will be a successful one for Bert Button, and his club, St. George's A.C., of New South Wales.

* * * * *

Heartiest congratulations to our winning Surrey Junior cross-country team; and to K. N. Wood, who gained the individual title. Our team consisted of Messrs. Wood (1), Herbert (4), Lucas (5) and Martin (6). Well done!

* * * * *

Prior to our cross-county team's visit to Paris, there was much comment in the Press as to the weak team which Surrey would be forced to field in the Inter-county race at Cheltenham which took place in the same week-end. We felt sure that those gaining their places in the County team, in the absence of some of our members who might reasonably have expected invitations but for our other fixture, would rise to the occasion and prove their worth. Accordingly we were very glad to find that Surrey proved themselves champion county, and that the fears expressed in the newspapers were not realized.

On Wednesday, January 27th, the winning Surrey County team, and our victorious team which competed in Paris, were televised from Alexandra Palace. Bert Footer addressed the public; and no doubt television set owners rather thought there had been some confusion, and that the Health and Beauty parade—due for the following Saturday—was being seen! How many film contracts, proposals of marriage, threatening letters, etc., Bert has received remains a secret. For the sake of our "Southern" and "National" teams we must be grateful that the number of television sets in use is so small, or we should assuredly have lost our Captain!

* * * * *

WITHOUT COMMENT.—On January 27th a Belgrave team was televised by the Baird process. On February 3rd the announcement was made that the E.M.I. process only would be used.

* * * * *

The Championship season approaches and a new Club tie becomes a necessity. The Jubilee Dinner also demands that every Club member should "show his colours." Our Hon. Treasurer will be happy to supply members on request, at a charge of 3/- each. Note his new address on page 1.

* * * * *

The 10 Miles Championships have both found new holders. Jim Ginty won the cross-country event by a three seconds margin from A. W. Penny; and in the Walk, Charlie Churcher won from brother Harry. In both instances the Champion also won the sealed handicap. Full details in our next issue.

* * * * *

Members are again reminded that literary contributions are always welcome, and should be addressed to A. A. Harley, at the address given on page 1.

Articles should be typed, or clearly written, on one side of the paper only, all proper names being printed to avoid mistakes. Abbreviations should not be used.

AU CROSS DU METRO.

The Hon. Editor

The Hon. Editor begins.

Les Belgrave Harriers gagnent très facilement le challenge par équipes en classant leurs six coureurs dans les seize premiers . . . . but enough of that! Let's start at the beginning.

Two months ago we were honoured by an invitation to compete in an international cross-country race to be held near Paris on Sunday, January 19th. The invitation was, of course, forwarded to us through the International Board of the A.A.A. and in consultation with the English Cross-country Union; and our Committee, recognising the implied compliment, decided to accept our first invitation to compete as a team abroad.

Accordingly ten runners (H. E. Footer, J. Parker, L. J. Cohen, J. W. Kemp, H. W. Shields, A. W. Penny, C. T. Carter, E. S. Stimpson, A. E. Taylor, A. R. Shaw), three officials (W. L. Bayliss, H. Parker, A. A. Harley), and six supporters (T. C. Jones, W. Sutton, J. Chamberlain, G. S. Doubleday, N. Isaacs, J. St. Leger) made the crossing via Newhaven—Dieppe and duly arrived at Paris, where they were met by M. Chauvet, President of the Athletic Section of the U.S.M., the promoting club, and M. Girard, whose kind assistance as interpreter and guide throughout our visit were greatly appreciated. And here let me explain that U.S.M. is an abbreviated form of Union Sportive Metropolitain, our hosts and the promoters of the events. This club is the sports organisation of the "Underground" railways of Paris, and, since its formation in 1928, has performed creditably in many branches of sport. In addition to catering for the male and female employees, special arrangements are made for the younger element to enjoy physical culture and games. The U.S.M. regularly fields 12 football teams, four rugby teams, eight basket ball teams (three of which are for women), and also practices athletics and cross-country running, walking, rowing, swimming, cycling, tennis, boxing, gymnastics and other sports and games. Truly a very comprehensive range of activities.

To resume, after disgressing somewhat: our party was conducted to the hotel, and after a wash and dinner, sallied forth to see something of Paris, the team being under orders to return early and retire!

Next morning, our President and myself were awakened by a ringing telephone, and M. Vrolix, President of the U.S.M., bade us "Welcome" and arranged to be at our hotel a little later. Breakfast over, a representative of the Ministry of Sport waited on us, and on the arrival of M. Vrolix, a coach was ordered by telephone and we were shown the main points of interest in Paris, and then proceeded to the magnificent quarters of L'Union Sportive Metropolitain at Le Croix de Berny, about twelve miles outside Paris itself.

While the team had a look at the course, the Club officials and supporters lunched in the pavilion in distinguished company, which included the Minister of Education and his wife, the Minister of Sport and several other distinguished Frenchmen. Our President sat cheek-by-jowl with the notabilities and conducted himself with dignity—but seemed a little off his food! Maybe his thoughts were twenty-four hours in retrospect!

The U.S.M. Sports Ground is splendidly appointed, with fine dressing rooms, a large gymnasium, many football, rugby and basket-ball pitches, and a running track. It was on the track that racing started and finished. A junior race preceded the main event, but we were not represented in this. It had been expected that Ted Stimpson would carry our colours in this event; but it was discovered that the French definition of a junior and ours were not in accord, and so it was arranged that he should run in the senior event.

At the close of the junior race, the seniors were assembled and each team trotted round the track and were presented by loudspeaker to the crowd. Then the line-up and start. The course consisted of four laps of very sticky grassland, returning to the track for the beginning of each lap; the runners being in view practically the whole time. Twenty-four teams were entered, and there was a good complement of runners. The start was a very, very fast one. At the half-mile it is doubtful if we had a man inside the first fifty positions. Then Pat Parker decided that it was time the Belgrave colours were taken further forward, and he gained dozens of places, closely followed by Tom Carter. At the end of the first lap they occupied sixth and seventh places. A few positions later came Les Cohen, with Bert Footer and Arthur Penny together in the early twenties, and our others not far behind.

Charlie Jones carries on.

On the second lap Parker moved up to fifth behind Guiomar, Bernard (Metro), and two Moroccan Soldiers of S.A. Verdun. Carter was 6th, followed by Penny 12th, Footer 14th, Cohen 15th, Taylor 17th, Shaw 21st, Kemp 24th, Stimpson 32nd and Harry Shields 33rd.

The next lap showed a great improvement in our placings and we appeared to have a great chance of winning easily, for Parker was now third only about sixty yards behind the leaders. Penny and Footer had moved up to 5th and 6th respectively, closely followed by Carter 8th, Cohen 14th, Taylor 15th, Shaw 16th.

On the last lap the positions were maintained and Parker eventually finished 3rd a short way behind the two Frenchmen, Penny 4th (having passed a man down the straight), Footer 6th, Carter 7th, Cohen 15th, Taylor 16th (giving us a score of 51 points, Shaw 17th, Stimpson 21st, Kemp, 29th and Shields 44th completing our team. The second team were the Moroccans (with a score of 130 points), third the Brussells team (who won the race last year) with 156 points, and fourth our hosts, Metro, with 157 points.

Our team must be congratulated on the fine performance in this race, having worthily upheld the traditions of English cross-country running, and also brought the name of the Club into the limelight on the Continent.

We received a fine trophy in the form of an athlete in bronze, and our first seven received prizes for their efforts.

Doubleday, in at the finish.

So, as you all know, Belgrave won their first Continental cross-country race. Although, so we understand, some of the leading Paris runners and clubs were absent (we would have liked to have seen them there) we can have nothing but praise and admiration for our team packing, and the support of our non-scoring men. One point of interest which arises is the fact that although our men are training for 10 miles they can still compete with the best at six miles.

After the presentation of the prizes, we all crowded into a "tram" to take us to the "Metro," whence back to the hotel, to place the trophy in a place of honour, and to have dinner.

During dinner, M. Vrolix called to say "Au revoir," and we were glad to have the opportunity of saying "Thank you!" to him and his organisation for the splendid time we had enjoyed.

Most of us came back on Sunday night, having a very turbulent crossing. Those who stayed in Paris another night had an even worse crossing—so they say,—but "Curtain please!"

But before it falls, on behalf of the Club, our sincere thanks to the U.S.M. and its officials. Also to those who served our Club so well as runners, officials and supporters on our first athletic trip abroad. May we end with one French word? Encore!

ASHCOMBE A.C. and T.V.H. at WIMBLEDON.

C. T. CARTER

By C. T. CARTER.

Yo! heave ho! me hearties! or words to that effect. No, I'm not writing of the Bay of Biscay, but of Wimbledon when there is water about.

After a fairly fast start, Les Cohen was in the lead at the Brook, but Art Penny was closing on him, and a Thames Valley man was in third position.

At the Windmill, Cohen still led with a six seconds margin over Penny and, with our other men following one another in quick succession, ensured us of an easy team win.

Our congratulations go to R. J. Powell, a young member of great promise, who won the sealed handicap. Another junior member, Merryman, was second, and J. H. Chantler took third place.

Finally, I did hear it was hard work against the tide!

"SOUTH OF THE THAMES" JUNIOR RACE.

H. W. SHIELDS

By H. W. SHIELDS.

The thing which always most impresses me about this race is the wonderfully keen support given by members, who seem to turn up in their dozens and make the Downs echo with the cry of "Up the Bels."; so that supporters' shouts from other clubs seem in comparison to be like murmurs.

This, I am sure, has had much to do with our truly remarkable performances in this event. We have been placed since 1932, and the previous years show an equally fine record, in spite of the fact that numerous men are "barred" each year.

Anyhow, to get on with the race. After a very good start the runners struck out for that very hilly piece of Epsom Downs by the cottages, where we were able to take stock of our chances.

At this point we had Murdoch in the first half-dozen, with Clements, Beaumont and Wood in the early twenties. From here it was obvious we should be placed.

On the second lap round, Wood was moving up amazingly. Clements was also passing men rapidly. Beaumont was still running strongly, occupying about the same position as before, with Murdoch, although dropping slightly, running very well as our fourth man.

From the appended result it will be seen that our men put up a very good show, especially when it is remembered that many were competing in their first race of any importance. Wood merits our hearty congratulations upon his placing in this event. We trust that in due course he will run similarly in senior events.

NOVEMBER 21st.

On this day we entertained Aylesford Paper Mills S.C.; South Staffs. Regt. and the Guards Depot (Caterham) at Wimbledon. We ran out easy winners of the team event, placing 13 men (headed by L. Cohen) in the first 15 places, the other two being from the Paper Mills. The soldiers turned out in very good numbers and our Hall was well and truly packed. Everyone seemed to enjoy the run, and the tea we provided for the visitors disappeared with wonderful rapidity! May we suggest that cream buns for tea would ease the recruiting problem?

L. Cohen

AT EALING.

S. T. RING

By S. T. RING.

On November 28th, 13 Belgrave Harriers visited Ealing, some of the absentees being with Mr. E. J. Holt's team at Cambridge.

The Bels started splendidly and for half-a-mile the home runners were well to the front. Bain set a corking pace and opened quite a gap, but over the fields our men gradually picked up. Allum assumed second place, followed by Stimpson, but they were too far behind to make any impression on Bain. Stimpson eventually took second place, and Footer, running through the field, came into fourth place. Final scores were: Belgrave 86 pts., Ealing 126 pts.

I noticed, amongst other things, "Sparrow," belying his name, hung up feet first on a stile, and our Social Hon. Secretary and one other "snaking" off to change near a fire.

THE SEVEN MILES CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP.

CECIL PENNY

By CECIL PENNY.

Two things occurred to me at the start of this race. Firstly, why is it we usually start so late that the majority of the runners complete the course in darkness? Secondly, it's about time a definite starting point was decided on, as this latter differs from year to year. Of what use are times if this is so?

The race itself had added interest this year as the boys knew a good showing would enhance their claims for a place in the team to visit Paris.

As is usual the cracks set the pace, Penny, Cohen (who is showing improved form this year) leading, Ring, Taylor, Stimpson and Parker being bunched just behind. It's the others I want to mention this time, however—those runners who enter our Championships year after year, their only reward a hard "bashing." "Champion" Smith seemed to enjoy his tussle with our old friend "Doub." Edwards, White, Tommy Morrell (making a come-back) and a not very well-known Bel, Herbert, had a rare battle all the way. Our one and only George Still finished fresher than most of those in front—probably due to Ben carrying him part of the way. They certainly seemed loath to part company. Tobin ran well, and with a year or so of experience behind him, may surprise us. The Patience brothers hardly justified their reputation, due, no doubt, to the fact they had arrived home only a week or so earlier from abroad.

Our two most prominent juniors, Stimpson and Wood, ran magnificently in such company.

In conclusion, I must express the thoughts of all: how we missed that diminutive figure, red of face, black of hair, running this year!

BELGRAVE H. v. EALING H.

A. S. LINDSEY

By A. S. LINDSEY.

This fixture took place on December 18th, and we had expected a Metropolitan Police team to participate. However, the Proclamation ceremonies demanded the services of many of our old rivals from Imber Court, and they were unable to field a team.

Over the first mile the field of thirty runners kept well together, but on coming to the horse-ride, some began to find their feet (others found them also, but with more difficulty !). Despite the burst of Spring-like sunshine, the Clerk of the Weather had put one over us, for in parts the course was like a quagmire. Bain, of Ealing, eventually ran out winner, beating George Chantler by 200 yards or so. Although we did not have our strongest team out, our runners put in some good work in the last mile, and our tenth man finished in 13th position, giving us a very easy team win. The course was somewhat shorter than usual, as the runners went straight out from the start, and omitted the Brook crossing.

THE BOXING DAY HANDICAPS.

G. DOUBLEDAY

By G. DOUBLEDAY.

For those sprinters who keep fit during the winter (and others !) there were four heats in the 120 yards race, the winners proceeding to the final. The starter got them all away first time in the final and Skelley (off 8) broke the worsted, with K. Crawford (8) and A. Mewes (8½) being 2nd and 3rd. F. Simmons was the unlucky fourth.

Then came the 2 miles run. This was won by one of our promising juniors, W. Selby, who, making his first track appearance, made good use of his 420 yards start, to win in 9 mins. 19 secs., about 20 yards in front of N. Isaacs (480). L. Herbert showed his versatility by running up from 190 yards to fill third place.

In the 3 Miles Walk L. J. Coleman maintained the improvement he seems to show every time out and won easily. He seems destined to become a Club champion in the course of a few years. J. G. Coleman and J. Rutland filled the minor placings.

So ended our annual Christmastide track meeting, which goes back to the Club's beginning; although in those days the Embankment had to serve for a track!

SURREY COUNTY CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP.

T. C. JONES

By T. C. JONES.

The Surrey County Championship was held at Epsom Downs again this year, on the 2nd January. We had a good team entered and had hopes of repeating our win of last year.

We were well placed at the start, and after about a mile and a half Hines, of Surrey, was leading, followed by Belton (Surrey), Thorogood (Mitcham), with about nine of ours in the first thirty. At three miles Bert Footer was on Hines' shoulder, having apparently done a bit of "snaky" running when out of sight of the spectators. Penny was next, followed by Carter, Cohen, Ring, and Wood (who won the Surrey Junior Race the week after) in the first twenty places. Our hopes of repeating our win of last year were now very bright; and we next saw the field coming round Tattenham Corner led by Bert Footer, followed by Belton. Then came Hines, Weatherill, Penny fifth, Carter sixth, Cohen seventh, Ring thirteenth, and Wood seventeenth. This scoring enabled us to win comfortably from Surrey A.C., thus making our tenth win in the last twelve years.

THE MIDDLESEX CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP.

H. W. SHIELDS

By H. W. SHIELDS.

Whilst Belgrave were showing a clean pair of heels to rival teams at Epsom Downs, Cadogan A.C. (consisting of Belgrave men with a Middlesex qualification) were endeavouring to emulate their feat at Enfield Lock.

This they all but succeeded in doing, losing by the narrow margin of 11 points, in spite of the fact that they were without A. Allum, Patience brothers, Jack Flower and Ted Stimpson, who was unfortunate enough to have trouble with his leg, which forced him to retire.

Of the race itself, I think very special praise should be given to our old friend Arthur Bird, and R. Tobin, for the magnificent way in which they endeavoured to make up for our missing men.

In the Junior race we had H. M. Quackenbos 15th, and Selby 19th; excellent performances for first time of running.

FIVE MILES JUNIOR CROSS-COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIP.

H. M. QUACKENBOS

By H. M. QUACKENBOS.

The "Junior" Championship for the "Savage" Shield, held on December 19th, attracted a larger field than usual; but we were sorry to miss E. S. Stimpson, who was prevented from turning out by business calls.

The runners were away in a pack to a slow start and kept well together until they were out of sight. About half-way, Clements managed to open up a gap from Wood and a mile from home had established a lead of two hundred yards, which was never reduced. The leaders were running strongly at this point but positions remained unchanged over the last mile. Clements is improving steadily this season and excelled himself to win in a time which is 16 seconds faster than that of Stimpson last year.

Wood was below the brilliant form he has displayed earlier in the season; and Martin, too, was unlucky in having an off-day and failing to improve on his two second places in this race in last years. Herbert agreeably surprised us with his running and will be a valuable addition to our "Southern Junior" team. The above and Lucas will form the nucleus of our team, and backed up by such keen and capable performers as Merryman, Powell; Field, Deadman and Selby, they should regain the "Southern Junior" title.

AWAY FIXTURES.

H. W. SHIELDS

By H. W. SHIELDS.

It is with much regret that I have noticed the serious falling-off of members at away fixtures.

This falling-off is almost entirely due to members who have recently joined the club, and members who do not consider they have any chance of getting into teams. It is to these members that I wish to address this note.

To new members I say, most earnestly, make a habit of attending all away runs. You will soon find how enjoyable it is to know other clubs, members and courses. Don't be afraid you will be too slow. These clubs go to great pains in order to suit you. Remember, races are not all run at Wimbledon and if you are anxious to do well you must get used to as great a variety of courses as possible. Lastly (but not least) you will find there is no better way of getting to know your fellow club-members.

To the other type of member I have mentioned, I can assure them that all fixtures are given the most careful attention by the Committee, and great trouble is taken to get fixtures of the right sort. Even if they do not get into teams they can be assured of getting the best out of athletics, both socially and competitively, in this way,—and this is, of course, the happy state of affairs we all desire.

H. W. Shields

Feature · page 15

LOOKING TO THE FUTURE !

The Cross Country season has now reached the stage when our lads must test their strength against other clubs in the various open championships.

The South of Thames "Senior" race is this year being held at Horsham, and I am sure a most popular result would be a win for that evergreen "youngster," Tommy Marchant, of the local Blue Star Harriers. As regards the team race, this seems very open, although the Navy boys should be dangerous. Although we have to find a new team this year, our newcomers are shaping into a very useful lot, and I fully expect them to put up a good show. Anyway, I hope our supporters will travel to Horsham on 13th February, and give the lads every encouragement.

On the 20th we are due to travel to Aldershot for a run against the Command runners, and look forward to our first visit to their headquarters. For those who cannot make the journey we have a social run at Wimbledon with our old friends the Herne Hill Harriers.

February 27th—Southern Counties Day.—Both the Senior and Junior races will be held at Farningham—or as it is better known, Horton Kirby, the venue of the Inter-Varsity race. The race was last held here in 1929, when the old war-horse, Bill Cotterell, won the individual race. The course can be termed ideal cross-country, and should prove a test for the best. In the Junior race we should figure prominently and with K. N. Wood, A. F. Clements and that very promising newcomer, E. H. Herbert, backed up by W. E. Lucas, E. J. Selby and H. M. Quackenbos, our Junior Captain, we have a more than useful team and are very hopeful of regaining the title which we held in 1935. This last remark also applies to the Senior race, and on the form so far displayed, it should be a titanic struggle between Surrey A.C. and ourselves. The Surrey boys have for the last two years beaten us in this race although each time we have reversed matters in the "National" race. This time, however, I really think we shall do the trick. Our team is definitely stronger than last season, and should certainly "pack" better than in previous years. You will not regret a journey to Farningham on the 27th, and will see the cream of the Southern runners in a great battle for supremacy.

For those of our members who can find the time to make these trips I should like to remind them of the R.W.A. Junior Championship at Leicester on March 6th. I am sure our walkers would welcome the same measure of support that the runners receive.

"NATIONAL" DAY, March 13th. Venue: Stratford-on-Avon. Already we have been announced as "National champions," so I trust our lads will justify that optimism. The Birchfield team appears to be up to the strength of previous years, and with their wonderful record in this race, that should speak for itself. The point to remember, however, is that with Pat Parker, Les Cohen and Dan Patience, back from his sojourn in Egypt, we have three men who were not in last year's race. With these men to support Arthur Penny, Bert Footer and Tom Carter, not forgetting Jim Ginty, it would be a super-pessimist who would foreshadow our defeat. It will certainly require some wonderful packing on Birchfield's part to produce a scoring six equal to ours; and as this appears to be Surrey's unlucky race I predict a Belgrave victory with every confidence.

I hope we shall have a good crowd of supporters travelling to Stratford-on-Avon. Particulars of train, etc., will be posted at the "Rising Sun," or can be had on application to the Hon. Sec.

On the following Saturday, March 20th, we entertain Aldershot Command Harriers on their first visit to Wimbledon. This should give those members who have not run the previous week a chance of an outing, as it will be an all-to-score affair.

April 3rd is the day of our 5 Miles Road race with a sealed Handicap in conjunction. We use this race as a basis for selection of our Brighton Relay team and I trust we shall see a good turn-out of our road men. For those whose hopes do not fly so high may I remind them of the Handicap? This will be their last chance during the Winter season. So will all those who have been trying to "work their mark" (I won't mention names) turn out on the 3rd and see what you can do!

April 17th—BRIGHTON RELAY DAY. At least that was the date Joe Binks informed us it would be held on, and so far we have not heard of any alteration. What the course is going to be (by this I mean whether it will be the old Brighton main road, or the new course used last year) remains to be discovered. Last year's was far from satisfactory, but our sympathies are all with Joe in the troubles and difficulties he has to contend with in a race of this description. Come what may, I hope we shall continue to be the first team into Brighton. Remember the "locals" are getting used to us by now, and any other result would probably cause a great panic in the town. Also, remember our Pat may one day decide to return to the scenes of his former "triumphs," and as a member of the victorious Belgrave team will probably receive the freedom of the town! Any other result on the 17th may have disastrous effects to our curly-haired "broth of a spalpeen"! As in previous years the Club will have the usual Supporters' buses on the road. We charge 5/- for the return journey, and with the excitement of the race en route, together with a few hours by the sea before our return to Town, I think you will agree that you get your money's worth. For those who would like to put down their names in advance, will they please apply, together with cash, to Alf. Harley, who is the usual charge d'affaires on such occasions.

With the Brighton Relay over, the Winter season, so far as the runners are concerned, comes to an end; and we begin to get our track shoes smartened up for the forthcoming Summer season.

This year owing to the dressing rooms at Battersea Park being reconstructed (a job which we understand will not be finished until the end of May,) we are forced to make a change in venues for our first two track meetings. We have booked Tooting Bec Track for May 12th, and May 22nd. The former is, of course, "Coronation Day" but I think an evening meeting on this date should be popular with our members. The usual Inter-Club relays with Croydon H., Mitcham A.C. and Epsom H. will also be held on one of these dates; and I hope our members will make it their duty to give Harry Shields and Les Bennett the support that is necessary for us to retain our hold on the Inter-Club Relay Shield.

Other fixtures during May include our usual visit to Drayton Green Track for the Inter-Club match with Ealing Harriers, this being on the 8th. Then on the 15th we travel to Broomfield Park for a four-cornered contest with Southgate H., Blackheath H., Polytechnic H. and ourselves. The local club are making arrangements for this meeting to be part of the Borough's Coronation celebrations, so I hope we shall be able to field a much stronger team than that which made the journey last season.

We have once again received an invitation to compete in the "Kinnaird" Trophy competition which will be held at the Polytechnic Harriers' meeting at the White City on May 29th.

Among the fixtures already arranged are visits to Battersea Park of the Leigh-on-Sea H. and Imperial College A.C. on June 9th, while on July 31st, the London Athletic Club will be our opponents. They expressed a wish to come once again to Battersea Park, and we are very pleased to welcome them.

The Club Championships have been fixed for Saturday, July 3rd. This is earlier than has been usual in past years, but it was thought that if our championships were decided before the A.A.A. General Championships, we should have a much better line on which to work when considering the men we should enter for the National Championships. It is also hoped that the earlier date will ensure larger entries for all the events, as many of our members are compelled to accept August Bank Holiday week for their annual vacation. In fact, we are confidently expecting this Club Championship day to be the most successful of the long series, and the earlier date to be a commendable innovation.

The thorny question of a Club Coach has been considered by your Committee; and we are very hopeful of securing the services of an old-time member who, we feel sure, would meet our requirements in this connection. Coaches are few and far between. The few well-known are already heavily booked up with various clubs, and if we can secure a man whose services will be exclusive to our own members, I am sure this will prove more satisfactory than having a man who has training interests in, at least, half-a-dozen clubs.

Just a few words to new members. If you feel that you are not getting acquainted with your fellow-members as readily as you would like, please do me a favour and drop me a line and I will do my best to remedy that state of affairs. Once you get to know our boys I am sure you will agree that a better set of chaps could not be found elsewhere, so don't get that "out in the cold" feeling. Get in touch with the Asst. Hon. Sec. at once, without delay.

OUR OPEN WALK.

One hundred and fifty entries were received for our 17th annual promotion at Wimbledon, and this must be considered very satisfactory when compared with entries for similar races elsewhere. Of the 150, 28 were Belgrave Harriers, constituting four teams, which shows that our own walkers gave their best support.

Our President started the race, and it was soon obvious that there would be a terrific struggle for scratch team honours. The positions fluctuated continually and it was only in the last few yards that Woodford Green A.C. could be sure of a win over Surrey W.C., and then only by one point. Our team was only a further five points away, and as our fourth man actually finished in front of Woodford's fourth, the keen nature of the struggle for team honours can easily be realised.

Individually, Stone, of the Poly. Harriers, defeated our Harry Churcher by a yard, with A. A. Cooper third, over two minutes away. The individual handicap went to L. J. Wells, of Surrey W.C., and a second-claim member of our club, with an allowance of 5 min. 30 sec. His actual finishing position was 13th, and on this occasion it could not be regarded as unlucky! Joe Coleman (11th) took second handicap prize, and Harry Churcher was placed third, with the assistance of a one-minute allowance. Despite the fact that our "A" team had two of the three placed men in the individual handicap, they were unplaced in the team handicap. Victory here went to Surrey Walking Club, our "D" team through the efforts of Messrs. Boddington, Clark, Earle, and Jarvis, being second.

Fifteen of the first 52 to finish the course were Belgrave Harriers, which is a splendid indication of good all-round strength. The prize for the first Belgrave Harrier not gaining any other award went to Len Fletcher, and a similar prize for M.P.A.A. members was won by Jack Bidgood.

In conclusion, we should like to express the Club's grateful thanks to those who helped the promotion along. We are particularly indebted to Messrs. Askew, Bellfield, Gordon and Thomson for generous donations to the Prize Fund; to Mr. Allen for providing one of the prizes; and to the R.W.A. officials, and the competitors whose annual help and support is much appreciated.

THE "JUNIOR" CHAMPIONSHIP.

December 12th was a miserable day, but considering the number of "Seniors" we now have in our ranks not eligible to compete for the "Horwood" Cup, the entry must be considered satisfactory. There were some walkers missing that we should have liked to see in action, and curtailment of Police leave due to the special calls of this time, robbed us of all but one of our Police members.

Joe Coleman took the lead at the start, but was ruled out after a mile, when he had a fifteen yards advantage over Charlie Speechley. The oldest junior in the race stayed in front for the remainder of the race, being content to do this and return slow time. Douglas Earle walked splendidly and his fastest race yet to take second position; with George Boddington repeating his good walking of our "Open" event and being placed third.

The sealed handicap went to Len Coleman, who shows improvement every time out, and will be with the leaders before long. Earle claimed second award, and Bill Snell the third prize. J. A. Bellchambers, son of our Past President, made his dêbut in this race, and as he matures physically will undoubtedly prove himself as valuable to future teams as his father was to past Belgrave teams.

Column · page 21

WITH THE WALKERS.

Five of our walkers paid Christmas penance and walked in the Boxing Day Croydon to Godstone and back race of 18¾ miles. Charlie Churcher, our Captain, distinguished himself by finishing third in his first attempt at this distance, being only 2 mins. 23 secs. behind the winner, D. Christie-Murray, of Surrey W.C. Jack Bidgood also did well to finish fifth on his initial walk over this course. Times, etc., of our men were: 3rd, C. Churcher, 2h. 40m. 28s.; 5th, J. Bidgood, 2h. 43m. 16s.; 14th, D. E. Brown, 2h. 57m. 37s.; 15th, W. H. Snell, 2h. 57m. 52s.; 16th, A. A. Harley, 2h. 57m. 55s.

Our team was second in the scratch event; and won the handicap team race, the successful trio consisting of Messrs. Bidgood, Churcher and Snell.

* * * * *

On December 5th we made our usual visit to Croydon, to oppose Surrey W.C. over their West Wickham and back course of 9¾ miles. We had fourteen walkers present, probably our best attendance yet for this race, and had hopes of winning the inter-club race for the first time. Harry Churcher walked wonderfully well to win, by nearly a minute from Astill, of the home team. Then came C. E. Churcher and J. C. Bidgood, and with three in four we had made an excellent start. Unfortunately for us, our packing was not so good in the middle portion of the race, and eventually our Surrey friends claimed a win, scoring 163 points to our 188. So ended a very close and exciting race. With the assistance of Len Fletcher, Joe Coleman and Jack Rutland, who were prevented from turning out, it would have been even closer and we should probably have claimed a win. However, away with "might-have-beens," congratulations and thanks to our good friends of the Surrey Walking Club!

* * * * *

On February 20th Valentine's S.C. hold their Open 10 Miles race at Chadwell Heath, and this will be the last try-out before the "Junior" Championship. This event has now taken the date formerly reserved to Ashcombe A.C., who promoted instead a seven miles race a month earlier.

* * * * *

March 6th is "Junior" Championship day, and the race takes place at Leicester. At the time of writing it is hard to weigh up the chances of the teams, but it certainly seems a very open race. After our win of last year, and the necessity of finding a new scoring team, our own chances of winning again would be ignored by most outside critics. Additionally, we are disappointed that Reg. Elson, E. C. Heather and L. Storey, all experienced "Junior" racing men, have not been able to train regularly this year, but we hope to have their assistance next year. Despite the foregoing, we are far from being out of the race, and a sound team will represent the Club. We should most certainly maintain our excellent record in this race and take a place; and with a little luck (or should we write "if the team can rise to the occasion") we may well find ourselves winners for the sixth time. Anyway, we can be sure the team will do credit to the Club, and so "Best of Luck!"

* * * * *

The County Walking Championships take place on March 20th, and a week later the Inter-counties team race will be held at Horsham.

* * * * *

The Club 20 Miles championship for the "R. W. Ricketts" Shield, and sealed handicap in conjunction, will be held on April 24th. A match versus Metropolitan Police W.C. will also add interest.

* * * * *

The National title over 20 miles will be contested in the Epsom neighbourhood, the grandstand being used as dressing accommodation. What are our prospects of taking the Championship from Surrey Walking Club? Time alone will show, but we are certain our team will not spare themselves in their endeavours to regain the title.

SOCIAL MATTERS.

Nobody needs reminding that this year is the Club's Jubilee Year, and that no effort should be spared to make 1937 the most successful year in Belgravian history. Those responsible for the organisation of the social side hope that every member will support the various social functions during Jubilee Year, and make them occasions to be remembered for many years to come.

The most important social function is the Golden Jubilee Dinner and Dance that will be held on May 1st at the Quadrant Restaurant, Regent Street, W.1. This venue must be convenient for everyone, being within one minute's walk of Piccadilly Circus. It is hoped to make this affair a really outstanding occasion, with as many as possible present. Dancing will follow the Dinner and, for the convenience of those who so desire, our Social Hon. Secretary and his assistants have arranged to accept payment by instalments during the month or two preceding the function, so that the expense will not fall too heavily on one week's income. This is a facility which all (and Hon. Editors in particular, I can assure you) should find most helpful.

Our next Dance takes place at the Queensberry Hall, South Kensington, on February 20th. Our New Year's Eve Dance at this Hall can be written down as a great success, and we are confident that those who paid taxi fares home, or who were late to work in the morning, can have no regrets.

A further Social at Wimbledon has been arranged for April 3rd. Note the date.

All enquiries and applications for tickets to: A. E. TAYLOR, 11, Fitzneal Street, W.12.

A. E. Taylor

THE HON. TREASURER'S NOTES.

W. G. WEBB, Hon. Treasurer.

Firstly, I desire to express my appreciation of the very kind wishes and thoughts tendered to me on the occasion of my marriage. I thank you also for the very serviceable and attractive clock presented to me; a most desirable article which will not only enable me to keep pace with the times, but will serve as a constant reminder of the many happy years that the Club has given me. It is on occasions such as this that one can readily be persuaded to believe that there is, after all, little or no wisdom in the Immortal advice of Punch.

Secondly, I should like to thank all those members who so kindly responded to my circular letter regarding subscriptions. As to those who have not already done so, I feel sure I can count on that spirit of steadfast loyalty that has always animated them and may expect to hear from them shortly together with, of course, the requisite enclosure.

Whilst on the subject of subscriptions, there appears to exist among members some misunderstanding as to when subscriptions become payable. Some members believe that the date of payment is governed by the date of their election to the Club. Others believe that payment is only at such times as they are inclined to athletic activity; whilst apart from those who habitually leave payment until the close of the season, there are those who genuinely believe that payment is not due until this time, assuming that the Annual General Meeting marks the close of the season. Actually the 15th September of each year is the date on which the Club Financial year closes, and the A.G.M. which takes place on the last Tuesday of that month marks the opening of the new season.

In any case, however, the subscription is required to be paid once only and in this connection I would draw the attention of all members to the Club Rules (which this year are printed in the Club Handbook) with particular reference to rule 9 (a), which states that all subscriptions become due and payable on the date of the Annual General Meeting.

And now there may be a few members who have not received my circular regarding outstanding subscriptions. This may be for one of two reasons, or both. (1) Change of address and failure to notify the Club. (2) Being in arrears of subscription for the past season.

To those under (1) I would suggest that they communicate with me at once.

To those under (2) I am writing, and in all probability my letter will be in their hands before this Gazette is published. It will be appreciated, and it will certainly lighten the duties of Club officials, if these members will kindly reply to this letter indicating one way or other their attitude as to membership having regard to the conditions of Rule 9, paras. a, b, c, e.

The record of the Club is unique and the Committee recognise with satisfaction the great position which members have won for themselves, and the Club in the world of athletics generally. It needs, however, strenuous effort and the close co-operation of all in all the many obligations if this high standard is to be maintained. The payment of subscription when due is no less important an obligation than any other.

W. G. WEBB, Hon. Treasurer.

W. G. Webb

A GREAT OPPORTUNITY FOR BELGRAVE SPRINTERS.

By L. G. BENNETT (Track Vice-Captain).

It is with a great deal of regret that I feel I am compelled to write this short article. In the past the performances of our sprinters have been mediocre. What are the reasons? In my opinion the reasons are:—

(a) Lack of regular and systematic training; (b) A certain lack of team-spirit.

Let me make it quite clear that I am not in any way referring to the small band of sprinters who, last season, gave their whole-hearted support when called upon by the Club to run in team events. The individual performances of these loyal Club-men show that the experience gained in the team races has proved invaluable.

I would like to see the sprinters self-supporting; that is, carry through in the various relay races entirely with sprinters, and not borrow runners who favour longer distances.

Sprinters! You can make your training a real pleasure by getting together and training together, thus creating and conserving the real Club atmosphere which is so vitally necessary to the well-being and even life of the Belgrave Harriers.

Competition for places in the Club's sprint teams should be really keen; and all of you should have the enthusiasm which goes to make just an ordinary team on paper into a winning team in practice. The WILL TO WIN is needed. The talent is in all of you and can be brought out by training with your colleagues in a regular and systematic manner.

All sprinters in the Club have this year a great opportunity of winning for Belgrave, in its Golden Jubilee Year, some of the highest honours on the track. So please take this article as "a call to action," and turn out in your dozens in the approaching track season. Seniors! encourage the Juniors.

In conclusion, let me wish you every success and happiness in the future!

L. G. Bennett

Letter · page 26

TO SPRINTERS.

Frank Simmons writes on similar lines to Les Bennett above and says: "The comparative weakness of our sprinters has been referred to in previous issues of 'The Belgravian,' and it has been suggested that the Club employs the services of a coach. Whilst favouring this, one wonders 'Is it worth while?' From personal observation last season, I noticed a well-known coach concentrating on one Olympic possible, and neglecting the others! Of what good is this?

"Our cross-country men have not needed a professional coach, and yet have given us a series of sterling performances. The secret of this success is, no doubt, regularity in training, and concerted training. If the sprinters really mean to do well, they must train together in the same way. In the past they have turned up at all sorts of times, and so invariably trained alone, with the tendency to do either too much or too little."

"If we are sincere in our desire to change all this, let us respond to Les Bennett's appeal and 'get together.' Then we shall really become a force to be reckoned with in all classes of sprint events."

Frank SimmonsLes Bennett
