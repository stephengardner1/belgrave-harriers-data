Editorial · page 3

CHANGES AHEAD

While at grass roots level little seems to be changing in the athletics world, at International level the gradual march towards Semi-Professionalism goes on unabated. The money which will come into our sport in the years ahead will pose special problems for Clubs and it is far from clear how it will all end. Take for example the pulling power of Televised events. From 1985 onwards the AAA's and BAAB will receive over a 5 year period monies exceeding £13.5 million from TV and sponsorship deals. Somehow much of this must come through to the Clubs and the AAA's recognise that without the support of Clubs, their officials, organising expertise and not least of all the support of the athletes themselves the whole thing could go badly wrong.

The year ahead should see many points resolved and it is important that Belgrave has a view and a say in these important decisions. Whatever the outcome we must realise that our top athletes will be part of a world which we have not seen before. Let us hope that their hard work, dedication and talent will be fairly rewarded. Let us also hope that they will remember their roots and continue to support all those who have supported them in the past and who will continue to support them in the future.

We were delighted to see President Tom Carter and Ethel at our British League home fixture in August. Stuart Paton's Club 1500m record has been bettered several times this year by Stuart himself, Gary Staines and John Gladwin. Kevin Sturman and Paul Richley departed our shores mid-summer for life at U.S. Universities. Kevin has returned to South Ilinois while Paul has gone to Iowa State with his sparring partner from Windsor - Andrew Pettigrew.

Gordon Doubleday still visits us from Bath whenever possible. Gordon has recently been elected a Life Member of Somer A.C. and in September was re-elected President of "The Centurians" whose membership is confined to those walkers who have covered 100 miles in 24 hours.

Our Vet sprints record holder - Charlie Gillett's hidden talents surfaced in a recent edition of The Sunday Telegraph's Colour Supplement. To quote the summary of a revealing 3 page article 'He is one of rock music's unsung heroes; an authoritative writer and broadcaster, a sharp talent spotter (he championed Elvis Costello and Dire Straights). His book, "The sound of the City", published in 1970 and now revised and re-issued, was a classic in a field which then hardly existed.'

Funny how conversations with strangers often turn to running. A recent chat with a Leatherhead football club supporter revealed he had shared a bunkroom with Ted Stimpson in the 1940-41 war. 'Taffy' Jones sends his best wishes to Sargent Ted of 345 Heavy Anti Aircraft Battery - Royal Artillery. Taffy remembers "Ted was always dashing off somewhere to race a mile".

Honary Coaching member Mike Dunphy and wife Ann are moving house from Welwyn Garden City to High Wycombe. Mike who has coached many of our fine middle distance men will be cutting back on some of his coaching commitments. We wish them happiness in their new house.

Simon Williamson indicated to Bob Bridges that he would be unavailable for Southern League duties this summer. On being pressed he revealed that wedding bells in September were the reason. We wish the bride and groom good fortune.

Remember those promising Youths -Andy North and Michael Blackmore? We hear that Andy has been invited to Michael's wedding in January but as it clashes with the Surreys we will have to lay on some high speed transport if our top Junior is to make both events.

We also hear that there is another little Gleave on the way. Congrats and best wishes to Mum and Dad.

DINNER DANCE

A new organising duo, a change of date plus a change of venue provided the backdrop for this years Dinner/Dance. It was with some trepidation therefore, that Bill Couzens and Brian Pritchard awaited the response as tickets went on sale. They need not have worried. JOE COLEMAN - PRESIDENT

Joe Coleman was elected a member on the 6th October 1927. From early days he was involved in all aspects of the Club and performed with distinction at Track and Field. In the mid thirty's he was scoring in our top Walking teams. He gained a first class standard in the 1938 AAA's Championships and won the coveted 'Fowler Dixon' style award. In 38 and 39 he was 3rd in our Open walk behind Harry Churcher. He achieved National 'fame' in 1939 when he and brother Len deadheated in the RWA National 10 mile Championship, and led Belgrave to a team win.

Like many top class athletes his career was interrupted by the 2nd World War but he came back to score again in many of our Walking teams. In 1952 Joe was elected a Vice President of Surrey CAAA for whom he had competed in the Inter County Championships in earlier years. His experience and knowledge was passed on to many young athletes when he became a Grade 1 Judge in the early 1950's. Since then Joe has enhanced the reputation of Belgrave in the Walking World as he has travelled the Country judging top class events.

A life time spent with the club and now the holder of its highest Office we can be certain that Belgravians will benefit from his vast experience and knowledge of the athletic world in the year ahead.

W.G. 'BILL' WEBB - 90 YEARS YOUNG

On 9 August 1984 Bill Webb celebrated his 90th birthday. As he himself said, "There is nothing particularly meritorious in reaching 90, I just happened to be born with the right genes!" But he was also born with a charm which has endeared him to successive generations of Belgravians.

Bill Webb joined Belgrave in 1927, following a period of six years at Polytechnic Harriers and several traumatic wasted years in the first World War. At 33 years of age he was a mature newcomer who made his mark immediately by finishing 8th in the Yacht '3' that year with 15:55 (off 25 secs). In only his second match for Belgrave he closed-in our scoring team just behind first-team regulars Wally Bayliss and Micky Pyer. The Belgravian Scribe was induced to write "The runners have lately been joined by W.G.Webb and A.Robertshaw and I suggest that the presence of these valuable men gives our team a new impetus of enthusiasm."

Webb and Robertshaw were just the injection Belgrave needed and they celebrated by gaining bronze team medals in the South of Thames Senior of 1928 - our first medals in the event for 24 years! Bill went on to be our second scorer in the "Southern" and our first man home in the "National" at Leamington. A final flourish in the London-Brighton Relay and he had in one season established himself as a remarkably consistent runner who would always respond to the occasion. Within a year he was elected Captain.

Bill Webb's charming disposition concealed a very determined athlete, but it also revealed a willing Belgravian prepared to step into the shoes of W.H.Hare who had done so much to improve our finances. In 1943 he was elected Honorary Treasurer, a post he held for 14 years, the longest on record.

His service to the Club was first acknowledged in 1948 when he was elected President for two years. His 90th birthday was the occasion for fellow-Belgravians to make a further acknowledgement. At Belgrave Hall on Saturday 11 August a large gathering of members enjoyed a social evening in the company of Bill Webb who enraptured his audience with a lucid account of those early days. To mark the occasion our President, Tom Carter, presented him with a cut-glass decanter bearing a silver necklace engraved with the good wishes of the Club to which he has remained so loyal.

Dear Mr. President,

OFFICIAL CHANGES

Structure

Our growing membership has resulted in greater activity in all sections and over the last few years it has become increasingly difficult to handle all Club business at monthly Committee meetings. Derek Crookes has been looking at ways of streamlining operations and his master plan was put to an EGM in the summer which approved the new arrangements. From now on the Committee will comprise of all Sectional Secretaries (or nominees) plus the Club's Trustees and 5 members elected at the AGM. Past Presidents may attend in a non-voting capacity and there will be many occasions when their specific past experiences and knowledge will be sought.

In parallel with this re-organisation a series of Sub-Committees will be set up based on separate Sectional activities and interests. These groups will be charged with promoting their Sections activities and will produce plans, targets and budgets. It is intended that as our finances move back into a healthier state following the Belgrave Hall re-development programme, each Section will be provided with its own monies following approval by the Committee of individual Section's targets and budget plans. In this way individual Sectional Secretaries and Team Managers will have greater freedom in day to day operations and financial matters. Naturally proper accounting will be required and at the end of each year results will be compared with targets. It will be up to each Section to argue its case and get the funding it needs.

We will need to feel our way into these new arrangements. New people are coming into the Management side. There will be difficulties but with our usual tolerance and understanding problems will be quickly ironed out and we can move to a simpler and more effective management system.

People

Each year at the September AGM we say farewell to the retiring President and welcome the new one. A hearty vote of thanks was given to Tom Carter for all his work during a year of office made particularly difficult by Ethel's illness. The meeting was particularly sad as both Derek Crookes and Jim Heathfield stepped down from office following terms of service spanning a period of time during which Belgrave's fortunes have seen a quite remarkable upturn.

HON. GEN. SEC. Derek Crookes took over the post of Hon. Gen.Sec. in September 1978 and played an active and major part in the amalgamation plans. It was mainly Derek's efforts which resulted in the influx of the fine athletes and officials from Surrey A.C. who now play an important part in our expanding activities. During his 6 year period as Hon.Gen.Sec. Derek was elected President in 1981 and the following year served as joint President with Geoff Davis. In his final year of office he took the initiative in proposing and developing the re-organised Management Structure which has now come into operation. His services will not be lost completely as he has taken on the vacant post of Coaching Secretary. We can expect that there will soon be improvements to our Club Coaching arrangements once Derek has turned his full attention to the problems which currently exist. (For further details of Derek refer to the October 81 Belgravian).

Bill Laws takes over as Ho.Gen.Sec. on the promise that the new Organisational Structure will make the job less onerous. Bill joined Belgrave in 1952 while at Emanuel School. His membership lapsed when he moved North and he rejoined in 1963. He became a Committee member in 1974 and organised the Dinner/Dance for 5 years. His forte is looking after special projects. He negotiated the Sports Council Grants for the 1976 Hall improvement and the G.L.C. Grants for the latest Hall developments. In 1982 Bill spent quite a lot of time persuading the GLC to invest in putting the all weather track at our home track in Battersea Park. Bill gained a first in Mech. Engineering at Brunel University. He is a Chartered Engineer and is a Fellow of the Institute of Mechanical Engineers and a Fellow of the Institute of Energy. He was Project Manager for Belgrave on the latest Hall Development. In his spare time he has Edited the Belgravian since September 1979 and after producing the last 10 Editions is looking for someone else to ease into the job over the next year or so.

HON. TREASURER Jim Heathfield took over the job of Hon. Treasurer in September 1979 and under his careful control funds soon grew to a level which permitted the recent Hall development project to proceed. Whereas most Club Officials perform duties different from their business activities, with Jim Belgrave work was very much an additional burden as he is an Accountant within the Ministry of Defence! With turnover now well into 5 figures keeping all the Accounts and cash flows under control, is practically a full time occupation, and it is much to Jim's credit that he managed to convey an air of complete calm during periods of difficulty. Unlike many Accountants, Jim was always anxious that our hard won savings should be put to work for the Club's future and his wholehearted support for our development projects has been an essential part of the success story. Sadly Jim is 'resting down' due to pressure of work but hopes to become involved again in the not to distant future. We thank him for all his efforts on our behalf.

Ron Severn has taken up the mantle of Hon. Treasurer and it would be difficult to find someone better qualified. A lawyer and Accountant by profession he has already made an impact in the short period he has been with Belgrave (see the April 84 Belgravian for a full C.V.!). Ron has been posing the sort of questions about our aims and aspirations which someone fresh to the scene can do. We welcome Ron to his first Belgrave Office and wish him good luck in the year ahead.

BELGRAVE HALL DEVELOPMENT

Well, its finished! And everyone who visits Belgrave Hall is amazed at the transformation of our Headquarters. Upstairs we have created one of the finest club rooms you could wish to find. With; new windows faithfully repeating the original pattern, a £6000 maple floor lovingly sanded and polished by Harry Porter, new gas fired radiators plus velvet curtains and carpets in club colours, we have a most fitting setting for the Club Honours Boards. Add to this delicate colouring scheme to augment the soft furnishings and it is no wonder that Bill Couzens has been inundated with requests to hire the new facility. Already bookings from Art and Exercise classes are near capacity and our investment is beginning to show the cash returns which the Finance Committee was predicting two years ago.

Downstairs the mens changing facilities have been remodelled to give a slightly larger floor area following the removal of the old staircase. The interest in the new Gymnasium has been fantastic. Within three weeks of opening Mike Winch and Judy Oakes reported full capacity with opening on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays from 2 p.m. to 9 p.m. International athletes from as far afield as St. Albans make the trek to Belgrave Hall to obtain the benefit of the new facilities. Many of our members including nine of our British League team now train regularly in the Gymnasium with Mike and we look forward to big improvements in their 1985 performances. Interspersed with the specialist training sessions are strength and fitness classes for ladies and other members of the public. All this is again providing a big boost to our income.

Now that the operating patterns are getting sorted out following the inevitable teething problems, we can look forward to a good social calendar and hopefully you will come along and take advantage of your Club's facilities.

We are indeed fortunate that a major scheme of this nature can be planned, financed and progressed solely through the voluntary work so freely given by a few of our loyal members. Let us, therefore, through the pages of the Belgravian thank all who gave so much of their time and efforts and in particular; Ian Horton whose original conceptual designs formed the basis for the scheme as it has evolved, Tom Carter who has acted as Clerk of the Works making frequent site visits to check progress, Bill Couzens who has been responsible for specifying and procuring the various services, Bill Laws who was Project Manager and finally Harry Porter who has saved the Club so much money by putting the finishing touches to the Contractor's structural work.

We also owe a big debt of thanks to our Architect - David Lawton, R.I.B.A. who fulfilled the targets we set with great dedication and professionalism and at a fee especially reduced as his contribution to this worthwhile project.

And finally a big thank you to all those who have contributed to the Development by responding to our Appeal Fund. Over the two years to June 1984 donations totalled £1,496 but were swelled in September following a direct appeal by Past President Charlie Manning which added a further £1,085 to the funds for the Hall development.

SOUTHERN LEAGUE ROUNDUP

After the disappointing 1983 season when the 'B' team slipped to Division 3 in the Southern League, the Bels came roaring back in 1984 to win every single match and have bounced straight into Division 2. Not only did every match result in victory but the points scores were phenomenal - our highest being a massive 160 out of a possible 180 (which would need a win in every event, 'A' and 'B'). Our average score over the season was as high as we have ever attained in any single Southern League match over the past 15 years.

Why the change in our Fortunes? The reason surely is our increasing depth of good performers in virtually all events, and here we must thank Eddie Bojtler for pushing so many athletes in our direction. Now that our 4th, 5th and 6th ranked men can produce 40 metre hammer throws, run sub-23 200 metres etc., it means that even with athletes barred through 'A' team duty in the National League side, or on holiday or injured, we can still turn out an impressive 'B' side.

Now it's time to think of 1985. Can we go straight through Division 2 to the Southern League's premier competition? Its possible. And with members now having to prove themselves to get an outing with our 'B' side, perhaps we should start thinking seriously about a 'C' team - if we can find the officials to get it going!

ERNEST OBENG'S LATE SEASON UPS AND DOWNS

1984 was a year of mixed fortune for our leading sprinter. He struggled to find his best form early on but it was Olympic year and, having missed out on Moscow, Ernie was encouraged enough to pay his own way to LA for probably his last chance to compete in the great event. Well no doubt you know the story of Ernie's expulsion from the Games by Ghanaian officials in what seems an incredible case of victimisation. It was obviously a great disappointment to him not to be able to compete and the character assassination that went with the affair added insult to injury.

It was perhaps some small consolation to Ernie that he should hit some of his best ever form in August - as if to show that he would have performed with great credit to his country in LA. For following a couple of ordinary clockings at the Nike Classic and in Budapest when still coping with jet lag, he flew to a 10.25 timing (his 3rd fastest legal mark) in Zurich on 22 August, only headed by Olympic Bronze Medallist Ben Johnson and Osvaldo Lara - winner of the Eastern Block 'Friendship' Games. Then in Rieti,Italy on 2 September he recorded 10.32 (+0.7) behind Penalver and Lara.

At the IAC/Coca-Cola meeting on 7 September, Ernie gave us at home the first glimpse of this return to form. He snapped away from the blocks to win his heat easing up in 10.45 into a slight headwind - but what a pity the start of the final was such a shambles with McFarlane getting a clear flyer and the race not being recalled by the starter. Although Lara's pick-up enabled him to win, Ernie,at a disadvantage with his own fast start pre-empted, couldn't quite make up the deficit and he finished 3rd in 10.38.

Ernest Obeng's Belgrave Honours badge well to the fore in the Peugeot Games held at the Crystal Palace on July 13th, - probably the last occasion he will wear Ghana's colours as he has vowed not to compete again for the Country which treated him so shabbily in Los Angeles Olympics. 29th SEPTEMBER 1984 CRYSTAL PALACE NIKE 25x1 MILE RELAY

Last year's first ever win in this Nationwide event led us to hope that with a better team (on paper) we could repeat our success. Sale running at Manchester had other ideas and as our 'A' team turned in times just marginally slower than we had anticipated Sale improved on their efforts of last year. We ran as usual at the Crystal Palace venue while 80 or so teams competed at 8 other venues. The race was close right up to the 18 mile mark when Pat Chester running for TVH ran a 4.08 effort to snatch the lead from the Bels. Nigel Clarke got a bit carried away on the next leg as the excitement built and ran his first 400 in 57.7! It was left to Peter Gilbey to take us into a lead which increased to 3 minutes over TVH over the next 5 miles. Everyone had been blaming the cold windy conditions for the 'slow' times, but either they were wrong or Gary Staines' 4.06 on the last leg was really something special. Running at even pace Gary finished in great style and even had to contend with being baulked by 2 Croydon runners who ran right in front of him as they changed the baton at the 2 lap mark. Several of our 'A' team men had just returned from holidays and it showed in their times. Our youngsters though ran superbly with Youths John Booth and Glyn Tiernan performing well as did Junior Richard O'Hara who is beginning to look a class runner.

In the 'B' team Francis Ward, who has returned from 2 years in the U.S.A. proved fastest with Andy Moore close behind. Our 'D' team comprised young members having an average age of 13 years and their general high level of performance promises well for the future.

Our thanks to Bill Couzens, Brian Pritchard and Derek Jones plus their families who put up with the trying conditions while organising and recording through the morning and afternoon sessions. Jim and Dave Mote organised the Young Athletes and yours truly the 'A'.

GARY STAINES' EXTRAORDINARY WEEK.

Team spirit has been a particular part of our continueing upward path in the British Athletics League. Perhaps the most remarkable example was the case of Gary Staines who in one hectic week in July ran 5 top class races. On the Sunday he ran a heat and won the final of the Inter-Counties 1 mile. On the Wednesday in an Open Meeting he ran a p.b. 800m in 1.50.0. On the Friday he worked in the morning and then dashed up to Gateshead to guest in the England v Spain v Sweden International where he ducked under Stuart's 1500m Club record and was prominant on Television as he led for 3 laps . And on the Saturday he travelled with the BAL team to Windsor and won the 800m as we achieved a massive points win over our rivals. With support like that our Officials know that all the hard work behind the scenes is worthwhile!

FIRST RELAY SUCCESS AS BELGRAVE WIN INAUGURAL LONDON RELAY

21st OCTOBER 1984 CITY OF LONDON 'ZATOPEK' 6x4½km THAMES RELAY

This inaugural race organised by ex 10,000m world record holders Ron Clarke and Dave Bedford was well publicised in the Local and National press and despite the previous day's National 6 stage relay received a great response from athletes and spectators. The course, chosen to keep clear of traffic, was not up to Championship Standards involving as it did several flights of steps. Nevertheless the atmosphere was there with commentary by Capital Radio's Kelly Temple and this coupled with free lunch and a drink plus party made the day memorable particularly for the loyal lady supporters. But to the race.

This was the first time we had got our strongest 6 together. Not all were fully fit and with many still beginning a gradual build up we weren't certain if the established relay stars would prove too strong for us. The start saw 60 runners streaming over Southwark Bridge with Oliver Foote tucked in in about 12th place. As the fast pace began to bite a group of 3 broke away and at the first change over Oliver was only 3 seconds down on Enfield's leader Keith Doulton. Dave North took it steadily moving into second spot as a gap of around 8 seconds opened between him and the leader. Looking more relaxed than usual and picking up the pace our man gradually pulled back the difference to hand over to Gary Staines in the lead. Gary moved off fast opened up a gap of 15 seconds, relaxed and then had a fright as Andy Catton of Ilford stormed past at the 2 mile mark. Not to be put off Gary knuckled down to some hard work and needed his sprint finish to put us back in the lead. Stuart Paton looked to be moving well as he opened the gap to around 25 seconds which Mark Sinclair needed as Kevin Steer of Ilford took off after him on the 5th leg. The race bubbled at this point as Kevin gradually reduced the gap to get within 3 seconds of Mark 600m out. It was at this point that Mark kicked and the Ilford mans efforts had been in vain. John Gladwin took off as though he was in a fast 1500 and broke Ilfords last stage runner to such an extent that they drifted back to 4th as Keith Palmer ran the fastest leg of the day to bring Highgate into second place 50 seconds adrift. Our'B' team men put up some good times to make a great day for Belgrave.

WITH THE LADIES

DEREK JONES Ladies Secretary

It has been quite an exciting season, with us finishing in 7th position in the Womens Southern League. This was our best position since 1980. In this League after 4 3rd placings we were lying 12th and we won the last 2. The last meeting was against strong opposition and we beat Aldershot, a team we had never beaten before so that was really pleasing. The Thameside League was a good replacement for the Lily B League and we hope that this will be continued for next season, especially as the athletes seemed to enjoy the competition. After a period of 6 years we re-entered the Midweek League (very similar in content to the Rosenheim League) and as we won every match we won the League very convincingly. There were plenty of places for the Non-scorers and everyone who came along was able to get competition.

Our Junior relay team was unbeaten in all 6 Southern League, all 4 Thameside League and all 6 Midweek League races which is another good record. During the Season Club records were broken on at least 40 occasions.

Three of our athletes gained their county vests. Wendy Dunsford represented Middlesex in a meeting in May held at Motspur Park, whilst Veronica St. Ange competed for Surrey in a representative match in July at Copthall and Rosemary Honeychurch represented Surrey in the Southern Womens Inter Counties Championships in August held at Crystal Palace.

Three of our athletes also won Gold in County Championships. Rosemary Honeychurch won the 3000m in the Surrey Championships, whilst Cindy James and Angela Abrams won the 100m and Hurdles in the Middlesex Championships. Our total tally of medals in both County Championships was 12, with 6 Silvers and 3 Bronzes. So congratulations to all the medal winners and all those who took part. This was our best haul of medals in County Championships as well.

Our walkers were well represented in the English Schools Walking Championships with London being represented by Gail Porter, Kim Marsh, Isobel Young and Charlotte Chirstou and Surrey and Essex being represented by Rachael Parrott and Joanne McGroder respectively.

Our Throwing team of Wendy Dunsford, Debbie Roberts, Veronica St. Ange as well as Perrican Bayar when she was available proved almost unbeatable. They won through although not always at their best and Veronica competed with broken bones in her foot. It is perhaps worth noting that we have competed in 40 Southern League fixtures since 1977 and Wendy has only missed 2 of them. She has been ever-present since June 1979 and in the Shot in the 30 matches since we were promoted to Division 3 has scored 139 points out of a possible 150.

Our Walkers have kept Belgrave vests to the fore and these have been led very well by Rachael Parrott ably backed by Isobel Young, Charlotte Christou and were ably backed by Kelly Owen, Julie Field and Teresa Thirlwall.
