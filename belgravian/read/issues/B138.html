EDITORIAL

Dear Fellow Members, It never rains but what it pours and before going to press we have been inundated with contributions for the magazine from all quarters. So please, those critics whose voices are exercised more frequently than their pens, don't itemise what has been omitted but be thankful that a far greater number of articles have been submitted.

Once again we have a fine weight putter in the Club, Don McCorquindale, who has made a fine start to the 1959 season. Charlie Jones must be greatly pleased with such an acquisition for our field events section.

Brian Morris and John Davies have already recorded good times for the 440 yds. and 1500 metres respectively, whilst Alan Black and Malcolm Robinson look set for their best seasons yet.

We are all justly proud of "our Sten" for his great win in Berlin. This was really one out of the bag and his margin over Matthews was quite astounding.

Lastly, a note of warning and possibly of hope. We have edited the Belgravian now for almost three years and as active marathon runners we feel that we have made our contribution towards the revival of the magazine and that perhaps some other aspiring editors in the Club would like to try their hand. When the A.G.M. comes round again we hope to be able to hand over this gazette to a new editor.

"HORWOOD" CUP CHAMPIONSHIP - DEC.13TH.

Ray Middleton repeated his victory of last year. Oscar Horwood, a great Club man, would have been delighted to see another very good winner on the list of fine walkers whose names are recorded on his cup. Ray has the potential of a champion just as surely as we saw it in others who have fulfilled that promise. He led in excellent style throughout. John Knifton, a few years younger than Ray, was placed for the third year in succession, which shows that hard discipline of training for several years is necessary to win this event.

A newcomer, K. Jones, did very well to finish third. Bob Barnes continues to improve and was unlucky to lose third place to this tough opponent.

The rest of the race was keenly fought, as always, and many unplaced were working as hard as those who gained the medals.

It was good to see Joe Wilson going so well after his accident and Bert East must have taken part in about twenty-five "Horwood Cup" races since the cup was instituted. At the same time there were novices who will be tomorrow's victors if they persevere.

Harry Evans, an old friend of Oscar's, was there to see fair play and to give advice where needed.

The ceremony of prize-giving brings a satisfying conclusion to an afternoon's sport and we were reminded of the thanks we owe to those who regularly organise the provision of prizes when, for once, the prizes and medals had, unfortunately, not arrived

HOG'S BACK ROAD RACE - DEC.20TH

Despite the counter attraction of a cross-country match, quite an appreciable number of Belgravians journeyed to Guildford for this inaugural 9¾ mile race from Farnham.

Dave Baulch and Chas. Walker were well to the fore early on, and with Colin Collis, Archie Bedford, Len Bishop and Bob Plummer quite well placed, it seemed as if we might gain a team place in this well contested race.

Baulch stayed up very well and left Walker who, feeling the effects of a cold, suffered on the two-mile downhill finishing stretch. Collis, when well placed, was beset with stomach cramp and forced to ease.

It was good to see some of our younger elements, namely D. Crookes and J. Radford finishing in the first half of a strong field.

No one could deny Portsmouth their amazing win, as they ran in together, 4th, 5th, 6th and 7th, while we finished a close fourth.

MATCH v. BLACKHEATH & H.H.H. AT HAYES - JAN.10TH.

Despite the Mitcham '15' accounting for many of our members we were still able to produce fifteen runners for this fixture, as against sixteen by Blackheath and nine by Herne Hill.

The 7½ mile course had been extremely muddy the previous week but had since frozen hard. As a result the going was particularly hazardous, and three runners retired with twisted ankles on the "plough" while many put caution before performance.

Mike Maynard set a fast pace at the start and was comfortably leading after a mile from John Moore and Chris Thompson with Clive Shippen and the Blackheath captain, A.W. Wood, a further 50 yards down.

After 4½ miles John Moore had established himself in second place, about 200 yards behind Maynard, while Wood and Shippen, still running together, had virtually caught Thompson. At this stage Chris badly twisted his ankle in one of the frozen furrows and although he tried gamely to continue he was forced to retire. However, he had done sufficient to show once more what tremendous potential he possesses, and Junior honours could no doubt come his way during the next two seasons.

Meanwhile John Davies and Jim Heathfield were doing battle with a string of Blackheath men a little further back. Both of our boys lost ground on the dangerous stretches with a sensible "safety first" policy but made it up in the last mile or so of even going; John, in particular, gaining several places.

Maynard won convincingly by about 450 yards from Moore who ran exceedingly well and was always moving away from the Blackheath man in third place.

Thompson's mishap did not affect the match result which was won by Blackheath's superior packing.

R.A.F. SEVEN MILES WALKING CHAMPIONSHIP.

B.E.

This year's R.A.F. race might be described as one of Belgrave's triumphs. It was held at Harpur Hill, in North Derbyshire, on 28th January; a day when the bright sunshine and clean, crisp air made racing pleasant in spite of the hilly terrain. The course consisted of one small lap and a larger one of fairly good roads with not too much traffic.

Terry Clark headed the field from the start and was never overtaken. The good walking of a "dark horse" in the person of Flt.Lt. O.G.J. Hotston during the early stages of the race gave rise to surprise and a measure of anxiety. Usually a cross-country runner for R.A.F. Lindholme, this Officer went after Terry and tenaciously hung on to second place until he began to tire. A.C. Desmond of R.A.F. Bridgenorth and Essex Beagles came through after the first three quarters of a mile in an endevour to get on terms with Terry and, in the meantime, Bernard Eglinton was battling it out for fourth place with Nigel Deeks of R.A.F. Cosford and Met. Walking Club. The former subsequently gained a lead of about thirty five yards.

At two and a half miles, Holston was slowing down perceptibly and the second Belgrave man succeeded in passing him at about three miles.

The course was now a steady climb to the five-mile point, followed by a steep downhill gradient and an equally severe uphill grind of about a mile to the finish. Terry quickened his pace and disappeared from view but Desmond seemed to slow a little on the hills. Deeks made a valiant bid for third place in the last mile and a half, actually drawing almost level with Eglinton on the last hill. On this occasion, however, age prevailed by a mere ten seconds.

SOUTHERN COUNTIES C.C. CHAMPIONSHIPS - FEB.14TH.

C.S.

Hosts for this year's event were Aylesford Paper Mills who, not for the first time, provided ideal facilities for championships of this nature.

The course was ideal for runners and spectators alike, while the weather man had assisted the arrangements by providing perfect conditions underfoot and a fine sunny day into the bargain.

YOUTHS EVENT (3 miles)

Belgrave's day did not get off to a very good start, for with four to score in the team event we were only able to field three runners - Eric Penny, Mike Smithers and Roy Bagley. How many of our absent youths appreciated just how much their presence was needed?

Eric, in 114th position, led our trio home; all of whom performed creditably. No doubt the efforts of these lads will be better rewarded when some of Tom Carter's promising youngsters move into their ranks.

The event was won by Dave Harvey (Mitcham) who ran an excellently judged race to beat Smith(Deptford Park). It is interesting to note that Harvey is second string in his club to Harris, a 'flu victim during this event, and also that all these Mitcham youths are enthusiastically coached by the ex-Belgrave Mitchell brothers. We congratulate them on their excellent results.

SENIOR EVENT (9 miles)

Influenza had taken its toll of many leading athletes, but this made the team race more open than ever. Unfortunately, for a variety of reasons, Archie Bedford, John Davies, Colin Collis and Clive Shippen were unable to run, so with six to score and a "12 cup" in the offing we were able to start nine only. The number was eight to begin with but Malcolm Robinson, despite a suspect ankle, made a fine gesture in offering to run, thus making our entry look more respectable.

Yet again the Arthur Whitehead "dustbin" sent the field on its way and after a ½ mile Pat Newell was 14th with Chanin(S.L.H.) leading from Ibbottson and Norris. John Moore had also got off to a good start, being in the forties, with Malcolm next man in the eighties. Charlie Walker, Eric Gebbett and Dave Baulch were labouring around the hundred mark.

At two miles, with Maynard(H.H.H.) leading from Eldon and Knight, Pat was 15th, John 46th and our sixth scoring man in the nineties. At six miles, Eldon had established a substantial lead from Knight and Sando with Maynard 4th. Our lads had shewn considerable improvement in their team placings and were steadily pulling back the opposition with six men now in 87 and eight in 126. In the last mile the whole team made an extra special effort, pulling back over forty places amongst them. Special mention must go to our first and last man; to Pat Newell on his excellent 14th place and Alan Bishop for his most creditable 151st on this very fast course.

Belgrave were the first team to finish seven, and also eight, runners but Old Belgravians will no doubt wince to hear that no club qualified for the twelve cup.

Ex-members, Geoff Goode and Steve Charlton were seen running for their new clubs. Semi-retired Bagnall would have been our third scoring man with his 56th place!!

Over 400 finished.

37 teams closed in.

JUNIOR EVENT (6 miles)

Apart from the unfortunate absence of Chris Thompson through injury, the Juniors were at full strength and following his excellent running the previous week hopes were pinned on a forward position by Alan Black. These hopes appeared to be well founded when, after a ½ mile, Alan was seen to be in third place, well backed up by Ken Miller and John Thresher in the early twenties and Trevor Hart in the sixties. A little after this, however, he had dropped back to join Ken and John and soon after dropped out altogether with severe stomach cramp; a nasty blow to both Alan and the team.

After 2½ miles Howard(Watford) led the field from Lindsell (Portsmouth) and Baker(Walton). Ken Miller was now 29th, John Thresher 50th, Derek Crookes 92nd and Jim Radford 107th; Trevor Hart being forced to drop back a considerable way with trouble from a shoulder ailment. Derek and Jim moved through very strongly on the last lap to reach the sixties and nineties respectively. Boquets go to Ken Miller for his fine return to form after his recent chest trouble and to Trevor Hart for pluckily finishing the course. Despite being without their two best runners the team still managed to finish 7th out of the thirty teams to close in.

Approx. 250 finished.

30 teams closed in.

An excellent meal was provided by our hosts after the Championships, and the prizes were presented by their General Manager F.W. Amies Esq. Arthur Whitehead produced his most valuable result sheets again for distribution, and as usual they disappeared like gold dust; a fitting end to a day of keenly contested and exciting competition.

COUNTY 10 MILES WALK CHAMPIONSHIPS. FEB.28TH

SURREY - at Kingston Although the 'flu bug was still affecting our walkers, and overnight we had word that Ray Middleton had succumbed, it was with good heart that our two teams faced the starter.

Our 'A' team won in devastating fashion with 17 points to Surrey A.C's. 33. The 'B' team, which was expected to be placed, was seriously weakened by re-arrangement to overcome the losses due to sickness and finished fourth.

Comments on individuals must be headed by the continued great walking of Eric Hall who finished first. He was ably supported by George Williams 2nd, Ray Hall 6th and Terry Clark(still a junior) 8th. The fine walking of John Knifton in his first 10 mile race was encouraging.

MIDDLESEX at Regents Park A very depleted team lined up for the start, and for the first time for many years we were unplaced. Ken Jones, who has the makings of a fine walker (he is an ex-runner) incurred the Judge's displeasure at an early stage and this further weakened our chances. How upsetting these decisions are, yet how important good judging is to the welfare of the sport. Do not be deterred Ken but take the opportunity to profit by your experience.

In the team race Cadogan A.C. were fifth with 75 points.

NATIONAL C.C. CHAMPIONSHIPS AT PETERBOROUGH. MARCH 7TH.

The National was, at one time, the event of the year for Belgrave, and both competitors and supporters talked of nothing else for weeks before. In this modern age, however, the race passes almost unnoticed - the runners treat it as a pennance and the supporters are practically non-existant. How can we expect to regain the championship with such an attitude. Our runners must approach this race with the will to win and not with the idea that providing they turn out they have done their duty. The non-active members of the Club, and those athletes not chosen to compete, must turn up in force to encourage, cajole, drive, plead and use every means in their power to ensure our teams meet with success. Nothing helps a runner more than to have adequate support and to know that the Club as a whole is behind him, willing him to give of his best.

Special trains and coaches are almost invariably arranged at low costs to transport supporters to the race so there is no excuse for missing events of such importance. There is much satisfaction to be gained from seeing your club mates racing in big championships and opportunity is also provided to see the "stars" in action. When a club is going through a bad patch, such as Belgrave is at present, the need for keen support is greater than ever, so if you want to see the "Bels" back to their past glories show an interest in their training, racing in Club events and particularly in major championships.

The least said about this year's race the better. We were never in the hunt and our final score of 980 points is an all time record, never, we hope, to be repeated. Even Pat Newell who was our main hope could not get going and finished in the most disappointing position of 59th. The remainder appeared to have no life whatsoever and were certainly not in the same form as at the Southern a few weeks earlier.

Syd Roberts

CROSS COUNTRY SECRETARY'S NOTES

These notes will reach you well after the end of the cross-country season but I hope you will forgive me for recapping on the winter activities.

To say that the overall results were disappointing is putting it mildly. At the beginning of September I was hopeful that 1958/9 would start a revival for Belgrave and we would recover some of our lost glory but the Surrey Road Relay at Woking found us taking a back seat behind Herne Hill, Walton and South London. The following week we took part in the Polytechnic Relay (8 x 3 miles) and it was encouraging to finish three complete teams. The 'A' team were fifth, the 'B' team were ninth for which they qualified for the medals awarded to the first 'B' team, and the 'C' team twenty-fifth. Good times were recorded by Baulch, Bhatia, Davies and Newell.

These two races,followed by our own 5¾ miles road race and 3 miles yacht handicap, preceded the London to Brighton Relay for Southern clubs on October 11th and we had every reason to think we were set for a place - in actual fact we had to struggle hard to secure sixth place to qualify for the National Relay in April.

On October 25th we revisited Chelmsford for their annual relay after an absence of some years. A strong team,on paper,took eighth place, 2½ minutes behind Woodford.

A cross-country relay at Reigate Priory on November 1st gave us seventh place with minor clubs like Deptford Park, Brighton, Hercules and Rochester ahead of us. On November 22nd we lost our hold on the L.C.C. Championship, taking third place behind Highgate and Queens Park Harriers. Charlie Walker and John Moore did well to take 8th and 10th places respectively.

The first major cross-country race, the South of the Thames Junior, was held at Epsom Downs on November 8th and Ken Miller(20th), John Moore(26th), Malcolm Robinson(34th) and Clive Shippen(35th) ran well to give us 3rd team award behind South London and Walton.

For the next two months we engaged in inter-club fixtures with reasonable success and also held our 7 miles Championship in which Charlie Walker caught Pat Newell on the wrong foot and, against the form book, chalked up another win in this race.

The first Saturday in January is always set aside for the county championships and this year the Surrey race was held at Epsom Downs. Although we packed well together we could do no better than fifth, with South London, Herne Hill, Walton and Surrey A.C. taking the earlier positions. Malcolm Robinson was unfortunately forced to retire when well placed, thus depriving us of a possible place.

The 10 miles Championship on January 24th reversed the result of the "seven" and gave Pat Newell an easy win over Walker with John Moore again taking third place. Eight minutes covered the first twelve runners, a very wide spread and not very encouraging for the big championships to follow in February and March. The first of these was the South of the Thames Senior which was held from our own headquarters at short notice owing to extensive flooding at the planned venue at Maidenhead. This is a race which we have won on a number of occasions and most of our leading runners are ineligible. Nevertheless our twelfth position was far from satisfactory over our own familiar terrain. The winning team Portsmouth, finished seven men before our first runner, John Moore, who was 49th, and South London, who were second, placed six men in front of John!

On February 14th we travelled to Aylesford for the Southern and were surprised by the much improved running of our team which took fifth place with 345 points after T.V.H.(169), Woodford(208), Portsmouth(283) and S.L.H.(305). It was grand to see the lads making steady progress throughout the race - another five miles would probably have secured us a place! Special mention should be made of Pat Newell who took 14th position in very good company - a great effort.

Three weeks later the National was held from the Hotpoint Factory at Peterborough but the hope of a good position, after our improved form in the Southern, did not materialize and we scored a record number of points - 980. Let the individual positions speak for themselves - Newell 59th, Shippen 136th, Baulch 158th, Gebbett 174th, Bedford 205th and Walker 248th.

The annual visit to Ilford for the local club's relay followed the National by seven days and produced second place behind Woodford Green who beat the course record with 3h.4m.42s. Our time of 3h.6m.25s. is the second best we have shown over this course. Strangely enough it was five years ago when we caned the record to win in 3h.6m.1s. - is this a reflection on our present strength when times generally are much faster?

On March 21st we held our road Championship and Pat Newell added yet another victory to his ever growing list of Club Championships with a record breaking run of 28m.21s. Dave Baulch took second place and Malcolm Robinson was third.

We visited Cranford for the T.V.H. road relay on Easter Saturday and started four teams but were without a number of our best performers, and in consequence were not in the running for leading honours. The 'A' team were 8th, the 'C' team 12th and the 'D' team 15th. Colin Collis, in a welcome return to form, produced the fastest time of our boys.

The last effort of the winter season is the National Relay over the London to Brighton course. Our team on paper appeared to be our best for some years, but again we were to be greatly disappointed and, after much floundering in the lower half of the field, we could do no better than 11th for the second year in succession, some nine minutes behind the winners, Derby and County A.C. Now we have to qualify in the race for Southern clubs in October in order that the "Claret and Gold" shall be seen on the Brighton road next April.

Thus the season came to an end on a despondant note and my hopes of six months earlier had not been realised, A few bright specks in the shape of the Southern and the Ilford relay, in an otherwise dark sky indicate that our prospects may not be as poor as they would appear. In fact I know that our past glories will return, but not before we have all taken complete stock of ourselves. Athletes and Officials alike must change their ideas. Obviously a club having a membership such as ours must again come to the fore, but if this is to be accomplished we must discard obsolete methods of training which produce a general fitness but do not bring the speed and strength required for the modern level of athletics. Circuit training and regular exercises with the weights must become standard to the schedules of everyone, not just the few. This combined with longer and more varied running sessions, a la Herb Elliott, will ensure faster times and increased confidence. It only needs an average improvement of 45 seconds per man for us to again win a Brighton relay and to reduce our National score by over 500 points.

This improvement is well within the capabilities of many of our runners and with the advent of our leading juniors such as Alan Black, Ken Miller, Chris Thompson, Alan Peckings and Derek Crookes, to mention just a few, in to senior competition with, I hope, the return of such performers as George Flanagan, Charlie Dabbs and Ron Linstead, I look to the future with considerable anticipation. If small clubs like Derby, Sheffield and Portsmouth and clubs like South London and Thames Valley who are no larger than ourselves can do well then so can we. Therefore I want you all to analyse your training methods to see where you went wrong - set yourself an improved target and don't give up until it is attained. Use Club events throughout the year to achieve your ambition and don't be sidetracked by other subsidiary interests. Remember that in trying to put Belgrave back on the map you yourself may also attain honours and I can assure you that there is nothing more satisfying than winning your county colours as a prelude to representative and International recognition. A revival of that pre-war spirit which took a small group of our members to team and individual honours, in combination with a greater attention to training, will produce the results which, I am sure, are not far away. Why not start now to build up that spirit by getting together with other members and resolving to become Belgravians whose names will be added to that select roll of "never to be forgottens". A sense of friendly rivalry will do much to gain this object - go to it!!

TRACK SECRETARY'S NOTES

Dave Shepherd

Members should by now have received their fixture cards for the track season and it is hoped that all concerned will note that they are expected to support all Club fixtures - it is, in fact, an obligation.

I would like to point out that these fixtures do not arrange themselves, but that a vast amount of work is put in by Section Secretarys, Captains and Vice Captains to make a coherent programme.

If you have any queries, get in touch with your section Secretary, Captain or Vice Captain. Don't wait for them to come to you - go to them. Tell them what you are doing in training or what you mean to do. They can only help you if you help yourself.

All the best!

Dave Shepherd

Despite a rather weak representation, those of our men who did turn up performed quite creditably.

Seniors

Charlie Jones took second place in both sprints, clocking 10.2 and 22.9; in the second string Alan Jackson was third in 23.9. In the 440 Dave Mitchell was second in 53.4 whilst Alan Jackson won the second string event in 53.5.

Juniors
