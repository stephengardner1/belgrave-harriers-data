Editorial · page 3

Congratulations to our three members who gained selection for the Commonwealth Games at Edmonton, Canada in August.

In the 30 kilometre walk, held in blistering conditions, Graham Seatter placed 7th for New Zealand while Allan Callow, representing the Isle of Man, was numbered among those who failed to finish. Graham's best performance of the year was without a doubt that magnificent display he put up at Stretford when he pulverised a selected field over 20 kilometres, winning by three minutes to record 87:37.

A successful summer campaign by the outstanding sprinter Ernest Obeng eventually gained him recognition from Ghana. Ernie has raced many of the best in the World this year and has not had a poor run in a season stretching back to March. After following his progress through the preliminary rounds of the Commonwealth 100 metres it was a thrill for all Belgravians to see him line up against two Olympic Champions. Ernie placed 8th in the final and then came home to run an electrifying 10.29 at Crystal Palace for his sixth Club Record of 1978. For this latter performance the Committee have awarded Ernest the "Tommy Green" Cup for the most meritorious performance by a Belgravian in 1978. (Last year he shared that honour with Graham Seatter.)

We must also remember the summer of 1978 for Cliff Brooks' narrow failure to earn selection for the Games. Setting his best ever decathlon score of 6,727 points (with veteran status not that many seasons away over the horizon), he was just short of the qualifying mark that would have enabled him to compete for Barbados.

Teamwise it was not such a good season but we must give recognition to our ladies section who gained promotion from their division of the Lily B League, and our boys and colts who did likewise in the Ebbisham League. All credit to the teams and their organisers.

MARATHON AGONIES

'Doub'

"I suppose you will want to see this" said my wife switching the T.V. to the film "The Games". This is about the Olympic Games and the climax is the marathon, and is set in Rome. The hero is a British Runner, Harry Hayes, played by Michael Crawford. When preparing for the part Michael joined the Bels and a 1968 magazine shows a picture of him training on Wimbledon Common. In the film he is the favourite for the marathon and his trainer wants him to run the first sub. two hours marathon!

The event is held on a very hot day and our hero draws away running at a fantastic pace, the half-way mark being reached in just under the hour. He is first to enter the stadium, but he has run himself out and he staggers round the track to be passed by six others and to collapse just after finishing.

A similar fate actually befell a former Belgravian in another Olympic Marathon - True or false?

True! In the 1948 Olympic Marathon E'tienne Gailly (Belgium) was first into Wembley Stadium only to stagger round and be passed by D. Cabrera (Argentine) and our old friend, Tom Richards of S.L.H. On crossing the line for the bronze medal E'tienne was whipped on to a stretcher. During the war he had escaped to Britain from occupied Belgium, and as a keen runner he joined us and trained with and competed for the Bels. "Steve" Gailly was well liked and when he returned to Belgium it was predicted in the "Belgravian" that he would make his mark. A few years later he was tragically killed in a car accident. A marathon is held annually in Belgium in his honour.

Also competing at Wembley in 1948 was Belgravian Bill Lucas (5,000m), Harry Churcher (10,000m Walk) and Bill Nankeville (1,500m).

The fastest marathon on record stands to the credit of Derek Clayton (Australia), who, in 1969, clocked 2h.8mins.34secs. at Antwerp. So the two hour barrier is still some way off - a tantalising and agonising one and threequarters of a mile.

You might like to experiment with this Collapse Point Theory. It works as follows:

~ You will stop or slow down dramatically at a point about three times your average daily distance for the past six to eight weeks.

Report · page 6

Graham Seatter 7th in Edmonton

The conditions could probably not have been much worse for the Commonwealth Games 30 Kilometre walk at Edmonton, in which two Belgravians were competing. The sun scorched the sixteen starters unmercifully and it is not surprising that four men failed to finish including our own Allan Callow representing the Isle of Man.

The Australians Sawall and Erikson blasted off, followed by Jobin the Canadian record holder while Belgrave's Graham Seatter (New Zealand) took things a little steadier. At 5KM Jobin led in 22:14 with Sawall 22:16 and then a gap until England's Olly Flynn passed the checkpoint with Erikson in 22:37 and Graham 22:54. Sawall pushed hard to lead at 10Km in 45:12, 11 seconds up on Jobin but Olly Flynn had closed up with 45:29 with our man still 5th in 46:15.

By 15Km Sawall led in 1:08:37 but Flynn had him in his sights, 13 seconds down. Jobin passed by in 1:09:27 and Graham was up to 4th in 1:10:05, 24 seconds clear of Brian Adams with Erikson apparently fading. Adams took Graham (1:35:55) in the next 5Km stretch and then up front Flynn moved into a lead he was to maintain to the end. By 25 kilometres Erikson stormed back into medal contention but Graham was in bad trouble as was Jobin and it must have been a desperate effort to keep going in the 26°C (80°F) temperature. Adams, Seddon (England) and Boeck (Canada) came past and eventually he crossed the line 7th in 2:31:48.9.

OUR PRESIDENT RAY C. MIDDLETON

John Keown

by John Keown

Ray places 2nd over 30km in a match v German clubs at Frankfurt

What qualities do we look for in a President? We expect him to be a person who is a leader; one who will inspire others to give of their best; one who has down the years clearly demonstrated his loyalty to his club and his fellow members. In Ray Middleton, Belgrave Harriers have found such a person.

Ray was born in London on 9th August 1936. At school he competed in the high jump and 880 yards but not being too successful in these events, in 1951 he tried his hand at race walking. Despite finishing third out of three in a one mile novice walk he discovered that he enjoyed the sport and so joined Belgrave Harriers.

Progress was slow but steady for Ray. In the R.W.A. Junior 5 Miles Championship in 1956 he finished sixth with a time of 41m.21s. The following year he began to win Club championships, and in 1959 at Wembley he won his first major race, the Southern "Garnet". He gained his first Surrey County vest the same year. In 1961, ten years after taking up race walking, Ray established himself as one of the top middle distance walkers in the United Kingdom. He finished third in the National 20 Miles, second in the Leicester Mercury 20 miles and second in the National 50 Kms when he split the two Thompson brothers, Don and Nigel. He gained his first international vest that year when he represented Great Britain over 50Kms in the Lugano Trophy Competition.

At Baddesley Colliery, Warwickshire in 1963 Ray gave one of the best walking performances of his long career when at his third attempt he won the National 50Kms with a world-class time of 4h.16m.43s. Two years later at Bolton he again won the National 50Kms.

Ray has represented his country on numerous occasions. Besides taking part in six Lugano Trophy finals he has competed in three European Championships, one Olympic Games and a Commonwealth Games. In the latter event at Kingston, Jamaica, Ray won the silver medal in the inaugural 20 Miles Walk.

During the present decade Ray has turned his attention to such established "classics" as the Hastings to Brighton and London to Brighton. In 1970 he won the Hastings to Brighton and when finishing second to Shaul Ladany (Israel) in the London to Brighton established a new Club Record of 7h.50m.47s. for the 53 miles journey from Big Ben to Brighton seafront. Just a month after winning the 1974 "Brighton" in an International 8 Hour track walking race at White Hart Lane, London, Ray crowned all his previous performances by establishing new World Best distances and times for 6, 7 and 8 hours, 40 and 50 miles.

During the last 20 years Ray has been a scoring member of practically all Belgrave walking teams at every distance from 5 miles to 53 miles The fact that he is the proud owner of nearly 50 National team medals clearly demonstrates his loyalty to the Club and his ability to inspire other members to bring top honours to Belgrave. His consistency over all distances will almost certainly see him go down in history as one of the all-time greats of British Race Walking.

Ray's activities have not been confined to racing. He has for a number of years been an elected member of the Southern R.W.A. Committee. Besides competing in National Championships he has successfully acted as local organiser of the longer championships. His knowledge of the competitors and their requirements made him a natural choice as chief steward in charge of the feeding stations at last year's Lugano Trophy final at Milton Keynes.

In all his endeavours Ray has always had the whole-hearted support of his wife Jean and his daughters, Debra and Lesley. Jean has, down the years, been an ardent and vocal supporter of Belgrave. Her ability to sell programmes, tickets, etc. to even the most reluctant athlete has gained her the grateful thanks of many a race organiser.

The election of Ray Middleton as President of Belgrave Harriers cannot do other than increase the Club's standing in athletic circles because his pleasing personality, high sense of sportsmanship and cheery cockney humour will quickly enable him to gain the friendship and respect of everyone he meets. Before his year in office is over some members may forget his surname but, as in race walking circles. his popularity will be noted by the frequent reference to "Ray" in conversation about Belgrave affairs.

Feature · page 10

Great sprinting from Ernest Obeng

Those interested in track athletics will remember 1978 as the year in which Scotland's Allan Wells rose from obscurity to the highest levels of world class sprinting. In Belgrave, however, we have another reason to mark the '78 season as a special one - Ernest Obeng's equally meteoric rise to very nearly the same standard.

In 1977 Ernest, a chemistry student at Chelsea College, University of London, had taken our Club Record for 100 metres down from 10.7 to 10.5 and also set new figures for the 200 metres of 21.5. His best fully automatic timing over the shorter race was 10.74 and it is interesting to note that the difference of .24 seconds between his best "auto" and "manual" times is exactly the conversion factor applied by track statisticians when measuring one against the other.

A winter of hard work with coach Martin Coomber ironed out the wrinkles in Ernie's technique and as early as March in indoor competition he posted a warning to his rivals by winning three 60 metre races on the same day, all in 6.8 seconds; then in the Blackheath H[illegible] Winter Meeting he scored his first outdoor success of the season - 1st over 100 metres in 10.8.

Representing the Southern Counties on April 26th he streaked to a resounding win with a new Club Record of 10.4 with the second man a full five metres behind and back on the same track, Crystal Palace, a week later, he proved the former run was no fluke by clocking 10.5 and also reducing our 200 metre best to 21.4.

Now came his sweetest victory to date and one which earned him respect far beyond Crystal Palace, the scene of his triumph. Lined up for the start of the Philips International Games 100 metres on May 24th were two fairly highly rated Americans, Britain's number two sprinter Mike McFarlane and the Olympic 200 metre champion Don Quarrie of Jamaica. Ernie stormed down the straight heading the field all the way to defeat Quarrie - 10.3 to 10.4 (photo-electric timing not in use).

The Inter-Counties Championships came and racing in Surrey's colours Ernie gave Belgrave their first champion in this meeting since 1966 when Geoff North took the 3 miles. His 10.46, without wind aid, was a Championship Best Performance. Likewise the SCAAA Champs, where a windy 10.34 kept him well clear of the field for another CBP, and where for good measure he also gained 3rd in the 200 metres and our Club Record for the longer event took another dive to 21.36.

Far from being unranked back in April among the top Commonwealth contenders, Ernie, who packs more power into his five foot six frame than many another six footer, was now being tipped to reach the semi-finals at Edmonton if only he could gain recognition in his native Ghana. Having met some of the best in the world in 1978 he was still unbeaten over his speciality, the 100 metres. However, after clocking 10.58 twice in the preliminary rounds of the AAA Champs on July 24th he suffered his first defeat at the hands of Sanford (USA) and Abrahams (Panama) in the final where a headwind kept them down to 10.42, 10.53 and 10.67 respectively. Now Ernie left the scene for a short while to compete in the African Games where Ghana at last recognised his talent and put him on their list of entries for the Commonwealth Games.

And so to Edmonton, Canada, and the XI Commonwealth Games.....

On August 6th the first round of the 100 metres took place and into a slight breeze Ernie won his heat just 1/100th outside his best time with 10.47. The second round had him matched against Quarrie again who this time got the better of the battle - 10.36 to 10.44 and Ernie's 5th Club Record of the season. A swirling wind the following day made things difficult for all the runners. The first semi was taken impressively by Wells (Scotland) in 10.31 but in the second race Quarrie hit his best form at the crucial time, equalling the Games Record with 10.19 ahead of Haseley Crawford (Trinidad) the Olympic 100 metre champion in 10.24. Gilkes (Guyana) placed 3rd in 10.31 and right on his tail came Ernie with 10.32 and a place in the final. By now the wind was gusting dramatically and the final got under way with a following blast of nearly four times the legal limit At 50 metres Crawford showed just ahead of Wells but as the line was approached Quarrie came through to take his 6th Commonwealth Games "Gold". Ernie placed 5th or 6th at 70 metres but faded at the very end for 8th.

1. D.Quarrie (Jamaica) 10.03 Games Record; 2. A.Wells (Scotland) 10.07; 3. H.Crawford (Trinidad) 10.09; 4. J.Gilkes (Guyana) 10.15; 5. M.McFarlane (England) 10.29; 6. P.Narracott (Australia) 10.31; 7. C.Braithwaite (Trinidad) 10.32; 8. E.Obeng (Ghana) 10.34.

Good running on the lead-off leg for Ghana's 4 x 100 metre squad enabled Ernie to share in a 39.74 timing for 5th place in the final.

One might have thought that this was a fine note on which to end the season but our member still had his best run yet to come. The Rotary Watches International Games saw one of the best 100 metre line-ups seen in the United Kingdom: Silvio Leonard (Cuba) - the world's fastest in 1977 with an incredible 9.98, Osvaldo Lara (Cuba) - a best of 10.11, Charlie Wells (USA), Allan Wells, Haseley Crawford and Ernest Obeng. The race was a cracker, producing the tightest of finishes. Leonard was judged the winner in 10.26 from Crawford 10.26 and .... Obeng 10.29! Scotland's Wells was 4th 10.33 with Lara and Charlie Wells back down the field in 5th and 6th. A tremendous time by Ernie and with the wind OK he claimed his 6th improvement on his own and Club's best performances during the '78 season.

One more run was to come. The Coca-Cola meeting 100 metres was run into a cool evening breeze and did not produce spectacular times but Ernie was still in form and obviously delighted with his 3rd place behind Crawford and Gilkes but ahead of Clancy Edwards of the USA (owner of one of the fastest times in the world for 1978) and our old friend Don Quarrie.

A remarkable season by our Ghanaian member.

Footnote: The African Games results were late coming in but reveal that Ernie placed 5th in the 100m final in 10.58 after a 10.42 heat (wind readings unknown). He also reached the semi-final of the 200m with a 21.46 heat but was then eliminated.

Southern League Division 4

Ray Hall

In this section we competed against five clubs (Portsmouth B, Hastings AC, Hercules Wimbledon, Bexley Borough and Dartford B) in three matches. All these matches were closely fought especially in the middle order where you will note we lost a place by one point in two competitions.

The first match meant a coach trip to Portsmouth where we had few winners but some fine performances from Shirley Rook (800m/1500m senior), Anne Slattery (senior javelin), Marjorie Woodward (3000m) and the 4 x 400m team made up of 3 field events girls Karen Smith, Wendy Dunsford, Anne Slattery and Valerie Bridges who ran the fastest leg. Of the juniors, the Taylor sisters Pam and Angela, Tina Couzens Barbara Dunsford and M[illegible] Sexton competed with success on the field whilst on the track Lorna Brown and Celia Estwick ran well in the 200m.

The next match, our home match at Wimbledon Park, was notable for Junior Club Records by Glenda Mahoney (100/200m) Angela Taylor (75m hurdles) and Veronica St. Ange (Javelin). Senior Shirley Rook gained club records in the 800m and 3000m and Dawn McNeil worked hard by competing in no less than five events. Our Captain Wendy Dunsford had two seconds and a third in the field. After holding high hopes for this match we were disappointed to only finish 5th but just five more points would have placed us 3rd, so closely contested was this match.

The last competition in the Southern League for us this year was at Erith. Our only win came in the Senior Shot thanks to Wendy Dunsford in the 'A' string and the ever present Anne Slattery, 'B' string. The meeting was notable for the help from our walkers, Jane Pritchard Linda Nicolls and Anna Matthews who ran in the 800m 1500m and 3000m to help the team. New names appeared in Belgrave colours, with the seniors Ruth Johnson (Dawn's sister), Angela Sharp (a converted sprinter from Durham) and Sarah Laing, who all competed in events they had not done before. We must mention a word about the courage shown by Dawn MacNeil who was injured in the high jump and had to retire, but came back later to anchor the relay team with a great run for their second fastest time of the season. You should have seen her later attempting to walk down the stairs at Clapham Junction.

100x1 mile relay

Bill Laws

JUNE 4th 1978 - CRYSTAL PALACE.

100x1 mile relay

For the many supporters who stayed to watch the closing stages of the 100 x 1 mile relay at the Crystal Palace the close finish between Belgrave and Cambridge Harriers provided the most exciting racing seen so far in this event. After 100 miles only three metres or half a second separated Laurie O'Hara from Cambridge's last stage runner.

The story started at 10am in perfect weather conditions when 11 teams at the Palace together with a further 29 teams at other venues began this marathon team event. The Bels. made a steady start placing 17th nationally and 7th at the Palace after the first 10 miles. Hopes for a rapid rise through the field were not realised as several of our middle distance runners failed to appear. It was fortunate that 18 reserves had turned up and as it turned out each of them was needed - the last being our President John Baker who had spent the day booking in team members (and chasing those who had not paid their subs).

By 80 miles Bels. had moved to 5th place at the Palace and 11th nationally. Everyone was confident that our last ten scheduled runners - all of them track specialists - would move through the field. So it was that at the 93rd mile Bels. passed Croydon and on stage 95 passed Hercules Wimbledon who had run out of senior runners. Cambridge, who were 1½ miles ahead, looked out of reach but a series of good runs meant that Laurie O'Hara took over the last stage some 600 metres down on the Cambridge President. A first lap by our remarkable veteran in 64 seconds rapidly cut into the lead and by now all the spectators were cheering, fully anticipating the prospect of a close finish. And so to the last lap. Laurie entered the finishing straight in a sustained sprint some 50 metres down and despite all his efforts failed by a mere 3 metres.

Belgrave finally placed 3rd at Crystal Palace and 7th nationally. The time was slower than in recent years, the only Belgrave record being set by Bill Couzens who once again sat through the whole day recording individual lap and mile times. Other faithful stalwarts also worked flat out all day and special thanks are due to Brian Pritchard, Gordon Doubleday, Tom Carter and John Baker.

It was good to see how many of our boys and youths are progressing and special mention should be made of Mark Waller's, Matthew Cannon's David North's and John Searle's fine runs. Several old faces made a welcome re-appearance and rumour has it that old 'TK' will be battling it out with Tony Fairclough this winter. John Stow made a welcome return to clock fastest of the day Bill Laws.

(It would be remiss of us not to mention Bill's own contribution to our day's competition, co-ordinating the Belgrave effort as usual.)

This is the largest number we have had for some years and maybe this is a good sign for the future but with such a number, some of us should not become disinterested and expect someone else to turn out all the time. Each individual should feel part of the team whether or not he actually picks up the prizes or medals.

Over the last few months we have had a few more useful additions to our numbers. The most prominent of these is Derek Howie. Having competed in three Nationals this year any doubts as to his authenticity to compete have been completely dispelled. In December last year the Club was contacted with regards to his application for membership. A Scotsman, living in Scotland, he was partially coached by Bob Coates and wanted to join Belgrave but there was one small problem (to us not to him) for he is virtually blind. This is obviously something that has to be considered as regards competing on the roads but the Club took the bull by the horns and accepted his membership. Derek had never raced on the roads until January of this year although he had done a few track races with credit.

Since then Derek's performances have been astounding. In the National '10' he was 14th, in the Brighton to Steyning over one of the most arduous and difficult road courses he was 8th, and he was selected to compete for Scotland against Greece in Athens. To top this he then placed 12th in the National 30Km to win the Sidney Bayliss Cup for Belgrave's first man and also gained a Club honours badge. In the National 50Km he was second Belgravian home and in the Hastings to Brighton event he led our team to victory with an individual 5th place!

Graham Seatter made a short stop off to England in June en route for Canada and the Commonwealth Games. He competed for us on a few occasions including 4th in the A.A.A. 3Km. Since his return to New Zealand last November he has notched up two club records - these being for 10 and 3 kilometres.

John Knifton, on tour with the U.S.A. Olympic squad, competed in the National 50Km at Stretford and was our first man to finish.

On the Girls' side we have had rather a quiet summer with nothing outstanding to report. Exams and other commitments have rather restricted progress but with these now out of the way for most of them, we can look forward to a hard and successful winter with full team turn outs now that Linda Nicholls has become eligible for team events. On the lesser known side of walking is the Lily B League for Girls under 15. As with last year we have been able to produce some placings in the two walks that are held in these meetings and Barbara Dunsford, Bernadette Sexton and newcomer Caroline Carne have been able to keep up the same standard this year.

Overall the Club can look forward to some good results during the coming season if everyone pulls together. We start off with the National Junior, Youths and Boys Championships and although we may struggle a bit in the latter championship there should be no excuse in the other two. On the Girls side we very nearly got medals last year so we look forward to some good results.

As a result of the National 50Km in which we could only finish 6th our final National placing in the McSweeney Trophy was 6th, our lowest for years. Although it looks disastrous at first, a closer look and some quick calculations show that had we started a Youths team in the championship we could well have been placed 2nd if not 1st. So, next year an all out effort and .......

Carl Lawton, Walking Secretary

Gordon Doubleday

MARCH 18th 1978 - CRYSTAL PALACE.

The R.W.A. 10

With race programmes diminishing in size and detail the programme for the 32nd Annual National Ten Miles Championship was excellent value at tenpence. Not only did it give the names of officials and competitors but it was the first occasion upon which walkers individual numbers were worn (i.e. permanent ones issued to them by the R.W.A. through their clubs and to be worn at all open events). Then came lists of the first 3 to finish and first three teams since the first race in 1947. Here, it may be recalled that immediately prior to World War II the Bels had been unbeatable in team races over the seven to ten miles range but unfortunately there was no National Championship. After the war our walkers soon got down to things at Belgrave Hall and our winning ways continued and it is no surprise that we won the first National 10 held at Sheffield with Harry Churcher and Jack Rutland taking the first two places. Charlie Churcher was 8th and didn't get a team medal!

Looking down over the years we find that Sheffield and ourselves have won the title eleven times each, with ourselves claiming eight seconds to their four and three thirds to their six. Prior to the war the Inter-Counties Walk had been held annually since 1934 as a separate event and in 1948 it was held in conjunction with the National Title. Naturally a number of Belgravians have helped their counties.

It is perhaps worth while going back over the years and recalling the Belgravians who have served us so well in this event, the most popular of the National Walking Championships. Composition of winning teams given:

Incidentally the first Belgravian to finish holds the Frank Thomson Trophy for a year. Frank was a Past President of the Club and the R.W.A. (of which he was also an elected Life-Member). A great Battersea Park man, he also judged and coached Track and Field Events.

Allan's Back

It was good to see the cheery figure of Allan Callow on arrival at the Crystal Palace for this years R.W.A. '10'. He was already in his tracksuit ready to battle for the Bels. again. There was some sunshine but a decided nip in the air. The start had been delayed as news had come through that most of the Sheffield team (the holders) were still on the road having been delayed on the motorway. Unfortunately they didn't arrive until after the "field" was off. In fact it was over six minutes after the "off" that they were able to pin on their numbers and get going - a gap that no aspiring winners could hope to close.

Another hitch was that due to a landslip the original course of seven laps couldn't be used owing to mud and water on the course, an alternative course of four laps (out and home) had been quickly planned.

At the end of the first lap (covered in less than 17 minutes) Olly Flynn (Basildon) had opened up a gap on Brian Adams (Leicester) who was clear of the rest of the field. Carl was in the leading group with John well placed and it wasn't long before Allan came through with Ray moving strongly to complete our four. At halfway the leader was just inside 33 minutes, clearly a fast time was in view - or was the course suspect?

Olly and Brian kept their places to the finish with very fast times - it turned out that the course was short so fast times and personal bests were in plenty. A good race for third place resulted in the bronze going to young Graham Morris (Steyning A.C.). Carl led the team home again and John maintained the fine form he has been showing. Allan celebrated his return home with a good turn of speed and Ray, dependable as ever, closed the team in.

The team positions were difficult to assess so it was wait and see! As things turned out Ilford A.C. were clear winners with Leicester W.C. (who started only the minimum of four) a few points ahead of us. So, once again, our walkers rose to the occasion to gain third team medals. Congratulations to our walkers for adding to our many past successes in this race.

We must not overlook the great performance of our new member Derek Howie. Making his first appearance in a National Championship he competed as an individual and finished in 14th place in 71m.53s. What is remarkable is that this was only his third road race and he is almost completely blind. He is a student at Stirling University and should be a strong contender for a Scotland vest.

APRIL 29th 1978 - CROYDON - ENGLISH SCHOOLS CHAMPIONSHIPS

Five Years Old

The fifth English Schools Athletic Association Race Walking Champs were held from Monks' Hill Sports Centre, South Croydon. Testing courses had been planned over undulating laps with the Southern Committee of the R.W.A. providing key officials.

The first event, Junior Girls 2,500m, had Tracy McDermot (Mddx.) lining up for her fifth race and in clocking 14:55 to finish 31st she showed the improvement predicted in the report on her first effort at Enfield in November. We also had a representative in the Boys' 3,000m, P.Porter who finished 36th.

The Intermediate Girls' 3,000m saw two of our girls in action. Linda Nicholls (Kent) finished 9th in 17:22 and was unlucky not to get a team medal. Kent tied with Hereford and Worcester for 3rd place but were placed fourth under A.A.A. Laws. Jane Pritchard was London's only representative; a great team walker, she finished 19th in 18:25. For the complete programme of six events London could only muster six competitors, for most of the capital's walkers came from the outer boroughs and so represented various home counties.

The Intermediate Boys' 5,000m saw three of ours in action. Michael Nicholls did well to finish 8th in 24:54 and collected a winning team medal for Kent. This gave particular pleasure to Harold King, himself a Kent County Champion in the 'thirties. Kevin McDermot (Mddx.) put up a PB to finish 20th in 26:05, whilst S.Sethi did a creditable 27:03 to finish 29th. Peter Morris was on the sidelines as a reserve for Surrey, unfortunately he had an "off-day" on the day of the trial; had he walked he would have gained a good placing.

Anna Matthews lined up for London in the Senior Girls 5,000m and soon settled down in 4th place, a position she held to the finish where the watches showed 29:02. She had made a great effort and seemed distressed afterwards but soon recovered.

The Senior Boys 10,000m had quite a "classy" line up, many of the competitors having done well against Seniors in open races. After the first of the seven laps Richard Dorman (Surrey) and Trevor McDermot (Mddx.) were in a group in 4th position. As the laps went by our two pulled away and it looked like 4th and 5th for us. Coming into the finish Richard had a short lead but Trevor put in a great effort to catch him. However, Richard found sufficient speed to keep him away. Great stuff from these two which highly pleased Club supporters, their times were 49:59 and 50:05 respectively.

These championships, now five years old, look like being here to stay - they get fine support from the R.W.A. Some great walking from Britain's stars of the future - it was good to see ten Belgravians carrying their county colours with such credit.

Feature · page 29

John Keown has drawn our attention to the feats of a great Belgrave long-distance walker of the past. Between 1921 and 1929 J.N. Thomas became the first walker in history to successfully complete three successive London to Brighton and Back Walks (104 miles).

He started walking as a novice at the age of 44 in 1919. Two years later he tackled his first B & B - 24h:23m.42s. In 1926 at 51 he did it again in 22h:28m.04s. In 1929 at 54 he put up his best performance of 21h:35m.07s and he figured prominently in several ' 'Brightons'. He died round about 1932.

In 1971 Surrey W.C. decided to stop using the London to Brighton road for this race and instead a ten lap course at Ewhurst (Surrey) is used. John, having completed two Ewhurst 100's could well equal Thomas' feat when the race is held next year.

Early Olympics

The first Olympic Games are believed to have been held as early as 1370BC. In 776BC Coroebus won the Stade race (192.27 metres) from which comes the word stadium. The Games were a bloodthirsty affair in those days although in 884BC King Ephitis of Elis spoiled the spectacle by introducing a sacred truce, banning legal disputes and the execution of death penalties during the Games. The terms of the truce were engraved on a discus and set out in five concentric rings which in modern times were used to symbolise the five continents of the world.

At last our team showed what they are capable of. Against very good opposition from Croydon and North London, solid performances all round left the Bels in 2nd position, almost doubling our points for the season.

As it turned out this final fling prior to expected relegation just did the trick. When the qualifying matches for the National League came around no fewer than four Southern League Clubs made the grade: Epsom & Ewell H, Windsor, Slough & Eton AC, Bedford & Cnty and Chelmsford. Early Olympics

The ancient Games, before 393AD, grew from one race to a five day programme. Cheating was a serious crime and very respected judges were carefully appointed; an initiation rite marked the seriousness of their noble task. Athletes were sometimes beaten for false starts! The five days were as follows: 1st day - the opening ceremony. 2nd day - the pentathlon (discus, javelin, running, wrestling and jumping. 3rd day - running over a distance approaching 5,000 metres. 4th day - wrestling, boxing and pankration (involving gouging, strangling and limb breaking. 5th day - those still able mounted the pedestal.
