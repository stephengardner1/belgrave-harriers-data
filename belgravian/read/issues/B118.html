EDITORIAL

YOUR EDITORS.

Dear Fellow Belgravians,

The " Jimmy Belchamber Memorial Trophy " Fund has now been closed, and a fitting trophy to perpetuate the memory of Jimmy will shortly be purchased and awarded for an event to be decided by the Committee. At the same time we place on record our gratefulness to Mrs. Smith, daughter of the late Frank Thomson for the presentation of a very beautiful cup, which will keep Frank's name for ever connected with the Club he so ably and unselfishly served, and which will also be awarded for an event to be decided by the Committee.

With the addition of these two trophies our total of Club Cups and Shields will be brought up to the splendid total of some 35, and now cover every event in our curriculum of Athletics.

It should be noted by all members, especially the newer members, that these trophies bear the names of illustrious Belgravians, some of them happily still with us and, we hope, for many years to come.

We ask you, when admiring your trophies, to give a moment of thought to the donors who are still with us, and a reverent thought for those who have passed from us.

These gentlemen and their colleagues of yore are those who pioneered so steadfastly to give you the Belgrave Club, to which you must surely be proud to be a member.

The inscription on The Frank Thomson Cup reads : " Life Member for 60 years.—President, 1940-1942.—Presented as a token of life-long service to Athletics."

It is truly so, and let it be an incentive to you—you for whom he did so much, many of whom he never knew. Let it never be said you knew not of him or his contemporaries or of what they did.

YOUR EDITORS.

Jimmy BelchamberMrs. SmithFrank Thomson

CROSS COUNTRY SEASON, 1953-54

DENIS BRICKWOOD.

I think that the main reason for poorish support during the last few seasons has been that members have been a little uncertain of what was expected of them. At the same time I believe that the Section's activities have not been broadcast nearly enough to start off on the right foot.

Before I go any further I would add that my mandate for these remarks is that I am a Vice-Captain of the 1952 vintage, still in office until the A.G.M., and partly responsible for next season's activities.

No matter what is organised, it will not go well if members do not give support, but I am hopeful that the programme and organisation this year will warrant this support. Firstly, the opening run will be Saturday, September 19th, earlier, of course, than usual. It is fully realised that some will not be present owing to track commitments, but there are still a lot available, and although they may not be first team men, they will be wanted for fixtures during the season.

The fixtures this year are much nearer home and there should be no cry about the distance being too far. There are Home and Away fixtures, for example, with Surrey A.C. and Herne Hill Harriers ; and just another word about support—these fixtures are intended for all, and not just " A " Team men.

These matters have had to be dealt with, but it will be up to the new Captain and Vice-Captains to put them into practice. Whilst on the subject of these officials, it might be a good plan to outline the responsibilities of the Captain. Firstly, he LEADS the Section, not follows it. He is responsible for implementing the season's training, i.e., he handles the tactics of his teams ; he organises the training of the packs and has the final say in their composition. He selects teams with the assistance of his Vice-Captains. He warrants your support if you give him the job.

And now a word on training. I hope that the Captain and Vice-Captains, Senior and Junior, will do what they can to stamp out " Fast and slow " training and in its place can we have " Fast and medium " training. Probably the majority intend their training to be fast and medium, but I have noticed that quite often the fast is so fast that it is almost impossible to continue afterwards. Cross country running is three-dimensional—it brings in the additional element of rough ground and the resultant breaking up of rhythm. Many cross country runners indulge during the winter in track running, but whilst track running is beneficial for speed, which should have been acquired in the main during the track season anyway, it is very necessary to acquire many other manners of movement over different types of ground. Speed running takes its place amongst them and fast runners obviously make the most of good fast going. Cross country is a rugged business and it demands every quality, both physical and mental, and it has yet to be proved that it upsets track runners, particularly if they train like Pirie. Anyway, we have some very good examples in Jack Brown and Bob Taylor. What most young Belgravians seem to lack is pace and judgment, and I should like to see some concentration on them. They are first principles. Those who run with me last year will understand more easily what I am getting at ; they will remember the change of pacemakers and the resultant fluctuations in the pace of the pack. These changes were made to produce concrete examples of lack of judgment and an opportunity to get to grips with the problem.

Another thing I should like to see is all the runners going out in the official packs, and coaches directing their changes in this way. Without any enlargement, the Country is a team game and the Club should learn to run as a team.

Finally, I hope I shall see members running in their proper packs and conforming with the pack leader's instructions and ready to go out sharp at 3 o'clock.

In these few notes on training, I realise that more justice could have been done to them, given time. They are essentially my ideas and can be wrong. Even in the twilight of a fairy athletic life, I am still learning.

DENIS BRICKWOOD.

FIELD EVENTS

JACK CHAPPELL.

Let us begin by saying, most definitely, that the Field Events Section is having a good season and certainly an improved one up to the time of writing this report.

We have done well in the Sward, Brockman, Kinnaird and Heathfield Trophies so far, which gives me considerable encouragement to look to the future.

Two of our new Plaques have been won this season, viz., G. T. Gaworski winning the Discuss with a throw of 135ft. 7ins. at Walton, and P. Luxon winning the Javelin with a throw of 178ft. 7ins. at the Kinnaird Meeting.

Porter is doing very well with the Hammer, and we are expecting something very good from him before the end of the season. Van Sukavachana is Pole Vaulting very well and should encourage more Club members to compete against him, when we hope to see an improvement in this branch of our Field Events.

Here I would like to say that I am very pleased with the Juniors, who are turning out very regularly and getting in plenty of training and making full use of the coaching of F. Stamfl and Frank Simmons.

Finally, we have to thank our Starter, Mr. J. Fell, for presenting us with a Cup in memory of his father, who was Club Starter before him for many years, for our new 120yds. Hurdles Championship, which will be held at Battersea Park on August 22nd. We hope to get a good entry for this new Championship and I want you to send your entries to me early.

Please me and help yourselves and the Club by keeping up the good work.

MATCH AT CHARLTON PARK

This was the first track fixture of the 1953 season, and although most people may think that March 28th was a bit early to make a start, it did give some of our lads who were in training a chance of a run.

In the 100 metres 1st string our sprinter from British Guiana, Ivan Mayers, showed a clean pair of heels to his rivals to record 11.2 secs. In the 2nd string race Ron Morrell finished third, with P. Cornish in second place.

The 300 yards 1st string event was won by Dicky Bartlett in 35.4 secs., and John Benstead won the second race in 34.5.

Ian Badiali was first home in the 1st string 660 yards in 87.4 secs., and Bob Taylor recorded 86.5, to set up a new track record in the second race over this distance.

In the ¾-mile event Ray Tooby was first, with Frank Wright in second place, and Ray's time of 3 mins. 15.9 secs. was also a new track record.

Jack Brown won the 1½ miles in another new track record time of 6 mins. 58.2 secs., while in the 3 mile event we had Bob Belmore 4th and Don Maclean 5th.

The medley relay saw Alan Bishop give us the lead over the half-mile stage, but Essex Beagles beat us into 2nd place in 3 mins. 54 secs.

P. C. Luxon scored a good double by winning the Long Jump with 19ft. 11½ ins., with D. B. Ansell third, and Luxon threw 137ft. 6ins. to win the Javelin Throw.

T. J. Gaworski did 40ft. 0½in. to win the Weight, and the final points placings were : 1, Belgrave H., 71 ; 2, Essex Beagles, 56 ; 3, W. J. O'Bree's Team, 39 ; 4, Peckham Police, 23 ; 5, Deptford Park A.C., 8.

Also included in the programme was a 2 mile Walk, which was won by Eric Hall, with our new member, Demmon, in second place.

It was pleasing to see such a good turn-out by our lads, and we hope that they keep up the good work throughout the season.

THE " NATIONAL " ROAD RELAY

The third race for this trophy, promoted by the " News of the World," was held on April 11th, and although it was a fine day there was a head-wind against the runners and it looked unlikely that any records would be broken.

On the first stage to Purley we had Jack Brown, and he ran well to hand over in second place behind Frank Sando, of Aylesford, in 28 mins. 22 secs.

We then had Peter Bagnall, who was making his first appearance in this race. He caught the leader, but was himself passed by Don McMillan, the Australian Olympic runner, who was representing the Polytechnic H.

Len Herbert went off on the third stage, which includes the stiff climb through Redhill. Len ran well to maintain our 2nd position, and his time of 22.46 was the third fastest.

At this point the Poly. were leading us by 32 seconds, while we were 56 seconds in front of T.V.H., 1 min. 23 secs. in front of Birchfield, and 2 mins. 28 secs. in front of S.L.H.

When selecting our team we had thought to consolidate our position over the 4th and 5th stages, but it did not work out that way.

Chas. Walker did 27.01 for 5th fastest time on the 4th stage, which was well behind Arthur Bruce's record for this leg, and his time was beaten by Dalton, of Birchfield, to set up a new record.

We then had Ray Tooby, who holds the record for the 5th stage, but Ray did 24.44 for the 4th fastest time, and was also well behind his record run of last October.

Our position was still 2nd, behind the Poly., but the other teams were closing up and although Bob Taylor caught the Poly. runner to give us the lead, it was evident that this was not going to be as big a margin as we had hoped for.

John Bromley went off with the lead over the long 7th stage, but with Pirie and Olney running stride for stride, they soon overhauled him and went well away into the lead. John endeavoured to hold on to Bobbie Reid of Birchfield and Jimmy Wood of the Poly., but was troubled with stomach cramp over the last mile and was forced to give ground so that we were now 5th.

Bill Lucas ran the 8th stage for us. He did the fastest time on this leg and although gaining ground he could not catch any of his rivals.

We then had Archie Bedford on the 9th stage and he ran well to record the 2nd fastest time and almost caught the Poly. runner, who was now in 4th place.

Mike Stearman, also making his first appearance in this event, was doing the 10th and final stage, and as he lives in Brighton he was running over familiar ground. Mike ran well to catch Ward of the Poly. and so took us into 4th place, and his time of 18.15 was the 2nd fastest for this leg. It was interesting to note that Nankeville only did 18.43.

Our total time of 3.53.44 would have won the race on previous occasions, so it was not a question of our chaps failing, but of the other clubs improving.

We congratulate Thames Valley on their win, but here again they only had faster times than our lads on stages 2-5 and 7.

From our point of view it was a similar race to the one in 1950, which was only won by T.V.H., with ourselves in 4th place, and in all the races held since 1932 we have never once been outside the first four places, which is a pretty good record.
