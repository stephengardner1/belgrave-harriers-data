Editorial · page 3

COMMENT

COMMENT

Christchurch representatives apart, we have had a rather quiet winter season in all sections but the long awaited inception of a Club coaching set-up heralds exciting times ahead.

Many youngsters have been attracted to Belgrave Harriers in the past twelve months and we now find that twenty-nine per cent of our members are under the age of twenty-one, and hopefully that percentage will be pushed a lot higher still. Only a relatively small number will become champions but good coaching and an interest shown in the development of our youngsters by older members will maintain their enthusiasm for the sport of athletics and spread the word that ours is a Club that can provide the facilities they need. The broader we can make our base of solid Club athletes, the more stars we will produce to lead our teams and take us through the rest of the 'seventies' and beyond.

It is not a miracle that we need to put Belgrave at the very top of athletics instead of its middle reaches, just hard work. Hard work that has been begun and which now needs as many broad shoulders as possible to share the load and make our movement upwards irresistible.

Unfortunately CHARLIE SMART suffered a setback recently in his fight back to good health and he is unable at present to get out of the house. Visitors would be more than welcome at his home - 25 Borneo Street, SW15. Meanwhile, all the best Charlie, and may we soon see you about once again.

Congratulations to Vice-President BERNARD EGLINGTON upon his recent marriage. He joined the Club about thirty years ago when he was a full time member of the R.A.F., and was previously with Derby & County. He made the switch from running to walking and soon made his mark over the longer distances and figured in many of our winning teams. By walking step for step with the late Cyril Yescombe from Brighton to London and back he became Centurion No. 344. Now living in the Colwyn Bay area he keeps himself fit and we are always glad when he makes a trip to town to line up with the Bels team. We wish Bernard and Grace many years of health and happiness.

Unhappy news comes from GORDON DOUBLEDAY. It seems that he is having to take things very quietly at the moment after suffering a patch of bad health. Treatment has consisted of having to sleep sitting up and taking a vast array of pills amongst other things. Let us hope that his recovery is swift and that once again,armed with stop-watch,he will be presiding over the finish of our races.

WHO KNOWS WHERE WE ARE GOING ?

Bill Laws

Bill Laws writes:-

WHO KNOWS WHERE WE ARE GOING ?

I am sure that all active members were delighted at the choice of our current President and awaited with anticipation the first actions from this energetic man. We soon learned that his self-set task for the year was a fund raising appeal and I, like many others, eagerly responded. However, with the first flush of enthusiasm gone, it occurred to me that the reasons for the fund raising were not clear. Perhaps some new scheme, carefully nurtured but still to see the light of day, was afoot. Certainly rumours had been circulating. The rebuilding of Belgrave Hall was mentioned. The possibility of selling the Hall was being considered and even a deal with the local Council involving new facilities at Wimbledon Park track had been hinted at. The possibilities seemed endless and surely our President's appeal must have some direct relation to one of these plans. But no, re-reading the last "Belgravian" made it clear that the fund raising aims were to improve liquidity and [illegible] provide for a building maintenance fund. So perhaps there were to be no new plans, no new building and certainly no fresh appraisal of where we as a club are going and what our targets for the future should be.

So what about it? How does Belgrave rate as a club now? Are we satisfied with the way things have gone over the last ten years and do we see any need to establish new aims for the future? I should like to see members from all sections of the Club seriously thinking about these matters and the following should stimulate discussion.

During these last few years, which have seen the amalgamation of many clubs, the performance of Belgrave has declined. Fewer trophies, and fewer road and country wins have been the trend. The same old faces have provided stalwart service but where are the new names? We are not attracting and holding enough good youngsters and I believe that we must change our organisation, finances and facilities to compete with many of today's other attractions so here are some proposals:-

1) Widen the membership of the Club. Initiate a ladies section (Yes, I've mentioned the unmentionable). Start a family jogging section and build up a supporters club. Aim for a total membership of several thousand. 2) Re-build, preferably on a larger site, to provide adequate social and recreational facilities for all the groups mentioned above. Provide facilities for additional leisure sports, say table tennis, badminton and squash. 3) Finance these projects initially through an appeal fund but later through increased membership fees and profits from peripheral activities. 4) Make Belgrave a community club and get financial support from local Councils and Government agencies.

By now several members will be pouring their second whisky and soda. But let them consider this. We have modified our role before (remember we were once a boxing club) and we can adjust and expand if we have the desire. In my view we must do this to establish ourselves as the premier athletics club. By introducing the joys of running to a much wider range of people we would attract and hold the membership from which new champions would surely emerge.

WALKING

An uneventful winter season has produced no outstanding performances. In fact it has generally been a depleted Belgrave team that has faced the starting line.

BELGRAVE WERE THERE

BELGRAVE WERE THERE

JANUARY 29th 1974 - CHRISTCHURCH, NEW ZEALAND THE TENTH BRITISH COMMONWEALTH GAMES

Halfway round the World, Belgrave were well represented in the 10th British Commonwealth Games. The first appearance of our Walkers was in a pre-Games 10 kilometre track event in which they achieved a promising 1, 3, and 4.

The 20 miles Walk was held over an out-and-back course with a 5 p.m. start to avoid the worst of the sun, and of the fifteen starters four were Belgravians, with Len Duquemin and John Moullin for Guernsey, Carl Lawton for England and Allan Callow representing the Isle of Man.

England's Warhurst and Thorpe led all the way. Allan was in the second group for a while but gradually fell off the back. John Moullin was going steadily until the turn when he found the going extremely tough due to the head wind and the cold. Len Duquemin also suffered but it was Carl Lawton who again faded on the big occasion. He was seventh at the turn in 79:25 but slowed drastically before eventually retiring at 14½ miles.

Overall a disappointing result for the Bels but a trip not to be forgotten in a hurry. Other Belgravians making an appearance were Vic Townsend who was 2nd in a veterans race and Dave De Bondi over from Australia to watch.

The Festival Of Culture

CHARLES DABBS (recently fired from the "Longford Committee") REPORTS:-

As I have often been criticized for my infrequent visits to HQ I was grateful to accept the invitation of the well known "impressario" (among other things) and Belgrave President Charles Manning to attend his very discreetly advertized Festival of Culture.

Much to my amazement, on arrival at HQ, I discovered that not only were our own sober gentlemen members in attendance but also the intemperate louts from the other local club which changes its name frequently enough for it to elude me!

With a delay somewhat similar to that at the start of a cross country race your actual culture commenced. A comedian, speaking mostly in very readily understandable vernacular, related some of his experiences to the discerning audience. At one point the writer, commenting on the intellectual appeal of the material, was verbally attacked but managed to survive. Following the comedian, the audience were considerably shocked by the unexpected naked appearance of the female of our species - not normally seen unattired in our revered HQ. The effect of this arrival, followed by another (the President would not, of course, be satisfied by just one - would he?) was to stir the audience into the edging forward of the chairs, the eyes protruding, etc., etc! Not only two naked females but also a ventriloquist's (that's how it's spelt) dummy with an intelligence way above that of some of the runners (especially those of the other club previously referred to) and almost equal to our own!

Eventually, after all the drinking, all the bawdy jokes and all the T's and B's the evening closed as another great Belgrave success. In retrospect, considering the attendance and interest shown, there may well be a future for this type of activity as a regular feature of the revitalized Belgrave Training Programme.

Tom Pritchard

It is with great regret that we have to advise the death of Tom Pritchard who passed on at the age of 66 years. Tom joined the Club on the 10th June 1924, and I think he must have been the longest serving active member - 50 years.

Tom was never a champion but was known for his enthusiasm which he showed in Open and Club Handicaps, win or lose.

He was of a quiet disposition and would never push himself but was ever willing to help others either as Track or Field events official. During the last few years he had taken on the position as Assistant Treasurer. Due to his helpful nature Tom was elected a Life Member and a Vice President and would no doubt have been elected President in the near future. Tom was also a Past President and Life Member of the Veteran's Athletic Club. He will be missed by all.

We mourn one who has served the Club well and we offer our condolences to his wife Flo and daughter Jill and son Brian who is a member of Belgrave Harriers.

BICOURT 4TH IN N.ZEALAND

BICOURT 4TH IN N.ZEALAND

JANUARY 26th 1974 - CHRISTCHURCH, NEW ZEALAND

THE TENTH BRITISH COMMONWEALTH GAMES

What a line up the Commonwealth Games 3000 metres Steeplechase produced; Benjamin Jipcho the World Record holder, Amos Biwott the Mexico Olympic Champion, Evans Mogaka who was ranked sixth in the World in 1973, and amongst those from these Isles our own John Bicourt who so narrowly missed the British Record last year.

John suffered a throat infection at Christmas and it still hadn't cleared up as the 'Games' approached. The team doctor diagnosed the trouble as a touch of bronchitis and with only nine days to the race, advised no running for three to five days! Better prepared was John Davies the Thames Valley Harrier representing Wales. He was lucky enough to spend two weeks in Nairobi over Christmas, getting the sun to his muscles.

So the race was on. The Kenyans made the running with the Welshmen Davies and Hayward following. John, not feeling too confident after the doctor's advice, played it safe and kept back down the field. At the half-way mark Biwott was dropping back but our man was still running steadily in seventh place. Running the race of his life, Davies stepped up the pressure with two laps to go. By now John was up with Hayward about 40 metres behind the leaders.

Shortly after the 'bell' the incident occurred in which Mogaka and Davies fell, leaving Jipcho to run home comfortably. They were both soon up again and heading hard for the finish but John Bicourt too was now moving really fast, feeling that he had something left in him. He had overtaken Hayward with just over a lap left and by the time the finish was reached there was a margin of six seconds between them. Mogaka, just out of reach, crossed the line in the bronze medal position ahead of him.

John wasn't disappointed with the result. How could he be with his second best time, running out of season and with a slight lack of confidence due to the illness. But still, he finished so strongly that he is certain that he could have run a faster time if he had felt more sure of himself early in the race.

During the period of dispute that followed, he at no time considered that he would move up into a medal position with the possible disqualification of Davies. As far as John was concerned Davies had run a magnificent race and thoroughly deserved his second place come what may. Thankfully the race positions were upheld.

A trip to Mount Cook with Arthur Lydiard made some impression on John who states," He produces the winners so you've got to listen to the man".

John's training consists of running a regular 70 miles per week the majority of which is at a steady pace. He has not liked the idea of fast time trials over the flat, feeling that he wants to save it for the races that matter. Lydiard's men, however, may run a trial at 90% of full effort only a week before a major championship, and include infinitely more fartlek type running in their training.

Talking with the famous New Zealand coach has influenced John to the extent that he now badly wants to improve his times on the flat over 1500 metres and 5000 metres. His hurdling is acknowledged to be fine and if he can work up some real pace he is convinced that it will give him that bit more edge and the ability to take on and beat the very best. He hopes to run in the Surrey County 5000 metres Championship and if international Norman Morrison, the Scottish athlete with a Surrey qualification, turns out that could be quite a race.

John's target now for the 'chase - 8 minutes 20 seconds!

THE FLYING TIGERS

by Thumper

THE FLYING TIGERS

by Thumper

As Battersea Park is almost on my doorstep I was more or less pressed into running the Boxing Day 2 miles by all my mates. So, there I was - new Club vest, baggy track suit bottoms and without a clue as I had only been in the Belgrave Harriers a few weeks and all this was quite new to me. I managed to get hold of an official looking fellow who entered my name and collected my money. He asked me what my handicap was and well, I almost said, "these two legs". After a bit of a haggle I found myself on a line way down the track.

Also on the line were two very fit looking types, a young lad with a bandage round his leg and Charlie Manning our beloved President. I still did not really know what was going on except that two miles was a hell of a long way on a Boxing Day. Then I noticed a peculiar phenomenon. All the runners were facing the wrong way. It took me a little while to figure out that I was wrong and I then gently turned myself round (it being the day after Christmas I was still a trifle numb).

Well, there was a sort of GO or OFF or something and we were all away at a fair old trot. Now the last time I was on a running track was sports day in "Wings For Victory Week" 1944 but I soon got the hang of it and with six laps to go my feet were thumping quite well. However, it soon became evident that I was building up a terrible oxygen debt. Legs were alright but my breath was pumping in and out in short pants, pyjamas and a scarf. Just when I thought that the Almighty had mis-read the instructions when he made me and had left out a lung or two, the young lad with the bandage dropped out and Charlie dropped back just to see if he was OK and keep him company. Thus there I was running at a fair old totter in the slipstream of the two fit heathens and with about four laps to go.

Admittedly we were moving at a good doddle but I was feeling as if my lungs were going to burst although the rest of me was tonking over quite well. I pottered on and then one of the heathens 'packed' leaving two of us all alone in front with half the Club howling on our tails. Two laps left and I gathered up all my strength from mind, body and soul and staggered on 'eyeballs out'. I forgot about breathing and I must have looked a terrible sight judging by the amazed look on the officials' faces as into the last lap I sped. With my famous 'Thames Embankment sprint' I flashed across the finishing line. I did not realise I had won (modesty prevents me from revealing my time).

Coughing and spluttering at the finish like an old steam engine that has past its best, I gradually 'came to' and trotted back to the dressing room for a good shower, a light massage and a little powder on my feet. I was soon as good as new. An organiser chap said that I must collect my prize (a very nice glass vase) and I would very much like to thank all those who made the Boxing Day meeting possible.

But the best is yet to come, dear readers, for my mother had bought me a pair of lightweight Tiger running shoes for Christmas and I was trying them out for the first time in this race. Hence the title of this story. I must say they do make a difference from running round the streets of London in ex-WD army boots, Zatopek style!

Obituary · page 35

Pat Parker

G. S. Doubleday

Pat Parker

Older members will be sorry to learn of the recent death of one of our international stars of the 'thirties' J. (Pat) Parker.

When he joined us he was already an Empire Games Bronze Medallist (London 1934) at 3 miles. He lived at Brighton yet rarely missed not only the Saturday race or training run but the Tuesday and Thursday training runs from the Rising Sun, Ebury Bridge Road. He was a Sussex County Champion and put up many fine performances for us over the country, on the track and in road relays. Perhaps his best effort for us was when he finished fourth in the 'National' at Stratford Upon Avon in 1937.

To him life and running was fun - a great Club Character.

G. S. Doubleday
