END OF BELGRAVIAN FAMINE

Your new Editor Charles Dickinson

It is nearly two years since Joe Coleman passed away. At the time nobody could have known that the March 89 Edition of Belgravian was to be the last under his Editorship. That issue of the magazine was itself a fine tribute to a man who worked so hard for the good of others. You will read in this issue of the loss of many Belgravians and it brings home to all of us that a great Club such as ours cannot survive without the introduction of new faces. Fortunately you will also read of a new Younger Committee and the involvement of several of our stars giving their time and efforts in support of us all.

In collating this Edition of the Belgravian I have decided to use material prepared in 89 although parts of it may have been overtaken by events in 90. To ease back into a more regular reporting situation I have set a cut off point at the end of the 1990 Track and Field Season, that is September 90. Hopefully it will not be too long before you are reading of events of Winter 90 and the 91 Season.

Your new Editor Charles Dickinson

SECRETARY'S ADDRESS / FORWARD / SCRIBBLINGS / REPORT

Sue Porter

It is very pleasant to be able to take this opportunity to go into print. This is my first office as General Secretary and, so I am told, I am Belgrave Harriers' first female to hold this office. This is not surprising though as females have only been allowed to be members of the club since 1977.

I took over the reins from Joe Coleman on the first of January 1988, and I am still getting to grips with the ins and outs of a prestigious athletics club, how its run, and how it fits in with regional and national athletics. I am trying to become familiar gradually with various sections of the club: road running; track and field; cross country etc. So far I've managed to extend out from my own section, race walking, towards the ladies section and even to running a 3000m race and throwing the javelin in the Womens Southern League.

Unfortunately, some of my races clash with major fixtures in the mens section and it is not possible for me to spectate and support as often as I should wish. Timekeeping for the Thameside League and the Ladies Southern League also keeps me busy. It is very enjoyable, even though hard work and time consuming, but certainly not dull and boring. If circumstances allow I may be able to look forward to being General Secretary for the next few years at least; the record secretaryship is held by A.Harley at 34 years, only another 32 years to go!

Needless to say, I am also proud to be a member of such a prestigious and historic club and to be involved as Belgrave starts its second hundred years. In a period when so much in athletics is changing it is important to continue to encourage grassroots athletics through schools and locally organised events. Continual upsurge of young potential in the area nourished by organised coaching schemes and supported by enthusiastic members can ensure an equally successful second hundred years for the club. I'm glad to be part of it !

Bill Laws - PRESIDENT 1990.

It is sometimes difficult to realise that many of our officials hold down responsible jobs, so hard do they work on behalf of our active members. Bill Laws, our 89/90 President, has worked continuously on the Belgrave Committee since 1974 mainly as a result of a good natured provocation from Charlie Manning. He has held most offices including Hon. Sec. and Coaching Sec. He organised the Club Dinner for 5 years and edited the Belgravian for 6 years but is best known as part of a double act, partnered by Leo Coy, which in 10 years has taken our 'A' Track and Field Squad from 35th ranking to No. 2 in the country. During this time he persuaded the GLC to upgrade our home track to an all weather surface and obtained grants and acted as project manager for the Belgrave Hall rebuild in the late 70's.

Bill is a graduate Mechanical Engineer who worked for British Steel for 17 years. In 1977 he established a private engineering Company developing new products for the Metallurgical Industries. In 1987 the Metals Society awarded him the Dowding Medal for his improvements to hot steel rolling and in 1989 his Company was one of only three U.K. companies to obtain both Queen's Awards for Techology and Export.

Bill joined Belgrave in 1952 while at Emmanuel School. He moved away; his membership lapsed but he rejoined in 1963. His athletic achievements he would describe as average Club Standard. However in 1978 as a vet he achieved some success finishing 2nd in the Club 20 mile Championships, 5th in the Surrey Vets Cross Country and with Laurie O'Hara and Brian Gorman won the Southern Counties Vets 10 mile team Championship. He ran 54.36 for 10 miles and that year made our 12 stage relay team.

He still has the ability to surprise and recently had the distinction (our Hon. Handicapper says 'Doubtful' distinction) of winning the Yacht 3 handicap in 2 consecutive years.

But it is on the management side that Bill has made the biggest contribution. Let's hope his best is yet to come !

IN MEMORIUM. JOE COLEMAN (October 1911 - April 1989)

Gordon Doubleday

Many of us saw Joe early in April, as usual busy helping things to run smoothly. We were all shocked to hear of his death in mid-April.

He had been involved in athletics for over sixty years - almost a lifetime.

Born in October 1911, he joined Belgrave Harriers in 1927 as a runner, taking part in handicap races, so popular in those days, and he was no mean shot putter. He then switched his attention to walking and gradually worked his way up the fields to become one of the country's leading performers over the shorter distances.

He was a regular member of the very successful Belgrave teams before World War II and also figured in a winning team for the Nijmegan Shield in the RWA 20 miles.

His dead heat with his brother, the late Len Coleman, in winning the RWA ten miles "junior" (based on performance) at Hampstead Garden Suburb in 1938, was a triumph he greatly enjoyed.

He was a loyal Surrey County supporter, winning the ten miles walk in 1939 and going on to finish second in the inter-counties 10. In those days there was no senior championship at ten miles, and this event brought all of the countries' top men at this distance together.

He was a regular competitor on the track, contesting the AAA Championships with distinction. Thanks to constant, hard and regular training, and attention to style, he gained a "Fowler-Dixon" award for style in the championships.

Then came the 1939-45 war when he served in the R A F but with the post war resumption of championships he soon got back to form and when RWA decided to hold a senior ten miles championship Joe scored in the winning team.

So he kept on for many years, helping many a newcomer with his advice.

On retirement from racing he took up judging. Over recent years he got much involved in the administrative side of the club taking over the Hon General Secretary's job at short notice, a job he did with his customary good natured efficiency. He also took on the job of editing the club magazine "The Belgravian".

His jovial and friendly manner will be missed by all who knew him. The respect in which he was held by Belgrave was demonstrated by his election to Life Membership, and in 1984 the ultimate honour of the Club Presidency.

Gordon Doubleday

JOAN GOSWELL R.I.P

John Keown

Joan was neither an athlete nor an official yet with her husband Jack she provided the sport of race walking over 30 years with a very reliable recording and results service. Joan probably attended more R.W.A. National Championships and Open races than many active competitors. I doubt if she saw many of these races except the start as she spent many, many Saturday afternoons tucked away in some small room often without heat or refreshment banging away at a typewriter in order to ensure that competitors could receive a complete result sheet shortly after the end of a race. I don't think that race walking fully appreciated the excellent service which Joan and Jack provided free of charge until it ceased a couple of years ago because of Joan's failing eyesight. Now it is no longer the norm to receive result sheets at Nationals in fact sometimes results are only available some days afterwards.

Although Joan was a very quiet lady she was held in high esteem by walkers who recognised her as one half of a very unique partnership which did much for race walking. The presence of Jack at a race without Joan usually drew the comment, "where is Joan?".

Rather belatedly in 1986 the Race Walking Association awarded Joan 'Life Membership', an honour which had been bestowed on her husband Jack in 1973.

Joan was a great lady who for the benefit of others was generous with her talents and free time and who, with Jack, gave yeoman service to Belgrave Harriers, the Race Walking Association and the sport of race walking.

Our condolencies to Jack on his great loss.

John Keown

A.F. "BERT" MITCHELL (1907 - 1989)

With acknowledgements to Ken O'Kell

We regret to announce the death of Bert Mitchell, donor of the Club's "Mitchell Shield" for the Belgrave Marathon Championship.

It was in 1931 that Ken O'Kell signed up Alf Taylor and Bert Mitchell, after beating the former in a 1 mile race at AEC Southall. After moving to Ford's Dagenham in 1932, Ken discovered that both lads were also Ford workers.

When Bert turned to marathon running he found that the alternating shift system at the works prevented him from training properly, particularly as he travelled by train from Wandsworth to Dagenham daily. He struck a bargain with his foreman. He could do a permanent early shift (6a.m to 2p.m) provided he agreed to produce 180 axle shafts a shift instead of the standard 160.

So he left home at 3.30a.m and arrived home at 4.00p.m each day. He never grumbled, never sought promotion; always said how lucky he was to have a job and to have time to train.

In the late thirties he achieved a second place in the prestigious Manchester to Blackpool Marathon. Because his fellow workers paid for his trip he wore a FORD Sports Club vest, a decision which brought a rebuke from that fiery little Belgrave Captain, Bert Footer!

One of his most prized awards was a special oak plaque, formed and carved by the King's woodcarver from Windsor Castle oak, presented for the Windsor to Chiswick marathon.

Bert was not one of our star runners in the highly successful teams of the thirties, but he was always there to provide back-up when needed. His will left a legacy of £100 to the Club: he always was a 100% Belgravian.

With acknowledgements to Ken O'Kell

F. RICKARDS

We are sorry to learn of the passing of one of our oldest members, Fred Rickards, one of our long-distance walking "greats". He was over ninety and had been a member for nearly sixty-five years. We recall the number of wins he had in the Police Barking to Southend Walk - eight in all, 1924, '25,'26,'30,'32,'33,'35 and '39 and helping his team, K Divison to victory.

He also had a great record as a team man, being in our winning teams in the R.W.A 20 miles Championship in 1925, '28,'38 and '39 and taking second individual place in 1934. He also helped the Club to win the Nijmegen Shield (for the complete team of eight) in '32, '38 and '39. Likewise in the 50 Kilos Championship he helped us to win in '34 (where he was third Individual), '35,'36 and '38. In addition he helped us to gain many team placings. He won the Club 20 miles Championship on a number of occasions, and had some fine performances on the London to Brighton Road to his name.

On retirement from the Police he spent his last years living at Jarvis Brook, near Crowborough, in Sussex. He was of a sturdy build and a powerful walker. He took his sport seriously and trained hard and conscientiously for the big events. His cheerfulness and ready smile plus a warmth of character made him many friends and admirers. He recently wrote expressing his pleasure at seeing the successes of our track runners as shown on television. Obviously a great team man to the end. Its been great knowing you Fred, you have helped in no small way in making Belgrave the great Club that it is.

Fred Rickards

Obituary · page 8

R.F. TOWNDROW

Gordon Doubleday

It is sad to think that Dick Towndrow died in September, a few weeks before attaining 50 years of membership of the Club. Born in 1908 and living at Barnes he enjoyed running in Richmond Park and on Wimbledon Common which he regarded as his "second-home".

His first club was Ashcombe AC which had their Headquarters near the Sheen Gate of Richmond Park. Later they became amalgamated with St.George's Services A.C, but this combination didn't last many years and he then joined the Poly. A good cross-country and road runner he was a formidable opponent and had many fine runs to his credit.

World War 2 broke out in September 1939 and in the November he joined us. He served in the Army and was able to continue his running. There were, of course, no championships but I remember a seven mile race over the old two lap Blackstaffe Shield course which he won.

After the war he represented us in many races. His best effort must have been when he won the Wigmore Open 15 miles in 1948. This race was held over a hilly course from Parliament Hill and his time of 87m.20s. was a very good one for those days. In the mid-fifties he moved to Mere, near Warminster, Wiltshire. His enthusiasm for the Club was such that he continued his membership.

A schoolmaster by profession he was a quiet modest and unassuming person, dedicated to his running and held in high regard by all. We have lost another of our long-standing stalwarts who many will remember with affection.

Gordon Doubleday

Obituary · page 9

DANNY WISEMAN (18.12.44 - 22.08.88)

(with acknowledgements to Alf Wilkins)

David "Danny" Wiseman died of cancer at the early age of 44 last August.

Danny joined the Club in 1954 at the tender age of 9 years. In 1959 he was second in the Boys Medal Competition, taking both the 100 and 800 events.

Alf Wilkins began coaching him in 1963, the year he first broke two minutes for the half mile. The following year he was a surprise winner at the NUTS winter track meeting at Hurlingham in a personal best of 1:54:4. He also represented Cambridge University against Oxford that year at the White City, a match which was televised live. He clocked a lifetime best of 1:52:2 for 880 yards in the 1965 Surrey Championships and gained a silver medal in the Maccabiah Games 800m.

The following year he was elected secretary of the Cambridge University AC but owing to injury did not complete again in the varsity match.

In 1973 Danny made a brief racing comeback and was very quickly into form. The following year he won the Club Championships in 1:54.7.

He remained a keen follower of athletics and attended all the recent World and European Championships and was planning to visit both Gateshead and Barcelona for the European and World Cups before his illness became so bad.

Our deepest sympathy is extended to his wife, Diane, and his two young daughters.

Obituary · page 9

DONALD MACLEAN

We regret to record the death of Don Maclean who died recently following an accident. He was 57.

Don and his brother were signed up for Belgrave by Tom Carter in 1948 when Don was 15 years old. Micky Pyer gave him a training schedule which soon began to show results, and the following season he ran three sub-5 minute miles. Improvement was dramatic the following year. He won the LAC Schools mile in the third fastest ever time of 4:28.2 (something special in those days), finished 2nd in the All-England Schools and beat Peter Driver in the Surrey Junior Mile with a record 4:25.6. He represented London versus Paris winning the 1500 metre and 2000 metres in the home and away fixtures.

Following military service in Korea he resumed training in late 1954 and went to Oxford University where he studied philosophy, politics and economics. He gained a 'blue' over the country and reduced his mile time to 4:14:7; but a troublesome stomach ulcer plagued his performances after that and his form deteriorated steadily. However, he still produced some very good runs for the Club and could claim that he had never dropped out of a race.

Don was an ardent Belgravian : the Club meant a great deal to him. Our sympathies are extended to Jennifer and her family.

LAURISTON COTTAGE SOLD

After 54 years of residence at Lauriston Cottage ARTHUR WHITEHEAD has returned to the land of his youth, Sedbergh, in the heart of the Cumbrian Fells.

Older members will recall running around the lawns of Lauriston House when Lady Fell, Arthur's step grandmother, was in residence. On her death the large house was demolished to make way for the housing estate which now occupies the land. Arthur retained the old stable block which, over the years, he converted to two dwellings and a running facility.

It was in 1945 that he had returned to Lauriston after being badly wounded in Normandy. His unique determination to rehabilitate himself led to his joining Belgrave. Gradually he built his weight up from 6½ stone and with regular, though painful, training runs and early morning swimming in Queensmere he eventually turned to competition road-running.

In 1953 Arthur achieved the performance that meant more to him than it could possibly mean to anyone else, he won a Belgrave Honours Badge in the A.A.A. Marathon of 1953. Following a successful completion of the London to Brighton Road Race he retired from competition to work for Belgrave and the newly formed Road Runners Club, of which he was a founder member. His contribution to our cross-country and road-running programme in the fifties and sixties was considerable, and his premises have always been at the disposal of Belgrave for the 12-stage relay and open '20'events.

The Belgrave Committee presented him with a limited edition of a new book on the history of Wimbledon, which is so dear to his heart. The leather-bound boxed presentation copy contained the following tribute:

"To Arthur, in appreciation of your 44 year long association with Belgrave Harriers (1945 - 1989)"

(Signed) W.R. Laws, President.

Harry Porter

Andrea Coore - Great Britain : Long Jump Morag Andrews - Scotland : Cross Country Sian Spacey - Wales : Racewalking

Well done Andrea, Morag and Sian!

However, our success in the Women's Southern League was dulled by injuries to both sprinters and mdiddle distance Seniors and also by having only a few Junior Ladies. The Schools' meetings held in Battersea Park over the early summer attracted a few more girls, and we strengthened our links with local schools.

We also had three Senior Ladies appearing in the UK 1989 rankings:

Wendy Dunsford : Shot Andrea Coore : Long Jump Ese Oshivire : Heptathlon

Well done Wendy, Andrea, and Ese, who also holds the British Universities Record for the Heptathlon.

Again due to injuries, 1989 was the first time in three years' competitions that Belgrave Ladies failed to reach the semifinals of the GRE cup.

Scoring in the Women's Southern League matches is by both Senior and Junior Ladies, unlike the position in the Men's Southern League matches which are for Senior Men only. This year, our lack of Juniors together with injuries to the Senior Ladies meant that Belgrave were relegated to Division Three for 1990, after four years in Division Two. This has also happened to three or four other London clubs who similarly suffer from a lack of Juniors, or even Juniors but a lack of Seniors. I have each year tried to have the rules changed, but so far nothing has been done to help clubs in the same position as us. We cannot rely on just one or two people to strengthen the Ladies Section, but need to encourage a concerted effort from all sections of the club, both normal club members and also committee members. The Ladies Section has a fine supporting group of Mums and Dads, officials and helpers. Well done everybody and thank you all very much for your support throughout the year, and good luck for 1990; we will need it!

A CASE OF 12.

WAYNE RACK.

You may have read that Oddbins were voted Wine Merchants of the year in 1989.At Xmas their colourful brochure dropped onto the mat and its contents made mouth watering reading. As usual there was the standard hyperbole. Strangely many of the descriptions seemed appropriate to our Road Running members and as my mind wandered yet again to the day when we might get our 12 best into the AAAs road relay I found the team selecting itself- and quite a presentation pack it made. Judge for yourself.

1. Thetford Mead 68. -High quality performer. Early hint of class now being confirmed. Stunning. Will mature further to give much pleasure in years ahead.

2. Vouvray Paton 84. -Light and sparkling. Vibrant. Refreshing up-front with a long aftertaste. Low alcohol makes it suitable for parties.

3. Chateau la Foote 63. -A fine performer with no delusions of grandure. Excellent balance. Considered by many to rank as best buy.

4. St Julien Chaston 68. -A favourite rouge with plenty of tannin(g). Elegant and rich with an excellent body.

5. St Matthew Chaston 67. -The second label of the famous Welsh lineage. Little to distinguish from its more up-front relation. A fine buy.

6. Coonawara Staines 87. -A tough customer produced by the best of Aussie knowhow. Travels well. A rarely seen vintage but a prize winner none the less.

7. Frascati Bizio Superiore.DOC. 85. -A clean and natural white- fresh from Italy. Careful preparation is now beginning to pay off with a good chance of true greatness. A long term investment.

8. Zinfandel Gladwin Blush 88. -Proves that local products can take on the best. Gold medallist. Classy,attractive,clean and quite long. Now making a comeback after several poor vintages.

9. Ward Cru Bourgeois 85. -Fruity and pleasant but can be unpredictable. Pushing some of the better names for recognition. Needs company for the best results.

10. Paul Masson Evans 88. -Not a Californian but a Northerly British equivalent. Distinct local flavour which has surprised Continental experts with its fine finish.

11. Gilbey NV(Non Vintage). -Ripe, ready now, will soon be over the hill. Full aromatic flavour-not to be sniffed at.

12. Clos de Hayes 89. -A good year following a poor growth. Frequently up and down but should eventually reward those who can wait.

GRAN RESERVES.

Also recommended with a good chance of making the top 12.

Saunders Shiraz 88. A newcomer who has outclassed several established names.

Sinclair 2nd Cru 84. Yet to obtain the standard of its illustrious forebear. Could be stunning.

Penfold LLoyd 88. Another great from Oz. Finding it difficult to get UK recognition. Needs time(about 3 months).

Schloss Maynard 86. Top of the list at Waitrose for past few years. Bold and inviting.

Darrell Mead 88. Disappointing year. Better 87"s

Adams Reserve 89. May confirm earlier promise but avoid the 89"s.

Fonseca Forrester 89. Ripe and round. Full bodied.

And there is more being laid down right now. But that is another story.

PAUL EVANS

1990 has seen Paul Evans, one of Belgrave's newer members, poised to make a great international breakthrough in athletics. He is perhaps best known for his fine performance recently in finishing 5th in the 20-stage Sun Life Great Race. However it should not be overlooked that Paul also headed the 1990 road 10,000m rankings and only missed the European Championship 10,000m qualifying time by a few seconds.

Paul came to athletics relatively late in life, only starting to train seriously in 1985 after running 33 minutes in a local 10 km fun run in his home town of Lowestoft. This was when he was 24. By the time he joined Belgrave in October 1988 he already had the reputation of being a very successful road runner, and had been Eastern Counties cross country champion. In the summer of 1989 Paul established himself as an important member of the Belgrave British League track team often racing 800, 1500 and 5000m for the club and picking up valuable first place points.

Paul begun 1990 with an impressive 2nd place in the New Year's Day Morpeth to Newcastle road race, just behind Paul Cuskin, but claiming the scalps of Steve Jones, Steve Brace and Geoff Wightman amongst others. Unfortunately a period of injury forced him to miss the major cross country championships and the World cross country trial. But in April Paul returned with an excellent 2nd fastest long stage in the Southern 12-stage relay, just behind Gary Staines and helping the Belgrave team to victory. Shortly after this he ran the fastest leg in the National 12-stage.

On the track Paul won his third consecutive Southern Counties 10,000m title at the end of April running a solo 28:36.2. A week later he broke the Southern Counties record in winning the 3000m in 7:58.82. He was rewarded with his first British International vest and won the 10,000m against Finland in Helsinki on June 7th. Here he lowered his PB to 28:18.94 a massive 45 second improvement from the previous year and only seconds away from the European qualifying standard of 28:15.

Paul was disappointed not to make the standard or the British team for Split. He thought possibly too many hard track races had taken their toll as he finished only 14th in the U.K. trial at Gateshead. However, by August he had returned to his best form producing the top U.K. 10 km road time in 1990 with 28:29 in Copenhagen.

Paul was uncertain whether to run in the 20-stage Sun Life Great Race in early September, but was persuaded by close friends and family. For the first week he mainly stayed in the pack not contesting the leadership. The eighth day, however, proved to be a landmark in Paul's athletic career. He had been given an ultimation by his employer to return to work on this day or lose his job. Paul decided to stay and run. He produced his first remarkable stage win that day which was followed by victories on stages 12, 14, 15 and 17. He was only just pipped at the post in the final stage at Westminster. His efforts were rewarded with a total of £15,000 prize money for his 5th overall place. As the first Brit home he had averaged sub 5 minute mileing for the whole 240 miles.

Will this success in the Great Race point to future marathon career ? Although many people think so, Paul is still very determined to break 28 minutes for 10,000m on the track, a time so rarely achieved by British athletes. We wish Paul luck and are very glad that he is running in Belgrave colours.

COMMONWEALTH GAMES REPORT - (or 'The Eddie Experience!)

by Bill Laws

The best laid plans of mice and men!

The start was inauspicious. Thursday 25th January turned out to be the day of the big storm. Gatwick Airport was closed and our New Zealand Airways flight eventually took off 22 hours late. As a result I missed the heats of the 100 and Gary's epic run in the 10,000m. Ron and Anne Wyld had been in New Zealand for 4 days and gave a full account of Gary trying to break a pack of 5 over the closing stages. A strong headwind down the back straight didn't help and he paid the penalty. He was inconsolable for a few days but was cheered by the genuine support of the various coaches and sports press who felt it was his best run to date.

Marcus Adam only just made the semi-final of the 100. Ron thought him lame but it turned out to be a loose spike plate. A change of shoes and he looked much sharper.

The Games, for me, started after 5 hours kip.

Sunday January 28th. The Sultan of Brunei had taken a floor of our hotel while our Patron Eddie K had staked his claim with a scattering of coaches and friends. My first pleasant surprise was the transport. Eddie had arranged for 2 stretch limo's - darkened glass, air conditioning, Union and New Zealand flags, the lot - to provide transport for the week. And that set the tone for what was to be a fantastic Games in more ways than one.

At the Stadium there was a brief parade and Don Anderson took over the responsibility of siting the Belgrave banner. Advised by former Belgravian Graham Seatter, who was working on the TV commentaries, he did a great job only losing it on the final day as Gary Staines and Andy Lloyd paraded it at the Closing Ceremony. Ron Wyld eventually recovered it from the Games Village so it will still be around this year.

On the track the final of the 100 was the highlight. All day the wind down the home straight hovered around 2.3m/s so that most sprint times were not valid for record purposes. John Regis managed a legal personal best, finishing 4th in 10.25 in the second semi-final, while Marcus Adam ran 10.31 to finish second in the 1st semi but easing over the middle 50m. In the final it was a different story with Marcus blasting from the gun in lane one to finish 2-100ths off bronze in a slightly windy legal personal best of 10.14 with John 7th in 10.22. The first day of the Mens Decathlon provided much excitement and in the Womens 3000m Carolyn Schuwalow (Mrs. Andy Lloyd who last summer trained from Belgrave Hall ) ran a splendid personal best of 8:53.89 to finish a creditable 5th.

Monday January 29th. The sprinters were back in action with the first and second round of the 200. Marcus was brimming with confidence and John knew he was back at his best. The races were a procession as follows: Round 1 Heat 1. 1st J. Regis 20.80; Heat 6. 1. M.Adam 21.00; Round 2 Heat 2. 1st J. Regis 20.40; Heat 4. M. Adam 20.35. Although windy the opposition were getting worried and the Games record of 20.43 was in danger; if only the wind would drop a bit from that tantalising 2.3 m/s.

Following the conclusion of the mens decathlon in which England's late entry Eugene Gilkes gained bronze, we awaited the heats of the 5000m. Belgrave cheers were for Andy Lloyd who finished a comfortable 4th in the second heat in 13:52.63. In the 800 heats and semi's Seb Coe was looking tired and new boy Matthew Yates did what we had seen so many times in last year's British League matches - left it late and kicked with devastating effect over the last 140m to ease into the final. Sadly in the Womens Shot Judy Oakes could only just manage Silver, much to her and our disappointment.

Tuesday January 30th. With this day devoted to the Men's Marathon we thought it was the one to watch on TV as all coast roads had been closed to traffic. Not to be beaten, Eddie had hired a Cruiser to follow the race, hopping ashore as appropriate. The limo's arrived at 6.30 and off we went with a fair sprinkling of names; Harry Wilson, Ron Roddan, Chris Brasher, Ann and Ron Wyld, Kevin Reed and so on. Despite misgivings everyone took to the water like hardened seafarers and apart from the marathon we got a guided tour of the Whitbread Round the World yachts which were due to race in the 3rd leg from Auckland the following Sunday - see what I mean about the Eddie experience!

Wednesday January 31th. Lisa Martin left them for dead from the gun in the Womens Marathon. We listened to the race on the car radio as the Belgrave contingent drove to Rautarua to view the volcanic activity and spouting 'geezers'. Well thats what we called them until the locals pointed out that 'geezers' came from the East End of London and we had better call them 'guysers'! We hurried back for an evening party for U.K. athletes hosted by Eddie K, President of the Sports Aid Foundation. Guest of honour was President Elect of the Commonwealth Games - HRH Prince Edward.

Thursday February 1st. The track and field programme was hotting up with an increasing number of finals. Sadly in the discus Abi Ekoku got it all wrong and had 3 no throws each of them out of the sector to the right. The first and last time we hope. Totally disconsolate, Abi was to learn that Neil Gray had hurt his back and that he would get a 'reprieve' and fill the 3rd England spot in the shot on the closing day. Time enough to get himself sorted out!

In the 200 semi's Marcus won the first in 20.33 and John striding effortlessly won the second in 20.35 - both windy but it looked good for the final. Later in the day the final was won by Marcus in a blistering 20.10 and John recording a windy personal best of 20.16, back to his very best; and to complete Englands medal tally Ade Mafe finished third with 20.26 - a truly great race and one of the highlights of the Games for me.

By now, Belgravians were homing in on the banner, first Paul Cuff then Charles Dickenson (Surrey Vets C.C. Champ of '88) together with his family came for a chat. They are on a world tour for 2 years and following a stay in the USA are now enjoying their sojourn down under. He's racing well but finding the competition tough. And believe it or not just sitting in front of me one day was Joe Dooley who I trained with at Battersea and last saw 15 years ago. Small world is'nt it?

And so to the 800 where Matthew Yates surprised all the critics with an excellent 3rd spot. We still had the 5,000m to look forward to. The event is now history, the series of trips, Ngugi's rush from his fall back into the lead and the thrilling last 200 in 24.2 seconds by Andy Lloyd to give him victory by the narrowest of margins. The Belgrave contingent were hoarse with excitement as the scoreboard proclaimed: 1. A.Lloyd (Aus) 13:24.86, J. Ngugi (Ken) 13;24.94, 3. I. Hamer (Wal) 13:25.63. Another great day!

Friday February 2nd. To represent one's Country for the first time, and to set a personal best is all one can hope. But to come away with a Silver medal is just a fairytale - and that fairytale happened to Ian Tullett in the pole vault following a slightly hesitant start in a difficult cross wind. The opposition quickly fell by the wayside as Ian cleared 5.25m to be assured of a medal. New Zealand decathlete Simon Poelman passed 5.25 and failed at 5.30 to give Ian Silver. The bar went to 5.35 and at this height he just failed by the narrowest of margins. Another Belgrave medal.

The heats of the 4x100m next with Marcus resting due to a slight groin strain. A red flag on the second handover caused a stir as John Regis was just on the line as Jarrett took the baton, but it was Ghana who were disqualified.

Saturday 3rd. Belgrave interest centred on Paul Edwards and Abi Ekoku in the shot. Paul, favourite for a medal did not disappoint with 18.17 for the bronze. But it was Abi who surprised us to throw a personal best in the fourth round with 17.45 for sixth spot. You could just see how that made up for the discus and he could enjoy the celebrations later.

Meanwhile in the javelin Nigel Bevan was looking menacing in the warm up throws. His coach Peter Byrne had been supervising his last minute preparations and we had been hearing rumours of 82/83 m efforts. But in this highest level competition the very best would be needed for a medal. Buckley opened with 84.90, Hill with 81.32 and the New Zealand pair pushing 78.00. Nigel could only manage 70.42, improving to 75.66. Going with the last round he found himself in 6th spot and it was here that his competitive nature took over. Everything was perfect as he launched a mighty throw - nearly the last of the competition - which soared towards the 80m mark. The New Zealand spectators held their collective breaths to see if the bronze was safe. It was, but only just, as Nigel's throw was measured at a new personal best of 79.70 to further extend his Welsh record.

Marcus came into the England squad for the 4x100 final and despite imperfect changing it was Linford Christie who ensured golds for the squad members in 38.57.

The Belgrave medal and points haul had risen to 4 Gold, 2 Silver, 1 Bronze, 3 fourths and a sixth. An achievement which put us top of the England Clubs, ahead of most Countries and one which was beyond our wildest dreams. Well done everyone.

And so it was over. A wonderful closing ceremony; totally more exciting than the Television portrayal. For me there are a host of wonderful memories, all pleasant, many amusing. I shan't forget John Bicourt arriving jet lagged at the Games Village in a downpour. The ever friendly Kiwi officials gave him the standard transparent thin blue mac - tight fitting, no arms just a small circle for the face. John was looking for athletes to race in Java. The last we saw of him he was trudging damp and miserable, towards the athletes quarters. Christ! I thought, he's missed his true vocation, he looks like an advert for safer sex! Farewell from the friendly Games.

BRITISH ATHLETICS LEAGUE REPORT

1989.

Match 1 Alexander Stadium Birmingham 13 May 1989.

A solid start to the new league season with 4th place at the end of the afternoon. Fine wins were recorded by Marcus(200m) and Nigel (Javelin) and by the 4x100 realy team (Lenny,Dave,John,Phil), who showed all the other teams how to run a relay. This 4x100 time and the 4x400 were to be our best times for the season. The most welcome sight was to see John Gladwin back in the Belgrave vest on the track, after a long illness. Nigel and Abi covered all the throws in a busy afternoon. One prediction came true in that we easily beat Haringey!

Match 2 Haringey 10 June 1989.

Another good result put us third in the league behind Birchfield and Haringey. John Gladwin, running the A string, showed that he is back to his best with a superb win over a good class field. Other wins came from Marcus(200),Simon ( B S/C), Femi ( a magnificent 16.38 TJ), and Andy Hutchinson (HJ). The high jump proved to be our top points event with Ossie and Andy for a 2nd and 1st. Paul Evans led all the way in the 5k to be caught on the line by Steve Harris. The pole vault proved to be controversial with the leading vaulters opting out, because of a dangerous take off box. Jimmy Summers would have none of it and proceeded to finish 4th in the A string and gained more points than in the Hammer!

Match 3 Alexander Stadium Birmingham 15 July 1989

With hopes high of consolidating our position we were brought down to earth by a disappointing 7th place, on our 2nd visit to Birmingham. We did not have a bad team on paper, but everybody else was at full strength. Femi and Phil Okoro combined well for a 2nd and 1st in the triple jump. Lenny was a hero with two sprint wins and he was ably backed by Phil Goedluck. Best performance of the day came from Andy Lloyd, who front ran the 1500 and left everybody for dead as he stretched out a very high class field. Marcello made an unbelieveable breakthrough in the chase and was rewarded by being asked to run the 5000, where he finished a brave 6th.

Match 4 Enfield 5 August 1989

The final match was switched to Enfield and there was still a possibility that we could be relegated. GRE BRITISH ATHLETICS LEAGUE - DIVISION 1 REPORTS 1990

Match 1 Depleted in several areas we travelled to Copthall with some trepidation. Fortunately other teams were experiencing similar early season problems and we finished on equal points with Shaftesbury but were awarded 2nd Match place due to our superior number of wins. Lennie Paul was a clear winner in the 'A' 100 and 200, Abi in the 'A' discus, Andy Lloyd in the 'A' 1500, Paul Evans in the 'A' pole vault while Mike Edwards arrived jet lagged straight from the U.S.A. to set a pb and a new Club Record by winning the 'A' pole vault with 5.30. His father, Mike Edwards Senior, did not stand idly bt but scored good points in the 'B' pole vault. Phil Goedluck scored a win in the 'B' 200 and our sprinters surprised Haringey in the 4 x 100 to win with Adrian Carter proving a remarkable substitute for an unfit Marcus Adam. The other notable performance was by one of our remarkable youths - 6 ft. 4 Stanley Osuide finishing second in the 'B' high jump. A quite remarkable debut for this young man in the U.K.'s Premier League.

Match 2 Crystal Palace could almost be classified as a home fixture but it proved unlucky. As with most home fixtures athletes made their own transport arrangements and the team managers were reduced to gibbering wrecks as several athletes failed to appear or announced late illness. Badly hit were the sprints with John Regis our only top ranked man losing a tight battle with Linford Christie in the 100. Norman Shute travelled at late notice from Gateshead but travel plus the competition proved quite a problem for him. Our middle distance men rallied well with Darryl Maynard standing in at 20 minutes notice to gain good 800 m points while we won both 1500m's with Stuart Paton gaining a faster win in the 'B' than Andy LLoyd in 'A' - but Andy then ran the 'A' 5000 some 30 minutes later to record a very creditable 14.04. Abi again scored maximum points in the discus and shot and Phil MaKay made a welcome return to ensure a clean sweep in the shot. Our javelin was again below par with Nigel Bevan still sidelined with injury. Mike Edwards junior edged the Club pole vault record higher as he continued his quest for maximum points through the season. All in all the team did well to finish 3rd and held onto 2nd place in the League.

Match 3 We travelled to Birmingham in the knowledge that several of ur stars had performed in Edinburgh at the previous night's International Match. Marcus Adam made a game effort to boost our results folloiwng a good performance in Edinburgh but it was soon apparent that lack of deep down fitness had caught him out and he just walked in in the 200. Gary Staines used this match to assess fitness after injury and his winning performance looked good for the future. But the big surpise was Olly Foote winning the 'B' to make it a first time double in BAL Division 1 5000 m's. Another double in the shot but Abi just couldn't master the wind in the discus and settled for second. Our 400 hurdlers produced a record number of points as Paul Beaumont set a new Club Record in his debut while previous record holder Neil Hammersley showed a return to form in the 'B' race. Mike Edwards continued his winning ways in the pole vault and Lenny Paul clocked up two 'B' string sprint wins. Another 3rd place for the team to narrowly maintain our 2nd League place but Birchfield look determined to catch us.

Match 4 Our traditional poor start was at least reversed as Phil Spivey made his debut for us to gain 4th place in the hammer followed immediately by a fine 2nd in the 'A' 400H by Paul Beaumont. Neil Hammersley gained his first 'B' 400H win with a sub 53.0 clocking to show there's life in the old dog yet. Our long sprints were a disappointment considering the depth we have in the 200m but Jay Busk performed above expectiations to gain a 4th in the 'A' 400. Other than another fine win for Mike Edwards the highlight was a javelin double win to herald the return to fitness of Nigel Bevan and a new model Jason Bridges who has proved an enthusiastic competitor willing to fill in as required - a boon to team managers which is also exhibited by Darryl Maynard, Ian Wells, Abi Ekoku among others. But despite their support there were too many gaps to prevent us finishing 3rd in the League. Our final points score was some 200 points higher than our previous best.

CIVIL SERVICE/BELGRAVE HARRIERS

The unofficial Belgrave club championships were very kindly organised by the Civil Service at New River Stadium on 9 August. Highlight of the day for Belgrave athletes was Ian Well's 5th succesive win in the 400m hurdles, celebrated in a vulgar display of arm waving! Ian also took 2nd in the high hurdles and Jay Busk won the 400m. In the field Chris Cornwell won the high jump and Phil Okoro the long jump. Phil did manage to jump a lot further after the competition but that's another story. Jay Busk also had a hand (leg?) in his department's winning relay team. Belgrave athletes took part in all Civil Service matches and formed a strong part of some very succesful teams although records of results are a little sketchy. Young Athletes.

Leo Coy

Round-up 1990

The most outstanding achievements for our track and field season were the performance of our two outstanding high jumpers - Stanley and Sean. They finished first and second in the English Schools Champs and were rewarded by gaining Schools International Caps. They both regularly competed for the British League Team and were instrumental in our B team gaining promotion to Southern League Div. 1.

John Abadon also represented his school at the English Schools and also ran for our promotion winning Southern League team.

Our season in the Woolworths Young Athletes was somewhat disappointing, our best result was in the first match and after that we steadily went downhill.

The Colts section were quite magnificent and almost achieved maximum points in every match. The team spirit among the Colts was really very good and we look forward to this being carried forward into the 1991 season. Peter Lloyd had a magnificent season and narrowly missed breaking the Club record for 800m. He has carried this form into the Winter CC season.

The Boys age group also performed well and with a great spirit. For example, Ben Cooper could not run because of injury, so he turned up and threw the javelin. Equally Toby Earle volunteered to throw the hammer and finally succeeded, but it may be for the last time !!

The Youths section was weak because of lack of numbers. Stanley and Sean turned out in most matches and we were lucky to find a new sprinter in Rickie. He competed in two YAL matches, as a first year Youth and we look forward to him playing an important role in this year's league matches.

The Thameside League was very disappointing with only a very small number of athletes turning out in each match. We could do welll but it would need everybody being committed to turning out in every round.

The coming season should be one of our best ever with a lot of new faces in the Boys and Colts sectiosn and a few more Youths to strengthen our team. If everybody pulls together and we cover all events then we will get promoted, so volunteers please for pole vault, hammer, steeplechase, 400h, etc., etc.

Finally thanks to Chris Knight for his help and to all the parents who assisted in the field events, with a special thanks to Andrew Hill's Dad for looking after the Thameside League.

Leo Coy

27TH AUGUST-1ST SEPT. 1990 XVth EUROPEAN CHAMPIONSHIPS SPLIT

KING JOHN

As the UK turned the tables on the opposition and finished with a record breaking 9 golds and a total medal tally of 18, the star of the European Championships was John Regis. Bronze in the 100m (10.07w), winner of the 200m in a legal 20.11, silver medal with Marcus in the 4x100 in a new UK record of 37.98, and another gold in the 4x400 with John running an incredible 43.93 split as the quartet sped to a new European record of 2:58.22

In the 5000m Gary Staines lived up to our highest hopes taking the lead with 100m to go, but having to succumb to Antibo's final burst still to finish with a fine silver in 13:22.45.

Many of the pole vaulters failed to qualify and among several of the elite who missed the cut was Mike Edwards who only managed a disappointing 5.10. Abi Ekoku also turned in a below par performance with injury reducing his best discuss throw to 53.80.
