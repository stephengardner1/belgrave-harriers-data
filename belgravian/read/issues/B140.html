EDITORIAL

Dear Fellow Members, This issue marks the arrival of a new Belgrave year, a new President, and as you can see from the head of this page - new Editors. We thank the Bishop brothers for helping to establish our journal as a regular, healthy and expressive organ of Club activity, and we will endeavour to maintain the high standards they have set. We hope to introduce new ideas in future issues and are quite prepared to face adverse criticism. A big post-bag indicates a lively readership, so let us have your ideas too.

The Autumn issue of the Belgravian always has plenty of news to cover. This,together with a back-log of results to last June, has enabled us to produce the biggest issue for many years - 28 pages. We should hastily point out however, that for ecconomic reasons it is not possible to continue publishing more than 20 pages, so the accent will be on quality rather than quantity.

Although the summer season was not one of our most succesful there emerged several performers who could well gain higher honours next year. In the forefront of these is Ray Hall who has been outstandingly consistent in his walking, and whose quest for Olympic honours will be followed with great interest. Peter Eldridge and Gerry LeRoy, in their first fully active season for some time, showed that very little lies between them and representative honours of high note, and Charlie Dabbs, who at long last triumphed over persistent injury and established himself as one of the country's leading distance runners by his magnificent performance in the R.R.C. 25km event. Success leads to success and as a result many other stars are likely to be swept along in the wake of these athletes. We are firmly of the opinion that Belgrave is now equipped with the right sort of material to commence a succession of highly successful seasons- and what better year to start than Olympic year, 1960!

Ray HallPeter EldridgeGerry LeRoyCharlie Dabbs

THE PRESIDENT - 1959/60

A.A.Harley.

Our newly-elected President, T.C."Charlie" Jones, has certainly grown-up with the Club for he became a member as far back as October,1926, and has been very much in evidence ever since in all our activities. His early interest in our sport was derived from his father who became a member on the Club's formation in 1887, and could tell many an interesting story of the early days; and of the matches Bels v Bats (the long-since defunct Battersea A.C.) on the roads over the Chelsea Bridge—Battersea Bridge circuit.

Charlie was also a cricketer of some skill, and as junior and youth athletic events were infrequently held at the time, he spent most week-ends in perfecting his fast bowling. Despite his activities on the cricket pitch he was at all other times to be found at Battersea Park Track- the focal point of all our Summer interest in those days.

The hurdles always fascinated Charlie and his first club award was for the event. Later he was to win the Surrey County 440 yards Hurdles Championships, and later still to gain Surrey County place medals in the Hop Step & Jump and Javelin Championships. He competed in our Junior Cross-country teams, and in later years established himself as one of our best runners at 440 and 880 yards. His long-striding action was a delight to watch, and only inability to translate that terrific stride into a sprint over the last thirty yards or so prevented him from carrying off many Championships. His interest has always been extended over the full range of field events, and he became a Club Champion at the triple jump and was responsible for many excellent performances in this event and the long-jump. To add to this story of athletic versatility must be added the fact that Charlie was always (and still is) a most accomplished stroller both at home and abroad; and had he decided to try himself out as a race-walker it is certain he would have achieved further successes.

Charlie had the good fortune, shared with many others, to be part of that tremendous enthusiasm which permeated the Club in the late 1920's and 1930's, when our star was in the ascendant, when each month saw more battle honours won, more athletic history made. Even the slowest man in the Club felt he was pushing the man in front of him, who was forcing the man ahead of him to give of his best, and so up to Club team level when the reserves compelled the selected team men to run every inch of the way. Charlie epitomised this intense Club spirit and was at every Championship urging and working for our successes.

On the administrative side Charlie has done yeoman work and served in a variety of offices and been in on higher councils for the greater part of his Club life. His legal knowledge has been of the greatest help to us, his especial work for Field Events and Coaching within the Club still continues; and his work in the administrative field has been almost as versatile as his active career in our colours.

We may be sure that Charlie Jones will serve us well as President. Let us resolve to serve him and the Club equally as well.

A.A.Harley.

THE PRESIDENT SPEAKS

T.C.Jones The President.

I view the bestowing upon me of the Presidency of this famous Club as the crowning glory of what seems almost a lifetime as a member. It comes to comparatively few and so is sure to be a treasured memory for the rest of my days. It came as the greatest surprise to know that I had even been considered for this honour. However having had it conferred upon me I would offer my sincere thanks and express my regrets that I was far away on holiday in Switzerland when the election took place. The latter however saved me the qualms of opening my mouth to verbally express my thanks.

Enough of thanks, which I hope I have adequately expressed, and on to our aims as true Belgravians. We have a fine crowd of athletes in all branches of the sport in which we participate. We have our Internationals, and good,mediocre, and indifferent performers - rabbits as well. That is what a Club such as ours should have, it helps to make Athletics what it is. The burning spirit behind all and each should be "The Club" - to keep, to make and enhance its name and reputation. All can help in this - team spirit is what will achieve it. The turning out of full teams in all Championships,Competitions and even Club Matches should be the aim of everyone, even if it be only for the sake of not letting your Club mates down.

We have had our successes in the past, but it is to the future we look, to repeat successes, achieve new ones and so to keep this great Club ever in the forefront of our sport. Being one of the few Clubs in the country which is so numerically strong in all its Branches -Track, Field, Cross-country, Road-running, Track and Road-walking - we have great opportunities and with your efforts doubled and re-doubled in the name of Belgrave successes must come.

T.C.Jones The President.

KINNAIRD TROPHY - CHISWICK - JUNE 13th.

Although the final result proved to be our worst performance ever - last out of nine teams, our track men ran particularly well and were quite justified in feeling they had all the bad luck it was possible to have.

The field event section were extremely weak and failed to collect a single point; most of the star performers being injured or committed elswhere.

In the heats of the 100 yds. both Charle Jones and Sylvan Dean narrowly failed to qualify for the final with 10.1 and 10.4 secs respectively. Charlie, however, succeeded in the longer sprint with 22.3, whilst Peter Eldridge was eliminated with 22.2! Peter had previously missed qualifying for the 440 final by the finest of hairs breadths. With 50.2 Clive Ryan qualified for the final, but was unable to reproduce a similar time.

With three to qualify in the 880 yards heats both John Thresher and Bob Taylor (Capt) finished fourth in their respective heats. John's time of 1:57.5 is a personal best . He is obviously a great prospect for the future.

John Davies showed a most welcome return to form in the 1 mile turning the tables on Mal Robinson - his victor in the Club Champs., with 4:13.7 for 4th place, as against Mal's 4:19 for 9th.

Yet another 4th place was recorded in the 3 miles where Pat Newell ran a creditable 14:23.8. Don Maclean was 12th in 15:23.

CECIL FRY TROPHY - WOODFORD - 4th JULY

Despite a strong wind to counter the 80 deg. temperature, the high humidity coupled with the loose state of the track made fast times impossible. This was not the only set-back, for we were minus all sprinters;they doing their best to record 2nd place behind the Valley, in the Southern 4 x 110 yards relay.

It was therefore left to Gerry LeRoy to open our scoring. This he did with a vengance winning the quarter in a scintillating 49.4 secs. Alan Jackson was unable to reproduce the 51.8 he did in his heat and finished 6th, in 53.9 secs.

Dereck Johnson ran as he liked to win the 880 yards, moving away from the field at 660, at which point our Dave Mitchell was lying second only to fade badly over the last furlong, and record 2:01 for 6th place. It was undoubtedly the heat which upset him, for as he said afterwards,"At 220 to go the heat hit me, and I folded up."

Running his first track race this season for the Club, Ranjit Bhatia did well to close in at 9th with 4;23, in a good class mile. This was some three seconds faster and three places higher than Mal Robinson, who was however, running with a heavy cold. Our last track man Ray Middleton walked well to close-in fourth with 15:31.2 in the 2 mile walk.

It was annoying to see only one field-events man but John Ware threw well to record fourth in the Javelin with 158'2½".

CEN. ROAD SERVICES SPORTS - 12th JULY

We received an invitation to send a medley relay and a three mile team to this fixture, held on a grass track at Langley Park.

In the medley relay only three teams turned out - T.V.H., Met. Police & Bels, so the boys were assured of ' a pot'. The first leg, an 880, was run by Dave Mitchell and he handed over to Alan Jackson some twenty yards behind Linford (T.V.H.) and Frank Wright, running for the Police. We lost a little further ground by the time Charlie Jones received the baton, and try as he could he was unable to make any impression on Dave Segal. With the quarter to go the Valley had some forty yards lead,with the Police twenty yards up on the Bels. It looked as if we were booked for third place. But Gerry LeRoy had other ideas! Running like a man inspired he had caught Brian Morris, running for the Police, by the 220 and was hareing after the Valley. With forty yards to go Gerry had caught and passed the Valley, but Morris with his customary last burst, moved up to pass on the line. A wonderful run Gerry!.

Rangit Bhatia and Mal Robinson finished equal second in the 3 miles, after a desperate last 80 yards sprint.

With half-a mile to go Anderson, the Police Champion, and Rangit moved away from Mal after the three had interchanged the lead all the way. When 300 yards out Mal began to sprint and had caught and had his nose in front by the home straight. But Rangit was not to be beaten and they crossed the line to-gether, with a time of 14:52.5, a personal best for each. Don Maclean and Charlie Walker finished 6th & 7th, for the team to take the team award.

MATCH v VICTORIA PARK - BATTERSEA - 15 JULY

July fixtures are not generally well supported, and this was by no means an exception. However, those present were equally divided between Vic.Pk. and ourselves, and a four-man field was found for most events; giving the match an International flavour!

Victoria Park, noted for their middle-distance runners duly won the 880 and mile events, whereas the Bels scooped the 100, 220 and 440 yards.

Gerry LeRoy won a sparkling 440 running in the out-side lane, and the ever versatile Jimmy Heathfield returned to the distance over which he was winning 12 years ago, to win the quarter in 54.6 secs.

Following his now customary warm-up from dressing room to starting line Don Maclean duly took third place in the mile (after having borrowed some spikes) with a creditable 4:26.6.

Norman Caves was in excellent form winning the high-jump with 584" and the hop-step & jump with 44'10". The ever present Jim McGrath was second to Norman in the high-jump, and also the shot, and it was most pleaseing to see Andorka competing once more after examinations, to win the shot with a putt of 43'1½".

In the junior match Douglas Crosbie, our most promising sprinter who is but a youth, was narrowly beaten in the short sprint with 11.4, but ran a perfectly judged 220 to snatch victory in 24.2. Under the watchful eye of Frank Simmons he should become a most valuable addition to our senior sprint ranks.

Bernard Linley, who will soon be knocking at the door of our senior teams, finished second in his first half-mile with 2:2.1, and soon after, over his favourite distance of 440 yards ran out a creditable winner in 54 .5 secs.

The steadily improving Eric Penny was second in the mile with 4:38.1 followed by Brian Kolbe in 4:47.8.

THE 8th ANNUAL BELGRAVE '20' - 15th AUG.

This was another event to suffer through fixture congestion - the Liverpool marathon and the Enschide event being held the same day. Despite this, a good field of 73 competitors faced the Deputy Mayor of Wimbledon (Councillor A.E.Ayres) to await the start in conditions summarised on the result sheet as "fine and warm, with a cooling breeze."

After the first of the four 5 mile laps W.'Digger' Pawsey, of Mitcham A.C. and Brian Cooke of Haltemprice R.R.C., were almost a minute clear of the field. Then came Len Bishop in 3rd. place Eric 9th, Bob Glover (coerced into running by Ken Stimpson) 10th!, with Alan Bishop, Joe Dooley and Bob Plummer running well together in 14th position - 1 min.49 secs. down on the leaders.

At the end of the second lap it was clear that the first two places had been booked by Pawsey and Cooke, but Lennie was hanging onto third place despite nagging attention from Hopcroft (TVH) and Shave (HHH), whilst brother Eric had moved up to 8th. Bob Glover was back to 12th with Alan Bishop, Joe Dooley and Reg Plummer hard on his heels. At this stage the Bels. and the Valley were neck and neck in the team race and excitement was mounting.

Necks were craned to watch the runners approach 15 miles in the hope that Len had established third place, but unfortunately he had not had a good time this lap for Hopcroft and Shave had well and truly dropped him, whilst Elderfield (TVH) had also moved ahead by some 23 secs. Eric was still 8th and Alan now 12th fighting it out with Davies (TVH) and Paxton (HHH). Bob Glover was fading badly and was now a further 1½ mins. down, having been caught by Joe Dooley and Bob Plummer. Ken Stimpson, Geoff Pearson and Pete Mathews were 23rd, 25th and 26th. when the fifty-six competitors remaining went out for the final lap.

Pawsey was a comfortable winner in the 2nd. fastest time ever recorded, 1hr 51:06 and Cooke's 1hr 52:12 had been bettered on only three occasions. Len Bishop was first man home for the Bels. finishing 6th, the same 23 secs behind Elderfield as he was with 5 miles to go. Eric Bishop had a most uncomfortable last lap owing to stomach trouble and dropped five places, however, the team result wasn't affected and the Bishop brothers were still our first three men home. Thus enabling Sam Ferris to write "Equally popular were the performances of the amazing Bishop brothers (Bishop A.C.) who placed Belgrave 2nd......"

Joe Dooley finished strongly to finish 14th, two up on Bob Plummer and six up on Geoff Pearson, who had moved through from 35th with the most even-paced run of the day recording lap times of 31:20, 31:29, 31:46 and 31:51. Ken Stimpson also ran exceptionally well to finish 21st. Bob Glover came almost to a standstill on his last lap (37:02!), but still recorded 2hr 07:57 and took 2nd place in the handicap for good measure. Pete Mathews also had an encouraging run while Arthur Penstone was another last lap sufferer.

T.V.H., won on superior staying power but with 10 in 29 the Bels. could be justly proud of their performance.

FIELD EVENTS REVIEW.

T.C.J.

The past season has been but a moderate one for whilst we have some good performers we have insufficient depth even up to a moderate standard. The main event for our men "The Sward", has been fully reported in an earlier edition. Injuries and illness beset us then and we have had a recurrence throughout the season, and more often than not have not turned out full teams in Trophy matches or even, to our disgrace, in our own home inter-club matches.

Derek McCorquindale has no doubt been our best class performer. His new Club Championship best with the Shot of 49'9" and later the same day a 51'4" to win the Bucks Championships were two of his best efforts; he was also selected for A.A.A. teams.

Dave Ansell has had quite a good season, attaining a place in the Middlesex Championships and winning the Maccabi European Championship with 22ft odd. He, Jim McGrath,and in the late season Reg Chamberlain (just back from the Forces) represented us in most of the Long Jumps.

Jim McGrath is another "Iron Man" of the calibre of Mal Ash. His efforts in two, three or even four events at matches has been of inestimable help and in true Belgrave Spirit. He tells me that he has not been so fit as in some years,but gave a creditable show in finsihing 10th in the A.A.A. Decathlon with 4,405 points; just missing Standard. Pole Vault is really perhaps his best event, although he has run well over the 120 yards Hurdles. It was unfortunate that in this event Robin Tolson was unable to overcome an early season injury, and was never able to race. John Sams has done virtually nothing hurdlewise, except the Sward, but Pat Fox ran one or two reasonable 440's as second string to Charlie Jones.

Norman Caves's early effort of 46'5" in the Triple Jump gave promise of great things but he suffered injury and it was not until late in the season that he came back with performances nearing his best. He again won the Beds and Hunts Championship. Dave Ansell has supported well in this event and Brian Jones, a new member, shows promise. Charlie Jones has also performed creditably in the triple and long-jump, but his hurdling seems to have gone to pieces; although in the Fulham Borough meeting a win in the 220 lows with 25.7 secs showed something nearer his true standard.

In the High-Jump we have been really weak but perhaps Alan Stanborough will get back to his true 6 ft plus form next year. Jim McGrath, Dave Rasey and Norman Caves have all reached 5'6".

The Javelin and Discus were weak events. John Ware has shown small improvement with a personal best of 169'10½" gaining second place in the Vancover Trophy, but is inconsistent. Bill Couzens found that Army life does not make for much improvement, more particularly perhaps because he has not been able to compete regularly; although he has thrown 169 ft and over in practice. Deszo Andorka has virtually had no Discus competition owing to examinations but we hope next year he will be able to get back his true form. Our other performers are all below 120 feet.

Percy Porter, in the Hammer, has not reached his best but it is good to see Jock Whyte improving, winning the Middlesex only to have a bad day in the Scottish Championships. Ted Arnold has also shown a small improvement.

Our Juniors seem non-existent, although Mike Smithers and Alan Blackett have appeared in the Jumps- but where are the throwers? The same might be said for the Youths, except for Dereck Wade in the Jumps.

Norman Dewberry has now taken over as Secretary and I hope you will all give him your earnest support to make the Club what it should be and ensure we turn out full teams next year. We can win or help to win most matches and Trophies just by turning out a full team. We hold our Open Winter Field Events on 5th March 1960, when I hope all will turn out. /cont foot P.12

One last note. I would like to thank everyone for their support particularly those who acted as Judges and Marksmen. T.C.J.

JACK GOSWELL REPORTS

Jack Goswell

The distance season finished with a first class performance by all the lads in the London-Brighton walk. And there emerged a new Club long-distance champion, in Ray Hall. He capped a grand season by finishing 3rd, followed by George Beecham fifth and Hugh McGreechan tenth. These three occupying the first three places in the Club Championships, held in conjunction.

We now start on a new Winter Season and look forward to a good crowd out pack training, from the Hall. Steady work through the winter always pays dividends when the Championships come along. We should try and start the season by annexing the Southern "Garnet Cup" race once again (it's a few years since we won this) at Trowbridge. So all you 'Junior' walkers set your target on the first week in February, for this ten mile event - and then progress on to the major Championships.

We welcome new members, J.Levison, Peter Tyson, John Unwin Peter Grant and John Taylor, and look forward to their steady progress. Our Junior team needs recruits so never lose the opportunity of enrolling likely lads!

We hope to have a first class muster of club men for our own Open 7 Miles, on Nov 21st. Forty years old, and the oldest open seven in the Calender, make it a "must" in the walking-world. Prizes for this event have always been of the highest standard and every help is needed to enable us to meet the high cost involved. Our thanks are due to all those stalwarts who have supported us in this endeavour. Donations would be gratefully received by the Treasurer, or Walking Hon.Sec.

We are pleased to learn that George Checkley is recovering from his illness, and hopes to start training again.

Congratulations to Terry Clark on his recent wedding to Janet. Several Club members attended the ceremony. Terry, who is an R.A.F. Technician, is due for Australia and we offer him the best of luck and a resumption of his brilliant walking career when he returns.

Bernard Eglinton has found another sister! This time in, of all places, Cyprus where he is to stay for two years. Best wishes Bernard may your family grow, we will miss you on the Road.

Stan Vickers won a very good twenty kilometer walk in Varese, Italy on Sunday 27th September, when he beat his arch rival Ken Mathews by over two minutes. He certainly pulls out the stops on his trips abroad.

After a four month lay off, due to leg-injury, Eric Hall is doing that steady work which is so necessary to reach peak.

May a good winter season reach a climax in the Championships, with all those improving walkers making their presence felt. And doing their best to topple those at the top.

All the Best, Jack.

LONDON TO BRIGHTON WALK - 12th SEPT

E.W.H.

The Brighton this year again dissolved into two distinct races, the two Metropolitan men, Thompson and Misson were locked together until just after Crawley, but Belgrave supporters were more interested in the third place and team race.

Met. have relieved us of several long distance trophies this season and although they have provided more individual winners than any other club they had yet to win the Edward Knott Memorial Shield.

George Beecham was more restrained than in other years and did not attempt to lead early on but as the leaders were doing over 7½ m.p.h. it was not surprising. The lads soon settled down and forgot all about the two wee men in front who were already burning up the road - someone said that as the morning was coolish they were keen to get to the seaside before sun-up!

At Croydon Hugh McGreecgan was the leading Bel. in third place and as the field passed it was obvious that this year the team event would be a close thing. Several of the lads were not as fit as we had hoped and I heard one wife remark that her husband only turned out so that she and the children could have a day out! The gaps were appearing and with the forecast of a warm day now being fulfilled there was much speculation about times.

By 20 miles Ray Hall was third in the cracking time of 3:3:00 he was being whipped along by "Digger" Clark who returned from his honeymoon to attend Ray. George was bashing along with brother Eric shedding a few stone on the bike, it is a question each year of which gives up first - the bike or Eric, but somehow they both survive. As for George I believe he smells the sea air at Streatham, Mac had eased up for a while and was developing a blister but with only 34 miles to go was not worried. Harry Shields was intent on time and motion study earlier in the year but this was not evident at this stage. However, you can always rely on the "old man" giving of his best when it is most needed. Newcomer, Jack Keown was making hay before the sun shone and Bernard Eglinton, the enigma, had begun to move through to his real place. Cyril Yescombe was very short of training but was having a go for several reasons. Percy Wilson was this year representing the Wilson clan and the other Bel. was Fred Rolfe who unfortunately had to give it best at Crawley.

The race dosn't really get under way until Crawley and although Met. led in the team race all the way it was a close thing as only Don Thompson had a trouble free race; and we always felt we had a chance. Ray really consolidated his third place and George was not very keen on the idea of relinquishing his Club title, so he tore along in hot pursuit and with Don was the only one to record a faster time than last year. Mac was really in a bad way but with Fred Denny attending was able to peg back the vital place here and there. Harry closed in as fourth man, 'pot-hunter' Shields the veteran award winner; Bernard made a good recovery after a bad patch on Dale Hill and he just pipped Jack Keown by two seconds, Jack walked very well for a first-timer and was heard to say that it was easier than an Open 7. Cyril and Percy walked steadily from Crawley and gained several points but Met. with first and second were just too good, and won by a point!

The attendants had the worst time, this year for instance one was given a bottle of stuff he couldn't open, somewhere around Gatwick he asked "How the hell do I open this", to which the walker gave the obvious, "Jam it in a door." Next year the attendant swears he'll take a door with him. Our attendants performed excellently and are to be congratulated!

Well done Met. - but, keep the shield clean it's only on loan! Congratulations to: Don, on a great performance with the weather against you; Ray, for gaining perhaps another International vest for Belgrave and also on winning the Club Champs., and finally to Harry for the first Belgrave vets award since 1934.

LONDON to BRIGHTON (SOUTHERN CLUBS) -10th OCT

W.E.L.

It seems to have been a foregone conclusion that Portsmouth A.C., with the help of Hyman and Tulloh (with or without shoes) would win this race. And so it proved, for in no uncertain manner this 'minor' club ran away from the rest of the field to come home easy winners, nearly three minutes to spare.

It seemed a travesty of justice that after months of excellent weather this should be the day for the break to occur - the rain started soon after the start and continued without a let up for the full distance.

We can be satisfied that we have qualified for the next National, but much work must be put in before we can hope to close the gap between us and Portsmouth, even supposing that this club can annex the April race. At least thirty seconds must be knocked off every runners time -no easy matter but by no means improbable.

1st Stage. Ranjit Bhatia (21.17). The usual dash along the embankment did not deter this runner from India, who had endeared himself to all with whom he has come into contact. Using his head in experienced manner he dogged the leaders and moved up at Cedars Road Hill, where he proved to be better fitted than most of his opponents. Briault of Brighton, romped away to a fourteen seconds lead and our lad came in third to give us a good start.

2nd Stage. Charlie Walker (26.22). No lack of experience here although everyone crosses their fingers when 'What do you think' Charlie sets off. When the Pompey runner moved away it seemed Chas. was to have one of those days , but when Haith (T.V.H.)and later White (Woodford) caught up it seemed to act as a tonic and he stayed with them to the end, where with a typical final flourish he kept us in third place, only thirty secs. down on Pompey.

3rd Stage. Pat Newell (29.49). Pat was switched from his usual leg to this stage as he had to return to work. He held his position by steady running, until half-way up the aerodrome stretch of Purley Way when Foord of Brighton, running in fine style, sailed past. Just before reaching Purley cross-roads Herring (Blackheath) caught Pat, and on the run in John Thorpe (St.Albans) also took him, so we were back to sixth and the gap between us and the leaders, still Portsmouth, was now forty-eight seconds.

4th Stage. Clive Shippen (26.24). Our new cross-county captain running in his usual reliable fashion set about his task in steady style. Unfortunately the runners ahead with the exception of St.Albans were in better form and the gap was widened. On this stage 'Dippy' Richards of Poly brought his club into the picture for the first time with the fastest run of the day. Belgrave still sixth but 75 secs behind T.V.H. now a short lead on Pompey.

5th Stage. Jim Masters (23.05). Jim must know every inch of this section of the road and we hoped that his knowledge would help us. But although maintaining his position by passing Brighton,by losing a place to Woodford he lost considerable ground on all the other runners ahead and we finished this stage over two minutes in arrears.

6th Stage. Alan Black (13.56). Alan did the fifth fastest time of the day, and set up a new Club record, but it must have been discouraging to him to find that three of those who beat his performance were ahead of him, with the result that we remained in sixth place.

7th Stage. Mal Robinson (19.10). Our three mile track champion also set up a new Club record for this stage, and gained on three of the five teams ahead, but was unable to do more than maintain our 2min 24sec deficit. Walton, with Judd as their representative had, however, passed Malcolm who in turn had moved ahead of Blackheath. In passing Gatwick Airport, how Mal must have wished for some jet propulsion!!

8th Stage. Charlie Dabbs (31.00). On the approaches to what is probably the most difficult, and longest stage, Charlie must have felt a little overwhelmed by the company in his coach - Hyman, Peter Cl[illegible]rk, Brian Fernee, Chapman, Roath, Perkins, Mike Barratt, George Brown, Maynard, Palmer and Sando just to mention a few are not designed to rouse one's confidence. Nevertheless Chas. was not upset by these "Names" and only four were able to better his magnificent run of 31mins (yet another Belgrave best). By the time Handcross was reached we still were in sixth place but had been put in touch of the three clubs immediately ahead. Portsmouth by virtue of a record breaking run by Hyman (29.14 and 26 secs off the previous best) had gone into a 2 min lead from Thames Valley.

9th Stage. John Moore(31.35) Despite the perils of dashing down Handcross Hill rapidly, John could not resist the urge, but paid for his folly by getting cramp and having to give ground on all the runners ahead. He did recover on the latter part of the leg but the damage was done. Tulloh was taking Portsmouth into an even greater lead and T.V.H. were consolidating their second place. We were now five minutes thirty seven seconds behind.

10 Stage.Len Bishop(21.16) If Len could have kept up the pace he showed over the first mile we would have wiped out Jim Peters' name from the book, and Belgrave would have moved out of sixth place. After making up some lost ground we fell further behind at the foot of Dale Hill.

11th Stage.Jim Radford (21.25) This was Jim's first taste of the Brighton road and although his time of 21mins25secs was not outstanding he can be well pleased with his initial run in bad conditions. It compared favourably with the runners ahead and a slight gain on the leaders was made for the first time during the whole race. The fastest time of the day was less than 30 secs better than Jim's, and the experience he gained will be invaluable.

BOY'S AND YOUTH'S SECTION

Les Stokell

It was obvious to officials chiefly concerned with these sections at the beginning of the year, that the development of the 'Belgrave spirit' amongst the newcomers was the first most important essential. A group of youngsters had to be gathered who could be depended upon to train enthusiastically, to turn out for the Club when required and in whom could be inculcated some of the traditions and 'esprit de corps' which made Belgrave such a power in the athletics world until a few years ago. I think the course is proving a correct one, and because so much importance is attached to the 'morale' side of the lads' training, the development of the physical side i.e. fitness, strength, techniques etc., is proving so much simpler. When such keeness is shown it seems almost invidious to select particular names, but I think a few must be mentioned.

David Cocks appears likely to prove a very bright star in the not too distant future; if his 4:43 mile and 2:06 half mean anything. Middle distance talent is almost in abundance so with challengers like Hampshire,Jarvis, Alan Mead, Hammond, Wiseman, Brown, Yeo, Redwood and Satchell not very far behind, David cannot afford to flag in his efforts.

Our field events future is beginning to look equally bright and here I must pause to congratulate the winners of the boy's and youth's all-rounders medal competitions. Pottinger won the boy's with a score of 23 pts out of a possible 24, and J.Williams won the youth's. Pottinger,incidentally, has done twenty feet in the long-jump - Not too bad for a lad just fifteen years old. Young Wade, too, is beginning to make a name for himself, particularly in the jumps, and with Paddy Fyman,shot, Tony Moritz, discus 137 feet, and Chris Page,javelin, it appears that this side of athletics is being well taken care of.

Our outstanding sprinter is undoubtedly Crosbie, who runs remarkably well every time he turns out for the club.

Last, but by no means least, the youngest members of the Club; boys like Berry, Pymont (a very promising hurdler) and Nelson, having reached the ripe old age of thirteen or thereabouts have apparently made up their minds to "throw away their childish things" and to get down to serious training. Fine, but don't make it too serious yet. Remember there are still footballs to be kicked, trees to be climbed and,(dare I mention it!) exams to be passed.

Les Stokell.

ANNUAL GENERAL MEETING - 1959 BELGRAVE HALL -18thSEPT

H.W.S.

The attendance of the A.G.Meeting was a little below that of recent years but even so, it was pleasing to see the goodly array of faces representing the various Club interests; especially the Youth's,Junior's and new members'. This is the one occasion when democratic priveliges can be exercised to the full, in order to ensure that those members elected to office continue to steer the Club on a progressively expanding course.

Generally there were no fireworks; rather the opposite. The feeling was that the major offices should remain unchanged, and it was hoped that their re-election was a vote of confidence backed by the firm intention of giving them even more support in their many duties.

The high-light of the meeting was the election of T.C.Jones as President of Belgrave Harriers. He was charmingly introduced by the retiring president F.R.Webb, who spoke of the great service that Charlie has given the Club during his membership as an athlete and official of over 30 years.

A presentation to Stan Vickers to mark his European Games victory, and to commemmorate his English Native Record for the 1 hour and 8 miles walks was made amid acclamations from all. Stan expressed his appreciation of the gift and gave an amusing and most interesting account of his experience in Moscow and Helsinki.

GREAT RUN BY CHARLIE DABBS.

"Charlie Dabbs Causes a Surprise". This was the masterpiece of understatement in Athletics Weekly with which Sam Ferris headed his report on the R.R.C. and Walton 25KM. Track race, on 17th Oct.

After his fine effort in the London-Brighton Relay 7 days earlier, Our Chas. was reported to be feeling in even greater condition, and keen to undergo an even more exacting test on his newly acquired fitness. Ivor Edmonds (Bristol A.C.) was favourite for the title with J.Heywood (H.H.H.) probable runner-up.

At 5 miles Dabbs and Edmonds had a clear 44 secs. lead and a mile later were timed at 31.11 (this would have placed Chas. second in the Club 6!). After 7 miles Charlie dropped his rival and was never again troubled. En Route, Tom Carter's 10 mile Club Record of 20 odd years standing went by the board, with 52.06,and 11½ miles were covered in the hour; bettering Dave Baulch's hour record by no less than 450 yards! New Club marks were almost certainly set for 7 miles and upwards.

The 15 miles was covered in 78:29.4, and although Fred Norris's English Native Record was set en route to a longer distance, Charlie was only 65 secs. outside the official mark. His 25 KM. performance ranks him fourth in the World Best-ever, behind Zatopek, Popov and Norris! His 3 mile split times were 15.37; 15.34; 15.40; 15.48 and 15.50 - remarkable consistency for an athlete who had never previously raced further than 10 miles.
