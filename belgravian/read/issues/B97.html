EDITORIAL

As we go to press so comes the good news we have all waited for, Victory in Europe. Amidst the rejoicing and thanksgiving, members' thoughts may have been turned towards the Club, and how it has fared during these last five odd years. The Club has many good reasons to be proud of its services to athletics and to its members. Belgrave Hall was open not only to our own members but to members of clubs less fortunate and to visiting athletes and servicemen. Much could be written of the fortunes of our many members. A large number, probably the majority, of our active members was called up for service in the Forces. We had, however, many members in reserved occupations, the Police and Civil Defence. These, in spite of long working hours and extra duties and less leisure time, have been able to keep the Club going. We were also pleased to welcome services members when on leave, and they proved valuable helps to the Club's teams.

We have also been fortunate in having a live and efficient Committee and hard working officers. They gave up much valuable time in the interests of the Club. It is perhaps little realised how much work is done behind the scenes.

We do not know how long it will be before athletics are restored to their pre-war scale and scope, but we can confidently say that when they do Belgrave will be to the fore. The past winter has shown us that we can still lead the way and we congratulate our runners and walkers on a good season's work. Once again Derrick Burfitt has merited special mention. He won the National Youth's Cross-Country Race by a convincing margin. He also took other junior titles in his stride.

Soon, many of our Services men will be coming back to us and we look forward to seeing them line up with our teams. Others will be drafted to the Far-Eastern zone where we already have a number of members. To those who will still be serving we send our best wishes for a speedy and safe return. Until that much-awaited day, they will often be in our thoughts.

Our transatlantic friend, Max Quackenbos, who was Junior Captain before the war, pays us the following tribute: "It is amazing to me that you fellows are still keeping it up in the most adverse conditions—a lean diet, poor transportation (of which we are beginning to know a little), dark nights and the bombs (which surpass our powers of imagination). But you're not only just keeping it up, you are surpassing yourselves." Perhaps with the lapse of time and the return of plenty of food, training and rest, we ourselves will wonder how we did it!

Derrick BurfittMax Quackenbos

FROM THE HON. SECRETARY

Belgrave Hall has once again been the scene of great activity this winter season. Its easy access from all points has made it a favourite with both associations and individuals alike. Many have been the letters of appreciation which have been received in recognition of the help given by the Club and its officers.

Two new fixtures were added this season, both mid-week (Wednesday). Raynes Park County School, newcomers to the sport, entertained Wallington County School and although beaten, will, I feel sure, give a much better account of themselves with more experience and training. Rutlish School, Merton, was the other fixture. Senior and junior races were held, and the school literally turned up "in hundreds" either to run or support. I believe all had a thoroughly enjoyable afternoon. Both schools wish to convey their thanks to those who so generously turned up to help—Tom Carter, Frank Thomson, Harry Shields, Charlie Cook, Bill Webb, and especially Alf Taylor who, being on night work, gave up his sleep to attend.

* * * *

Once again we nave to thank that staunch band of "backroom boys" who are always willing to help on a Saturday afternoon when there is a race at the Hall. Few, perhaps, give a thought when they pass them at, say, half distance, as to how they got there, or how they get back. It is usually the pointsman's lot to arrive back hot and thirsty to find that all the tea and cakes have been gobbled up by the competitors! Amongst regular helpers are Dick Savage, George Clay, Tom Cotton, Charlie Jones, Dave Shepherd and Norman Dewberry and others.

* * * *

We are indebted to our old friend Wally Jewell for the results of the Southern Counties Races.

* * * *

The Hon. Secretary apologises for the delay in answering letters, but would explain that so many are being received nowadays that some delay is inevitable. However, all will be attended to in due course.

* * * *

Club notices have been appearing in various local papers, due to the good work of George Clay, Charlie Jones, Ernie Duffett and Tom Morrell.

* * * *

We have much pleasure in reproducing the following letter:—

"Dear Mr. Parker,

May I take this opportunity of expressing my sincere appreciation of the generosity and hospitality afforded to me by Belgrave Harriers and yourself? Being a serviceman and finding myself in strange company, I have become fully qualified in judging the friendliness and hospitality of numerous companies. Here and now I would like to say that the friendship and sportsmanship which I found abounded at Belgrave Hall is hard to be surpassed anywhere. In the future, should the opportunity occur, my hand will be extended to assist any Belgravian who finds himself competing in the North. I wish the Club, collectively and individually, every success in the future. May I add in conclusion that I am proud in being able to say that I belonged to Belgrave Harriers?

Yours very sincerely, J. W. JOHNSON, Sub. Lieut. (E), R.N.V.R."

Johnny was on board a famous submarine which had a long list of "kills" to its credit. We congratulate him on gaining his commission as an Engineer.

* * * *

We would also like to quote from a letter received from a new member. "I thank you for your cordiality and I am glad to say that all members I have met so far have been very nice and have gone out of their way to help me. The Belgrave Harriers certainly possess the right atmosphere."

* * * *

In reading recently of the activities of a very prominent Midlands club I was very much impressed with a scheme they have of appointing honorary club coaches from their members. Applications were invited for these positions and it is their obvious intention to form a coaches section. This appeals to me very much and I would like to hear from any members who would care to undertake such duties for us at the Tooting Track or other centre. In this respect George Clay has had a sprinters' class in action at New Malden.

* * * *

Extract from a letter from Len Richardson, Hon. Sec. of the English Cross-Country Union, to Tom Carter. "I trust that our mutual friend Footer will return safely to this country, to again be an inspiration to the great Belgrave Harriers." Thanks, Len.

NEWS OF MEMBERS

We announce with deepest regret the following death:—

Alec Mewes was killed in France. He was 29 years old and joined us in 1928 when only 13 years old. He was one of our best known sprinters in pre-war days, and figured in many relay teams. He was also a useful long jumper and had represented us on countless occasions in Club matches. He was a keen athlete, modest and popular. Our deepest sympathies are extended to his widow.

Prisoners of War

Les Bennett. We learn from Mrs. Bennett that no news has been received from Les for well over a year, when a brief message stated that he was fit and well in Java. Our sympathies are extended to Olive and we all sincerely trust that Les is well and hope for a speedy return.

Dan and Alec Patience. Last heard of in Stalag XIA (between Magdeburg and Berlin. We hear from brother Ron that they are fit and well but, unfortunately, have little facilities for exercise.

Alf Miller and Arthur Beales were at Stalagluft III at Sagan and were both in good health.

Frank Halls was at Stalag 344 at Lansdorf, and his latest news was that his leg was making progress and that he was in good health.

* * * *

The daily papers have kept us informed of how the occupants of these camps were marched 400 miles across Germany. With the Allies' occupation of Germany, the liberation of our five members should soon be announced. We hope that we shall soon be welcoming their return.

* * * *

Alf Harley was recently promoted to Squadron Leader. Congratulations, Alf! It can now be revealed that he is stationed at Caserta. He has been getting some of the R.A.F. fellows out running (no, Alf did not run himself). He has met two old athletic friends in Squire Yarrow (Poly.) and Ron Pantling (London Vidarians). Like many others, his experience of Italian rain and snow has caused him to have decided views on "Sunny Italy."

Bert Footer visited the Hall early in the year whilst on embarkation leave. He hinted something about Burma and the East. Bert had a spin with Tom Carter and Co. and showed that being a Commando has definitely not affected his running. Bert carries our best wishes wherever he is and we trust that he will return soon.

Bill Sutton denies the rumour that he had gained the Military Medal; in fact, he's far behind the lines! He is still in Corsica, and recommends it as ideal walking and climbing country. Little scope for athletics.

Frank Elson, after his strenuous time with the R.E.M.E., had a spell in hospital for shock, but is now better again—in fact, he looked in at the Hall a short time ago and looked bronzed and very fit.

Jack Shotter. The latest news is that he is now in the fighting in Burma.

Len Coleman. His recent accident, we now learn, was a plane crash. He has been in hospital again to be "sliced open," and is now taking things easy. He is stationed near another member, Jim Hewitt.

Charlie Churcher is still awaiting the boat home. Is unable to do any training—even a few strolls. Had an enjoyable Christmas, food, beer, football matches and concerts, but he would rather do a Godstone and back walk.

Norman Graves has, by his running exploits, won a warm place in the hearts of the French people. He has turned out with the local club and performed creditably. He has won prizes and medals to bring home. He competed in an important race round the famous Bois de Boulogne (Paris). In another race, against French cracks, he was third, only few yards behind the winner.

Dick Towndrow has been training regularly and nearly broke a leg at football. He had a narrow escape on Christmas Day when he was in a building which collapsed. We gave him an extra stripe in the last mag.. he is a Lance Corporal.

Colin Sutton, has been regraded and may be coming home. Is still fit, as he was second in a 5,000 metres walk at the Bengal Olympic Games on February 8th. The winner was N. K. Das, time 27mins. 40.4secs.

Bill Snell has been transferred from A.A. to R.A.S.C. He narrowly missed seeing Charlie Churcher recently. He is now in Syria where there is plenty of ski-ing over the mountains, so we can guess he's pretty fit.

George Durrant is now in Burma where he complains of the heat and flies. In spite of constant sweating he is "as fat as a pig." Is hoping for 28 days "Blighty" leave.

Jimmy Kemp is still wandering the globe, last heard of in Somaliland.

Dick Stride has recovered from his wounds which he got in Italy, and has since been twice mentioned in dispatches.

Eric Keyworth met Len Spooner in Jerusalem and succeeded him as Sports Officer. Has collected 3 Palestine Championship Medals with relay teams. Has had moderate success in road races.

Percy Wilson found pictures, etc., of the 1936 Olympic Games when searching a house in Germany.

Reuben Bradshaw is now with the Welsh Guards in the B.L.A., and was very pleased to receive the Club Christmas Card.

"Stimmo" has been doing some running over the other side with the B.L.A. In a recent letter he expresses his gratitude for the advice and encouragement given to him in his junior days by Dick Savage.

Len Marchant still sends cheery letters from the Azores. He has contacted Ray Dunford who is also in those parts.

Will Rispin sends us greetings from the front line with the B.L.A.

Len Fletcher aroused the interest of the locals when he went for a spin on a track in Holland. We regret to learn from Len that his father died recently. Mr. Fletcher was a staunch supporter at walking events, and was full of encouragement for Len and Jack. Our sympathies to both Len and Jack on their loss.

Charlie Smart is still with the B.L.A., and manages to do a bit of running with a few French athletes.

Teddy Read is now a Fying Officer attached to the 14th Army; he is grounded for a time but is quite happy. This we learn from Lilian, who also informs us that

Norman Shrubb is also a Flying Officer, and is also Sports Officer covering almost every branch of sport. Is working very hard.

Frank Webb writes from Port Alfred, South Africa, to say that at last he has done some running. In a one mile race he was third in 4.43, and third in the half mile in 2.8. Frank found the going a bit hard after a two years' absence from running. He has been keeping fit by swimming.

Joe Edwards hasn't been doing any running lately as he's been spending most of his time in the sun on top of a telegraph pole in the desert in Tripoli. He is now in an office and has played a little football and hockey to keep his waistline in order.

Tony Bellchambers, with S.E.A.C., has had a short spell in hospital with malaria, but is now fit again. Francis is still with East African Command.

Arthur Beaumont has been rushing about in Italy but is as right as rain. Speaks in glowing terms of the welcome they get in the villages and the Italian girls. Longs for a sight of Battersea Park.

Etienne Gailley, who was whisked from our last issue to his native Belgium, visited us early in the year in the uniform of an "umbrella man." He was pleased to tell us that he had met his family again. He was also pleased to come back and see his running friends in England.

Ken Webb, who joined the R.A.F. and went to Canada and collected three stripes, is, we understand, back in England, doing his stuff!

Max Quackenbos must be feeling dizzy after the Indoor Season, running on a board track eleven laps to the mile. In the A.A.U. 3 Miles, he was 8th. At the Millrose Games he was 3rd in a Two Miles Handicap, off 75 yards. He was a close 4th in the Billings 2 Miles, won in 9-19-6, and 7th in the Toussaint Two Miles (New York A.C.), won in 9-17-6. "Quack" still sends us programmes profusely illustrated, which outclass most of our pre-war programmes. What would our American friends say to one of our wartime typewritten affairs with no photographs, no advertisements, and little space for scribbling in results and lap times (when given!)?

Jack Fletcher sends us a long and interesting letter from India. Beginning with reminiscences of running and socials, he then reviews his career in the Services. After two years in the R.A.P.C., "Nobby" went through various courses, and finally found his way out to India with an Infantry Brigade.

Cyril Pateman writes from Ceylon to say that it will not be long before he is back at the Hall where he hopes to be of assistance to us.

Len Herbert has made several appearances this winter as he has been stationed not very far from the Hall. His brother, Eric, paid us a leave and by now is probably over the water.

Percy Wright has recently been to Preston on an Army Education Course and may soon be blossoming forth as an Army teacher. The good work with the B.N.L. continues, including a personal letter to ALL overseas walkers. Just think of it, nearly 50 issues of the B.N.L. have appeared; with ten or a dozen walkers overseas, that means that he must have written some 500 letters! Percy still pays occasional visits to town for a race, and, of course, if there's any walking in S. Wales, he's in it. Recently he forwarded £2 10s. to the Hon. Treasurer, the proceeds from the sale of airgraphs. Money raised in this manner is earmarked for an "Overseas Cup" for the walkers. Incidentally, B.N.L. stands for Belgrave News Letter.

Jack Rutland is still at Manorbier, S. Wales. His training on Welsh roads has kept him in fine form—see walking results. He made a first-class effort in the Club Open to secure an Individual win for the Club, but was beaten into second place by a few yards.

Charlie Redgrave was recently "on the air" in "Merry-Go-Round," when he gave a turn similar to those he used to give at Club socials, a song with his banjo, interspersed with some tap-dancing. Charlie has a pretty busy time entertaining and arranging concerts for the Forces. Gave his shoes an airing to help the Club to win the wartime Surrey County Road-Walking title. Charlie, who is stationed in Lincs, visits our Brighton walker, C. A. Wattam.

Bill Jones recently married a V.A.D. attached to his unit at Leeds. Congratulations and best wishes to you both. He hasn't done any racing recently although there's not much scope for winter walking in those parts.

Jimmy Green has been resting his leg after he was "spiked." Is busy planning his big meeting at Gillingham, June 23rd. Don't forget the date!

Arthur Cook, our megaphone man, has moved from Dorset to Aldershot, which town he couples with "Blanco" and "Spit and Polish." Is hoping to come along and see us at track meetings. His wife presented him with twin girls last November. Double congratulations!

George Murray visited the Hall after five years absence to have a run with the boys before going over the water.

Ken Cuff was able to pay us a few visits when stationed near London. He is now up in Scotland with the Black Watch.

Doug. Woodhead has been temporarily grounded and has done little running lately.

Bill True is still busy at Sandhurst, but was able to visit us and turn out at the Open Walk.

Laurie Jackson is still undergoing treatment and is having a skin grafting at the back of his knee. He is now at St. Albans.

Will Applebee, as a member of the "Silent Service," doesn't say much except that he has been about a good bit, but has managed to get a few leaves.

Harry Churcher celebrated Boxing Day with a stroll from Manston to Deal. Is very busy at his station and is hoping that their efforts will help to shorten the war so that he can get home and back to the form which made him A.A.A. Champion at 2 and 7 miles, etc.

George Cockburn is up North, training for a Navigator Wireless Operator. At recent Service Sports he returned 10.2secs. for the 100, 23secs. for the 220 and 4.31 for the mile.

Len Camin writes that he is very busy with the R.A.S.C. in Gloucestershire.

Wally Ives arrived home to "Blighty" after some time in India, just in time to see the Open Walk. Although he has spent some time "in dock," he was looking very fit and was obviously pleased to be with us again. He will probably soon be demobbed and back on the trolleybuses again. We congratulate him on becoming a Sergeant. With him at the Hall were his two boys, who are members.

Ernest Reeve, one of our walkers of bygone years, from Chelmsford, went to France, came back via Dunkirk, and is now in Shropshire with the R.E.M.E.

Bert Smith is with the A.C.C. in the Shetlands and has been busy on the "grub-stakes." Paid a visit to the Hall, accompanied by his small son, during March.

Len Spooner managed an appointment with Percy Wright in a pub at Newport. Has turned out for us in a few "Opens."

Bert Clayton, home from Aden and sent to Wales where he, too, met Percy. When he visited his home town, Eastleigh, he had a few spins with Tommy Green.

P. C. Bannister is now back from I.o.M., and it is rumoured that he led the way home in walks.

Stan Botwright always seems to be on the move, his latest address being Norfolk.

Joe Coleman still very busy, but paid us a visit and had a spin in the L.F.F. Open Walk. Joe was obviously short of training, but gave us a chance to see the style which has won him prizes for both speed and style.

John Brown, our Southern Junior Half-Mile Champion, has been called away at a most interesting period of his running career and is now at Scarborough.

Derek Pollard is another youngster called to the services just when he had hoped to put in some good running. Derek was posted to the Guards Depot at Caterham. We learn from his father that he is in the Grenadiers, likes the life and is now a fine, strapping youth.

Doug. Earle, who used to race in the days of Len Fletcher and Don Brown, for they all worked at the same firm, is now in the R.A.F., stationed at Tangmere. We also learn that he is married.

Jack Field has been with an A.A. battery on the E. Coast. Took part in a services' road walk to find himself up against Tom Richardson, the Woodford Green Record Holder! Jack is anxious to trace a medal he passed on to the Club for engraving before the war. It is a winning team medal for the Surrey County Road Walk Championship, 1938. Can anyone give any information, please?

Tommy Scrimshaw paid us a welcome visit before going over the water.

Ernie Duffett, in spite of the flying bomb incident, reported in the last issue, still resides in and helps law and order in Croydon. He has other pressing calls, but he still knows enough about us to be our Hon. Handicapper—a fact regretfully omitted from the list of officers in the last mag.

Charlie Cook is now back in the S.W.11 district again after being bombed out. The bomb had a bad effect on his hearing and we all sympathise with him. But Charlie manages; he is undoubtedly our best recruiting agent, as a good proportion of our new members have been introduced to us by him. He is one of the "regulars" at the track. Nice work, and thank you, Charlie.

Frank Thomson has been much better this winter and has lent a hand at Club fixtures. He is also continuing the work behind the scenes with the A.A.A.

Bill Fish sends his heartfelt thanks to Club members for their kindness and sympathy during his recent illness. We are pleased to say that Bill is much better and we hope that he will enjoy watching many, many track meetings with us.

George Still has been one of the regulars at the Hall. George obviously doesn't mind the weather as he turned up on Boxing Day (remember how cold it was?) and gained second prize in the two miles run.

Jack Carne and father again send us good wishes from Worthing, particularly to their old Club friends.

Ken O' Kell has left Manchester and is now back in town (Chingford). I wonder what he thinks of doodle-bugs!

Stan Blunden writes from Kidderminster to say that he had a fairly good track season last year. Is hoping to see the lads when they are competing in the Midlands. He reports that their baby is progressing well.

Reg. Johnson sends a Club vest and badge from Stockport. Has done no running himself lately, but hopes that his two sons will do some C.-C. running in a few years.

"P. J." Gorner hopes to be busy on the track after resting his foot.

Bill Ashford, who went to Iran some years ago, turned up at the Club early in the year. After a leave at home he was due to return to Iran. Many will remember Bill running on track and country before the war, but in Iran things are different and he's had no running out there.

A DAY AT THE RACES

HARRY VERNEY.

Saturday, March 10th, 1945, has proved to be one day in these war-torn years that can remain a very happy memory for the South and, in particular, for the "Bels.," for on that day was decided the first National Youths' Race and the District Associations' Team Race in Dartmouth Park, West Bromwich. The weather was extremely fine, but perhaps a shade too warm for a fast, seven miles cross country race, and the ground conditions were firm; in fact, spikes or rubbers could have been worn with no decided advantage over either choice.

The Youths' Race which preceded the District Race attracted 17 teams and 10 individuals—101 runners started—and comprised two laps of parkland, each lap measuring a distance of 1½ miles. From the fall of the starting flag the race was dominated by our own Burfitt. At the end of the first lap he was comfortably ahead by some 90 yards and the farther he went the greater the lead he gained. Full of running, he finished some 250 yards in front of Farrell, of Hallamshire, with Rouse, of South London Harriers, third, 65 yards behind Farrell. It was bad luck for the Northern youth that he came up against a runner of Burfitt's calibre, for Farrell himself is outstanding and I predict in him a future northern champion at least, but Burfitt, of course, was super, as Farrell was only too ready to agree. Unfortunately, only three Belgrave runners turned up at Paddington and so we were prevented from starting with even a scoring team and the depleted team were made to run as individuals. The winning team was Barry Boys' Council School with 47 points—a grand team. Burfitt's time was 16mins. 58.2secs.; Farrell's, 17mins. 36secs.; and Rouse's, 17mins. 52 secs.

So to the Senior Race. This comprised four laps of the course covered by the youths plus an additional ¼ mile detour on each lap.

The end of the first lap saw Gosney, Hughes and Bunton running together in a bunch, with our "evergreen" Tom Carter in close attendance. Behind Tom came Reid, of Birchfield and Scottish International, to be followed by Cole, of Dudley, the Junior International. Bingham was about ten yards behind Cole and close to him followed two Northern men, Ellis and Tyrer.

The second lap saw no change in the leading positions, but Reid had jumped ahead of Carter, who was running on his own some 15 yards from the Birchfielder. Bingham trailed Carter, and a few yards behind Bingham came Hoath, who on this lap had moved up to 7th from 13th position. Jack Holden, the oft-times International, was 9th. The pace continued hot.

The end of the third lap saw no change in the first seven positions. Tom was still running in his own solitary company and appeared to be finding things a bit tough as he passed me, though he was running as determined as ever. It was obvious at this stage that the South were going to swamp the other districts and if the Midlands and North combined teams, the South would still win hands down.

The race finished up an incline of 600 yards, some 400 yards of it gruellingly steep, and it was for this finish that Hughes had been waiting to jump Gosney, who has no love for hills, a fact Hughes knew. So the Poly. man ran out the winner by some 35 yards in 37 mins. 2 secs. Gosney (37mins. 10secs.) easily held off Reid (37mins. 17secs.), who in turn held off Bunton (37mins. 31secs.). Tom came racing in very strongly to finish 5th (37mins. 36secs.). Not only had he got over any distress he was experiencing on the start of the final lap, but he was running more strongly than any of the men who had finished ahead of him, and he was moving up fast on Bunton. Incidentally, I consider that Bunton ran the wrong type of race. Instead of hanging on with Gosney and Hughes he should have gone to the front and forced the pace after the second lap. Still, experience will come with the years ahead. Hoath moved up on this lap to take Bingham's place in 6th position (37mins. 46secs.), while the Finchley man lost 7th place to Ferguson, of Birchfield, with Holden behind Bingham in 9th place. Thus did the South "rise and shine" to place six men in the first eight positions.

It was Herbert's distinct misfortune to experience a real off-day, not only in the running sense, but physically, too, for as he passed me on every lap he showed unmistakable signs of distress and it must have been sheer "guts" and will-power that enabled him to complete the course.

I understand that the team, on arrival back at Paddington, ran a "dead heat" to the nearest pub and wallowed in war-time "wobble" on a lend-lease basis until closing time.

JUNIOR NOTES

A. W. JANNAWAY.

Juniors would do well to remember that it is from their ranks that the post-war teams must be built up. Belgrave Harriers have been represented in big events for a decade or more by a combination of runners probably unprecedented in the history of athletics. It is difficult to realise that we are now in the sixth winter season since he war began and many of our established champions and others are slowly but inevitably approaching the veteran stage. The future reputation and good name of the Club is therefore in the hands of the seniors of tomorrow. Whether the name of Belgrave will mean so much in five years' time as it does now depends on the basis of sound training put in by the juniors of today. Regular training in mid-week is advisable, but regular training at Wimbledon is essential, for the art of running can only by learnt from experience with better men, and the necessary grit for strenuous work is best acquired by running in company. Information of any kind on training will gladly be given by senior members.

The undersigned would also like those juniors who really mean business to let him know what distances they fancy, with indication of performances. Finally, don't forget to fetch along any pal who is interested and shows ability. That's the way the Club was built up.

WITH THE WALKERS

As we come to the close of our sixth, and we hope, last war-time winter season, we can look back with a high degree of satisfaction at having been able to keep the Belgrave colours so much to the fore during such difficult times.

During the years immediately preceding the war, it was quite a common occurrence for us to finish anything up to half a dozen in the first ten in Open "Sevens" and on one occasion in the Ashcombe Open we actually took the first six places. Other pre-war successes included 1938 and 1939 winners of both Junior and National 20 Championships, 1937 and 1938, Harry Churcher first home in every open seven. 1938, Colin Sutton's hat-trick in being placed in the winning teams in the Junior, National and 50 Kilos; 1938, Joe and Len Coleman dead heating for first place in the Junior. What grand memories to look back on! Then came the war and our star walkers gradually trickled away to the various services, and so to all parts of the world, leaving mainly our police members to carry on.

As time went on they too gradually disappeared. Norman Dyne, Frank Halls and others. As far as conditions would permit, our Service members came from all parts of the country to help build up the teams. Percy Wright, Jack Rutland, Charlie Redgrave, Bill Jones, and many others. We naturally haven't been able to secure many youngsters as they, too, have been absorbed into the Services just when they would normally have started racing and so we have had to fall back on men beyond the calling up age and their best racing years, but nevertheless keen enough to turn out week after week and thus ensure that even if the Club colours couldn't always be so conspicuous amongst the leaders, it would be evident to the walking world and our absent Club mates they didn't intend to let the Club lie dormant during the war period. Without their assistance we should certainly have been hard pushed to find teams on several occasions and our gratitude goes out to them for their invaluable assistance.

We have, however, been able to secure two promising juniors in A. Joels and J. Scamell, the latter being introduced by his father, an old Club member. Both have been giving a good account of themselves during the past season. With these two and young Mac, we have the foundations of a good post-war junior team.

Jack Rutland and Percy Wright, who have for so long been trying to get the Army Athletic Board to give road walking its proper place in Army athletics, have at last met with a measure of success, insomuch as they were selected to represent the Army in the 15 and 20 miles opens. We hope there will be more to follow and that this is only a small beginning to bigger things.

Amongst the most noteworthy Club performances during the season are those of Jack Rutland, second to Whitlock in the Woodford Green Open and again second to Burgess in our own open. Eddie Jury, our Portsmouth member, has been first home on three occasions, each time beating Alexander, of Portsmouth City Police. Bob McMullen found his best form in the B.W.S.A. Open when he finished third

The season opened by us tying with Surrey W.C. for scratch team honours. In December, at Wimbledon, we brought off a double in the Surrey "Boxing Day" Five, being first in both scratch and handicap team races. In our own open we had first place in both team and individual handicaps and second in team and individual scratch races. Bob Readman and Harry Rouse finished second and third respectively in the Police Ten and the following Saturday we met with success in the Surrey County Race, R. McMullen and H. Rouse finishing second and third respectively and the Club taking team honours. The same day A. Joels finished third in the Kent race. With Eddie Jury in London, we took second team place in the scratch race and a similar placing in the handicap section of the B.W.S.A. Open. Altogether a very creditable season's work. May we look forward to just as successful a summer season and hope that by the time the next road season comes round we shall have some of our Services men back with us, until then we can rest assured that everyone will do their best to give the Club a good show.

The Club Open Walk was again a big reunion for our walkers past and present. The new three-lap course was used, which gave spectators several chances to see the race and urge on their clubmates. The entry of some ninety-odd was well up to the war-time average and many famous walkers took part. The race for individual honours was a close thing and Jack Rutland failed by a few seconds. Nevertheless, congratulations came to Jack from everybody for his great performance, which must also be judged from the fact that he had made a long journey from Pembrokeshire and that he does not get the necessary competition for a front ranker. We were pleased to receive entries from Godiva Harriers, Coventry. Although the race is not run for profit, our Walking Secretary, Syd. Roberts, was able to show a small balance. We thank Syd. and the many Club folk who turned up and helped to make the event a success. The Club is greatly indebted to Jimmy Bellchambers, Harry Evans, Wally Ives and Syd. A SCHEDULE FOR MILERS AND TWO-MILERS COMPILED FROM A. R. SHAW'S DIARY

Introduction

Older members need no introduction to Albert Shaw, but for others it will be of interest to be reminded that just prior to the war, Albert was one of our most consistent milers. He was several times runner-up in our Club Championships, consistently beat 4.30secs. and was in many winning two-mile team races. Apart from this he won many opens and was placed in several Business House Championships.

Anyone knowing Albert will appreciate the thought and energy he put into his running and I'm sure that the table will be of interest and use to members.

If the table does no more than encourage the study and reading of the various books on athletics which can be obtained from the library, it is well justified.

Foreword by A. R. Shaw

I take pleasure in submitting the following schedule on how to prepare yourself for a mile. It is considered that, starting in April, it will take three months to reach your peak performances.

It is assumed that you have had a season on the country to strengthen your body, so that what is now required is steady building up for speed.

Miling today is a fast business.

All work must, so far as possible, be to a stop-watch.

Be well and warmly clothed and do ample warming up before starting your run.

Keep good hours and regular habits.

Eat good food and plenty of honey, barley sugar, etc. Obtain by experiment your best racing weight and when it drops below normal, rest for a few days.

Work Club races in with your training, but do not race your distance in mid-week matches.
