EDITORIAL

Dear Fellow Members,

We apologise for the belated appearance of this issue, but since our last publication we have run into many difficulties, not least of which is lack of suitable assistance. It has become increasingly apparent that to produce the sort of gazette we desire, an editorial staff is essential.

Offers of help would, therefore, be welcomed from an experienced typist, reporters willing to cover a few events each month, writers able to submit articles or features occasionally and any other enthusiast who is keen enough to take an active part in the gazette's production.

We would also like to see members making greater use of this gazette for airing their grievances, contacting old friends, making suggestions or reporting items of interest - whether they be of present or past. Remember, this is your Gazette.

* * * * *

The most urgent matter requiring attention by our General Committee and the approaching A.G.M. is the state of Belgrave's field events section. It could be argued that no section in fact exists, but this would be unfair to that handful of members (if indeed they number five!) who endeavour to hold this beleaguered fort.

Field events Men are naturally more individualistic in their approach to athletics and are, therefore, harder to hold together as a team. They have no common denominator as the track section have; that is - running, and they have greater training difficulties. We are forever hearing the call for more and more tracks, yet compared with the needs of the field events man these calls are trifling. For instance, is there anywhere in England where the hammer, javelin, discus and weight men can train together ? Are there any special field events arenas where there are sufficient jumping pits to satisfy the requirements of the club athlete and the International athlete at the same time ? The answer is NO.

It is therefore all the more important that those responsible for Belgrave's field events men should keep in very close contact with the individuals of their section. Each man should feel that an interest is being taken in both his training and competition. Efforts should be made to organise gatherings so that the athletes become more closely acquainted with each other.

Enthusiasm is the most important factor in producing a successful athletics team and it is the ability to import this that is the essence of the successful coach or official. For every part of technical knowledge he needs ten parts enthusiasm. Enthusiasm must abound through and through, and until the team look forward to meeting each other again and the coaches and officials take a personal interest in all their doings, success will not be forthcoming.

We hope that at the A.G.M. members will make an effort to pick up the remains of this section, instil some life into it and devise some means of building it up over the next few years - a task of no mean proportions. It can be done, however, just as Tom Carter did it with the Boys and Youths, and Jack Goswell with the walkers.

Tom CarterJack Goswell

Obituary · page 2

ALLAN PECKINGS

It is with deep regret that we learn of the death of Allan Peckings, who was killed in a road accident whilst holidaying in Switzerland.

Allan, who was 23 years old, had just completed his final examinations as a Work Study Engineer and was hoping to devote more time to running activities. On restricted training he was already beginning to show promise and showed good form at the T.V.H. and Watford relays.

He was also an active member of the Boy Scouts Association, being a cubmaster and editor of his Group's magazine "The Echo".

Allan, who was cremated at Lugano on Saturday July 8th, will long be remembered by Club members for his pleasant personality and loyalty to Belgrave; our cross-country secretary could always rely upon him. Those members not actively associated with Allan will no doubt recall his forthright remarks at last year's Annual General Meeting, and it was hoped he would soon find his way on to the Club's General Committee.

We are indeed sad to suffer such a loss, and convey our sincere condolences to his family.

Allan Peckings

HON. SEC'S NOTES

A.A.H.

A feature of all our Meetings these days, both on Track and Cross-country, is the tremendous promise of our Boys and Youths. Their neat appearance, evident enthusiasm and undoubted ability lead us to wonder to what heights they will carry Belgrave in the years ahead. It certainly is a grand sight to see such convincing evidence of a great Belgrave future.

The personality who has done more than anyone else to bring about this pleasing state of affairs is Tom Carter. His great experience combined with ever-present humour, and his conscientious and self-less work with the lads has really produced first-class results.

Tom first came on the Belgrave scene in 1930, when his stylish striding around Battersea Park Track caused all the experts to predict a great future for him. Tom quickly had his minor successes, and he will be the first to admit that his size in hats increased somewhat for a short while! but the Belgrave "treatment" and Tom's innate good sense quickly corrected that! Suffice it to say that in 1931 he scored in our winning Surrey County cross-country team and from then on it took a very good man indeed to keep him out of our victorious teams. In 1936 he achieved every cross-country runner's goal by gaining his International vest and in the same year he was third in the A.A.A. 10 miles Championship. He was the winner of the Southern Counties C.C. Association's 10 miles title in 1946 and had dual wins in the Surrey and Southern 6 miles Championship in 1939. In 1937 and 1938 he had been placed second in the Southern 2 miles Steeplechase, and second place was his in the Surrey County Cross-country Championships of 1935 and 1946.

As a team man Tom's record will require some beating. Three times he was in our winning English C.C. teams (1935, 1946, 1948), four times in winning Southern teams, five times in winning London to Brighton Relay teams, and seven times in winning Surrey County C.C. Championship teams. In addition he has been a member of many other winning and placed teams.

Tom's likeable personality with his quick wit and irrepressible good humour has always been most popular. His poker-faced comedy acts at the Club Socials in the '30's can still be remembered with a smile, and Tom has served us well in many capacities, not least as President, and his generosity in presenting a Cup for the Club 6 miles Championship some years ago is still fresh in our minds.

Despite all his great work for the Club over thirty years - and it has indeed been great work - Tom may find his present services rate even higher, and if we find but a few amongst Tom's lads to contribute only half as much as Tom has done in his time we shall be glad indeed, and we know Tom will be a very happy man!

* * * * *

Back in 1916 Past President, the late Dick Murphy, presented the Club with a Shield for a walking event at nine miles, and this later became our 10 miles Championship Shield and now carries many illustrious names. During Dick's lifetime it was always his pleasure to come along and present the Shield, and to take care of any alterations as they became necessary.

In January Dick's son came along and was prevailed upon to make the presentation and we now acknowledge with grateful thanks a generous donation from Mrs. Murphy which will enable the existing small shields to be regrouped and new silver shields added to cover the next fifteen years. Mrs. Murphy can recall the Club's earlier days when there was little in reserve; a time when she made toffee for sale among the members for the benefit of Club funds!

* * * * *

All our readers will join in rather belated congratulations to our popular Past President Harry Evans, who attained his eightieth birthday in May. Harry joined the Club in 1906 and won a Walking Championship in our name in 1908. Harry never seems to miss a fixture, Summer or Winter, and we all hope his health will continue and enable him to be with us actively for many years to come.

* * * * *

We are pleased to acknowledge the gift of a framed coloured photograph of STAN VICKERS by ED LACEY which will adorn the wall at Belgrave Hall.

KEN O'KELL, our Captain and champion of years ago, has also promised us something of an endearing nature to mark his pleasure at being a Belgrave Harrier for so many years and your Finance Sub-Committee has the job of considering various possibilities.

A.A.H.

THE X-CTY. SECTION: Reflections and Forecasts.

C.S.

Midsummer is hardly an appropriate time of year to discuss activities synonymous with mud and slush but a new season will be upon us very shortly and our prospects are worthy of comment. If a resumé of last season's efforts over the country amounted to anything worth relating I would do so. But it does not - so I won't! Instead, I would rather look ahead, reflecting only when it is not too painful!

Why is it that Belgrave's performances in the road relays are so superior to those over the country ? The immense popularity of these events obviously has something to do with it, but other clubs manage to do well at both, notably Portsmouth, TVH and Woodford, in the South. Perhaps it is because we have never stopped tasting the fruits of success in one, whilst in the other few can recall our last victory of note. One thing is certain, the mental approach of our lads at the start of a cross-country championship is entirely different from that of a road relay. In the former it is taken for granted we shall be unplaced, whilst in the latter it is accepted that we will always be fighting it out with the leaders - each man striving to make the team and on doing so giving his "all" so that the next runner can have every yard of advantage possible. A bad run by one of the team seems to produce even more fire and determination from the remaining runners. What a different story from the country. Perhaps we are in need of an Honorary Psychiatrist!

In time I think this "road relay enthusiasm" will spread to the tougher cross country events, simply because the right men are working their way into our teams. Ken Miller, John Thresher, Mal Robinson and Bill Kerr are no respecters of reputation and will fight tooth and nail for Belgrave in any race going. This young nucleus will soon be attracting other "bodies" and the "energy" given off will be well worth following.

What of the coming season ? Well, as usual, it consists mainly of "ifs" and "buts". However, the acquisition of Bill Kerr will almost certainly ensure an all round improvement. This popular scotsman, formerly with Victoria PK.AC., has shown tremendous improvement over the last year, and his bounding enthusiasm and singular sense of humour should be of as much value to our teams as his unquestioned running ability. "Evergreen" Pat Newell is running as well as ever these days and we can no doubt look forward to another consistent season from him, while Jack Brown, although getting no younger, is still likely to show a clean pair of heels to his rivals, especially when it comes to relay running. These two should provide the experience a young team must have, particularly as the younger element is quite rightly growing fast. The average age of Messrs. Kerr, Robinson, Miller, Thresher Black & Forey is only 22 yrs., so bearing in mind the success they have already shown this could become a Championship-winning combination for many years to come.

This encouraging picture could become even more exciting if there were added to this group two of athletics' fittest performers - Dabbs & Limstead. Both Chas. and Ron have already proved themselves in dazzling fashion and when fit and free from injury their addition to the team improves it immeasurably. Unfortunately, they have both had more than their ration of illness and injury and we rarely enjoy the sight of seeing them together in the same team, but when we do - what a difference it makes! The London - Brighton Relay of October 1960 was a good example. At present, they are tremendously fit and have their troubles well behind them. This could be the turning point.

The strength of every team is governed by its weakest man, but with experienced men like Charlie Walker, John Davies and Eric Gebbett to complete our teams it is difficult to see where our weakest link can lie.

However, as I said last year, "Rome was not built in a day", and although the foundations are nearing completion we cannot yet enjoy the luxury of the finished product. A lot of hard work and determination to do what is best for the team is still called for, but, given a fair share of good fortune, the coming season should show an all-round improvement, especially over the country.

C.S.

THE SOUTHERN & NATIONAL 1961

"We've not a ghost of a chance in the National, but with a bit of luck, and if everybody turns out, we might get placed in the Southern", so said Jack Brown just after the Club '10'.

But the Southern "place" did not materialise. Our 577 points secured us (if that is the right word!) TENTH position. Pat Newell and Malcolm Robinson started well and were soon up in the first twenty, but after one of three laps over this fast Ewell course Pat had dropped to 27th, which he maintained to the finish, while Mal was fading rapidly some way behind. Jack Brown was eventually our second man home, having moved steadily through the field to gain 60th position. Thereafter, the rot had set in. Chas. Walker, vociferous before the start, revised his views after sampling the fast start and finished 100th, just ahead of Ken Miller (116), whilst Eric Gebbett (136) finished two places ahead of our recent county "vest" (!) Mal Robinson.

Jack's view of our chances in the National was borne out when at Parliament Hill Fields we finished 23rd with a new club "record" points total of 1386! With this type of performance it was of little consolation to find our old rivals Birchfield Harriers just behind. Little can be said of credit, though Eric Gebett having lost both shoes gained 70-odd places on the last lap to take the "Harry Parker Memorial Bowl" with 129th, and Clive Shippen, after some hard work in the previous few weeks, improved on recent form to finish 196th. Walker (230) and Robinson (285), like Gebbett, ran barefoot after losing their shoes in the heavy mud. Sawyer made his National debut with 256th place and the evergreen Eddie Short completed the side with 290 - non-scorers being Joe Dooley (326) and an injury-stricken Ken Miller (401). Over 700 started.

The Juniors faced much the same. After a false start in the Southern, John Thresher and Cyril Forey fell back from 25th and 60th to 95th and 96th, leaving Mike Heck to carry our colours up front and finish 25th. Ted Churchard was 130th and our 346 points total gave us 17th place.

Mike Heck was unavailable for the National and our first man was John Thresher (103). The other three scoring members were, a much improved Eric Penny (207), a below form Cyril Forey (211) and the promising Ted Churchard (241). The team finished 32nd with 762 points.

SPOTLIGHT: RONALD HOLTUM

M.E.R.

"I run because I like running, and I shall continue to do so all the while I get enjoyment from it". Yet this light-hearted comment was by an athlete whose introduction to the sport was via a medical consultant's table.

Born 33 years ago at Dover, Kent, RONALD HOLTUM, was advised by Dr. Woodward to run "as far and as often as you can", in an endeavour to heal an injury which threatened to leave him with a permanent limp. This injury had been sustained whilst playing football.

For one who entered athletics so late - he was 23 years old - his path to the top was exceptionally rapid. Within a year he had taken second place in the Middlesex County 100 yards and had won the Brockman Trophy short sprint.

The following year he startled everybody by defeating the almost invincible "black flash", McDonald Bailey. The positions were reversed a few weeks later in Scotland where, on a grass track, Ron was beaten 9.6 to 9.8 (Unfortunately, not an official A.A.A. time). In 1954 he had his most successful season, recording 9.9 on several occasions to eventually take 4th place in the final of the A.A.A.'s 100 yards event at the White City.

But it is as a member of our winning relay teams that he is best remembered. Wherever, and whenever, we entered a team Ron was an automatic choice; usually on the last leg, where his fast pick-up never failed to gain those extra few yards which would take us to a win or place. He was a member of our 4 x 110 yards relay teams which won the Middlesex County Championship in 1954 and 1958, the Southern Counties in 1954 and 1960 and the A.A.A. title in 1954. The teams of 1954, which included such athletes as Ivan Mayers and Clay Gibbs, have long since retired - excepting Ron of course, who is still most active.

His consistency year by year has not been due solely to his natural ability, for at 9 stone 4 lbs. this 6'1" tall athlete was far too light to absorb the strain of a full season's top class competition. To remedy this initial weakness he began weight training during the winter months to help build himself up. He now scales around 12 stones, which is an ideal weight for a sprinter.

Unfortunately, domestic difficulties and a crushed foot have prevented him from racing this season, but he assures everyone that he has not hung up his racing shoes and will continue to don the "claret and gold" for a good while yet. At a time when the Club is lacking in sprinters this is most welcome news, for his experience will prove invaluable to our younger members. For Ron has often asserted that the only way to get a well-blended and healthy team for the future is to go all out for the youngsters.

He has always shown an interest in the youth of the Club and is not one for words only. In 1959 when Frank Simmons was in hospital Ron took over the weight-training classes at Chelsea Barracks and did so again last year when sessions were held at Ravenstone Road School.

He has been actively interested in Club activities as a whole and in his time has been a member of the General Committee, Track Secretary and Track Captain, and has rarely missed supporting our teams in the London - Brighton Road Relay.

Ron, married and with a five year old daughter, now lives in Wandsworth Bridge Road, Fulham. He is a Chartered Structural Engineer by profession, having managed to combine many years of study with his running activities.

Most successful men owe part of their success to admirable wives, and as Ron is no exception it is all the more regrettable that Mrs. Holtum is at present in hospital for a period of several months. Fortunately it will not be long before the Holtums are back to normal again, so we can expect to see RONALD HOLTUM amongst our entries once more and, who knows, he might well anchor the London sprint relay team again as he did last year at 32 years of age!

BELGRAVE LOSE TITLE AT ILFORD

W.E.L.

March 18th, 1961.

We travelled to Ilford as the holders of the Ilford Road Relay Cup, which we won in 1960, but left with neither trophy no place medals this year. Our 4th place was by no means a disgrace, however, for despite being without the services of Newell and Miller our time was only a minute slower than last year's and was our third best ever. Had Pat and Ken been available we would certainly have been contesting the lead with T.V.H., Woodford, and Mitcham, who finished ahead of us. The proof that we had the beating of these clubs was demonstrated a few weeks later when we finished ahead of both T.V.H. and Mitcham in the National relay (Woodford had not qualified). The story of the race is one of improvement throughout.

Eric Gebbett faced stiff opposition on the first leg and although putting up the good time of 18.27 could do not better than 12th - 38 seconds in arrears.

Charlie Walker took the second leg and in doing 18.25 improved to 9th place - a good solid effort. We were then 42 seconds down on Polytechnic, who had gone into the lead by virtue of an excellent run by B. Fernee.

John Davies, pursuing his even-paced running theme, gained another place to 8th, but his time of 18.45 was below the standard we would expect from a man of his capabilities. Mitcham had taken the lead at this stage and we were now 53 seconds down.

Alan Black brought us up three places with an excellent run of 18.26 but we still lost ground on the leader. Polytechnic had once more taken up the running through a first class performance by Brian Turner (17.44). We were, however, within striking distance of the 3rd and 4th teams.

Clive Shippen, who has had a poor season owing to domestic troubles which have reduced his training to a minimum, came into the team in the absence of Pat Newell and on the whole ran well to do 19.06. He was forced to give way to Collins of Shaftesbury who went from 6th to 2nd and Lewis of Woodford (10th to 4th), but was able to displace Blackheath. Mitcham at this stage had regained the lead - our deficit now being 80 seconds.

Charlie Dabbs, still recovering from a chest complaint, was competing for the first time for some months and was an unknown quantity. He beat 19 minutes by just 2 seconds, but was obviously far from his best and was forced to give way to "Kipper" Herring who put up the second fastest time of the day in taking Blackheath back to 3rd.

Next came Ron Linstead, also an unknown quantity, but with a time of 18.31 he showed that he was regaining his form. His fine effort took us back to 6th as Polytechnic were now falling back. We were, however, over 2 minutes behind the leaders who were now T.V.H.

John Thresher took the eighth stage and gained a further place although his time of 19.07 was a little disappointing. T.V.H. had gone further ahead and our deficit had increased to 2 minutes 43 seconds. We still had some big guns to come though, so were still hopeful of a place.

Malcolm Robinson on the 9th, with a time of 18.29, took us up to 4th and gained 18 seconds on the leaders. We were, however, out of touch with the teams ahead and this left little chance for our last runner.

Jack Brown had the place of honour, but despite the excellent time of 17.53 could do little more than gain on all the teams ahead except Woodford. Jack is certainly happier on the road than on the country and we look to him to strengthen our relay teams for some time to come.

T.V.H. ran out the winners in record time and Woodford who finished a close second were also inside the previous best time. Mitcham in third position showed they are again a force to be reckoned with.

W.E.L.

BELS. 5th IN NATIONAL RELAY: ANOTHER FINE PERFORMANCE ON BRIGHTON ROAD

'Olympian'

Many clubs never cease to wonder how Belgrave maintain their high standard in this race. Over a period of many years our record is second to none and this year proved to be no exception. Our opponents look down our list of entries, which is usually devoid of "star" names, and write us off as "also rans" but we pop up in the leading places with amazing regularity to confound the critics and surprise the forecasters. Why this is I cannot say for our form over the country for many years has been deteriorating, culminating in an all time low at Parliament Hill Fields in 1961. As soon as the National is past our runners take on a new lease of life and in the Ilford Relay, traditionally held the week after the premier cross country event, we begin to show signs of the strength and depth of our middle distance runners. A new spirit arises and the sole topic of conversation is "The Brighton". The nerves of the team selectors become frayed and the telephone wires between Purley and Shepherds Bush hum with the up-to-date rumours of injury, sickness, loss of form, and other prevalent ailments common to all athletes prior to important events. This year seemed to be worse than most - the running order was changed with alarming regularity - the permutations to be used to cater for every eventuality were more extensive than any football syndicate could devise! In the end it all turned out for the best and those added grey hairs produced by the Running Hon. Secretary during the two weeks prior to the race proved to be quite unnecessary.

To finish fifth in such company was a great performance. Even the most optimistic of our supporters gave us no chance against Derby and Portsmouth, so the best we could hope for was third, and there were many clubs who could also lay claim to such a position. Coventry eventually took this place and South London managed to hold us off to take fourth spot. However, these events were unknown to the 20 starters in Old Palace Yard, Westminster, that morning. Not until the first stroke of Big Ben at 10 a.m. did the story begin to unfold.

STAGE 1 - Malcolm Robinson (20.32, 3rd fastest). Sheffield's Ken Wood set a brisk pace right from the start but not until Chelsea Bridge was crossed did the pace really step up. We were delighted to see Malcolm respond in a convincing manner and maintain an excellent run right to the end. Ken Wood broke the lap record to give Sheffield a lead of 18 seconds, but Malcolm was close up to the second man and failed to beat the club record held by George Flanagan by just one second.

STAGE 2 - Alan Black (25.28, 8th fastest). Alan is one of our most stylish runners and has a great athletic future, so we knew this stage was in good hands. He ran steadily throughout and although forced to give way to Derby and Tipton he managed to catch and pass the Coventry boy to finish his run in fourth position. Derby, through one of the Keily brothers, had established a lead at Mitcham of 51 seconds over Tipton and we were 64 seconds in arrears.

STAGE 3 - Pat Newell (28.57, 8th fastest). It had needed the diplomacy of our General Hon. Secretary to get Pat on his mark for the run to Purley. Great fears of a printing strike had hung over our heads during the preceding week as the price for Pat's presence, but Alf's negotiations with a leading "daily" proved successful and a fit and happy Newell set off to fight every inch of the way on this crucial part of the race. The whole pattern changed in a few miles. Heatley came up from ninth to take over the lead and to lower the course record by 17 seconds, whilst Pat, despite an excellent run, was pushed back to eighth by other "big-guns", namely Connolly, Craig, Langridge, and Herring. However, we were still only 63 seconds behind the leaders!

STAGE 4 - Eric Gebbett (25.47, 12th fastest). Eric had been given this leg following his return to form over the preceding few weeks, during which he had performed so well in the Wigmore '15'. Unfortunately he was not at his very best and although running steadily throughout and giving a plucky display we had to drop some ground - although still maintaining our eighth position. At Mersthan we were 1 minute 40 seconds in arrears. It was on this leg that Portsmouth became prominent for the first time.

STAGE 5 - Charlie Dabbs (22.19, 8th fastest). Owing to illness which had curtailed his training for many weeks during the Winter, Charlie was only a shadow of his best and had to relinquish the later leg on which we have been accustomed to seeing him run so well. The fast downhill start on the stretch into Redhill did not really suit the Dabbs' style but nevertheless he gained a place and although not covering himself with glory can be said to have performed up to expectations.

STAGE 6 - John Thresher (13.55, 9th fastest). This is the shortest leg of the race and John, still a junior, was running in his first "Brighton". His performance was above average and compared most favourably with his predecessors on this stage; a good effort which will undoubtedly be the forerunner of many more outings in this race. At the end of this leg Derby were out in front with a 40 second lead and we were lagging by 2 minutes 46 seconds in 7th place.

STAGE 7 - Cyril Forey (19.01, 10th fastest). Cyril is a relatively new member of Belgrave and one of our brightest prospects; a lad who gets fit very quickly, has a good judge of pace and can be a champion if he makes up his mind to stick to the hard work needed to take him to the top. This was his first run on the Brighton Road yet he established himself by taking Alan Black's club record for this leg, by one second! and moved us up to 6th just behind TVH. The distance from the leaders, Derby, had, however, widened to over 3 minutes.

STAGE 8 - Jack Brown (30.25, 10th fastest). Jack protests that he is getting old, but there are few in Belgrave who can hold him on the road; as he proved by lowering Charlie Dabbs stage record by 18 seconds to maintain our sixth position in the face of heavy opposition. This is the leg where the race can be won and lost, and names such as Hyman (who produced a fantastic new record - 34 seconds better than his own previous best), Wilkinson, Perry, North, Reed, Edmonds, Hogan and Foord appeared - enough to frighten the stoutest of hearts. Portsmouth at this point had built up a lead of 1 minute 11 seconds and we were 4 minutes, 28 seconds behind them.

STAGE 9 - Bill Kerr (29.07, 6th fastest). To the uninitiated Belgrave appeared to have gone from the face of the earth at this stage, for the claret and gold vest was nowhere to be seen. Not only that but we were running a comparative stranger, for Bill had only been in our ranks just long enough to gain entry to this event. Unfortunately the club vest which had been provided for him and which was resplendent with his correct numbers had been whisked away in the official coach whilst he was warming up and he could do no more than run in what he wore. How well he ran is shown in his time of 29.07, beaten only once by a Belgravian and that non other than our Indian Olympic runner Ranjit Bhatia just one year earlier. When Bill Kerr passed Mick Firth of S.L.H. to take us up to 5th place we knew that the form he had shown in our road race two weeks earlier was no flash in the pan and our confidence in entrusting him with this crucial leg was justified. This was our greatest "find" for many years and we welcome our new Scottish friend to our midst.

STAGE 10 - Ron Linstead (20.55, 9th fastest). How Ron suffered on this day in driving himself over this stage, and the state of nerves he built up is known only to himself. Sufficient to say he spared himself nothing and although losing one place he put up a most courageous effort. The subsequent reaction concerned us greatly but we are glad to know the effects were not lasting. Ron must indeed have been pleased to see Charlie Walker waiting for him at the foot of Dale Hill.

STAGE 11 - Charlie Walker (20.54, 5th fastest). With only two legs to go we were now sixth and nearly 5½ minutes behind Derby who had again taken the lead. Charles set off up Dale Hill in his indominatable manner and ran a well judged race throughout, shaking off the persistent Roberts of TVH. Unfortunately the S.L.H. boy, Barber, was in full cry and forced Charles back into 7th place despite the encouragement of our supporters. Nevertheless the three clubs immediately ahead were not out of reach and all depended on the last leg.

STAGE 12 - John Davis (17.42, 3rd fastest). John had the dubious pleasure of watching Gordon Pirie set off exactly 30 seconds ahead with Bristol and Blackpool in close attendance. Not only that but T.V.H. (Nottage) were on John's shoulder after 300 yards. Our runner had built up over a period of months a judgement of pace which, although not always to the liking of supporters in its early stages, invariably produces a first-class performance. Showing interminable patience John drew slowly away from T.V.H. and with our supporters suffering every agony imaginable gradually closed the gap on Bristol and Blackpool, who were fighting together some 100 yards ahead. A great effort over the last mile took John into the finishing funnel ahead of his rivals by just 4 seconds and 9 seconds respectively - the best judged race we have seen for a long time. Although our 5th place was far better than many had expected, the gap of 6 minutes between us and the leaders (½ minute per leg) indicates just how much we must improve, but we can anticipate being much closer next year with men like Dabbs and Linstead in fitter condition and our younger runners older in both age and experience.

JUNIOR ~ SECTION Incorporating Boys & Youths: Cross-Country

"Of four inter-club matches this season our Youths had three wins," writes Junior Secretary Jack Hampshire Snr. "These were over Sutton, Surrey and close rivals Mitcham."

After such a start, and more especially beating Mitcham for the first time, hopes for a good performance in the Surrey Youths were high. However, the race at Guildford proved a disappointment. We were sixth. But the course was very much unsuited to the track style of our leading runners, save Jackie Hampshire, involving three laps of stiff hill-climbing.

Next came the highlight of the season - the Southern Championships, at Ewell, where we finished third of the thirty-eight teams. Over 400 started on the one lap three-mile course.

After a mile only Tony Fairclough was anywhere near the leaders, but after a further half mile he had been joined by Hampshire and was in the twenties. Alan Morrison, Ken Redwood and David Cocks were together in the forties and Chris Steer near the seventy mark. The leaders had separated from the field as they came down the farm yard and turned sharp right to cross over the plough. Fairclough and Hampshire were still together, with Morrison and Redwood drawing clear of Cocks.

With little more than a half-mile to go, up the football field across the stubble and onto the finish, Jack began a powerful stride which swept him past seven runners for 13th position. Six behind was Tony. Ken was 47th and Alan, who just beat in David after a hundred yard sprint for last scoring man, was 68th.

We have to go back to 1952 for when the Youths were last placed in these Championships. It reflected credit not only on the lads' but also on the methods of training carried out by Tom Carter, (Combined PACK training of both the good and the not so good boy). Secretary Jack Hampshire says it was a wonderful tonic to all those associated with the club who were present.

Parliament Hill Fields was the next venue - for the National. The lads did not take too kindly to the heavy mud. That is, all with the exception of Jack Hampshire. Jack ran his usual strong race, face set in concentration, arms swinging vigorously to come in 25th - and so gain his club 'honours' badge. Next to make the short sharp climb to the finish was Chris Steer (123), just ahead of Mitcham's sub 4.20 miler, Malcolm Burton, with Tony Fairclough 150, closely followed in by Ken Redwood 159. A notable absentee was Alan Morrison who had been confined to bed during the week. The team took 18th of the 66 places.

All in all the Youths can be said to have had a good season. The team has been considerably stregthened by the inclusion of both Tony Fairclough and Chris Steer, and with the seasoned Hampshire, Redwood, Cocks and Morrison, not forgetting 'near misses' Howard Satchell and John Jarvis, they can be expected to improve on their performances next winter.

Tony Fairclough took his place on the starting line just ten seconds before the race started! Due to fixture 'mix-up' the two cars which set off for Ilford landed (!) elsewhere; arriving just prior to the referee closing-in the teams. The excitement experienced getting to the venue was maintained by the lads in their running when they very narrowly failed to take second place - closer than the time suggests.

TRACK ROUND-UP

M.E.R.

The highlight of this season, so far, is undoubtedly the struggle for supremacy between Peter Eldridge and Brian Morris, in the quarter-mile. After Peter's excellent start when taking third place in the Inter-counties, he looked to appear on top. But Brian, notorious for his slow first 220, relying on a fast last burst, ensured victory for Belgrave in the Continental Relay at the London Clubs' meeting at the White City. His 440 yards was clocked unofficially at 47.3 (!) and was followed a week later at Southampton when in the colours of Metropolitan Police he raced through the quarter again, in a Continental Relay, in 47.8. To crown it all he took fourth place in the A.A.A.'s final with 48.5 - after equalling the club record of 48.2 in his heat. Peter was pushed out in the semi-final with 49.0. Brian, Peter and Alan Secton now share this record; although Gerry Leroy's 47.7 400 metres must not be forgotten!

South African Bob Norton is yet another member of the club to have run in the Inter-Versity match. Last year it was Rangit Bhatia in the mile; the year previous Clive Ryan in the 440. This year Bob ran in the event sandwiched by these two - the 880 yards. His 1:52.4, to win the Oxford University title, was a new Club Record. This time is exactly 3 seconds faster than our next fastest man this season, John Thresher, who ran 1:55.4 in the Ryder Trophy at Southampton. Another to establish a personal best at this event is Dave Mitchell. His 1:55.7 was accomplished with virtually no training - he has just graduated from College.

A new face which has been of great help to us this season is that of Scotland's Bill Kerr. An athlete who suffers so little from nerves that either before or after a hard race he can still keep wisecracking, Bill came to us from Victoria Park four months ago, and has already set us a new Club Record in the 2 miles. His 8.55 when placed third in the London Club's 2 mile race at the White City erased Jack Brown's mark of 8.59 from the record book. Others to set up personal bests in this event are Malcolm Robinson with 9:08.2, Pat Newell 9:09, Dan Maclean 9:11.6 and John Davies 9:15.

Malcolm, who lost his club mile title to John, is the fastest miler so far this season with 4:12.2, set in the Southern Championships, Bill Kerr is second with 4:16.0. Pat leads the list in the three mile with 14:00. This time was accomplished in the Middlesex Championships where he was placed 3rd - and was on a racing watch! Other more reliable watches had him inside the magical "14:00". In the six miles it is Malcolm again who leads, with 29:47.6. These five runners could well produce yet another record in the Club '3'. By the time this magazine has been distributed the result will be known - no prognostications from this writer!

It is in the sprints that we are particularly weak. Apart from our quarter-miles we have none in action - save Graham Worrell, who defeated last year's Junion Champion John Strachan in the quarter-mile against Old Rutlishians. But then Graham is really a quarter-miler. He, together with Peter Eldridge, Brian Morris and Gerry Leroy (who clocked 48.9 when winning the Ryder Trophy event) could pull off the 4 x 440 A.A.A.'s title. However, the way seems very clear for almost any sprinter to assure the mantle of leading-sprinter.

Not so many years ago we won the Sward Trophy for field events - in 1956, but now we are so lamentably poor in this section that we can't even field a team. Bill Couzens, now entering the marriage stakes, seemed at the beginning of the season to be in for a battle with George Edwards in the Javelin. This has not materialised, however, and despite his 167'0½" in the Kinnaird Bill has improved no further, mainly due to training difficulties, whilst George does not appear to be around much these days. There is an almost complete absence of men in the discus and shot. Roy Hollingsworth is only second claim for the discus and R. Shuter has no one to extend him in the shot. Of the remaining field events the long jump has the wealthiest look. Mick Smithers, in his first year as a Senior, has jumped 20'11" - with several over 21'0" disallowed, and Derek Wade (still a Junior) has leapt 20'8" - yet he has set his sights on breaking 2 minutes for the 'half'! Not so far behind is Heinz Leyer, who came to us at the beginning of the season from Germany - he has leapt 19'6" so far, but with the right encouragement this likeable and enthusiastic all-rounder could go much further.

THE BROCKMAN TROPHY

CROYDON, JUNE 17TH.

Even though eleven clubs were competing in this Herne Hill Harriers promotion, Belgrave proved themselves to have the day's most powerful track team - yet they could only finish 5th in the final result!

Why ? Well the composition of our score was as follows:- from 10 track events, 41 points; from 8 field events, 1 point. As we finished only 9 points behind the winners (T.V.H.) and 3 points behind the runners-up (Blackheath) we needed but a very modest score from the field to secure this important trophy. Needless to say, the team were very disappointed.

We had two winners. Both were equally convincing. Peter Eldridge, running in the inside lane, had removed his stagger handicap before the runners had entered the third bend! He won by a good 15 yards in 49.0 seconds. Trevor Tolson was a clear winner in the high hurdles, recording 16.0 seconds against the wind.

We had three runners-up. Most impressive of these was Bill Kerr, whose effort in the 3 miles showed what great potential he possesses. After nearly 2 miles of pace-setting by Stacey (T.V.H.), Bill with Longridge (S.L.H.) broke clear. With 660 yards to go our runner made his effort and managed to hold off his opponent's noted finish until the last 50 yards. Bill, by no means distressed, recorded a noteworthy 14:04.2, only 0.8 of a second down. Pat Newell's second place was gained in the six miles event. Card (T.V.H.) was a clear leader for 4 miles but faded badly enabling Pat, who had run a steady race, to gain just one more place and finish second in 30:10.2 - 18.6 seconds behind Major of Woodford. It was in the 3,000 metres Steeplechase that our third set of 5 points was gained. Clive Shippen set the pace from the start and by 2 laps had shaken off the last of the opposition. With 3 laps to go and a lead of 50 yards he seemed assured of 6 points, but Clive could not maintain the pace and, showing little fight, reliquished his lead at the bell to finish a thankful second with 9:49.0.

We secured two third places. Gerry Leroy, still not entirely free of injury, managed to win his heat of the 100 yards; following this up with a fine 3rd place in the final, recording 10.4. And, in the final event of the day, our 4 x 440 yards relay team were just pushed out of second place by T.V.H. After Terry Newell, Randy Glenn and Peter Pepe had run we were abreast of Blackheath, a good 15 yards clear of the remainder. Although he had run a half-mile heat and final during the afternoon, John Thresher took last stage. He was obviously no match for Blackheath's Sampson, but could he hold off an impressive trio of runners who were pounding down on him from behind ? John was caught at the start of the long finishing straight, but showing tremendous fight and determination he held them off until the last two yards when the T.V.H. man got the verdict with a drop finish.

Our final collections were one 4th, one 5th and two 6ths. Gerry Leroy was late for the start of the 220 yards final and had no time to use blocks. As a result, he was completely left at the gun; the man on his inside being level after 2 yards. Gerry made ground down the final straight but had to be content with 4th in 22.8, 0.3 of a second behind the winner.

Our field place went to John Thresher in the 880 yards final, yet he was only inches short of second place. In a blanket finish John was given 1:59.0 to the runner's-up 1:58.9 - by such margins are trophies decided!

Our two single point donations came from a jaded looking John Davies in the 1500 metres (4 minutes 1.6) and from that all-round field event man, George Edwards, with a 9'6" pole-vault.

The spirit in which the various events were contested, and the support the various clubs received, were reminicent of earlier days. More enthusiasm abounded at this meeting than all the other trophy meetings put together. Perhaps it was because there was a lack of certain notable stars. This left the majority of events wide open and allowed a more friendly type of rivalry to prevail.

THE 1961 CHAMPIONSHIPS

RON LINSTEAD

RON LINSTEAD reports :

Run in cold and very windy conditions this year's meeting, held at Hurlingham Park, was rather disappointing. The stands were almost empty and our stars of yesteryear conspicuous by their absence. Surely on this one particular day everyone should make an effort to attend. The childrens' races were even cancelled, due to lack of support - perhaps we have lost our virility as well as our cross-country titles!

Star of the show was undoubtedly Gerry Leroy, who had four easy wins. His performance in the 440 yards was especially praiseworthy, coming as it did near the end of the programme. It was a pity there was no one capable of pushing Gerry, as he would certainly have gone even faster than the 49.4 he was credited. Randy Glenn, Terry Newell and Alan Jackson produced a blanket finish for 2nd, 3rd and 4th, all recording 51.5.

The hardest fought race of the day was the Senior 880 yards. Alan Black was the early leader but 220 yards out Jackson, Thresher and Glenn swept by to each lead in turn down the long finishing straight before Jackson forged ahead once more in the closing stages to record 1: 58.6 - his future is indeed bright. Thresher, whose studies have made his training schedule one of the lightest in the club, was second in 1: 59.2 and the powerfully built Randy Glenn third in 1: 59.4 - his fastest time.

A cloudburst and gale-force winds greeted the start of the Senior mile and made conditions even worse. As if this wasn't enough there had to be a false start! For two laps the holder, Malcolm Robinson, led the field through the elements until Don Maclean took over with Pat Newell and John Davies on his heels. With 300 yards to go these three were clear and Mac & Pat made their efforts with John hanging on grimly. A superb fighting effort by Davies on the last bend brought him back into contact with the leaders, and down the straight his superior speed took him to a well deserved victory in 4 : 26.0, having run the last two laps in 2 : 8.0. Mac, of late a much more consistent runner than he has been for several years, was second only a fifth of a second down, and Pat third in 4 : 27.6. Robinson was an unhappy fourth with a time some 20 seconds outside his best.

The 2 miles walk resulted in an easy win for George Williams with 15 minutes 9 seconds. Much of the interest in this event was lost owing to the absence of Vickers, Hall and Middleton.

The most impressive of the Juniors was David Cocks who took the 440 (53.1) and 880 (2:02.2) in magnificently relaxed style. His low, smooth, economical leg action makes it all look so easy.

In the Boys events, strongly built Roger Hudson broke the 100 yards record in his heat with a time of 11.2. In the final, however, he was forced to give way to the other heat winner, R. Bevan, who clocked 11.3. Roger, primarily a quartermiler, made amends for this set back when he took the 880 yards in 2:09.5. Had he shown some restraint over the first lap - he went through in 59.0! - he might well have done about 2:04.

The performances of our Boys, Youths and Juniors certainly indicated that, with continued careful coaching, we have some bright prospects in the making.

ROAD RUNNING

One of the outstanding performances this summer has been Ron Linstead's record-breaking win in the Isle of Man Road Race, held over the 37¾ mile T.T. course on May 22nd. But let the front page of the Isle of Man DAILY TIMES tell you the story with the following abbreviated extract:-

"Records tumbled in yesterday's T.T. course road race. The first three men home were all inside the 3 hours 57 minutes 5 seconds target set by Tom Richards in 1956. And the new lap record holder, Ron Linstead (Belgrave Harriers) smashed the previous best by 6 minutes 25 seconds!.... (he) was home 4 minutes 15 seconds ahead of the second man, last year's winner, Don Turner (Epsom and Ewell Harriers)........

"Linstead, ....... had his first look at the course on Sunday when the runners were taken on a coach trip of the T.T. circuit. He was one of 13 newcomers in a field of 33....... But if he was unfamiliar with the circuit he was unafraid of the T.T. course's reputation. For at the first check point - the five mile mark - Linstead shared second place with E. Elderfield (T.V.H.) half a minute down on the leader, K. Heaton (Clayton-le-Moors)".

"By the ten mile mark at the top of Creg Willys Hill, Linstead had taken the lead that he was to hold right to the end. But matching him stride for stride was Elderfield. They had opened a two minute gap, covering the ten miles in 63 minutes 2 seconds....... The 15 mile check point saw Linstead and Elderfield still running easily side by side, with a slightly increased lead. They passed the timekeepers in 1 hour 32 minutes 23 seconds. Watt & Smith were through in 1 : 34 : 36, then came Turner (1 : 34 : 38) and Lucop (1 : 34 : 49).......".

"With 20 miles gone the leaders, Linstead and Elderfield, passed Kerrowmoor in 2 : 02 : 25. They had lost ground slightly to Watt and Smith, through in 2 : 03 : 44. But, significantly, the leading pair had stepped up their pace. They covered the second ten miles 4 minutes faster than the first 10. This was the first hint of the records to come.

"At Kerrowmoor Turner held 5th place in 2 : 04 : 04 and sixth was H.W. Dennis (T.V.H.) in 2 : 06 : 15........".

"The leaders were "clocked" at almost exactly 10 m.p.h. for their fourth 5 mile stint, and the record began to look in danger. Through Parliament Square Linstead headed Elderfield by several yards, and although he took the wrong turning - heading up Brookefield Avenue for a round of golf! - spectators turned him back to his correct path before he lost his lead. The lanky Elderfield was beginning to look tired now."

"And Linstead passed the 25 mile post on his own, in 2 : 31 : 30. He had 17 seconds in hand over Elderfield, who was blowing painfully......into sixth place had moved B.C. Sawyer (Belgrave Harriers) with 2 : 37 : 15. It now seemed that Linstead must win. He was running easily up the mountain with no sign of strain. His partner of 15 gruelling miles, E. Elderfield, was not however faring so well, and the killing climb from the Gooseneck forced him to walk, while rival after rival edged past."

"Linstead had a three minute lead with just 7¾ miles to go and the mountain climb behind him. Last year's winner, Turner, still had hopes of catching him, however, and called to the timekeepers for Linstead's lead. As the leader rounded Creg-ny-baa there was still no sign of Turner.........and the only question that remained was, could he break the record."

"Yes, indeed he could. Ron Linstead crossed the line 3 hours, 50 minutes, 40 seconds after starting, to record his first big win in long distance road racing. After 37¾ miles Ron was not even breathing hard. His comment on the course - "The mountain is the only thing. I had a good race"."

Apart from Linstead bouquets this season go to Gebbett for his excellent 4th place in the Wigmore '15' (1:22:26); to Sawyer - whose thirst for distance racing must make him our most travelled athlete; to the much improved Dennis Jones for his 6th place in the Kent '20' (1 : 55 : 44) and his 9th in the Chichester to Portsmouth '16' (1 : 33 : 45); and to Norman Stairs, who runs only in the summer, for his winning of the "A. P. Mitchell" Shield for being first Belgravian home in the Poly. marathon (2 : 36 : 05).

AROUND THE COUNTIES........ AND THE AREAS

SURREY

Ray Middleton proved that long distance training does not necessarily blunt the speed for shorter races when winning the 2-miles walk title. He equalled his previous best time with 14 : 29 : 8, with the deposed holder, George Williams, in second place (14:34:4).

Other runners-up for Belgrave were Gerry Leroy, 49.0 seconds 440 yards and Mal Robinson, 4 : 20 : 4 mile - after passing through the ½ mile in 2:17! Fifth in this latter event was John Thresher (4:26:0).

In the qualifying heats of the 3 miles, held two days earlier, competitors had to achieve 15 minutes to qualify for the final. Don Maclean and Clive Shippen reached the final (Clive 15:00 exactly!), but Arthur Bruce 15:06) just failed to make it. Unfortunately, both our qualifiers produced slower times in the final and were way down the field.

Alan Black (mile) and Pete Mitchell (880) were eliminated in their heats.

MIDDLESEX

At one time we used to have a healthy number of entries for this County's championships, but this year we had but one competitor, Pat Newell. However, Pat carried our colours high and gained a most worthy third place. His official time of 14.00 minutes exactly was given on a running watch! Several other watches placed him inside the 14.00 minute "barrier" - a tenth of a second here can mean a great deal to an athlete. Pat's effort was nearly 15 seconds faster than his previous best.

BEDFORDSHIRE

Peter Eldridge pulled off the treble once again. His times this year were, 100 yards - 9.9 seconds (equals Club record), 220 yards - 21.9 seconds, and 440 yards - 49.8 seconds. Great running from one of the best athletes we have ever had.

YORKSHIRE.

Once again Trevor Tolson annexed the 120 yards hurdles title. Despite a troublesome head wind he recorded 16.2 seconds.

SUSSEX.

Brian Morris finished first in the final of the 220 yards with 22.6 seconds, and later came out to make it a double by retaining his 440 yards title with a winning time of 49.4 seconds.

........AND THE AREAS

SOUTHERN

There were too few Belgravians competing in this year's championship, held at Motspur Park. The number of "claret and gold" vests seems to dwindle year by year. Of those members who competed on this hottest day of the year, pride of place must go to Brian Morris who was 4th in the 440 yards. Halfway down the final straight he was seemingly doomed for sixth place, but a superhuman effort took him past two rivals to record 48.3 seconds - an excellent time. Gerry Leroy, not quite at his best this season, was eliminated in the semi-finals.

Mal Robinson failed to qualify for the star-studded mile final, finishing sixth in his heat with 4 minutes 12.8!

No one envied Pat Newell, who ran in the 3 miles main event. For besides the oppresive heat he also had to contend with Messrs. Pirie, Hyman, Eldon & Co. Pat ran, relatively, as well as anybody in the field to record 14:14, but was outside the first six.

Alan Black & Clive Shippen ran in the 3,000 meters steeplechase, but both showed poor form, Alan recording 9:51 and Clive just outside 10 minutes.

NORTHERN

Trevor Tolson hurdled beautifully to record 15.2 seconds in the 120 yards high hurdles and finish second behind international R. Birrell.

Trevor, who first gained an A.A.A. vest 10 years ago, says he will keep at it until he breaks that magical 15 seconds. Incidentally, his time of 15.2 sets a new club record - improving by one-fifth of a second on Mal Ash's mark set in 1955.

MIDLAND

The ever-reliable Peter Eldridge was another to do credit to the name of BELGRAVE. He finished an excellent third in the 220 yards final behind Peter Radford and Robbie Brightwell, recording 22.4 seconds.

Peter followed this up in the 440 yards final by recording 48.2 in second place behind Barry Jackson, another International.

SCOTTISH

Travelling to his homeland in an endeavour to record times to qualify for the A.A.A. championships, Bill Kerr unwisely ran in the six-mile event on the Friday evening. After passing the halfway mark in 14:32 he was obliged to drop out.

The following day, in a slow-run race, he was placed third in the 3 miles with 14:31:2, being beaten for speed at the end.

Don Maclean (with a name like that I suppose he must be Scottish!) was also running at the Championships. Don also claims Welsh qualifications and had their meeting not been on the same day as the Southern he would have run their too! Anyway, this cosmopolitan character finished fifth in the mile event with 4:25.

As we go to press we must congratulate Ray Middleton and George Williams on their being selected to represent Great Britain in the first round of the Europa Cup Walking Competition to be held at Wimbledon Park in conjunction with the Great Britain v Switzerland match on August 12th.

Britain's opponents in this European zonal competition will be Belguim and a combined East and West German team. The winners will compete against other zonal winners at Lugano on October 15th.

George will take part in the 20 kilos. event and Roy the 50 kilos.

Ray MiddletonGeorge Williams
