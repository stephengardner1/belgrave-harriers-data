FROM OUR PRESIDENT.

O. HORWOOD (President)

Looking back into the past, I well remember the lean years of the Club. At one time I marked the track, combined the offices of Hon. Secretary, Hon. Treasurer and Hon. Handicapper; and carried on with the help of a few members who, for love of the Club, were determined that we should not be disbanded. For fear of leaving out any name that should be mentioned, I will not give any names. It was wonderful how we stuck together, facing all sorts of troubles and anxieties to keep the Club going.

I well remember how, when we had collected a few new members and were beginning to show winners in open handicaps (which to us at that time was like winning A.A.A .Championships to-day!); one member came to me and handed in his resignation. When asked the reason he said he wished to support a better club, one that had trophies and relay teams. I remember appealing to him very strongly to stay with us. I remarked that we should not always be a poor club and right down; but that we had hopes of having trophies and relay teams in the future. The reply was that Belgrave would never be a big club; or have trophies as long as they were in existence. This annoyed me, and made me more determined than ever that with the help of others, the Club should one day be held in high esteem.

At the recent Annual General Meeting, I went over these times in my mind again, and when I recalled the recent wonderful performances that our members have made in all parts of the world, I felt well-rewarded for all our past work. When our retiring President, Mr. J. R. McKay, spoke on my behalf proposing me as your new President, and his remarks were seconded by Mr. A. A. Harley, I was amazed at the warm welcome the members gave me and wish to express my heartfelt thanks. Throughout the meeting I noted the keenness and good spirit that exists among the members and could well understand how, with such team-spirit and co-operation, Belgrave has achieved its wonderful position to-day.

I should like to address a few words to the Juniors. The Seniors who have placed the Club where it is cannot last for ever, so let us remember what they have done and how they have done it; and to train with the same spirit. Aim not only to hold the various titles they have won, but to do even better! Mr. R. J. Savage, in charge of our Junior section, will, I am sure, welcome all our Juniors and watch over their interests; and for their part, it is up to them to accept and carry out the advice that is given. Let our Juniors make their motto : "Still Better."

Yours faithfully, O. HORWOOD (President).

THIS AND THAT.

In our last issue we had occasion to refer to the scandalous way in which Jim Ginty has been ignored when the England team to meet France was selected; and expressed the hope that some enquiries should be instituted, and an attempt made to secure some satisfaction.

Our Committee had, of course, acted in the matter, and were informed that Ginty had not been selected as it was thought that he had an Irish qualification; this in spite of the fact that confirmation or otherwise of his qualifications could have been given inside a few seconds, if called for, and that the true facts had appeared in the Press a clear week before the International Meeting. This explanation was considered unsatisfactory by our Committee. A further letter was written, and our delegate to the A.A.A. undertook to take the whole correspondence to Birmingham on October 12th, and lay the matter before the General Committee of the A.A.A.

This was done, the Middlesex County A.A.A. delegates supported us, and further support came from all parts of the Committee room. The result was that the Selection Committee admitted their error, and extended an apology.

So the matter ends, and it is good to know that even if such a wrong cannot be righted, we have, at least, been able to secure some redress and respect for our opinions.

* * * * *

Heartiest congratulations to Mr. F. G. Thomson upon his election as President of the Road Walking Association.

Such an honour is the reward for much hard work for Walking and, indeed, Athletics generally; and we wish him a most successful year of office.

He is the first Belgrave Harrier to be so honoured; and it would be very fitting for us to regain the "Junior" and "Senior" Championship trophies during his year of office, and set them by the side of the 50 kilos. Championship Cup which we already hold.

* * * * *

Here follows a letter received recently from the Birmingham Walking Club :—

"Arising from the report presented to a record attendance at the A.G.M. of this club on Tuesday last, it was pointed out that your club had beaten the Birmingham team by a single point in the 50 kilos. National Championship.

"I was asked to convey the meeting's congratulations on this win, together with your club's popular success, when, among others, you beat our friends the Birchfield Harriers, to win the English Cross-country Championship, thus completing a unique double.

"Will you kindly pass the appreciation on to the members of your club, together with our wish that the relations between the two Clubs will continue to be of a most friendly nature. "(Signed) B. BASHFORD, Hon. Gen. Secretary."

* * * * *

We were glad that Tommy Scrimshaw did so well when running for England v. France, at the White City; and against Germany, at Munich. On both occasions his running in the half-mile stage of the Medley Relay Races was virtually responsible for England's wins in these events.

We all hope that Tommy will achieve Olympic honours next year, when the good wishes of all of us will be with him. Incidentally, we understand that he is learning German this winter, so! . . . .

* * * * *

It came as a great shock to us to learn on the evening of the A.G.M., of the sudden illness of Bill Rice, our popular Social Hon. Secretary and Track Captain. For four days, with the help of oxygen, he battled as we all know he can; and now we are happy to write that he is making slow, but definite, progress. Very soon now he will be with us again, and we wish him a rapid and complete recovery of normal health and fitness.

Of our other one-time invalids, Ted Saunders is out walking with us again, and Harry Shields has made a marvellously quick recovery from his recent operation, and will probably be running again over the country before you read these notes. Edgar Stammers, our popular Vice-President, is another to make rapid progress, and we expect to have his smiling face among us again before long.

Our heartiest congratulations and very best wishes go to Joe Coleman, Bert Field and Jim Ginty upon their respective marriages. We wish them all years of health and happiness.

* * * * *

Our recent novice races have been won as follows : J. Amsden (100 yds.); J. Paterson (1 mile walk); A. H. Beaumont (3 miles run); S. R. Wood (3 miles walk). Entries for these events have not been good, and we can only assume that our members make a point of introducing new friends immediately they realise their interest, and do not wait for novice events. Anyhow, we are always glad to welcome new members, and it must be said that recent introductions have been numerous, and proved themselves good clubmen.

* * * * *

Our readers will remember that in our last issue some news was given of B. C. Button, and many will recall him as a promising young runner in our ranks in 1926—1927. He is now Hon. Secretary of the St. George District A.A.C., New South Wales, and in a recent letter, after recalling old times, etc., he proceeds to give some details and insight into Australian athletics, as follows :—

" . . . . I often tell the fellows out here about these and other incidents, and you would be surprised at the interest they take in any overseas happenings. As you say, the Club has made great strides over the past two or three years, and you certainly have a huge membership. I get the "Sunday Pictorial" each week, and although they are very sparing with their Athletic news, I often notice Belgrave's name appearing in the major events and, needless to say, I pass it on to the fellows out here and even take some credit for it! Fancy being able to win the London to Brighton relay. This was beyond our greatest expectations at one time as it looked like being perpetually in the hands of the northern runners!

"Now for some local news, so that you can get some idea of the Athletic activities out here.

"We have, of course, track and cross-country fixtures, and although in the cross-country season we have quite an attractive programme, the total number of members engaged from all clubs is only about 200. My Club has a membership of about 80, with an average cross-country attendance of 20, and we have more or less been the strongest distance club over the past few years. At the moment we hold all the major N.S.W. titles. We have three big races, viz., the five-mile Novice Championship, open to runners who have not been placed within the first ten of any major cross-country championship, the 10,000 metres C.C.C. and the 10 miles C.C.C., with the 15 miles Modified Marathon in September each year. In the second and third of the above Championships, there are three teams' races. You nominate all your men, and the first six to finish count for the "A" team; the next four in the "B" team, and the next four for the "C" team. On August 17th this year we have the Australian C.C.C. in Sydney, and I have been appointed as one of the three Selectors to choose the N.S.W. team, and also as the Manager. Each State takes it in turns in having the Australian C.C.C., which is held once every two years. When you realise that South Australians have to travel nearly 1,000 miles, Tasmanians about the same distance, and that all visiting competitors have to get a week off, you can see that it is not so easy to stage these events.

"By the way, I was recently appointed as 'Sporting Commentator' for one of our Broadcasting Stations, and each Saturday night at 6-30 p.m. I give a review of all sports results, including, of course, Athletics.

"Apart from the Championships, we have Open Handicaps in which all clubs compete and the entries usually total about 90 runners. In these events the runners start off their handicap time allowances, and it is seldom that we have sealed handicaps. In Victoria the running is on a much better basis, and in their cross-country opening run they have anything from six to seven hundred Athletes competing. In N.S.W. we have so many sports, viz., Rugby League Football, Rugby Union Football, Soccer (English Football), Baseball, Hockey, etc., etc., and in the Summer time the surf is a hard factor to overcome, particularly in N.S.W., as around Sydney we have numerous surf beaches which really are most attractive. On the track we have numerous competitions. Firstly, for seven or eight weeks, the Inter Club Competition, in which all Clubs meet on one central ground—each club enters two or three grades. There are four grades, viz., (a), (b), (c) and (d), and they provide good competition. Next comes the "Dunn Shield" Competition, which is limited to selected Athletes from each Club, then the State Championships, followed by State Relay Championships, etc.

"Believe me, in N.S.W. we produce some really first-class athletes, considering the conditions we have to overcome, as we do not have track facilities, etc., such as you do.

"My Club is fortunate in having the use of a good Oval for its Track Meeting every Tuesday night, and at our Annual Carnival we usually attract five to six thousand people. The locals are good supporters if you give them good, fast sport, and we usually intermingle athletics with cycle races and one or two displays. Jack Metcalfe is probably known to you, as he won the Empire Games High Jump Championship. Speaking of the Empire Games, it looks as though there is every chance of them being held in Sydney, provided they can raise £10,000, so if any Belgrave men are chosen, don't forget to give them my address.

"Jack Metcalfe is good for a consistent 6ft. 3ins. or 6ft. 4ins. in the high jump. Now, in my Club I have a young chap called Basil Dickinson, who clears just over 24ft. 11ins. in the broad-jump, and is so keen that I really think he has an excellent chance of seeing the next Olympic Games as an Australian representative. He can also clear over 50ft. in the hop, step and jump. We also have Bill Mackenzie, the Australian discus and shot putt champion in the St. George Club, and he is a really first-class Athlete. By the way, he has just been appointed with Jack Metcalfe and another man to form a N.S.W. Coaching Committee, and if you have any spare pamphlets on any aspect of coaching, etc., they will be very much appreciated. They have already purchased some good slow motion films which they show to Clubs at fixed intervals.

"The walking standard out here is terrible, and with the present control they have there seems no chance of any immediate improvement. There is nobody to coach young fellows interested in the game, and the judges do not seem to understand the elementary rules of walking.

"Must close now. Best wishes to all over there, and again thanks for your welcome letter. "With kind regards, "Yours sincerely, "B. C. BUTTON."

FIELD EVENTS.

This season has proved the most successful yet in the history of the Club, and in winning the Field Events section of the Southern Amateur Athletic League, we have demonstrated that we have no superiors at all-round field events in the London area. We should probably be justified in going a step further and saying "in the whole country." The men who have brought this distinction to the Club, and have competed in one or more S.A.A.L. event on our behalf, are as follows: S. Jones, F. Grassau, G. H. Powell, F. Waters, C. A. Melchoir, K. Robertson, J. Dodd, J. Ginty, E. Byther, H. Vassall, E. W. Stannard, J. Holder, J. Tierney and W. Sutton. Final positions in the S.A.A.L. are :—

Performances in the "Harry Wright" Shield Competition have been on a higher level than in previous years, and the standard has risen to such an extent that the fifth man in this year's competition surpasses the winning percentage of two years ago.

The winner of the Shield and Club medal is J. Dodd, as shown below, and to him we extend our heartiest congratulations.

Generally, we have every reason to be pleased with the season's performances and results, and yet we realise there is considerable room for further improvement.

More men are required to interest themselves in one or other of the field events. Our greatest need for improvement is, at the moment, in the High and Long Jump events. True, we have Dodd in the former event, but it has not always been possible for him to assist us, and we have had to call on men whose performance in this event can only be described as moderate. Young Robertson is a bright hope here, however, and in a year or so should be a first-class jumper. Since Clemens moved to Manchester, we have been without an outstanding long jumper; although Gray proved in our last match of the season that he has great potentialities. It is surprising that more of our sprinters do not show an interest in this event. Scores of instances of first-class sprinters who are also good long jumpers are available; and it is an undeniable fact that almost any sprinter can jump at least 19 feet once he has discovered the right length of approach. Given a little natural ability and the determination to persevere, there is little doubt that we have several moderate sprinters who could transform themselves into good class long-jumpers, capable of gaining A.A.A. standard and their Club Honours Badge—and at the same time carry on with their sprinting activities. J. Skelley is one who shows promise in the pit, and we expect his long-jumping to improve as his sprinting powers—already well-developed—improve.

With a little improvement in these two events, and studied application by all to their particular events during the Winter, we should have no difficulty in retaining the S.A.A.L leadership, and in gaining valuable points in the various match events of next season.

We cannot conclude these notes without paying a tribute to the Sectional Captain, W. Sutton, for his excellent work at all times; and in particular, to his labours in arranging for our representatives to be at the various fixtures—no easy task, as a glance at the past season's fixture card will show. To him, as much as to anyone, is the credit of such a successful season due. It is good to know that he will again be at the head of affairs next season, with the capable assistance of George Powell; and, given the continued loyalty and support of our athletes, we are certain that the activities of the Field Events men will bring still more credit to the Club name.

THE BRIGHTON WALK.

Percy Wright

Ideal weather prevailed, and with an entry of 91, a great race for team and individual honours was expected. At the first stroke of seven o'clock the walkers were off, watched by the usual miscellaneous collection of onlookers, attendants, well-wishers, and early-morning workers.

Despite the absence of Tommy Green, who was nursing some slight strains, we had every reason to feel confident of success, for we had last year's winning team and, in addition, Fred Rickards.

V. W. Stone, of the Polytechnic Harriers, quickly went to the front and, completing five miles in 43 min. 44 secs., continued to draw away from the rest, passing "The Pied Bull" at Streatham with a twenty yards or so lead. Of our lads at this time the two Freds—Bentley and Rickards—were together in tenth position, Martin fourteenth, and Paul Khlopin well up. Approaching Croydon, Whitlock drew level with Stone, and shortly afterwards went ahead. Even at this early stage it was evident that Ludlow's record was in danger.

At the "Chequers," Horley, approaching half-distance, Whitlock had a good lead, about three minutes inside record. Stone was second, with Henderson (Worthing and District S.C.), third. Our boys were in the same position, except Paul, who was having trouble and had dropped a few places. Surrey W.C. just led in the team race, mainly owing to the backward position of our fourth man.

Reaching Albourne Green (42½ miles) wild rumours were flying around that Whitlock was fifteen minutes inside record, but subsequently this was found to be an exaggeration. Bentley was moving up and Martin had gained a few places but we still lacked a good fourth man. Surrey W. Club's team was cracking a little, but still held a few points lead. Khlopin, although taking a "hiding," was going much better and looked as if he might recover and win the race for us. This was not to be, however, and eventually he retired a few miles from the goal.

Meanwhile, T. W. Richardson (Woodford Green A.C.) was going through strongly, upsetting the prophets, and eventually finishing second.

Whitlock broke the tape well inside eight hours, having completed the course in the astounding time of 7 hours 53 mins. 50 seconds. Henderson came in third, Stone falling back to fourth place.

In the team race, Surrey W.C. beat us by six points to record their first victory for five years.

Heartiest congratulations to them and their gallant lads on a great victory!

Our first man to finish was Fred Bentley, who, although inside his last year's time, when he was third; this year could only finish eighth. This gives some idea of the great all-round improvement shown by the competitors.

The first four men all beat last year's winning time. Our second man home was Rickards (10th), and Martin (12th) next for us. Thus Fred Bentley again wins the "Belgrave Cup" and the Club Long Distance Championship, with Rickards and Martin in the minor positions.

Our scoring team was completed by Bill Holman, who did well; and would be a first-class distance walker had he the necessary time to train.

A word of praise to our lesser lights: Allen, Bicknell, Yorwarth, Laurie, Spooner, Wattam and Willis for their efforts.

Also, many thanks to the willing band of cyclists who did yeoman work as attendants. We could not possibly do without their services, and so, once again, our grateful thanks!

Our supporters on the bus also did great work, although we suspect certain of our racing men of using the bus merely as an excuse for a "pub-crawl," but who am I to criticise such people? (Wait for it!—Ed.).

Well, we have lost a walking trophy—the "E. Knott Memorial Shield"—but we are not likely to sit down and moan. Rather, we shall await next year's race with added interest, and, who knows, with keen training and young talent coming along, we may exchange defeat for victory—convincing victory at that!

CLUB MEETING ON JULY 29th.

We held a series of Club handicaps at Battersea Park on this evening, and the 100 Yards (under 16) proved a "teaser" for the Judges, who gave the placings with S. A. Bailey 1st, R. Dunford 2nd, and G. H. Aylmer 3rd.

The 100 Yards Senior was won by E. J. Johns, our Boxing Day winner, and he certainly seems to have settled down to the sprinting game. B. Saunders, a promising Junior, was second with R. G. Michael once again squeezing into third place.

A very poor entry was seen in the Veterans race and here H. E. Jackman proved the winner, with that fine old-stager, Charlie Cook, second; and another one-time stalwart in Dicky Best, third.

The 440 Yards was won in fine style by F. W. Nash, who had little to spare over the scratch man, Tommy Scrimshaw, who finished second. W. A. Carnie, our Poly--cum-Belgrave member, was third; which was due reward for his many efforts in club handicaps this season.

In the 1 Mile, our Juniors had matters all their own way and with E. S. Stimpson 1st, G. R. Chantler 2nd, and K. N. Wood 3rd, we have the reason why we are Southern Junior C.C. Champions, a title we intend to retain. With the help of the three members above, plus D. W. Martin, we have a team who should fear nobody.

An innovation was the Discus Throw handicap, and here we saw a good contest which resulted in C. A. Melchior being first, with that Field Event stalwart, George Powell, second; and none other than our own Jim Ginty third. At least, he was given out as finishing in that position, but we believe Jim had been pulling Bill Sutton's leg!

INTER-CLUB MATCH AT IMBER COURT.

On Saturday, August 10th, we had a match versus the Metropolitan Police A.C., Polytechnic H. and Surrey A.C. at Imber Court, the fine sports ground of the Metropolitan Police A.A. The weather was perfect and in the running events we did as well as expected, in view of the fact that our team, owing to holidays, was not at full strength. The Poly were in the same position as ourselves, and so it was left to the Police to provide the main opposition to Surrey A.C.

The latter fielded a very hot team, supplemented by E. Sears, the Essex County Champion, and R. L. Trapnell, the Achilles runner. Sears broke a ground record in winning the 880 Yards race in 1 min. 58 1-10 sec., with G. A. Smith, also of Surrey A.C., a close second.

The 1 Mile team race, 1600 Yards Relay, and 440 Yards Relay were all won by Surrey A.C., with the Metropolitan Police annexing the 660 Yards Relay.

R. L. Trapnell aslo broke a ground record when winning the Mile race in 4 min. 28 sec. and A. R. Shaw, in securing second place, is to be complimented on his fine running. We lost to Surrey A.C. by 2 points in the team scoring, the Surrey boys making a great last lap effort.

As regards the Field Events, we have a brighter side to the picture. Our men won the Javelin with E. H. Finneron, our young member from Guildford, putting in a fine throw of 158 ft., and G. H. Powell, with C. A. Melchior, completed the team which registered our only win of the meeting.

The final points worked out as follows: Surrey A.C. 47, Metropolitan Police A.C. 30, Belgrave H. 24, Polytechnic H. 19.

A swim in the widened River Ember, tea in the Pavilion, followed by dancing later, completed a very attractive fixture.

CLUB MEETING ON AUGUST 19th.

Entries for this meeting proved very poor, and the question of whether we continue our present programme of evening handicap meetings will have to be seriously considered by the Committee.

The 100 Yards handicap was won by R. W. Archer, a reward for his perseverance, with E. J. Johns following up his win by now securing second place, and F. Stower, another consistent trier, third.

Then came the "turn-up" of the season. Dicky Best, that "young" veteran, actually walked away with the 880 Yards handicap, and in no uncertain manner, as his time proved. A. A. Bird ran well to finish second, with Frank Webb, finding some of his old form, third.

The 2 Miles Run resulted in a popular win for Tom Carter. He ran one of his best races and his handicap time was the second fastest for this race. As he ran from virtual scratch, Tom has proved beyond doubt that he is our best distance runner on the track this season. A. T. A. Jordan, who finished second, was another instance of a member competing without being on the programme, with the result that the handicapping had to be done on the ground, and it is hoped that this practice will not be allowed in future races. These remarks do not, however, detract from Jordan's performance and we are pleased to see him among the prize-winners after being for so long on the fringe. J. Chantler, a brother of G. R., was third; and it is to be hoped that he continues to improve as well as his brother has done.

The Walk gave Frank Jarvis (575) his first Club prize; the minor places being filled by C. E. Churcher (160) and W. A. Treacher (60). The time of 13 min. 57 sec. was on the fast side, and Jarvis has undoubtedly shown great improvement during the Summer months.

SOUTHERN AMATEUR ATHLETIC LEAGUE (Division 1).

On August 17th, at the White City Stadium, was held the 1 Mile Medley Relay, and with Tommy Scrimshaw turning out (although coming straight back from holiday) we considered we had a good chance of winning this event. Eddie Pack ran the 440 yards stage and did well in handing over third behind Whittingham and Pallett. Bill's lack of speed (due to his team work in distance races; or, perhaps, the sickness which was to strike him down three days later), was soon apparent, but, sticking to his task in fine style, Bill handed over less than 20 yards behind the leaders. Fred White ran next and surprised even himself by the way he performed. As the leader he was chasing was none other than the Inter-Counties 440 yards champion, F. S. Whittingham, Fred ran marvellously in handing over as he did inches in front, to put us back into the lead again. Dick Michael very wisely let A. C. J. Poole, the Kent County Champion, go in front and then kept close on his shoulder. Coming to the Long Jump pit "Mike" let loose a link, and rounded the bend a few yards in front. Poole, however, was not finished with; and coming again with a glorious effort, he beat Michael by a few yards in a race that had pulsated with thrills throughout. I ought to mention that the Polytechnic H. finished a poor third, with Mitcham A.C. fourth, Herne Hill H. and South London H. fetching up in the rear.

We heartily congratulate Blackheath Harriers on taking the title from us. Their teams throughout the season have done excellently, and they are worthy winners of our division.

With Herne Hill only collecting two points to our five, we thus beat them by one point for the second place in the League table. Our old friends Mitcham A.C. were 4th, Polytechnic H. 5th, and South London H. 6th. Thus the S.L.H. will have to perform in a lower division this season, but knowing their lads I fully expect to see them back with us after a season's absence.

THE CLUB CHAMPIONSHIP MEETING.

The Club Track Championships were held at Battersea Park on August 3rd, and a good afternoon's sport was witnessed; with the bulk of the performances sound rather than showy.

The meeting commenced with the 100 Yards Boys under 15, and in the winner, S. R. Gardner, we appear to have a boy who should make a name for himself in the sprinting world. His time of 11 2-5 secs. equalled the club record, and we hope he will do even better next year. The other boys placed were E. Langrish and S. A. Bailey.

The 100 Yards Senior came next, and here the holder, C. H. Brodie had bad luck in that at the moment of starting, the back of one of his holes gave way, and Brodie almost fell. The race was won by F. Stroud, a previous holder of the title, with B. L. Bone at last justifying the good opinions of him, second, and Brodie a gallant third. The time was on the slow side but the track gave no assistance whatever; being very loose and, indeed, rather dangerous for sprinters, especially near the starting holes.

A good field lined up for the 880 Yards and they kept well together until 300 yards to go, when R. G. Michael jumped right away to win in handsome fashion. Harry Shields battled his way into second place in his own indomitable style; and A. A. Bird finished very fast to gain third place. The time of 2 min. 1 1-5 sec. was distinctly creditable, and we trust that Michael will make every effort to get inside that magical two minutes next season.

The Senior 220 Yards saw the completion of a sprint double by F. Stroud. Eddie Pack made a game effort but could never head his opponent; and Stroud's strength told in a close finish. E. L. Kealey was third.

Then came the Junior (under 18) 220 Yards race, and here we have pleasure in proclaiming the unearthing of a most promising young sprinter, B. Saunders, to wit. He ran clean away from his field, and his time of 24 secs. equals the third best time for this event. C. E. Jones and G. Crilly, who were second and third, are also to be placed in the highly promising class, both being lads who are certain to improve if they stick to regular training methods.

The 1 Mile came next, and this is always considered by most people to be the tit-bit of the meeting. J. Parker, our new member from Brighton, was an entrant, and right from the start his huge stride took him into the lead. Tom Carter made a game effort to hang on but was plainly feeling it at the bell. Pat Parker, however, kept to his seemingly monotonous stride (in fact, his three laps hardly varied in time); and it was obvious that unless someone produced a startling last lap effort, Pat would win the race at his first time of competing. Albert Shaw, however, had other ideas, and he set about the task of overhauling the leader. He decreased the distance, but on reaching the dressing room, Parker still had sufficient lead to keep his opponent at a respectable distance, and won the race in 4 min. 32 sec.—time which equals the Club record. Tom Carter still had enough strength left to hold on to his forward position, and finished close behind Shaw in third place.

It is doubtful whether any Club Championship in the world has come up to that which we saw in the 2 Miles Walk. Don Brown retained his title, as was generally expected, but Bill Treacher (2nd), Len Fletcher (3rd) and Harry Churcher (4th) all finished inside 14 mins. 26 secs. Four men inside a time which has won the A.A.A. Championship—and on a track which was far from perfect! Truly a splendid showing! This excellence extended to the other competitors, and we may look back upon this race with pride, both for the performances and the size of the entry.

The 440 Yards final concluded the meeting; and, although we wish to give every credit to R. G. Michael for accomplishing a fine double win, it seemed that in attempting the 220 yards and 440 yards, E. H. Pack fell between two stools. Whereas the eventual outcome of his efforts was two second places, if he had concentrated on the longer distance he might easily be now a Club title holder. H. Jones, who finished third, is, we hope, going to get down to it seriously next season. With more concentration on a particular distance, rather than flirting with them all, he should be a danger to the best in the Club.

Skelly doing even better than previously with a fine actual jump of 19ft. 6ins., which secured him third prize in the handicap.

FINAL CLUB TRACK MEETING.

September 14th.

This was held at Battersea Park on Saturday, September 14th, and a torrential downpour just before the meeting was due to commence rendered the track on the heavy side. We had a triangular match versus the Metropolitan Police A.C. and Polytechnic Harriers, and the 100 Yards match was the first event.

Poly had A. W. Green, the Midland Champion, out for them; while we were very pleased to welcome our member, E. Gray, who is the Hants County 100 and 220 yds. Champion. Gray ran finely to beat Green in 10 3-10 secs. and is without doubt going to prove a decided acquisition to our sprinting strength.

In the 1 Mile, E. J. Hengle, the Police 880 yds. Champion, ran a well judged race to beat our men, L. J. Cohen and A. R. Shaw, while W. D. Symes (who is also a member of the "Bels") filled 4th place for the Police.

Then came the 220 Yards, and here W. W. Pickard, of the Police, narrowly beat A. W. Green, with E. Gray a good third.

E. J. Hengle completed a good double by winning the 440 Yards in which Fred White and Hugh Jones were second and third for us.

The last of the running events was the 1 Mile Medley Relay, and A. A. Bird ran splendidly in beating A. H. Pettet, of the Police, over the half-mile stage; and then Michael further increased our lead by running a fine quarter. Les Bennett did the 220, and kept his advantage; so that when Gray went off for the final 220 he had a comfortable lead. Pickard, of the Police, made a strong effort but our man won easily in quite good time.

As regards the Field Events, E. Gray, a positive glutton for work, won the Long Jump with a fine leap of 20ft. 10ins.

F. Grassau credited us with first place in the Shot Putt with 38ft. 6¾in.; but in the absence of Finneron, and with George Powell competing under difficulties, second and fourth was the best we could manage in the Javelin.

The High Jump was well beyond the reach of our youngsters, but we hope they will not be discouraged and thank them for turning out as they do.

In the Discus Throw, F. Grassau was second to D. L. Grigg, the Police Champion ,with S. Jones filling fourth place.

Points were scored 5, 3, 2, 1 for the first four places in each event (except the Relay, which was 4, 2, 1) and the final points scored were: Metropolitan Police A.C. 45, Belgrave H. 39, Polytechnic H. 22.

Parker go and worked up to his man gradually. When he did catch his rival he soon went in front, and Pat was well beaten long before the finish. He managed to retain his hold on second place, and Arthur Allum and Syd Ring had a fine tussle for third position, with Allum proving the speedier when they reached the home straight. Arthur Penny, our Empire Games 6 Miles Champion, made his first appearance of the season after his long enforced rest, and ran very wisely in qualifying for 2nd Class Standard. Even so, he finished 5th, with Cecil Mains, who has also been in hospital following an accident, 6th, and G. R. Chantler also gaining the standard awards. The time of 15 min. 19 2-5 sec. was, considering the conditions underfoot, very good; and on this form Tom should have a very successful Winter season.

It is a great pity that his schoolmaster's duties take up so much of his time during the Summer. We hope that next year he may see his way clear to turn out more often.

Goold (400), and G. H. Boddington (400), all M.P.A.A. men as well as very loyal Belgrave Harriers, filled the first three places. They are all rapidly improving walkers, and the struggle for places in our Championship teams during the Winter should be very interesting.

THE THREE MILES ROAD RACE.

By J. E. Flower.

Again we had a fine day for the annual road race, and all the spectators looked forward to a fast race. Sure enough, we got it!

Our old friend, George Tyrer, was away first at a nice, steady pace. Seven minutes later Arthur Penny started in pursuit of a large field of runners, looking as if he meant to catch them all.

Were my eyes deceiving me or was it a tree I saw moving slowly? No! it was Ben—better known to the boys as "King Konk."

Ben was using his head and saving his legs by taking a short cut. He also informed me that he was feeling very seedy (but not politely as I have put it). Tough guy, he calls himself!

Getting back to the race, J. C. Edwards had put in some very fine running. Edward's effort was a very fine one; and Chamberlain's running, which took him into third place, is full of promise, and shows that we have yet another junior of more than average ability.

T. C. Carter must be congratulated on doing the fastest time for the day, and it is very pleasing to see Arthur Penny running so well again.

FORTHCOMING CROSS-COUNTRY FIXTURES.

E. A. Duffett, Asst. Hon. Sec.

The season has already opened and from the numbers turning up at Wimbledon every Saturday, and from the entry in our Road Race Handicap on October 5th, it would appear that we shall have a most successful season in every respect. The evening runs on Tuesday and Thursday from the "Rising Sun," Ebury Bridge Road, which is quite near Victoria, are not, however, so well attended as in the past. Maybe it is because the season is yet young, but I would like to point out to members, new ones especially, that here is a fine opportunity of becoming better acquainted with your fellow-members. The atmosphere, to say the least, is most congenial and the runs which take place on the adjoining roads and round Rotten Row, in Hyde Park, are certain to be enjoyed. Packs to suit all are sent out in charge of an experienced runner, and all you need is your ordinary running togs plus rubber shoes. If you can afford proper road shoes so much the better, and it is preferable to wear a sweater, (white if you can manage it), as the winter evenings are apt to strike cold until you get warmed up. The packs go out about 8 p.m. and you can arrive at the "Rising Sun" any time after 7 o'clock, so as to make sure you will be ready when the pack leader shouts "All out."

After these few remarks, we will now give you a little idea of our coming fixtures, as per fixture card, and if you have not yet received one of these let the Gen. Hon. Sec. know at once so that he can remedy it.

On November 9th we have the first of the many Open racing fixtures. This is the South of Thames "Junior" team race, which will take place from the Grandstand, Epsom Downs. The word "Junior" does not apply to age but to ability. All those who have scored for any of our Senior teams are automatically barred. We usually enter three or four teams and all members who are selected to compete for the Club will be advised.

On November 16th we have a double-barrelled fixture at Imber Court, the headquarters of the Metropolitan Police A.A. Both our runners and walkers will be in opposition to the might of the Police, and I trust our members will turn up and sample the hospitality of the Force.

On November 23rd we shall have a fixture with Ashcombe A.C. and Ealing Harriers at Wimbledon. Our team will be selected (with due regard to the strength of the opposition) and members advised accordingly.

November 30th. The day of the Club Open Walk at Wimbledon, and in view of the large numbers competing I wish to point out that NO runners will be allowed to change at the Hall on this day. We trust our runners will appreciate that every inch of space is required to house the walking competitors and so we have accordingly arranged an away fixture for them. This will be held at the Village Hall, Warlingham, the quarters of the Croydon Harriers. Mitcham A.C. and Epsom H. will also be there and packs to suit all will go out over the surrounding country, which should prove very interesting, and probably a little different from that we encounter week by week at Wimbledon. There are frequent Southern Railway trains to Warlingham and if any members wish to have further particulars, I shall be pleased to advise them.

The Surrey County Youths' Cross Country race will be held on December 7th, from the headquarters of the Rothery A.C. at Redhill. All members possessing Surrey qualifications, and who are under 18 years of age, are asked to get in touch with our Junior Captain, R. J. Savage, who is always in attendance at Wimbledon. On the same day we have our old friends, Herne Hill Harriers, paying us a visit for a training spin with our lads.

The first of the Club Cross Country Championships will be held at Wimbledon on December 14th, when the 7 Miles race for the "Blackstaffe Shield" (Holder, A. W. Penny) will be decided. There is a sealed handicap open to all Club members in conjunction with the championship and I hope they will support this. After the race we shall proceed to lay the table for Tea, and to see some of our lads feeding is really better than a visit to the —— well, I'll let you use your imagination there! After tea the tables are cleared and the floor is all set for dancing, which goes on until 11 p.m. You can bring as many friends as you like, and ladies are doubly welcome. My wife does not read the Gazette, so I feel on safe ground in saying this!

On the 21st December we have our Club Junior Cross Country Championship for the "Savage Shield" (Holder, G. R. Chantler). This is held over our 5 miles course and is open to members who are under 21 years of age on day of race. The same day we are entertaining the Metropolitan Police A.C., and St. George's Services S.C., and here is a good opportunity for those who have not as yet sampled any of our racing fare.

On Boxing Day, December 26th, we hold our annual Track meeting at Battersea Park. This commences at 11 a.m. and the races held are: 120 Yards Handicap and a 2 Miles Running Handicap. Last year the entries for the sprint were very poor, so much so that it was a moot point as to whether we continued this fixture, and I trust that on this occasion our members will turn out in good numbers and so prove to the Committee that they do appreciate the chance that is given them during the Christmastide festivities. Entries (1/- each event) should be sent to me at least a week before date of meeting, for which many thanks.

This brings us to the end of 1935 and the fixtures for 1936 include the County Cross Country Championships on January 4th. The Surrey race is being held at Epsom Downs; but at the time of writing, the venue for the Middlesex race has not been decided upon, although Colindale is a possibility.

A week later the Surrey County Junior C.C. Championship will be held from St. George's Services S.C. headquarters at Raynes Park, and I hope our boys will make every endeavour to secure their first win in this race.

Another good move is the bringing forward of the Inter-Counties C.C. Championship, which will be held on January 18th. This race has usually been the "Cinderella" of the cross-country season, and I hope that the alteration now made will become a permanent one.

We then come to the Club 10 Miles C.C. Championship for the "Macher Shield" (Holder, H. E. Footer), which will be held on January 25th, and by then our teams for the Open Championships will have begun to take shape and our task of defending our title of English Cross Country champions will have become the sacred duty of each one of our cross-country men. Knowing them, I am confident of their ability to do this successfully.

WITH THE WALKERS.

On the occasion of our Meeting at Battersea Park, on Saturday, September 14th, we co-operated with Woodford Green A.C. in promoting an attempt on the World's 5,000 metres Walking Record by A. A. Cooper.

Special arrangements were made to have the track in really first-class condition, but these efforts were largely negatived by a torrential shower of rain just as proceedings were about to begin.

The start was delayed, but after a while it was found possible to stage the event, and Bert Cooper was able to record new World's figures for the distance. Times were as follows: 1 mile (6 min. 54 sec.), 2 miles (14 min. 1 3-5 sec.), 3 miles (21 min. 10 3-5 sec.) and 5,000 metres (21 min. 52 52 2-5 sec.). The best figures previously recorded for this distance were 21 min. 59 sec. by A. Schwab, in May, 1931.

Congratulations to Cooper upon his fine effort, and in overcoming terrible weather conditions. P. G. Wright and C. F. D. Sutton, of ours, assisted in the event at short notice.

THE BELGRAVE STROLLERS.

Hundred Per Cent.

By Hundred Per Cent.

Every Sunday (sometimes even overnight on Saturday) they sally forth in search of the beauties of Nature; the quest of health, the wind on the heath (to borrow a phrase), and all that. Every Sunday afternoon they look about them, seeking to throw down the gauntlet to some unsuspecting tradesman who is rash enough to advertise an ad lib tea. It's a challenge not to be resisted, and like most challenges the Belgrave lads seem to have a way of coming off best! Faith may be able to move mountains, but it's got nothing on Harry Shields. It is not generally known that he had an operation recently which has eliminated his appendix, and increased his scope considerably.

He captains my team versus "The Rest of the World" in "The Grub Stakes." My vice-captain would be Mick Walker (who tends to overtrain) and the team completed by the inclusion of Cecil Penny (ranking equal 2, and on his day equal to two); and Big Ben May, who will improve as he grows. This team is in a class by itself, and the introduction of reserves would weaken it considerably; but Pat Parker, A. A. Bird and G. S. Doubleday (the latter specialising in boiled eggs) are training on rapidly. The team is essentially a strolling side. No home fixtures can be entertained!

It is an interesting sight (albeit tinged with pathos) to observe a victimised caterer spellbound; torn between pleasure at seeing the sincere appreciation accorded his wares, and pain at the thought of rapidly approaching bankruptcy!

Whether these Sunday outings consist of strolls between meals, or meals between strolls, I cannot presume to say with any certitude; but how Napoleon would have rejoiced in such followers of his "army marches on its stomach" dictum!

The humorous element is supplied by all at some time or the other; but without a doubt, the most notorious offender, especially in the verbal fields, is Bert Lindsey. He is awarded the prize for The Worst Pun of the Month—a cellophane-wrapped bottle of prussic acid. It's a walk-over! Say what you will, he can always cap it!

And talking of caps, Bert has a life-long friend which he sometimes uses to keep his hair from becoming untidy. I believe this originally came from Newington Butts, and I can well understand that thoroughfare having its doubts!

To turn to more pleasant subjects, Charlie Churcher (if one excludes oneself) must be acknowledged as The Most Gentlemanly Stroller and is duly gazetted to receive The Purple Garter of the Belgravian Order of Chivalry (1st and 3rd class, Sundays only); and is permitted from to-day to wear his sideboards one inch longer.

Musically, Bird (the Cheerful Sparrow) is the chief provider. His favourites are from Gilbert and Sullivan (especially the one about the General); and he usually ends his recital with Chopin's "Dead March," possibly out of respect to the tunes he has murdered.

Some of the gang remain unmentioned, but just come along one week-end, and hear them speak up for themselves!

THE ANNUAL GENERAL MEETING.

A good crowd of members assembled at "The Rising Sun" on September 19th for the Annual General Meeting. The Hon. Secretary read the minutes of the last A.G.M., which were duly confirmed; and was then called upon for his Annual Report.

He rapidly gave an account of the past year's successes and progress; and his report was unanimously accepted. The Hon. Treasurer followed with his financial statement, which disclosed an excess of expenditure over income, despite careful management. After a few questions had been satisfactorily answered, the report was adopted.

A proposal from the Committee to honour W. J. Fish and A. A. Harley by making them Life Members was unanimously agreed; and they made suitable response.

The business of Election of Officers for the ensuing year then occupied the Meeting's attention. Mr. J. R. McKay proposed from the Chair, that Mr. Oscar Horwood should be the new President of the Club; and told the assembled members of the years of service rendered by Mr. Horwood in many capacities. A. A. Harley seconded, and Mr. Horwood was duly elected, and installed in the Chair. He acknowledged the applause of the meeting, and made an especial appeal to the Junior members to carry on the good work of the Seniors.

Other officials were then elected as per the Club handbook, now in the possession of all members.

Several suggestions were made under the heading of "Other Business," and were passed to the new Committee for their consideration.

THE OPENING WALK.

The usual 5 miles Handicap which brings the realities of another busy imminent Winter season home to us, was held on October 5th; and attracted a fine entry of 26. Dick Northover was first away, followed 45 seconds later by Don Heath, and then came the others with varying allowances, until Don Brown left the post of honour.

As last year, Harry Churcher again showed that he requires practically no preparation (unlike most of us who have to train for months to achieve something like fitness!) and won fairly easily, recording fastest time in doing so. Colin Sutton won his first prize with us during his three years of membership; and now that he has settled down to training again he should claim his place in our "Junior" team. Jack Rutland took third place and continues to improve. Len Fletcher was noticed moving in something like his old style; and two newcomers in S. R. Wood and W. C. True experienced their first Club race and should develop into useful performers.

BUILDING FUND NEWS.

T. Cotton

By T. Cotton.

There is one thing puzzling me greatly just at present, and that is where do Belgrave Building Bricks go after being issued? Since September, 1934, we have handed bricks out in plenty, and were inclined to pat ourselves on the back, until we found the discrepancy between exports and imports. Sometimes, very rarely, our young lives have been made joyous by a box suddenly finding its way back. This, as I have already said, very rarely. We can only draw mental pictures of bright yellow boxes used to support aspidasras, or gold-fish bowls; and are considering having some done in pink, or light blue, for the more dainty type of member. Really, we should be hearing more about these bricks. The Supporters' Club scheme is a great success, but one feels that the original plan of the "Building Fund" was so sound that it is disappointing to note the poor efforts made with these bricks.

Concerning pinboards, I shall welcome payment for all those completed. We still have a few of the larger boards on hand. Please note that no member can secure more than one "600" board at a time.

As everybody seems to know, our Supporters' Club is a great success. Some of the figures I have heard regarding profits have been amazing (especially to Frank Elson and myself, who are responsible for the scheme), but to be candid, we are very delighted with the support given. There are some ways in which you can help Frank and myself a great deal. You know by now that one or other of us is always present on Tuesday and Thursday evenings at "The Rising Sun"; at Wimbledon on Saturdays; and at Battersea Park on Sunday mornings. Please try and square all accounts as quickly as possible, so that we can complete our accounts speedily. Then there is another important point being neglected by some. It is essential that we have the name and address of every member of the Supporters' Club on our books. If you have any outstanding names and addresses, please let me have them immediately. To be of full value, these details should be legible.

One hopes after the fine efforts made by all just recently, and with the day when we really can make a move drawing nearer, that many will remain to enjoy the advantages of our our headquarters. I cannot help thinking that a bachelor really does perhaps get more out of such things and I must give warning to my friend the Editor. During our holidays I exerted every possible effort to keep him from the wiles and trickery of women. On board the boat going over I did have a spot of bother over one lady who was greatly impressed by his method of leaning over the rail when sea-sick, but it passed. For two whole weeks did I exert all my known good (?) influence, and many the good talking-to I gave him.

Then, gentle reader, then when we have returned with unblemished reputations, I stroll into the "Swan" on the night of our first Social, and behold a sight that tears my heart with pain. The Hon. Editor, plus cigarette and beer, standing by the piano, crooning! Oh, Alfred! beware!

[Editorial Interpolation.—Seeing that the above comes from the pen of our old friend, Tom Cotton, it is hardly necessary for me to make reply. You all know what his remarks are worth. (Thank you, Bert!)

But what base ingratitude this youngster shows to one who has taken him by the hand for the past five years; and safely seen him through his holidays!

Were I given to washing dirty Cotton linen (something wrong here!) in public; never again would waters run clear and undefiled. And so, I refrain, conscious of the old adage, " . . . . where angels fear to tread"]

A new "Building Fund" Sub-Committee has been elected and consists of A. E. Taylor, A. A. Harley (the same), H. Parker, E. Gordon, F. H. Elson and yours truly; and, of course, the President, Gen. Hon. Secretary and Hon. Treasurer, ex officio. Frank and I have been specially placed on this Sub-Committee because of our known ability for being cursed by people who cannot obtain sufficient membership cards. So don't mind us, carry on cursing!

IMPORTANT !

At the Special General Meeting held at "The Rising Sun" on October 31st, after discussion, the proposal of the Committee to increase subscriptions to 8/- per annum for Senior members; 4/- per annum for Junior members (under 18); and 1/- per annum for Boys (under 15); was approved, and becomes operative immediately. Arising from the above, all members are asked to settle their liabilities for past "Belgravians" immediately, and so enable the books to be closed. A considerable amount is outstanding and prompt settlement would be appreciated.
