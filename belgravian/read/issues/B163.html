EDITORIAL

"But the waiting time, my brothers, Is the hardest time of all"

Well, it took several seasons to come about, but it was all the sweeter for waiting. The trophy win we needed so badly came our way on July 8th at Wimbledon Park when we won one of the seasons premier contests - the BROCKMAN TROPHY.

The sucess reminded us of the Presidential Address when Frank Jarvis said "..... plans could be made now by the track and field sections to make a considered and determined on-slaught on the trophy meetings during the summer of 1967. I feel convinced that if the Club were to win ONE trophy, the victory would lead to others". How right he was; and how well our track and field secretaries responded. In turn, the right spirit permeated through our captain and coaches and we went into that match with only two defeats behind us this season. Morale was high and we proved to be the best of the twelve top teams invited, by a comfortable margin of 13 pts.

Success breeds success and, sure enough, we now have three further trophies to our credit. Belgrave took part in eighteen fixtures this season and won no less than sixteen a record deserving of the highest praise and one that reflects credit on every single member of our track and field sections.

Our successes will not go un-noticed and we have every hope that Polytechnic Harriers will consider us worthy of re-entry into their Kinnaird Trophy meeting next year - a contest that has been dubbed the unofficial club championship of Great Britain.

The name of our revered Past-President Harry Evans, who died earlier this year after 60 years of Club service, will live on. Harry's sons and daughter contributed a cup to the Club as a perpetual award for an event to be agreed by your Committee. We are pleased to announce that the "Harry Evans" Cup will be awarded to the individual winner of our oldest annual promotion - the Open 7 Miles Walk. In this way Harry's unfailing presence at walking fixtures will be continued in spirit and his name regularly brought before the walking fraternity. The 48th "Open" will be held from Belgrave Hall in November - it would be fitting if a Belgravian's name was the first to be inscribed on this important cup.

Frank JarvisHarry Evans

HON SEC'S NOTES

A.A.H.

There is a proposal for increased Club affiliation fees to be discussed at the Annual General Meeting of the AAA to be held in October and the governing body hope this will find acceptance and so go dome way towards assisting finance problems.

This proposal has taken the place of the various registration-of-athletes schemes which were mooted at one time, but which it is now generally agreed would be too complicated in administration, cause serious problems in Club organisation, and carry an impossibly high expense/income ratio.

If passed, we shall be called upon to pay approximately five times our present affiliation fee, and this obviously means that we all, as individuals, must expect to pay a little more on this account, in addition to any extra income required to run the Club successfully and efficiently in the inflationary world we live in.

* * * * *

Sexton, without any doubt at all. Harry Hare had already raised this point; but now Charlie Speechley, eighty years of age next January and a member since 1919, confirms that No.23 is C.P. A photograph has negligible value without key information; and I would suggest that everyone should form the habit of noting lightly at the back of photographs the date and, detail-----for the benefit of posterity!

A PRESIDENT'S Adieu

Frank Jarvis, President

As this Belgravian year of 1966/1967 comes to an end, I wish to convey to every member of the Belgrave Harriers my sincere and deep-felt thanks for the support accorded to me as your President.

When, on the 21st September of last year, I was elected to that Office, I was concerned whether or not I would be able to follow and uphold the position so well established by the previous holders of the Presidency.

To be a President of such a Club as the Belgrave Harriers demands qualities from a man of which he may not be aware. In accepting the Office - an Office which is highly esteemed by other Associations- I was aware of my shortcomings; this awareness made me appeal to you all for your co-operation and assistance as without this help a President is helpless.

The performances of the Belgrave Harriers during this year are now written in the history of athletics. What is not written is the spirit and determination displayed by the athletes on behalf of the Club.

An indication of this Club spirit has been shown to the public by the gaining of three Athletic Trophies ( up to the time of writing to you). I know that the cancellation of ' The Sward Trophy' meeting upset certain plans to win the ' 'Sward' . So it was that the 'Jack Lewis' trophy was the first to be gained. This success was followed by winning ' The Brockman Trophy', the pleasure in the hearts of all of the officials and athletes when this victory was announced can be imagined. Then to show that these victories were a part of a pre-conceived plan, the Club won the ' Advertiser'(Carshalton) trophy. These successes indicate the dormant strength in depth now developing in the Club. The near-misses in the National Cross-Country C'ships. at Norwich and the National Road Relay C'ship at Leicester( but, in the Relay, the Belgrave Harriers would be the first to accord tribute to R. Taylor for his running on that day) together with the Club's success in gaining the 50 kilos. Race Walking C'ship must be a source of satisfaction to all of the officials and athletes concerned and of pride to all members.

All of these successes were gained because of the indomitable team spirit, and that a Belgravian does his best for the Club until the finishing line is reached.

A WORD FROM YOUR TREASURER

Hon.Treasurer.

As you will see from the notice for the A.G.M. it is proposed to alter Rule 11 which concerns annual subscriptions.

I am sure all members of the Club realize the necessity of this action which is brought about by the increased Affiliation fees to Associations, increased costs of printing, stationery, engraving, etc. Belgrave Hall is also a liability which, owing to its age, is in constant need of repair.

As pointed out at the A.G.M. last year, for each £1 received the Club paid out £1.2.4d. This position has not improved, we must pay our way and we cannot call on reserves to subsidize our Athletics.

Although I know the raising of subscriptions will help, it will also be necessary for all members to support any Club Fund Raising Schemes that are proposed.

We all know that Belgrave is the Best Athletic Club in the country and we to ensure that we remain in a position of being able to send teams to all the fixtures and to continue to promote our very popular Open Events.

Its up to all of you to do your best for the Club.

WALKING

P. D.

As if in reply to the President's electoral speech our walkers presented him with the National 50 Kilometres team championship. Once again Belgrave showed that when conditions get tough it is the other clubs who retire. A significant factor in all the RWA championships this year has been the comparatively high scores which have still won the top three positions. Although this does reflect the way talent is more evenly spread among the clubs it also means that it is considerably easier to win the titles. Some years ago when the national scene was dominated by two or three clubs winning teams could almost be considered a closed shop but now this is no longer true which makes it all the more important for our middlemen to be prepared to shoulder this responsibility when necessary. Both John Keown and Bernard Eglinton have backed up our recognised distance men but youth is not on their side and I am sure that they would be as pleased as the rest of us to be forced out by a rising tide of enthusiasm from below.

W.H. Hare

In his first two articles, W.H. Hare (Past-President, former Hon. The photograph was taken in Denmark Road just before the start of the Club walking championship of 1923. This was the 5th contest for the R.Murphy Shield, instituted in 1916, for the trophy presented to the Club by 'Dick Murphy, who appears here (No. 17.)

The inaugural race was over a 6 mile course and was won by C.McLellan, a Scots Guardsman at that time. Wounded in France, he returned to Scotland and later won their National Championship and set up a number of individual records. Owing to worsening war time conditions no further race was held until 1920, when the distance was increased to 9 miles and won by R.W. Ricketts (No. 28), who repeated his win in 1921.

With new members now coming along, the 1922 race was won by H.F. King (No. 14) who went on to win the R.W.A. 'Junior' Championship at Regents Park; leading the Club to their second team success in this event. Harold retained the Club championship in 1923 and again in 1924 (now standardised at 10 miles) before taking up an appointment on the Gold Coast of Africa. He returned to collect more 'gold' by winning once more, in 1927.

Of those in the photo not competing, a very notable personality is J. Sawyer (No. 1). The father of 4 members, of whom two were casualties of the war, he gave to the Club the J.G. Sawyer and the A.J. Sawyer Memorial Cups that remain our oldest track trophies.

Joe was a regular attendant and helper in those early days, and was relied upon as the unofficial Clerk of Course; a familiar figure with his yardstick made in the form of a giant pair of dividers. It was the late summer of 1920 that I made my first contact, before coming an active participant in Club proceedings. Attending Battersea Park to watch a 2 Mile Walk Handicap, in which a business colleague was competing, I found the course pegged out on an open field situated between the tennis courts and the S.W. main gateway. To my critical, if inexperienced eyes, the going looked 'distinctly rough' and recalled that line of the poet Gray "Where heaves the turf in many a mouldering heap". It was certainly a 'graveyard' for fast and fair walking, and this may have accounted for the lack of progress that the backmarkers made towards the middlemen who dominated the final placings.

There was no lack of spectators who, attracted by the sport provided, gathered in force both outside and inside the circuit. Inevitably, the crowd closed in, and in the closing stages of the race the competitors found themselves walking in a steadily narrowing funnel. ....Cont'd on page 23.

All good things, however, come to an end; much to the relief of the Parkkeeper deputed to maintain order. Joe collected his flags and marking pegs and soon "The curfew tolled the knell of parting day" - Closing time.

So a page of history was turned; the cinder track in its original form, 3 laps to the mile, was opened for use in 1923.

JULY 8th - WIMBLEDON PARK

In 1965 we were 8th of the twelve clubs that compete annually for Herne Hill Harrier's "H.G.Brockman"Trophy with a measly 12 points. 1966 saw us improve to 5th with 53 points and now in 1967 we have beaten all the top clubs in the London area to win the event with 86 points. A magnificent performance!

The afternoon started not too hopefully for us when in the first track final - the '6' - an injured Trevor Hart retired after a quarter of the distance had been covered. Another blow came when Tony Brooks was unable to qualify for the 440yd final although recording a time that would have almost won the other heat. However, the 'Field Boys' were already making up for the unexpected loss of points - Bill Couzens making the final six in the Hammer and Nicholas Head, in great shape, heaving the discus 123'11" for 4th place (a sandwich in his non-throwing hand)

The standard in the sprints was amazing, both winning times being faster than those of the Invitation events put on for the visiting South African team. John Mitchell clocked 9.9 to qualify for the final of the '100' which was eventually won by Bob Frith (Poly) with 9.7 into a stiffish wind. John was again timed at 9.9 (the fourth time this year) for 4th place. Mike Hauck (TVH) took the furlong in 21.6 with 'Our Jim' Vivian well ahead of the rest in 22.0.

The 1500 metres was a pre-AAA Champs warm up for John Thresher and he absolutely romped away from a field including Hugh Pullan (SLH), two S.African Internationals and arch rival Bob Holt (Herc). Back on the field the 'Bels' were still steadily notching up the points. Derek Wade made 2nd in the Long Jump, Denzil Winsborrow vaulted into 4th place despite a treacherous wind that removed the bar without any help from the competitors, and Barry Townsend recorded his best marks of the season with a 16.5 High Hurdles effort and a 5'11" High Jump.

We were now equal 3rd with TVH in the team result behind Surrey AC and Blackheath H but now came two events that drastically altered the points situation. On one of his last throws in his speciality, the Javelin, Nick Head launched a mighty throw of 211'3" - a Club Record and four feet clear of any other thrower. Bill Couzens was sent into ecstasies,and as Peter Whewell ground away the opposition in the 3 miles to knock 20 secs off his best and finish 2nd only to Tim Johnston (Portsmouth) who wasn't scoring anyway, even announcer Bill Lucas had to chime in over the'mike' that the 'Bels' had taken the lead.

Only the Steeplechase and the 4 x 440yds to go. An unknown Surrey man forced his way into the lead in a do or die effort for his club but it was soon the 'new style' Alan Black forging away from the field at every barrier with even Dai Davies (TVH) hard pressed. Alan hammered away and was rewarded with a best ever of 9:13.2. It looks as if John Bicourt is not going to have things all his own way. The relay was unfortunately divided into two separate races, the result being decided on times. TVH and Hercules were well clear in the first heat and it was a great pity that we had not been drawn with them for the opposition in our own race was almost nil. Tony Brooks gave us a good lead on the first leg which was steadily improved upon by John Mitchell, Chris Martin and Jim Vivian and we finished streets ahead in our race but 3rd overall in 3:24.5 our best time for ages.

The 'Brockman' was in our hands again after 11 years. A happy Bob Taylor was presented with the 'goodies' by the Herne Hill officials and it's the 'Advertiser' and 'Boughey' trophies next on the list!

A FAIRY TALE .... by Enid Blighted

Enid Blighted

Once upon a time there were two gnomes. They lived in a big forest. The part of the forest where they lived was called the Bell Glade. The two little gnomes had a special job. You see, because not many of the other fairies and elves could write, our two little friends used to collect all the news of what happened in the Bell Glade and write it all down on big leaves and give it to all their friends. The fairies and elves in the glade looked forward to getting the news and would read it excitedly to see if their names were mentioned.

The two gnomes worked very hard. But as time went by the fairies and elves in the glade did not get so excited when they received the leaves with all the news written on them. 'Oh Dear' said the first gnome, whose name was Eddie Tore 'no-one gets excited by our leaves anymore. What are we to do?' 'I know', said his friend, who was called Ass Ed,'Instead of just writing news on our leaves, everyone's heard it anyway, let's give out extra leaves with jokes and things like that.'

They both thought this was a splendid idea so they asked another gnome whose name was Charlemagne to write some jokes. Charlemagne was a very witty gnome who spent all his time chasing butterflies and moths round the glade. But he found time to write some jokes and when the next leaves were given out everyone laughed and said'Aren't we lucky to have such clever gnomes in our glade. The fairies in other glades haven't got such good news-leaves.'

But the King Elf and some of his friends were angry because they thought that the fairies and elves shouldn't laugh at the affairs of the glade, even if they were sometimes funny. So they called a meeting of all the important folk in the glade and the important fold said that the two gnomes were very naughty and in future they must treat their work more seriously.

Of course this made the two gnomes sad because they had only been trying to cheer other people up. They were so sad that they packed their bags and ran away to another glade. So from that time on the fairies and elves of the Bell-Glade didn't get any news at all, and, as the King Elf said, 'No news is good news.'

The moral of this story is - Don't worry, you can't please all the people all the time.

IT'S FAIRCLOUGH AGAIN

Battersea Park---August 9th

Running in his most relaxed and confident manner, Tony Fairclough shook off all the opposition just after half way to become our 6 mile champion for 1967.

Don Maclean may have been last to arrive at the track but the momentum of his speedy change and dash to the start carried him into a first lap lead, closely tailed by the barefooted Trevor Hart. The latter towed the field through the next two laps at a fairly leisurely pace until Gerry North, the holder, livened up the pace with a burst to the front. Laurie O'Hara tagged him closely but Fairclough was nearly caught napping and took half a lap to close up again, whilst Dick Piotrowski failed to get out of the ruck in time to maintain contact.

There were many surprised faces 2½ laps later when Laurie and Tony moved away from Gerry. The two leaders looked extremely comfortable, running shoulder to shoulder, with Fairclough in the second lane. The remainder did not look too good, except perhaps Piotrowski who also passed Gerry a lap later. The leaders passed through 2 miles in 9:40 with Dick just 6 seconds down, followed by Hart and North (9:54) and Dance (10:06).

The first three steadily drew away and the lapping commenced---an event that called for the full concentration of six or more officials, for some of the 27 runners were to be lapped four times before the end.

Three miles saw the leading pair through in 14:43 with Dick now 15 seconds back, followed by Hart (15:06), North (15:17), Dance (15:23), Mann (15:26), Steer (15:32) and Hilliar (15:34). Just one lap later Fairclough decided to go it alone and went into a 2 second lead, but Laurie hung on well and 5 laps later was still only 4 seconds in arrears. Meanwhile Hart had retired ("the pace was too fast"), Bill Dance was another to pass the still unfit North and Peter Hilliar was suffering badly with stitch.

Four miles saw the first three through in 19:44, 19:48 and 20:13 and at five miles the leading positions were, Fairclough (24:45), O'Hara (25:00), Piotrowski (25:32), Dance (25:48), Steer and Mann (25:53) and North (25:57).

Don Maclean faded rapidly in the middle stages with blistered feet, and on noting the lap recorders' confusion as to his position prompted them with his own count-down on each of the remaining circuits!

I WAS THERE

Clive Shippen

No.4 : by Clive Shippen

The A.A.A. Championships of 1960. Do you remember them? It was the year of the Rome Olympics, so you probably do; but I wonder which event stands out in your mind.

Perhaps it was the 3 miles where those ace finishers Tulloh, Ibbottsor and Salvat all hit the final 300 yards mark together and when "Ibbo" unleashed his final effort Frankie Salvat stayed with him for 80 yards before letting loose probably the fastest 220 seen before or since in this event. To open up a 25 yards lead during the finishing bursts of two such rivals was a sight few will forget.

Or perhaps it was the shot or hammer, where the two champions proved to be in the twilight of youthfull careers. British athletics has never recovered from the early retirements of Arthur Rowe and Mike Ellis. The former's 59'2" shot win and the latter's 210'7" hammer heave make their counterparts of today look modest performers indeed. Field events enthusiasts will no doubt remember those Championships thro' their nostalgia for Rowe and Ellis.

It could have been the 100 yards - Radford's 9.5 secs. is still unbeaten - or even the mile where the exiled Hungarian, Lazlo Tabori, beat Wiggs and Michel Jazy and all eight runners bettered the Championship best performance.

Many will remember the occasion (Charlie Dabbs in particular) for Gordon Pirie's comfortable win in the six-miles, where his final burst lapped the Belgrave man in the finishing straight.

But, for me, none of these memorable incidents matches the event that stood out above all others on July 16th, 1960. I speak of the contest that brought together two men who had, for some time, swept all before them. Two men who, between them, were destined to gain an Olympic gold, a European gold and an Olympic bronze. They were athletes who adopted a "professional" approach to their training and who brought glamour to an event that needed it badly.

Yes, their names were Stan Vickers and Ken Matthews and the start of the 2 miles walk at the A.A.A. Championships of 1960 was the signal for the bars to be emptied of their beer-drinking occupants instead of the reverse which is now customary. The Public's imagination had been captured by the clash of two giants both making a bid for the very suspect 56 years old world record of 13:11.4 by George Larner. Both walkers tackled their track training in a similar way to their running counterparts - liberal doses of repetition speedwork - and their faultless styles travelling together at speed were a sheer joy to watch.

Few would dare to forecast the result with any certainty, though Vickers had the slightest edge perhaps over this short distance. Two weeks earlier he beat his rival narrowly on a grass track at Peterborough but this was not regarded as a serious pointer to a Championship event at the White City where Matthews was not only the Holder in a best of 13:19.4 but the previous year the Midlander had beaten Stan by a 27.6 seconds margin.

There were 18 starters, including four East Germans and two Indians, including Zora Singh who had some good times to his credit. The crowd, fully aware of the record attempt, hushed as the walkers lined up. Eric Hall gave his fellow Belgravian a good luck wish as he stood alongside and the tension mounted rapidly, almost producing a false start. The gun exploded, the watches clicked, the scoreboard clock snapped into motion and the crowd's roar swept the field round the first bend with the turbanned Zora Singh to the fore.

Singh scampered thro' the first lap in 94.3 secs. revelling in his moment of glory with Matthews, Vickers, Colin Williams, Adjit Singh and Lindner hard on his heels. A further circuit was sufficient to convince the judges of his faulty style and he was ruled out just after being passed by his followers. The lightning pace also accounted for the departure of two of the East Germans. Matthews refused to let the pace lapse and even at this stage he and Vickers were visibly straining to do the utmost. For every yard of the way it looked as though the Midlander was making "a last desperate effort" to shake off his Belgrave rival; and for every yard of the way it looked as though Vickers would hold on for no longer.

"Time at one mile, 6 minutes 30.2 seconds" roared the Tannoy in it's effort to overcome the crowd's cheering, but few heard. All could sense that the record would go but I have never been so uncertain of a result as I was on this occasion. Was it humanly possible to actually walk past a man travelling at Matthew's speed?

The mile and a half was reached in 9:51.4, averaging 98.6 a lap! And still the two raced round in tandem a full straight ahead of East Germany's Lindner.

Black thunder clouds during the early afternoon of June 24th did not augur well for a summer's afternoon sport. Sure enough, as the runners were arriving, the skies opened and the deluge began. After changing in a (very) small marquee, the runners lined up in pouring rain and after being told to do one lap of the (grass) track set off on their wet way. Immediately the absence of markers became apparent. Half the leading bunch turned to complete a full lap whilst others headed straight out of the nearest gate. The latter proved to be the more popular course. Soon, however, the field sorted itself out with Steer and Hart with the leaders and Dabbs and Mann close by; having started rather less impetuously. O'Connor and Merry were near at hand but Bernard later paid for his fast start. The race progressed and the leaders gradually became more strung out, only pausing occasionally to ask the way. At 3 miles the leaders were Merriman (Reading) and Gevers (SLH) with Hart, Steer and Dabbs in convoy in 7th, 8th and 10th places. At this point the Belgrave runners were suprised to see a car suddenly scream to a halt beside them and the occupant pointing feverishly in the opposite direction. Trevor and John Snowdon (Surrey) gently suggested that the leaders should be informed whereupon the driver set off Surtees style after them - they had a considerable lead by this time. Eventually all the runners were corrected and Trevor was suprised to find himself in the lead since all those in front had to retrace their steps. Needless to say most people had ceased to regard the race with any seriousness and merely ran to finish the course and get in out of the rain. For the record Brian Bacon (Reading) was adjudged to have won and Belgrave were given third team place. The medals were nice though.......

BRACKNELL 5 MILES---5TH AUGUST

When reporting this race last year we bemoaned the lack of a good third man to complete our scoring team and put us among the awards. It is therefore now good to report that we made amends this year, by having 5 teams doing battle over this very pleasant traffic--free course, where we succeeded in taking 2nd team and first 'B' team prizes.

With a very fast pace set throughout by Bruce Tulloh(Ports) our A team packed in extremely well, with Laurie O'Hara just managing to get the better of Tony Fairclough after they had come back into the track together. Gerry North completed the scoring three, making a welcome return after a prolonged injury to the knee. Dick Piotrowski making his debut for us on the road and his first outing as a first claim member fully justified his 'A' team selection just beating our old friend Mel Thorpe (Coventry G)--our second man home in 1966.

Whilst Messrs. Hart, Steer, and Mann quietly set about securing the 'B' team prizes for themselves, other titanic struggles were going on further down the field. Up and coming Pat O'Connor put in another very strong run whilst Eric Penny took nearly two minutes off his last year's time. John Walker, inspite of a recent spell of "special coaching and altitude training" by brother Charlie in the Isle of Wight, found Charlie Smart just too good for him again, as did Glimpy Jones---rumours that Glimpy travelled down overnight and went round the course in the morning have not yet been proved!.

BELGRAVE 20

Hard working Gordon Biscoe gained the reward that all race secretaries look for when he received a record entry of 180 runners for the 16th annual Belgrave '20' at Wimbledon on July 22nd. Road works at Tibbet's Corner had made the organisation particularly difficultand required quite a few hours perambulating a surveyors wheel around the course to ensure that the proper distance was covered. Work like this is not only wearying- it can be compromising. What was Gordon to say when interviewed by the long arm of the law in the small hours while busy outside the Vietnamese Embassy with a brush and a bucket of white paint. The conversation can be imagined. It certainly started with the words " 'Allo, 'allo, 'allo. What's all this 'ere then?" Road race secretaries are never taken aback, however. "Aha," says Gordon, "I'm painting mile markers on the road so that the runners in the Belgrave '20' know how much suffering has been and how much is to come." Anyway, a persuasive chap, Gordon. All the marks were there for the race.

Graham Taylor, who set a magnificent course record of 1hr.39m.47s. the previous year, was unfortunately prevented from turning out by injury, and with the withdrawal by Peter Yates, the race seemed from the start to lie between Scottish international Jim Wright of Tipton, and Barry Collins of Surrey AC.

The 126 starters soon sorted themselves out in three groups of varying speed, and were soon sweating nicely on an oppressively hot day, with Wright and Collins well up and Barry Watson, last year's third man also well to the fore but soon to retire.

By the end of the first of the three main laps, the groups had broken up. Every man for himself. At this stage, the Bels chances for the team race looked bright. Charlie Dabbs, Bill Dance, Chris Steer and Lionel Mann were all well placed. In the individual competition, Wright had cut out in front and Collins was a clear second. The individual places at the head of the field showed little change after that - except for a strong breakthrough in the later stages by Dai Davies of T.V.H. and K.Darlow of Reading, who who finished 4th and 5th respectively - D.McFadzean (RNAC South) having sorted out a comfortable third place for himself.

The team picture altered drastically, however, as first Chris Steer and then Bill Dance was forced to retire. Thames Valley kept going well to take the race, with Paignton gaining a popular place as runners-up, and Cambridge H third. The Bels "A" squad of Charlie Dabbs - another to move through strongly as the race went on, finishing up in 7th place - Lionel Mann, 15th, and John Hilton, 44th, was fourth.

Charlie and Lionel were first and second in the Belgrave Championship, but third place went to the first "B" team runner, John Baker, who was 38th in 2hrs.08m.01s. John has only just switched from walking to running and has made a quick impact. We look forward to even better things from him in the future.

Autumn Relay Prospects

Clive Shippen

Clive Shippen Reviews the forthcoming Road Relay season

Of the six autumn road relay races held last year we chalked up 2 wins (Belgrave and Walton), 2 seconds (Johnstone and Blackheath), 1 fifth (Surrey) and 1 sixth (Bristol/Weston). A fair performance---but there were weaknesses. The Surrey County event at Woking Lido is a notorious one. The holiday season affects it every year and our multi-county membership also restricts our entry, but we could still field stronger teams than hitherto. The venue is in a most pleasant setting with the start and finish in the park, on grass, and the amenities of the Lido available afterwards. Perhaps the atmosphere is not "cut-throat" enough for us! With John Bicourt not expected back from North America until late September, if at all, we are weakened even before Bill Lucas sends out his selection cards. Nevertheless, a team from Gerry North, Thresher, Black, Fairclough, Steer, Major and Dance should stand an excellent chance of pulling off the title---providing no more than one cries off! Non-Surrey men can take part in the 'B' and 'C' teams and with names like Geoff North, O'Hara, Hart, Piotrowski, Kerr, Dear, Williams, and Buxton there could be a ONE/TWO in it for BELGRAVE.

Little has been seen of Gerry Williams and Brian Buxton since June and we hope they will be fit and available once more during the coming weeks. With Mel Thorpe's departure to Coventry Godiva Harriers we were doubly pleased to welcome Dick Piotrowski to our ranks. Dick, formerly a member of Bracknell AC, is stationed with the Guards at Chelsea Barracks (just local to ur training tracks at Battersea and the Duke of York's) and has become first-claim Belgrave under the hard-cases rule. He has turned in a 9:16 steeplechase and already proved his worth as a replacement for Mel by beating him in the Bracknell '5' road race--albeit by a few seconds.

Another missing from our Belgrave Relay dozen of last year is Joe Reilly, but with Geoff North standing down on that occasion we should be stronger this year, despite the loss of Mel and Joe. If Peter Whewell is still with us in early October and Bill Kerr and Adrian Major can keep free of injury then our depth should carry us through to some big wins.

One event we should win for the third successive year is our own Open Relay. With twelve stages involved we have a clear advantage over Portsmouth and TVH, our only serious rivals. But what of that 8-stage classic the Bristol/Weston?. Well, our eight runners will have to run about five minutes faster than last year to win it. Looking at last year's result shows just what they are up against. Gerry has yet to regain his form of 12 months ago and even if he does so in time can Dance, Fairclough, and Bicourt run a joint 4 mins-20secs faster than they did last year when we found ourselves 13th after three stages? Yes, the Bristol/Weston is as tough a nut to crack as the National, but with five weeks of selection ahead of it we have every opportunity of finding the right eight for the job.

There is another 12 stage relay, other than our own promotion. This is Hornchurch Harriers' "Johnstone" relay to be held on Sunday September 24th. Our performance last year was better than our "National" effort 6 months later, for we finished second to Coventry by a 1:40 margin and had Tipton, Longwood, Manchester and Derby behind us for good measure. These latter teams have all improved since and we will have to raise ourselves even to maintain a second placing.

The Blackheath Road Relay clashes with our non-championship '5¾' again this year, so one team only will be sent to Blackheath to put right that 3 seconds defeat by Portsmouth. Meanwhile our own race will be setting out to establish form for the three big relays and a battle royal can be anticipated from the expected 40 strong field.

Each time we approach the Spring or Autumn road-relay season there are a few members who assume they are automatic selections. Be warned! All the dark horses are at peak fitness and there is invariably someone who records a "breakthrough". No one can afford to be complacent. Remember, even Geoff North could only make first reserve position last year in our Open Relay, yet 3 months earlier he was running for Great Britain versus U.S.S.R!.

To remind our runners of the competition they might have from within the Club I name just seventeen candidates. Before anyone takes their place for granted I suggest they note the names carefully and assume that all of their rivals are running betterthan ever before---because the twelve that are picked probably will be!

At least five of these will have to stand down---still confident? With Pat Newell, John Dear, and Lionel Mann (amongst others) also waiting for an opportunity to nip in I would say the position of every runner is precarious to say the least. And remember, experience has shown it unwise to rely on one good effort for selection.
