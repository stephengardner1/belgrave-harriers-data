British Athletics League Premiership: League team on the ropes

Match Three, Alexander Stadium, Birmingham, 7 July 2007.

League team on the ropes

Just when it seemed that a recovery was being made after our dire start to the season, we were knocked back down again with last place in match three – our worst performance since we rejoined the British League in 1983. Again it was clashing international events that pulled away members of our team.

In fact there were a few bright spots, the most notable being the performances of multi-eventer Louis Evling-Jones who cleared 2.05m in the high jump, triple jumped 13.88 and ran sprint hurdles and a leg in the long relay. It would have been a very poor team without Louis!

Darren Chin was also in good form with sharp times in the 100m and 200m, and there seems to be no stopping Joe Ive who took maximum points in the pole vault with 5.30m. But even in the events where we are traditionally strong, points were so hard to come by and performances were depressed.

So, the final match will be very interesting. Although we are just out of the drop zone with three matches gone, those famous escapologists Shaftesbury Barnet Harriers are right behind us and will no doubt turn on a good show to finish the season on their home track. For the first time in a quarter of a century a battle to avoid relegation looms.

UK Women’s Athletics League Division 2: Another small team

Match Two, Eton, 7 July 2007.

Another small team

It’s understandable that only a small Belgrave team might want to travel to Wigan for match one – but disappointing that the head count was only increased by only two in what could be considered a “London” match. And yet, somehow, in spite of being without any sprinters or pole vaulters, the team maintained their position in the second highest division in the country. Doesn’t say a lot for the state of club athletics does it!

The middle distances were again a happy hunting ground for points as Michaela Hutchison boosted the team by winning the 1500m with the ever present Catherine Bryson backing up with a B 3rd place having already placed 3rd in the A 3000m.

Hannah Douglas is in a class of her own in the 400 hurdles and her partnership with Jodie Favell was a productive one as they went on to tackle the sprint hurdles as well. In spite of the home club picking up points at will, we also scored well in the 400 flat thanks to Susan Bovill and Sarah Gailey. Sarah seems to think nothing of using the preceding 800m as a warm-up.

It was good to welcome Suzanne Cookson into the team where she ran what we believe to be our fastest ever W35 800m.

So, one match to go and our hard working but small team look as if they should be able to hold on to a division two spot.

BAL Cup

Round 2, Copthall Stadium, Barnet, 10 June 2007.

With the aim of completing 10 events in 100 minutes, the revamped cup competition was more aptly named than we realised. With many athletes preferring to miss their specialist events prior to the UK Champs & World Trials, or travelling to Solihull for the BMC races, it seemed that only 10 in 100 athletes wanted to take part.

Highlights for Belgrave were the return to 400 hurdling of Ryan Dinham after injury - he won comfortably - and a masterly 3000m win from James Kelly.

A couple of months ago Ryan was undergoing surgery to sort out his problems but although he was disappointed with his time here at Copthall, he actually looked pretty smooth over the hurdles and got in his intended stride pattern. Times will come with a few more races. He celebrated his turn to BAL athletics by lobbing out the discus for a couple more points and anchored the relay team.

James Kelly was content to let Medway’s Mike Coleman take up the pace for three or four laps, sitting at the back of a bunch of four ... and then three ... as the laps ticked away. Then, moving into the lead just after half-way, James increased the pace until only Coleman was left in contention and slipping a few strides astern at that. A cloudburst over the last lap heralded the Belgrave man's final push for a clear win.

Late arrivals had those holding the team sheet fretting. Mark Humphrey arrived just in time to to line up for the 800m - and proceeded to run a season's best - while Set Osho was too late to warm up for the 400m and we thanked our lucky stars that Ian Deeth was there to step into the breach.

Cameron Johnston, with a late start to his season, looked comfortable at 4.40m in the pole vault and young guns Kwesi Menyah and Melvyn Anukam welcomed the chance to step up from Southern League competition. Jason Hussain filled in at shot minutes after completing the 200m, to ensure that we had a full team, but unfortunately we were not even near a qualifying position for the final.

Southern League climb back begins

Match 3, Horsham, 14 July 2007.

Southern League climb back begins

By mustering three-quarters of a team with a mixture of injury returnees, novices and others who had not recently competed regularly in the British League, we raised the standard of this division four match with a liberal dose of performances the like of which are rarely seen in Men’s Southern League Division Four (South). Although nominally a Sutton/Belgrave joint team, only ID Akindele from Sutton joined in the fun.

ID himself looked impressive as he smoothly streaked away from the field in the 100m but stepped into a non-scoring role for the 200 to let in 16 year-old Melvyn Anukam. Mel’s first ever athletic competition resulted in a B 200m win - and as he ended the afternoon in the winning 4x100 team can point to a 100% win record in his short athletic career. It was great to see Ryan Dinham back in action, winning the A 200m and contributing hugely to both relays; we wish him luck in his late quest to get something out of the remainder of the season.

Two fine runs over 400m came from Ian Deeth, recovering from hamstring problems, and the experience which he passed on freely to young Mel and others was invaluable.

Triathlete and world aquathlon champion Richard Stannard turned out for the 5000m and immediately ran away from the field, lapping runners within four circuits and moving on to overtake the whole field at least once with three laps still remaining. It required full concentration by the lap scorers and his 15:13-odd was just three seconds outside his best (he has only run one track 5k before) and was almost two minutes faster than the second man across the line in our match.

James Kelly demolished the 1500m line-up with Mark Humphrey piling up points with season's bests in the B 1500m and A 800m. New man Kwesi Menyah did some useful triple jumping and Cameron Johnston's 4.40 pole vaulting, backed up by Louis Samms was a tonic in the jumps department.

The throws were good too, thanks to veteran Chris Privett, ubiquitous trainee team manager Michael Peacock and James Everard, who was surprisingly beaten with a last round throw in a high standard javelin competition.

A couple of hurdlers, a distance man or two and some high jump competitors, and we could have romped this match. It won’t always be like this of course, especially when BAL and MSL matches clash and we have less hands available for transport, etc., but our climb back up the Southern League divisions began here at Horsham.

From The Belgravian

1927

Eighty years ago we had a new Editor for the Belgravian. Ben Knifton took over from Harry Hare when the latter was elected President as a result of the death of Bob Ricketts. At the time we were reminded that it was only a few years since the membership was almost in single figures. Harry finished his Presidential address by saying “the Club is, and is judged, as you make it and by your example let the Belgrave Harriers be ‘second to none’ ”.

Our cross country and road relay runners along with the walkers took the words to heart in the ensuing years.

1937

In our Jubilee year, ten years later, we gained third place in the AAA 4 x 440 yards relay, Pat Parker was 3rd in the 3 miles earning him international selection in the match against France, Don Brown was 3rd in the 2 mile walk being followed home by Percy Wright (now 93 years old and our senior member).

“The Sporting Life” compiled a points table based on the AAAs championship positions allowing 6 points for a 1st place down to 1 point for 6th place. The result shows that Achilles topped the league followed by Germany, London AC 3rd, Hungary 4th and Belgrave in equal 5th place with Greece! This without two of our best men, Pack 4th in the 440 yards and Dodd winning the pole vault who were tied representing others on that occasion.

Earlier in the year we had finished 2nd in the English Cross Country Championships and then 2nd in the Brighton Relay - due very much to Mitcham’s Brickwood beating us to the line. Denis Brickwood is now our 2nd oldest member. To finish off with the “oldies” Bill Lucas (3rd senior Belgravian) reported on a match at Drayton Green Track in May when we won the first match of the season.

What a finish!

The crowd on Madeira Drive, Brighton, gasp as with just a yard or two to go Denis Brickwood, then representing Mitcham AC, overtakes Arthur Allum of Belgrave to win the National London to Brighton Road Relay by just 1 second.

News

Cockerell’s Corner

After three glorious years of Skipper’s Corner, a much visited spot on our website on the Monday after a busy weekend’s competition, Witty Will has been snapped up by Athletics Weekly who are desperately seeking something a little different to spice up their issues. If you are a buyer of AW (or are one of those who skip through it in W.H. Smith’s at lunchtime!) watch out for Cockerell’s Corner.

Another four minute miler …

In our list of those Belgravians who have cracked the magic four minutes, published in the previous Belgravian, we left out Aussie Andy Lloyd who clocked 3:56.52 on 27 May 1990. The Belgrave sub-4 Club has a membership of 12.

Congratulations …

… to George and Marlene Beecham on celebrating their Golden Wedding Anniversary on August 3rd.

At the celebratory lunch a guest asked our correspondent, Eric Hall, “Are you also a member of the Belgrave Harriers?” The enquirer was astounded to meet two members of “that great Club” in the same place at the same time! Eric didn’t bother to introduce him to Mavis as he thought that thee Belgravians in one place might be too much for him!

It was a busy time for George. He had hardly paused for breath after completing his 16th Nijmegen Marches (four successive days each of 50 kilometres) before setting off on a celebratory cruise with Marlene. Ever the active man, we just hope the blisters heal quickly George.

Run Fat Boy Run

Occasionally we get called up by agents looking for runners to appear before the cameras for adverts or to act as extras in films.

A few years ago an agent turned up at Sutton Coldfield on the day of a road relay to check out our team for tall skinny men – plenty of those – with large noses and droopy moustaches. None of our lads got the job and the job of “118” in the TV adverts went elsewhere.

Shugri Omar was luckier. When an enquirer phoned up The Belgravian offices asking, “could we come up with a skinny North African lad who looked like a marathon runner,” we knew the very person. “Shugs” sailed through the audition and later starred in a John West advert – the one where the leader in the Prague Marathon (“Shugs”) gets overtaken in the finishing straight by … a pilchard!

More recently we supplied runners for the film Run Fat Boy Run – we’ve read the reviews and don’t think we’ll be going to see it – but an excited Mark Humphrey ‘phoned up soon after it went on general release to tell us that he’d been to watch it and had seen himself seven times (or maybe had he been to see it seven times … not sure now).

Coggie and TVH vest

Our old mate Paul Coughlan in New Zealand wrote to let us know that a member of his club Olympic Harriers was approaching his 50th year of membership. Thames Valley Harrier Bill Hobbs emigrated to New Zealand in 1957 and one of the first things he did on arrival was join Olympic. Now in 2007 his club were going to hold a special race to commemorate his long membership. What Coggie wanted to know was “could we get hold of a TVH vest?”

Nothing is impossible. we got in touch with TVH official Dave Huffer who rustled up a blue and black singlet in no time, arranged for it to be handed over to Belgrave officials at the 10 in 100 event at Copthall Barnet – and within a week it was winging its way to the other side of the world. It arrived just in time and we were pleased to see photographs on the Olympic Harriers website of Bill wearing his new vest with pride.

No bell prize

Goes to Carl Lawton. Battersea Park track provide a bell to be rung to indicate the last lap of races, but track judges will have noted that this year it lacked a clanger. Carl Lawton, on hand to help organise a walk, produced a spanner wrench from his van to give the bell a hefty whack every time a hardy competitor completed 49 circuits. (From The Essex Walker.)
