RECORD SEASON

This season has been truly amazing for we have seen a great burgeoning of our activities and achievements. On all fronts we are able to report advances. The Belgrave Hall Development is to proceed in January, Battersea Park track has become all weather, Norbiton track is likely to go all-weather in 1984 and our athletes have re-written our record books to confirm the progress for which you all have worked so hard. In this issue you will read of 32 new Club records!!!

The strengthening of our British League team continues. We look forward next year to emulating our performance in winning this season's Division 5. Although we will face tougher opposition in Division 4 our performances this year would have placed us well up in Division 3. Winter training is essential for early season track and field successes and we are proud that UK Champions Mike Winch and Judy Oakes have started coaching sessions at Belgrave Hall as a prelude to building up a centre of excellence for strength and mobility training following our Belgrave Hall Development Programme.

ON THE RIGHT ROAD?

You will also read in this issue of the influx of new top class middle distance runners. There is a fear that we will not be able to offer enough of them competition at the highest levels. It would seem that what with representative matches and controlled racing schedules many of our top middle distance men are not always available to turn out when needed. So in practice everyone will get a fair share of top Club competition. But, also it would seem that more could and indeed should be done to encourage our top middle distance men to race in open road races and in relays.Wouldn't it be great to see our road and country traditions re-established. We have the men, but we need to plan and organise our road activities better and it is time we considered appointing a Road Secretary.

grapevine

Two years ago the Belgrave 100m Championship was won by Philip Oppenheim. 27year old Philip has been less active recently and no wonder! In June he became the youngest Member of Parliament. His views on capital punishment received widespread TV coverage. In his spare time he edits a specialist magazine on high technoloy subjects.

For those of you watching the Helsinki World Champs on Channel 4 our comments about John Bicourt in the last Belgravian will have begun to make sense. Yes it's John who keeps taking Olympus kit 'To hell and back'.

John's interest in steeplechasing has been re-kindled by new member Mike Hayes whom he is coaching. Both represented us in the last British League match and John is down to buy the drinks as Mike came home first by 6 secs.

Congratulations to Carl Lawton on his election as President of the Southern Road Walking Assoc. and to Gordon Biscoe who is this year's President of The South of The Thames CCA.

Mike Nouch is back running better than ever and we learn that he was married in the summer. It's worked wonders.

There's always news on the stork front. Ann has presented John Rayner with their first son Jack, while Liz and Leo Lyons are counting their blessings because their second daughter, who was due in December, arrived 12weeks premature in September. Other new arrivals for Belgrave parents are; a son -Steven -for Alan and Ann Painter and a daughter -Heather- for Chris and Glynis Tapling.

Colin Gaynor has gone up to Loughborough University for 1 year to study Recreational Management. In joining Ernest Obeng who is in the second year of his Doctorate and Mark Waller he strengthens the Belgrave supporters club.

Our British League team to Crewe were intrigued to see one official cheering for them. He turned out to be Gerry Warr who walked for Belgrave from 1949 to the late 1950's. He is now a field referee and is Chairman of the Lancashire Schools AA's. He lives in the Wirrall and asks to be remembered to his past team mates.

We are pleased to hear that Bill True is back on the road again after illness and injury. He caught a chill while officiating earlier this year and then strained a hamstring. He is now 76 and is planning to have a break from official duties.

George Williams, mentioned in GRAPEVINE earlier this year, has put on his boots again and has competed for the first time in 16 years. He has again joined Belgrave and hopes to turn out in the coming winter. He was a British International in the early 60's.

Albert Frost who joined us from Liverpool Pembroke just after the war and competed in our Southern and National teams was awarded the C.B.E. in the 1983 New Year's Honours list. Before retiring he was the Finance Director of ICI.

We have received a most comprehensive account from Graham Mackie in New Zealand of the grand tour which he and Debbie took when returning home at the beginning of the year. While in Delhi he met Ranjit Bhatia and was well received. Graham bussed across India, Nepal; flew to Siam ,Indonesia and then on to the magic of Bali. He ran out of money in Australia and worked for two months to raise the funds to complete the journey back to Wellington. His aim this winter is to make the N.Z. cross country team.

Following JB into the film business is Kevin Sturman. We can't say too much at present but there is a connection with Sean Connery and Greece. More next time!

Feature · page 5

The President.

It is a very special set of circumstances which result in a Belgravian being elected for a second term of office as President. It is a very special person who would qualify for such an honour. Such a person is Tom Carter. Tom, now in his mid 70's,is still a 100% Belgravian devoting all his spare time to our Club and it is incredible to think that this devotion has been sustained for over 50 years through good times and through bad times. Whatever our fortunes Tom has been there giving advice and encouragement both to Officials and to new members alike and never failing to help out by transporting younger members to races plus helping out with all the usual jobs at races. There can be nobody better known to all Belgravians than our Tom and the debt we owe was only partly recognised in 1980 when we held a special '50 Anniversary Party' for him and of course Ethel who too has supported us for all this time. Behind every great man there is a great lady!

Tom joined Belgrave in 1930 and almost immediately made an impact in our teams. He was in many of our medal winning National teams winning team gold in 1934. In 1936 Tom was 9th Englishman home in the National and gained an International vest running for England at Blackpool.In 1939 Tom won the Surrey 6 mile track Champs in a new record time. He also won the Southern CC title and the Southern 6 miles track title. He crowned a fine athletics career with other great performances including a 3rd place in the AAA's 10 mile track Championships.

After such an illustrious career you might be forgiven for thinking that a spot of rest was in order. But not for Tom! He pitched in with training younger members after the war and worked through most of the jobs undertaken by our Officials. In 1957 Tom was honoured with the Belgrave Presidency. He wrote in the Jan. '57 Belgravian,words which are as relevant today as they were then.

"Thank you all for the great honour which you have bestowed upon me in making me your President for this year. To me this wonderful Club of ours has no equal, for what other club can point to so many successes in all branches of athletics.

I should like to draw members attention to our motto "Semper Paratus" which means "Always Ready". To me this means always ready for anything for our Club; I hope this means the same to you also."

That's good enough for us. 'Rock on Tommy' in this your Presidential Year.

AGM - GOOD AND BAD NEWS

This year's A.G.M. held at Belgrave Hall on September 15th was the best attended for many a year the numbers being swelled by several of our top track and field men plus a good turnout from our younger members and their parents.

Those present were treated to an excellent review of the season's successes by Hon. Secretary and joint President - Derek Crookes followed by a detailed breakdown of the Club's accounts by Hon. Treasurer - Jim Heathfield. The successes are recorded elsewhere in this issue and so it is worth highlighting the key points from the published accounts and Jim's talk.

Subscriptions for the year to June 1983 totalled £2712 thanks to the untiring efforts of Membership Secretary - Carl Lawton. This sum was only just topped by monies from donations, fund raising and grants so that our total revenue was £5440 and this sum just about met our total expenditure of £5447.By far the biggest single cost was that of running our mens, womens,young athletes and walking teams the overall figure reaching £2165. Next came printing, stationary, and postage at £1643 followed by prizes and medals which this year cost £647. So, once again subscriptions came nowhere to meeting the Club running costs thereby reinforcing the arguments of those wishing to see subs raised to more realistic levels.

Those of you who keep an eye on the state of finances will know that Belgrave Hall is treated as a separate cost centre. This year the running expenses totalled £2926 and were met from bar profits and income from the hire of the Hall.We now pay the local Council £576 in rates and the ever watchfull Revenue around £200 as tax on Hall income. After all these deductions the net profit arising from the activities at the Hall was £1521.

And so on to the cash balance which we carry forward to support the imminent Hall rebuilding programme. This now stands at a respectable £12411 and will be supplemented by Grants and Loans which have been promised by The Sports Council and several other Sporting Organisations.

Increased income will be needed in the years ahead if the expansion of our activities is to continue. In recognition of this the Club's Committee and Trustees recommended to the A.G.M. that subs be increased from July 1st 1984 The present levels of subscriptions had remained constant for the last 4 years and the proposed increases were unanimously accepted by all present without a single dissenting voice. The new rates will be ;

SENIORS £12(Was £10), UNDER 20 £9(£6), UNDER 17 £6(£3), ASSOCIATE AND SECOND CLAIM MEMBERS £6(£3) ........................................

And now for the bad news. Both Derek Crookes and Jim Heathfield indicated that the workload on behalf of the club had increased to such an extent that they would be forced to step down at the end of this years term of office. There is no doubt that many of our officials are rapidly approaching this situation and we must find ways of relieving the load of the hardest pressed of our management team.

BELGRAVE HALL

Well we're doing it! The Belgrave Hall improvement programme is to proceed. Bill Couzens has cancelled his regular bookings for the Hall during the period January to March 1984 and builders will be moving in directly after Xmas. So far we have raised £29,000 of which £11,000 is in the form of loans from; The Sports Council,The Southern Counties AAA's,plus the London and the Surrey Playing Fields Associations

The exact scope of the works to be carried out will depend whether or not we are able to obtain further monies ; and so we have 2 contingency plans both of which include the following; Extending the floor right across the Hall and building a new front entrance hall with an enclosed staircase leading up from the entrance area. The existing wooden staircase and store will also be deleted at this stage and a new partition wall built to separate the existing mens changing room from the gymnasium area. This work will start in January.

But our hope is that further monies can indeed be found to still further improve the standard of the gymnasium and to incorporate sauna facilities plus a small office area which will be used to centralise Club records. In addition improved changing and shower facilities for the Ladies Section will be built on the ground floor to complete the full re-development of the Hall.

It might be helpful at this point to remind you of the improved functions and facilities which the full development will provide. The Hall will provide a much wider range of activities than now and will have to earn extra money to pay off the loans which we have obtained. The Hall will be divided into upper and lower levels each of which will have their own entrances and toilets. Thus it will be possible to run separate activities on both the upper and lower levels at the same time. The upper floor will be used for art and recreational purposes during the day and social functions in the evenings and at weekends. The lower floor will be used to provide gymnasium, sauna and changing for Road and Cross Country training and the aim is for these facilities to be available to members at most times as opposed to the limited opening of the Hall as at present.

Our aim then is to use the developed Hall to provide greater chances to use art,recreational and sports facilities by the people of South London. A key improvement will be due to the use of the gymnasium by Mike Winch and Judy Oakes who will for three days per week be running special strength and mobility sessions not only for athletes but also for other Sportsmen and members of the general public. We plan,with full support of Judy and Mike to make Belgrave Hall a major centre for training Field events athletes. In addition Mike and Judy have complete training schedules for all athletic specialities from sprints to the marathon.

So, Please be patient during the re-building phase. We shall try to organise the works so that training will not be interrupted. In the meantime we still need donations so please be generous and send a'once and for all'substantial contribution to Jim Heathfield at [address withheld].

Report · page 8

WALKING

There are times in the annual cycle of walking when it is difficult to be unbiased or disinterested in activities. As a competitor it is hard to criticise the actions of a team member when one is in the position of possibly doing the same in the next race. With that in mind I extend my thanks to those who have tried and have done their bit this year, and that covers virtually all our walkers. We have not done as well as we could have in the Nationals but we've had that problem before of not getting it together on the day.

We regretfully lose the services, hopefully only for 10 months, of Paul Warburton who has gone on an exchange to America. Also gone is Paul Blagg who has decided to go to pastures new for his own reasons. He had come from a junior to reach International class, but was not often available to turn out in team races so the effect on team results will be minimal.

Team turnouts in the ultra distance events have this year been disappointing. We admittedly like other clubs seem to lack that dedicated group of long distance men and have to hope that the short distance men will fill the gap. We have picked up a few new members but if we are to lead we must find some method of attracting and keeping new talent.

A word in passing on Ron Day. He was the only Belgravian to take part in the recent Quadrathon; 2 mile swim, 50 km walk,100 mile cycle and a 26.3 Marathon run. Unfortunately he was withdrawn after the cycle event having failed the medical check. But congratulations on getting that far. Incidentally three other walkers competed with distinction; Ed Shillabeer was 3rd, Arthur Eddlestone 4th and Stephen Till 6th.

Congratulations also to John Keown on completing 25 consecutive ' Brightons ' 1300 Miles !!!!

It is good to see our girls in action again thanks to Harry Porter and Dave Sharpe. Individually Gail Porter leads the way with 3rd in the Southern Counties WAAA Girls Championship.

Ladies Secretary Derek Jones

YOUNG ATHLETES

Ted Moore

With increased activity and competition for our younger members it has been necessary to try to give the right amount of support from officials by re-organising and co-opting new members to help out. The major change has been the setting up a team of people to help out during the summer. Geoff.Davis, in the year of his joint Presidency and coping with the marriage in the summer of his daughter, was actively involved in the organisation of officials and our home meetings. Ted Moore took over the tough job of managing and getting out the various teams ably supported by Jim Mote and, as the season progressed, by an increasing band of helpers to whom we must express our sincere thanks. Our other joint President Derek Crookes whose pet project - The new Thameside Leagu[illegible] - got under way and was a huge success, also put in a large number of hours to support the Young Athletes and this was greatly appreciated. Ian Horton, who will now concentrate mainly on managing the winter teams, also provided support together with coach Ted Warren and it was pleasing to see Duncan Ross helping out at all the boys and colts meetings.

Ted Moore has prepared the following summary of what has turned out to be a very satisfactory season for our younger Belgravians.

Following the merger between Belgrave and Surrey AC, Belgrave took over Surrey's place in the Dunlop National Young Athletes League. Last year Surrey were relegated from the Southern Premi[illegible] League to the South central League. As a result we were in a League comprising of 6 Clubs and met the same Clubs on each of 4 occasions.

The first match at Norbiton highlighted the problems that the Club would face, i.e.,A lack of field events people. A look at the score sheet showed that Belgrave had no competitors in 25 out of the 74 events. Needless to say the result was a 4th place. there were some fine efforts and a total of 13 wins. The following achieved doubles;- Phil Gray in the Javelin end Discus([illegible] Andy Moore in the Youths 800m and long jump with the spectacular Adrian Ross at the age of 11 winning the colts 100m and 200m crossing the line with an enormous dip.(Shades of Ernest Obeng[illegible]

The second match should have been held at Wimbledon Park but had to be postponed due to a doubl[illegible] booking. So the second match was at Portsmouth. The Belgrave cotingent competed this time in 62 of the 77 events and the tally of points rose from the previous matches 212 to a respectabl[illegible] 267. This put the team in 3rd place behind Portsmouth and Epsom & Ewell who scored 333 and 297 points respectively. The highlight of this match as far as we were concerned was Clive Gardine[illegible] winning the Best Performance Award. This award is made to the best overall athlete at each meeting but can only be won once otherwise no doubt Clive would have won it again at some of t[illegible] other matches. Clive won the Boys 80mH in 12.4 and the 200m in 23.9. The Southern Counties Championships were also held the same day resulting in only a small group of Youths turning ou[illegible] for the Club. A great number of points were lost as a result of this clash, but those who did turn out did very well recordind 10 wins.

At Wimbledon, there was another clash of events, this time with the London Youth Games, and as a result our tally of points was restricted to 261 - sufficient for 3rd place. With 14 non-scores but 11 individual wins we had now climbed to 3rd in the League.

The final match gave a glimpse of what might have been. This time we fielded a large group of Youths . This time we again finished in 3rd place but our points total improved to 318, to Epsom and Ewell's 337 and Portsmouth's 349. This time the number of Zero's on the score sheet had been reduced to 10. Dick Addison achieved 3 wins in the 200/400/800(Y) while Clive Gardine[illegible] also achieved a hat-trick of wins in the 100/PV/80H(B). There were 12 other winners.

It is impossible here to list every winner nor is it possible to list the 12 or so new Club records which were broken during the course of the season. But I can say that during the year the Youngsters gave a lot of pleasure to a lot of supporters. It was great to see how many of of the parents came along to support the team.

Congratulations are in order to the following who were selected to run in the English Schools Championship;Dick Addison, Richard O'Hara, Andrew Moore, Sean Richards, Philip Goedluck, and Clive Gardner. I hope this will be extended to include a full track and field programme next year. There were 4 matches. In 3, Bels were second to Camberley but in the last match Bels were 1st. As a result the final League position was that Camberley won by just a single match point ahead of Belgrave. The beauty of this competition was that a lot of boys and colts were able to try out other events and all enjoyed themselves,

It is important to note that we started the season with an untried team against clubs who for years have fielded Young Athlete teams. Against this background the Club has done extremely well and the results point to many young members moving ever upwards eventually to enjoy athletics at senior level.

Bill Laws

26th JUNE 1983 BATTERSEA PARK RE-OPENING MEETING.

In the last issue of the Belgravian we reported the good progress which was being made towards the early completion of the all-weather surface. Well, you guessed it we spoke too soon and instead of holding home fixtures in May and June as planned we found ourselves having to hastily re-arrange venues at the last minute. It was difficult to explain to those affected that the length of the delays were unpredictable due to first a strike at the docks which kept the essential materials shipbound and later to delays in finishing the surface within specification. Indeed the contractors and their Yugoslav labour were well and truly on their way when it was decided that the inside lane was well below the required thickness. As a result the track was again taken out of action in August for re-laying the inside lane and once again our Ladies Sec. was left at the last minute to book other tracks for League events.

During all these problems the GLC people were terrific and we sympathised with their difficulties. And so to the grand opening. Everybody wanted to see the fine new facilities properly launched and the GLC made the necessary funds available to us to get the thing away in style. Because of the wish to try and open the track on the 26th June to co-incide with Capital radio's London Venture Day we had but three weeks to get things done. With over £1000 worth of prizes and the two 800m's organised by the BMC an advert in AW created an enormous response and on the day it was like an International affair. Then we had Seb Coe warming up before the start and the huge crowd were well entertained. All told 124 athletes competed including 32 Senior/Junior athletes of International fame. Athletes travelled from as far afield as Torquay, Isle of Wight,N. Wales, Manchester,Loughborough and Colchester to compete and posted an impressive set of tarck records. Fast times were recorded and the consensus view among the sprinters was that the Spurtan 'B' surface was very fast indeed and up to the best Continental ones. The pole vaulters too were particularly enthusiastic.

Personal bests were set by 5 Belgrave athletes and Stuart Paton in winning the 800m won a TV set. Mark sinclair ran 1.56.3 in the 'B' 800m, Alistair Morfey ran 3.56.9 in the 1500m to finish just behind Gary Staines, Oliver Foote ran a 8.20.0 3000m. Roy Vassall achieved 7.00 to beat Derek Williams in the long jump and Mark Shippen vaulted a pb 4.40.

Many of the top GLC people attended and were appreciative of our efforts. My thanks to all those who helped with the organising both before the event and on the day. Particular thanks to John Bicourt who organised the 3000m S/C and to Dave Mote who liaised with GLC officials and produced a really first class Souvenir Programme.(Copies may still be purchased to aid Club funds). Our thanks too to one very special non Belgravian- Dave Cocksedge who acted as announcer for the day and, armed with his stats books,managed to keep everyone informed about who was doing what.

Bob Bridges / Alan Mead

One thousand miles of travelling led us to five Southern League matches (our home match was rained off) in which we never placed higher than 4th. We were later disqualified from 2 matches for fielding a new member who had not resigned from his previous club and we were relegated to Division 3.

The stronger our 'B' team the harder our 'A' team members will have to work to keep their places..

One or two points to bear in mind: 1. We have a large membership and when athletes drop out at the last minute it is not always easy to contact reserves who might want to compete.If you are interested in competing a phone call to Alan Mead or Bob Bridges will at least let us know who we can call on. 2. If you cannot make a match after being selected PLEASE let us know. To keep a coach full of athletes waiting for someone who may or may not turn up is inexcusable and also gives us no chance to contact reserves. 3. If you are usually an 'A' team competitor don't look upon a 'B' team outing as beneath you. We always need to field the best two teams we can and you will find that an afternoon out with our SL side is a pleasing experience where youngsters of 16 rub shoulders with the old stagers who are 'over the hill' and those who are shooting for first team status.

Blackheath H~ 25 x 1mile

25th SEPTEMBER 1983 CRYSTAL PALACE NIKE - BLACKHEATH 25 X 1 MILE.

The first 100 x 1 mile relay was held in 1969 to celebrate the Centenary year of Blackheath Harriers. Since that event after a break of several years the long distance track relays organised each year by Blackheath have become one of the family highlights of the track season In 1977 to celebrate the Silver Jubilee the relay was shortened to 25 x 1 miles and this format has proved to be the most poular since then. The organisation needed to get over 80 teams started at 8 separate venues around the Country is considerable and Blackheath rely on sponsors to help out by financing the effort needed to promote this event. This year Nike again provided this support with excellent prizes for the first three teams. The reward for winning was a Nike Greyhound suit for every member of the winning team and our track men realised that this year we could improve on last years third place and that we might even topple the mighty Alderhot team. On paper things looked good for our 'A' team and Bill Laws produced a prediction that not only could our strongest team win but that with reasonable times we could just shave Aldershot's U.K. record of 1hr 52.00.2. Well we almost made it! On the day illness prevented John Gladwin from running and Leo Coy had to travel abroad at short notice. Stu Paton and Paul Richley both had bad colds but turned out to support the team effort while John Barry travelled down from Redditch. We also saw Kevin Sturman in Belgrave colours for the first time and nobody would have guessed from his styllish run that this was also to be his first race since injury in the summer.

It is always difficult to highlight individual perfomances but certain exceptional runs must be mentioned. To start at the beginning 17 year old John Warren gave us a better than expected 3rd place on the first leg and recorded our 4th fastest time with 4.21. 16 year old Richard O'Hara showed great maturity in recording 4.38 - some 2 seconds slower than namesake Laurie who at the age of 51 never fails to amaze. Gary Staines was our fastest man with 4.10 and this time made him fastest of the day so he picked up a pair of Nike spikes. Mark Sinclair recorded our 2nd fastest time and once again confirmed that not only has he broken through to a new level of performance but that he has the strength to string together several good runs in close succession.

So, with all our 'A' team well under 5 minutes and an average of 4.32 miling we turned out easy winners with Aldershot just pipping TVH for 3rd place by the incredibly small margin of 0.9sec.

It was pleasing to see some of our stalwarts such as Martin Lake contributing to this win and even more pleasing to see some of the 'B' team men turning in good times. Derek Clack and Mike Nouch continue to improve and young Andy North made a welcome return after a back injury which has put him out for the last year.

Our young athletes easily broke the club age record for this event the average age being 13 yrs. Gary McGurren almost broke 5 minutes and 11 year old Adrian Ross who only knows how to run flat out recorded a quite astounding 5.41.

As far as the Belgrave organisation is concerned our thanks to Ian Horton who stepped in at the last minute when it looked as though we would not get the young athletes out; to Brian Pritchard and Bill Couzens who not only organised the morning 'C' team but also ran the 'B' team and helped out with the afternoon's teams. Both Bill Couzens and Simon Pritchard carried out the task of team recorders and Bil Laws managed the 'A' team and its selection.

Coaching

With no income this year from the London and South East Sports Council but with a very welcome contribution from the Wandsworth Sports Council we have been able to provide some financial assistance to Club coaches as well as to individual athletes. We have also provided specialist equipment for some of our field event men. Much remains however to be done before every member seeking coaching can have the attention which will ensure maximum development. Unfortunately we are still unable to find sufficient people willing to embark on the Club Coaching Courses which really are not too demanding either in terms of time and content.

In recognition of the work put in by top Coaches through our 'Masters Coaching Scheme' the Belgrave Committee has approved a new grade of membership - Hon. Club Coach - which has been offered to and accepted by Mike Dunphy , John Sullivan , and Mike Winch. Mike Dunphy has moved his middle distance squad to Battersea Park where Clive Shippen has also been active with a Pole Vault squad training every Tuesday evening. Our other Club coaches have continued to give stirling service with Frank Simmonds looking after the sprints both at Battersea and at Tooting Bec while Battersea was being relaid with its all weather surface. Ted Warren has worked wonders with our Colts and Boys and some fine performances have resulted. Also pleasing has been the way our A and B team members have helped out with Dave Mote, Alister Johnson and John Druce turning out whenever possible at Norbiton to help younger members.

We continue to seek ways of improving our Coaching and a very important step forward is about to take place..... Judy Oakes is starting Wednesday evening Weight Training for any members seeking strength and agility workouts. This is a prelude to the Belgrave Hall developments. Early in 1984 when the development of Belgrave Hall has been completed both Mike Winch and Judy Oakes wil be running the newly constructed Gymnasium initially for 3 afternoons and evenings per week. As the planned scheme includes a Sauna and multi-gym we expect Belgrave Hall to become a centre of excellence for strength training in S.E. London. This can only help to further build our track and field teams.

DUNLOP YOUNG ATHLETES LEAGUE

We just missed promotion from the Dunlop Young Athletes League this year mainly due to the problems of getting our strongest teams together for the early season matches. Next year we shall try to implement the major single point we picked up that is that the most important step towards winning these matches is to have a sufficiently large team to cover all events both at 'A' and 'B' events.

UK PREP SCHOOLS CHAMPION

11 year old Adrian Ross has put in some very useful sprint times this year and elsewhere you will read of his excellent leg in the 25x1 mile relay. It is perhaps to be expected that he would run a good 400m. Well he did. On June 20th at West London he won the London Area Prep. Schools title in 65.2. After this he was selected to run in the National Prep. Schools Champs where he improved to 64.4 in the semi-finals and then won the final in the excellent time of 64.0.
