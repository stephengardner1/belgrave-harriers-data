EDITORIAL

May we wish you all the very best of health and success in 1962. We could express the usual wishes about a renewal of the Belgrave athletic fortunes but we prefer to emphasise the importance of individual effort within the framework of the Club. If we can find that essential nucleus of champions then assuredly team results will also improve and major championship successes follow. Let us all bear in mind that actions speak far louder than words and the basis of all good athletic performances is TRAINING.

Since our last issue there has been a change in the Editorship. Dave Mitchell has succeeded Malcolm Robinson who recently gained a scholarship at the University of Southern Louisiana, U.S.A. — we wish him the greatest success both athletically and scholastically.

We should like to take this opportunity of welcoming into the Club the many members who have joined us since our last issue — in particular Gerry North. Gerry will be moving to the Wimbledon area in February and if any of our readers know of any furnished flats suitable for him and his wife we, the Editors, shall be pleased to hear from them. We congratulate Gerry on his excellent win in the Inter-Counties Championship and hope he will have many similar successes in Belgrave colours.

Our thanks are extended to Arthur Bruce for the excellent cartoons we include in this issue, and also to Ed Lacey for his many photographs.

Obituary · page 2

R. J. SAVAGE

A.A.HARLEY.

It is with very profound regret that we have to advise the death of Dick Savage, and our sincere sympathy is extended to Mrs. Savage and other members of the family. We shall miss him tremendously; and also the work and enthusiasm he contributed in such ample measure over so many years to the Belgrave Harriers.

It is fifty years since Dick joined the Club as a young man in his early twenties and immediately he made his contribution to the Club, both as a runner and an official. His membership of Prices A.C. helped to forge links with the Belgrave Harriers which were mutually beneficial for very many years. After the First World War, Dick was one of the first, with Bob Ricketts, Jimmy Belchamber, Harry Parker, Oscar Horwood, Harry Evans and others, to start re-building the Club. It was hard work indeed at first; but before long the results began to show, and with the acquisition of new members such as Harry Hare (who was a most efficient Hon. Secretary & Treasurer within twelve months of joining the Club, and Hon. Editor of our first printed Gazette) and stabilisation of our Winter Headquarters at Wimbledon, Belgrave began to forge ahead.

Dick played his effective, though unobtrusive, part within the Club's progress, and was always available and always willing to help in any way possible. Thus the writer remembers him as pointsman, slow-pack leader, doorman at our Dances and Socials - always present, always helpful. In later years he became a respected President of the Club, a most efficient Timekeeper, an R.W.A. Judge, and presented us with the "SAVAGE" Shield for Junior Cross-country Competition. In several years, he served as Junior Captain, placing his experience and knowledge at the disposal of the younger members, doing work which was of the utmost value.

For fifty years Belgrave was a major part of his life, as indeed it has been of Mrs. Savage, who accompanied her husband whenever possible. In recent years, he had played the part of "Father Christmas" at our Childrens Party; which seemed to give added point to those virtues of loyalty, gentleness, kindliness, good-humour and generosity with which we associated Dick as one of Nature's Gentlemen.

We owe him a great debt we can never repay; and we are grateful indeed for his fifty years of ever-helpful membership.

A.A.HARLEY.

THE PRESIDENT

Ernie Duffett

In introducing Arthur Penny as Club President we here print the speech made by Ernie Duffett at this year's Club Dinner.

"Arthur Penny joined the Club in February, 1928 and was known as 'Nutty' in those days, the reason being obvious when you gaze on his shining bald pate. He soon made his mark and was placed 3rd in the Surrey County C.C. Championships in 1929, with myself 4th, Bill Mitchell 5th and we had our scoring 6 in the first 15 to finish. We thus took the team title from South London H. and actually won this championship 8 times out of 9 years and Arthur was our key man in most of these victories.

He was also a member of our winning South of Thames 'Senior' team in 1929, and won the individual title in the Surrey C.C. Championship in 1931, also the 1933 Southern Counties C.C. Championship, which was won by Bert Footer.

In those days it was impossible to see the name of Penny without that of Footer being close alongside, and they became known as the 'Belgrave Twins' and Laurie Richardson, who was then the Secretary of the English C.C. Union had a very high opinion of their capabilities. They finished 4th and 5th in the National C.C. race at Alderley Edge in 1933, in what Arthur has described as his most thrilling race. Arthur also finished 5th in the 1934 National, and was 2nd in the Southern Counties C.C. at Taplow, where Belgrave won the team title for the first time.

In the International C.C. race at Ayr in 1934 Arthur was placed 3rd with Bert Footer 4th, and they helped England to an easy victory in the team championship. I think that Arthur and Bert were the first to start the 7-day week training routine which is now commonplace, but in those days most athletes were content with a run on Tuesday and Thursday evenings and then Saturday afternoon.

Arthur was also a member of our winning London to Brighton Road Relay teams, which was then the National race only, which scored a triumphant hat-trick of wins in 1934, 1935 and 1936, a feat never before accomplished. They were great days for Belgrave and we were almost invincible over road and country, and it was quoted by a well-known economist that 'The sterling qualities of the Belgrave Harriers are clearly based on the right use of the Penny.' Arthur was made a Life Member of the Club in 1934, and then was out of athletics for the whole of the 1935 season due to a fractured bone in his foot. He was back to form in 1936, however, when he again won the Surrey County C.C. championship and also led our team to victory in the team race. He just missed selection for the 1936 Olympic Games, and in 1937 was placed 4th with Pat Parker 3rd, Bert Footer 6th, Tom Carter 7th, Les Cohen 15th and Alf Taylor 16th in the Union Sportive C.C. race near Paris, in which Belgrave won the team race, beating all the leading French and Belgian clubs, and they appeared on television under the Baird process, after which it was agreed by the powers that be, to use the E.M.I. process.

Arthur was also a great man in 2 and 3 miles team races, on the track, and his crowning glory was when he won the Empire Games 6 miles track title in 1934 at White City, London. He also won the Club 3 miles track title many times and from the number of times he won the "Blackstaffe" Shield for the Club 7 miles C.C. Championships, he almost made it a permanent possession on his sideboard. I also remember when he won a Club half-mile handicap off 65 yards and then later he won the Boxing Day 2 miles run off the scratch mark, which takes some doing. Arthur was a very useful runner at all distances, and in 1938 was placed 3rd in the A.A.A. 10 miles track championship. I understand that we have some useful youngsters coming along, and if they will only set their sights on the heights attained by Arthur Penny, then we can ask no more of them.

I ask you to be upstanding and drink the health of our President, Arthur Penny."

3 MILES TRACK CHAMPIONSHIP

Kerr, Newell, Davies, Walker, Black, Dabbs, Linstead, Maclean. How's that for a line up to set a new 3 mile record? Yet these were all the names that did not run. Each no doubt, had excellent reasons for missing this Club Championship, but their absence only helped to boost the ego of the holder, Mal Robinson, who must be forgiven for feeling like the heavyweight boxing champion whom no-one wished to meet.

Mal showed that he too can be unsociable, and after one lap decided to retain his title without company. This was an uncharacteristic move, but despite a very strong head-wind in the back straight, it paid dividends. Jack Brown, who has won this trophy on four consecutive occasions, made every effort to close the gap, but halving it was the most he could manage and so had to be content with second place.

Barry Sawyer moved through the field after a mile and had little difficulty in gaining third place, while Dick Shackleford - always to the fore - finished fourth. New member Laurie O'Hara, principally a footballer, ran very well in his first "three" to finish a surprise fifth.

Little can be said of the remainder. Bruce and Shippen were lethargic and Lennie Bishop faded after a promising start; but young Alan Morrison should feel encouraged, performing as he did in this his first race over the distance.

6 MILES TRACK CHAMPIONSHIP: Robinson's last lap sees him through!

Held on a somewhat cold and blustery evening, this, the last of the club track championships for 1961, was unfortunately made notable by the absence of several prospective champions. After three somewhat abortive laps by Clive Shippen, Malcolm Robinson was in no mood for pleasantries and proceeded to 'sit in' content in the knowledge that his was the faster finish. In the earlier stages Charlie Walker remained in close contact with the two leaders but gradually he was made to yield ground and left to make certain of third place. Some five hundred yards from home Malcolm left Jackie dis-spirited by his enforced role as leader, tip-toed round a final lap of 62 secs. and finished some 70 yrds to the good. This was his last race before leaving for America.

TRACK ROUND-UP

D.E.M.

To be frank, this has in most respects been a disappointing season. There have been too few individual and team successes. Certainly the track reputation of the Club has been enhanced almost solely by the performances of our quarter-milers whereas the field events section has been most notable by its virtual absence.

Pride of place must surely be given to the four Belgrave boys, Peter Eldridge, Brian Morris, Graham Worrall, and Gerry Leroy, who gained second place in the A.A.A's 4 x 440 yds relay. Faced with a Birchfield quartet of international calibre our lads proved themselves to be a most powerful and courageous combination. They finished in a time of 3:15.3, only 2.1 seconds behind the Midland club, broke the existing A.A.A's record, and established a new Club and Southern Counties best performance.

We must also pay tribute to Messrs. Gordon, Thresher, Pepe, and Leroy for their excellent second place in the Surrey County Championships on July 29th. "Evergreen" Gordon battled round the first lap to put us well in the hunt, John kept the pressure on, Pete put us into the places, and Gerry pulled out a 48.6 last lap to put us exactly two seconds behind the Hercules team who broke the existing record set in 1959 by a Belgrave team.

Brian Morris finally established himself as our fastest one lap man of the season by returning 48.0 in the L.C.C. Championships at Hurlingham early in September. As usual Brian ran an 'easy' first 300 yards and in fact only just 'got up' to pip Poly's Mike Potter on the post. If only Brian's pace over the first couple of hundred yards was a little hotter he would almost certainly be down among the 47 second men. How about it then Brian?

Peter Eldridge has again proved himself a versatile, consistent, and capable performer. Once more he completed the sprint hat-trick in the Bedforshire Championships and has had several representative runs throughout the season, won an invitation furlong at the White City, finished second in the London v Rhineland inter-cities quarter at the same venue.

Apart from the quarter-milers and our now flourishing Juniors, there have been few memorable performances or performers. One successful athlete, however, has been a newcomer from north of the border. Bill Kerr has proved himself a popular character, a fine athlete, and a valuable Club member. He established a new club record of 8:55 for two miles in the London Clubs race, and capped a season of fine running by finishing a good second to T.V.H. International Haith in the Melbourne Trophy 3,000 m. at Tooting. He beat several established stars to clock 8:21, a new club best performance.

John Thresher ran several courageous races for the Club, his determination compensating in some measure for his comparative lack of training. His 1:55.0 half-mile placed him second on the club list to Bob Norton for the 1961 season.

Finally some mention must be made of Mal Robinson. His departure to America for a college course will leave us quite considerably the poorer. Impetuous and outspoken he may have been, but his loyalty and enthusiasm were undoubted. Two club track championships became his during the season and his winning of the Kinnaird Trophy mile was a little masterpiece of timing and confident determination. If Mal's spirit were more prevalent throughout Belgrave we would once again be winning the championships and trophies. We wish him all the very best and hope that we shall not have to refer to him in the past tense for too long.

D.E.M.

GREAT RUN BY KERR IN POLYTECHNIC'S RELAY

CHISWICK Sept. 16th (8 x 3mls)

A sensational last-leg stint by Bill Kerr lifted Belgrave into a noteworthy second place, just two secs. ahead of rivals, T.V.H. Amid intense excitement, Bill set off in 4th place 18 secs. behind Herne Hill and a full half minute behind the Valley, and after what must have been the fastest 2 mls. ever run on this course had passed both his rivals! With half a mile to go he was a good 20yds clear, but the fireworks were by no means over, for Saunders of T.V.H. put in a desperate effort to close the gap on the tiring figure ahead. Inch by inch he moved up, until with 100 yds to go, they were level. Accompanied by the frenzied shouts of their supporters, the two runners raced for the line, but Bill proved too strong for the gallant Valley man and in clocking 13:02, became the second fastest runner of the day.

Forty three teams started in this popular event, and Belgrave fielded four of them. Eric Gebbett ran his usual reliable first stage, keeping us in contact with the leaders and Charlie Walker consolidated this by moving us up to 3rd position. On the third leg, Jack Brown maintained our place with an excellent 13:36, and although he was the "A" team's slowest man, John Thresher (14:09) ran well enough to take us up to second, 43 secs. behind the eventual winners - Polytechnic Harriers.

John Davies, Ron Linstead and Alan Black ran the next three stages all within 15 secs. of each other, and although we dropped two places we were still within striking distance of second place - as Bill Kerr showed only too well!

Our "B", "C", and "D" teams also produced some good performances notably from Maclean, Sawyer and Forey, and all but the "C" team closed in. An excellent turn-out.

KERR WINS 5¾ ROAD RACE

Used for assessing form for the forthcoming Brighton Relay, this race was held on a heavy sultry day, not conducive to good running. The whole race was run in a lethargic manner and it was soon obvious that, barring accident, Bill Kerr would be the winner. Jack Brown made a valiant effort to keep the gap as small as possible between himself and the loping Scot ahead, but Bill seemed to have the matter well in hand at all times.

The early part of the race had seen the field well bunched for the first lap of the Common, but down Copse Hill, the leaders were strung out with Kerr, Brown, Gebbett, Walker and Sawyer prominent. Linstead took a steady start which in the end paid dividends, for he moved through to third position. Lower down the field John Davies and Cyril Forey were running together - the former running within his obvious capabilities, the latter short of training and feeling his way carefully.

It was good to see John Moore back in club races, we need his help and hope he can regain his position of a year or two ago.

The final times were disappointingly slow, probably due to the weather, but it was pleasing to see the leaders fairly well bunched.

SOUTHERN LONDON-BRIGHTON RELAY: DISAPPOINTING 6th BY BELGRAVE

'Olympian'

Our performance in the National Relay in April when we finished fifth, encouraged us to think we would be amongst the honours for this event, which is limited only to Southern clubs. On paper our team appeared to be strong enough to give even Portsmouth a run for their money, but we were to be disappointed. Our confidence proved to be ill-founded and we were to finish no higher than sixth.

The usual crop of scares during the week before the race kept the selection committee on tenterhooks but in the end the team that turned out was almost up to full strength.

The day of the race dawned fine and clear and the team manager at Westminster thought he would have little or nothing to worry about. Two runners and a reserve are instructed to report to Great Smith Street Baths at 9.00a.m. for the 10.o'clock start. One of the runners and the reserve duly appeared, but the second leg runner was missing, and the minutes ticked by without a sign of the absent Belgravian. The team manager's fingernails got shorter and shorter and his nerves more and more frayed. The anxious reserve was duly despatched to Clapham Common and the race was started without news of Pat Newell.

STAGE I - Alan Black ( 21:14. 7th fastest) Alan set off along the Embankment not knowing to whom he would pass his baton. This may have had some effect on his running for he could do no better than seventh, 26 secs. behind Harvey of Mitcham who gave his Club a perfect start. Alan has run well to Queens Circus but seemed to lose his grip on the race over the next half mile; a set-back from which he did not recover.

STAGE II - Pat Newell ( 25:18. 3rd fastest) On arrival at Clapham Common we were delighted to find Pat Newell on his mark, but it would have saved many problems if instructions had been carried out, or a 'phone call made to Bill Lucas. Pat set off in grand style and soon closed the gap on the teams ahead, with the exception of Portsmouth, who moved quickly into the lead with an excellent run by B. Cooke. Haith of T.V.H. caught and passed Newell by the time Tooting Bec Station was reached, but Pat kept his head and in the end took us up to third position, 36 secs, behind the leaders; an excellent effort.

STAGE 3 - Ron Linstead (29:47. 9th) Ron was soon passed by Richards of Polytechnic, but hung on gamely and at the beginning of Purley Way was still in 4th place, although losing ground on the leaders. Lewis of Woodford, running at his best, and Perkins of Ilford were making great progress from behind and up the long slope past Croydon Airport forced Ron to concede further places. At Purley Ron overtook Stacey of T.V.H. and we were fifth at the change-over, 77secs. behind Portsmouth who had consolidated their lead and were never again to be headed.

STAGE 4 - Barry Sawyer (26:15. 8th) This stage had been given to one of our most improved runners who was performing in a "Brighton" for the first time. Barry runs immense distances in training and is a very fit man, but lacks the necessary speed at the moment for good relay running: it is possible that a day's rest from the hard daily grind before a race such as this would produce a little more snap. Nevertheless Barry ran well and although losing two places to Mitcham and T.V.H. had established himself and will without doubt earn many more team selections.

STAGE 5 - Eric Gebbett (22:36. 8th) Always prepared to run on whichever leg he is most needed, Eric was unfortunate enough to set off through Redhill with a gap of 45 secs. between himself and the runner ahead. This was not the ideal set-up for Eric, he runs at his best when in close contact, and in consequence we did not benefit from Eric's considerable experience as we had hoped. Eric reached Earl's Wood still in 6th place and nearer to the fifth team, but was nearly three minute behind Portsmouth who were increasing their lead on every leg.

STAGE 6 - John Davies (13:59 4th ) John did not produce the performance of which we all know he is capable. In the past year or so he has seemed afraid to really let himself go. He puts in many training sessions and with a 4:10 mile as a basis should be one of our fastest runners over distances up to four miles. This leg is the shortest of all and is generally given to the weaker members of each team, so although he produced the fourth best time today, and gained one place, his effort was still - to some of us - somewhat disappointing.

STAGE 7 - Charlie Dabbs (19:06. 7th) Coming into the team as a result of the illness of John Thresher and an injury to Brian Hallowell, Chas. ran a steady leg across Gatwick Airport but was obviously still feeling the effects of his enforced layoff a few weeks earlier when he underwent a nasal operation.

STAGE 8 - Jack Brown (31:50. 15th) Jack took over still in 6th place but 3½ mins. behind Portsmouth. Jack was obviously not at his best, and although plugging along on what is the hardest stage of all, was noticeably losing ground on all teams ahead. We have become accustomed to take for granted the fine performances of this experienced runner, but his run on this occasion gave us the final proof that this was not to be our day. At Handcross we were over five minutes behind.

STAGE 9 - Bill Kerr ( 30:16. 12th fastest) Once again on the leg over which he had run so well in April, Bill could not reproduce similar form and we were destined to fall even further behind, although keeping in 6th place. Bill had been suffering with a slight leg injury during the previous week and this may well have had a bad effect on his running, combined with the sight of a road ahead bare of runners - not exactly the right incentive for a fast run.

STAGE 10 - Charlie Walker (21:09. 5th fastest) Chas. gave a little on T.V.H. and Mitcham, but the gap was far too great to give the supporters any hope that we would finish higher than sixth. Charlie's effort was completely solo, T.V.H. being 2½ mins. ahead and Essex Beagles 1½ mins. behind!

STAGE 11 - Clive Shippen (21:09. 4th fastest) Clive made further progress up Dale Hill and it was good to see our cross-country captain regaining the form he showed on this leg two years ago. Of the five teams ahead, we gained on Mitcham once more, and on Polytechnic, who were proving to be the surprise team of the day.

STAGE 12 - Cyril Forey (17:50. 2nd fastest) Cyril set off on the last stage into Brighton with the gap ahead still over two minutes. He nevertheless put in an excellent run which gave him 2nd fastest time of the day and we arrived in Brighton in the 6th position we had held since the half-way mark.

We have to do much better than this next April if we are to be amongst the leading teams. Perhaps we need some new blood in our teams. Heck, Miller, Thresher, Moore and Johnston are but a few knocking on the door to 1st team honours. There are also those up-and-coming lads Cocks, Hampshire and Fairclough who will be dis-placing a few old-timers before long.

THE A.G.M.

HARRY SHIELDS

HARRY SHIELDS REPORTS.

Another cycle of club life was marked by the A.G.M., when we were called to review our hopes and failures of the past 12 months to formulate our plans for the ensuing year, and to exercise our democratic privileges of criticising and electing Officers to whom we entrust the well-running of the Club.

The nature of athletics is such that there is a large turnover of men entering and leaving the sport and although for stability it is essential to have the continuity of a number of grey-heads, it is even more important we delegate responsibility to younger members who have the necessary enthusiasm and interest of the sport at heart.

At this year's meeting, we missed a number of the more voluble members from whom we have grown accustomed to learn our defects, but were amply compensated by a good gathering of youths and juniors, whose contribution to the discussions and willingness to join actively in the administration of the Club was a pleasing feature.

Of the proceedings, we first had a report by the Hon. Secretary on the past year, from which we saw that our walkers still reigned supreme, the runners had many brilliant performances to their credit, but the field events men required strengthening and better leadership. The Hon. Treasurer's report showed we had little to fear financially.

THE CLUB DINNER - 1961

Ladies seem to spend all day preparing for it, speakers are supposed to practise for hours in front of a mirror, and the day after usually means an overdose of aspirins and liver salts. What is it? Why the Belgrave annual dinner, of course!

This year's function was held in the Ardington Rooms at Clapham Junction on November 18th, and as usual was made memorable by an overall atmosphere of warmth and fellowship. This keynote of friendship was initiated by our new club President, for Arthur and his wife were on the threshold, as it were, to welcome us into the fold.

The after-dinner speeches were all quite brief and lively. Mr. E. Clynes, Secretary of the A.A.A, paid tribute to the great contribution our club has made to athletics. Harry Shields, with the help of Kipling and Shakespeare, made a most suitable response and managed to pull Bob Barnes's leg into the bargain. Alf Harley introduced and welcomed several guests in his accustomed informative manner - by the way, did you know that Harry Evans is now 80 years young and was invited as a guest to the dinner? Ernie Duffet stepped into a last minute breach and told us of the mammoth achievements of Arthur Penny in the '30's - I bet many youngsters were thinking, 'I didn't know he was that good'. Finally, Arthur paid tribute to his clubmates such as Bert 'Syrup of Figs' Footer, and expressed his pride in being honoured with this year's Presidency.

After these somewhat informal formalities (!) dancing (and drinking of course) continued until half past eleven and Sid Roberts and the Social Committee were happy in the knowledge that a good time had been had by all.

THE BELGRAVE OPEN YOUTHS ROAD RELAY

OLYMPIAN

October 21st 1961.

Facing the starter this year were some 37 teams, certain of whom had travelled from places as far afield as Sheffield, Coventry, Luton, Reading, Oldbury, Brighton and Chelmsford to compete in this 4 x 2¼ mile event. This fact offers some indication of the increasing popularity of the event.

Belgrave were fielding the strongest team and the first leg was entrusted to Alan Morrison, who brought us home in 9th place - somewhat disappointing but nevertheless a good effort in the face of such strong opposition. Next came Ken Redwood, who set about the teams ahead in no uncertain fashion, moving up to fifth position by the time the village had been reached. Unfortunately, a section of the field went off course and about 10 runners took a short cut which sliced off some 500 yds. and brought them into the early places. Redwood therefore found himself handing over with no less than 13 teams ahead. Jackie Hampshire thoroughly enjoyed himself and passed 10 of those ahead to bring us up to 4th - an excellent performance which gave him one of the fastest times of the day. David Cocks running the race of his life, gave everything he possessed and took us up to 2nd only 3 secs. behind the Luton boy Hyde, who had previously caught and passed him to put up a first-class performance.

The confusion caused by those teams mistaking the course presented a most difficult problem for the officials to resolve. In view of the high standard of organisation this was a most unfortunate incident and it can only be hoped that it will not occur again.

The result from the Belgrave viewpoint was most satisfactory and points to a good season for our ever improving youths. Their success will be almost entirely due to the guiding hand of Tom Carter.

We hear from people:

Have you ever thought what photographer Eddie Lacey thinks about this training and racing business?

Well Eddie, following hard upon our appeal for magazine material sent us some jottings that summarised his views. In his time, Eddie must have met a great number of athletes and it seems fairly obvious that his opinion cannot be dismissed as mere pipe-dreaming. Whether or not the pained expressions and agonised features that he usually snaps led him to his conclusion is not clear, but he maintains that training does help!

"Some of us are blessed with natural ability. Some have none. To those who have it and can compete on little training, I say train harder. To those who have no natural ability, I say you are the ones who have to train harder than harder."

Furthermore Eddie suggests that a great many athletes train in too stereo-typed manner - "experiment with your training" he suggests - and although your resolution and determination must always remain unchanged, your training methods should be adopted and possibly discarded in a quest for the ideal schedule and routine.

Senior or Junior, champion or scrubber, runner or walker, however we are all Belgravians and that means we are proud of wearing the claret and gold. Above all it means that we shall never be content with our racing or our final positions until the 'Bels' are once again in the rightful position at the top of the athletics tree.

Thanks Eddie for writing to us! We now know at least a couple of things about you. Firstly that you're not just a sadist delighting in candid camera shots of athletes wallowing in the "slough of despond" at Parliament Hill. Secondly that you are a true Belgravian with the interests of the Club always at heart.

* * * * *

Ranjit Bhatia, now at St. Stephen's College, Delhi University, tells us he is beginning to find form again after a lengthy ankle injury which prevented him from running for a large part of the Indian C.C. season. He recommenced racing on September 23rd when he finished 2nd in the Delhi 6 ml. road race championship. A fortnight later, in the Delhi state track relays, Ranjit ran an excellent 1,600 metres leg in which he gave the state champion 30 metres and a beating! He says however, that he is nowhere near his 1960 peak of fitness yet. On September 28th he won a 3,000 metres in 9 min. 32 secs. and followed this nine days later with a 56.1 victory over 400 metres. Two wins in one afternoon - 1,500 metres (4:8.6) and 5000 metres (15:16.0) - were chalked up on November 18th and a month later he reduced his metric mile time to 4:5.8 in the annual fixture between Delhi University and Aligarh University. These performances are most encouraging after a lengthy lay-off and we look forward to return of the scintillating form he produced in 1960.

Ranjit has been endeavouring to change the fixed ideas Indian distance runners have on training. He tells us "the local athletes used to think I was quite wrong when I suggested that distance runners ought to do most of their training in parks and on hills and not on the track. Our coaches in India have got their ideas on distance running from the Americans,anyway after two of our men from St. Stephen's - at my suggestion - took to the English type training and improved by fantastic margin, they are beginning to feel that I am not off my head after all!" Both these men are now the top performers at Delhi University - a fitting tribute to Ranjit's guidance. Ranjit himself is still wearing Belgrave Colours and hopes he will be able to run " a Brighton or two" within the next few years. Nothing would delight us more than to see his hopes materialise.

To all at Belgrave Ranjit Bhatia sends New Year greetings.

Dave Mitchell

Immediately after last year's Club '10', your new Co-Editor, Dave Mitchell sent us the following letter which was not then included in the current Belgravian as that was essentially a track and Summer issue and cross-country matters were hardly topical. Since the present issue, however, should be in your hands at about the time this year's event, we consider the merits of this letter can best be judged with close reference to the Club '10' of 1962.

Dear Sir,

"What's good enough for Elliott ought to be good enough for us!" So retorted an onlooking Belgravian when several competitors in the Club 10 had the effrontery to complain of the hazards and difficulties presented by the crossing of the brook. Ignoring the rather infantile Elliott argument - for I'm sure that Jazy, Waern, Hewson, Lincoln et. al. will maintain that what's good for 'our Herbie' is most definitely not good for everybody else - I most certainly would suggest that some alteration of the course might be seriously considered to obviate any chances of the accident that annually threatens to take place. " Horses for courses" might well be an apt adage in this case - but I beg to remind you that unlike our equine counterparts, we have only two legs and furthermore we should like to remain that way.

The Witney '12' :

R.L.

A field of 60 runners took part in this event, run over three circuits of a 4 mile lap. Eric Gebbett was soon up among the leaders battling gamely, at this stage Kilby (Godiva)and Edwards (Bristol) were running first and second. What a tenacious runner Eric is, he went through one mile in 4:45 - not much outside his best for the distance - in an effort to hold his rivals. The most improved Belgravian of the year, Barry Sawyer, also started in one of the leading groups, whilst Ron Linstead was once more holding back. Over the later stages Eric tired somewhat and only held off Barry by 3 secs. Ron went by them both, to demonstrate the effectiveness of even-paced running. He overtook Barry at 6 miles and Eric at 9, although he fought hard. The final positions gave us third place in the team race behind two strong Bristol teams. It is interesting to note that our three runners were more than likely be the backbone of our Southern and National teams; thank heavens for plodding(?) marathon runners!

THE BELGRAVE OPEN '20'

August 19th

Walton's Ken Honney was a fine winner of the 10th Belgrave Open '20', scoring a well-deserved victory over Hicks of T.V.H. in 1hr. 50mins 13 secs. - the fifth fastest time on record.

This year's promotion was poorly supported, however, and there was a record 'low' of 49 finishers. This was probably due to the ever-increasing number of road-running fixtures and the previous week's Enschede Marathon which was supported by many home runners.

After the usual fast start a group of five runners detached themselves from the field and it was quite apparent that they would not be troubled by the mass behind. With 3 miles to go, Ken Honney broke clear to score a popular win, with Gobey (H.H.H) just pushed into third place.

Belgrave were without Sawyer and Linstead, but Denis Jones ran very well and with the support of Norman Stairs and Len Bishop, we were placed third.

Hogs Back 9½ :

Held on December 16th over a one-way course finishing in Guildford a depleated Belgrave "A" team could only manage to finish 11th of the 40 teams competing. At the half-way mark, with the long uphill drag over, Barry Sawyer was still in contact with the leaders and Clive Shippen was making steady progress through the field with a group in the forties. Our new Australian member Doug Chugg, was in the sixties having faded after a fast start but this was his first race of the season so a few more races should give him that competitive edge. Following up were Johnston and Jones neither quite at their best. With 2½ miles to go and only the long downhill run to come, Barry was lying ninth and Clive 29th but neither was happy with steep descent and they faded 5 and 8 places respectively.

Although disappointed with his run, Barry had held his own against top class opposition for a large part of the way. Yet this race was used only as part of his strenuous training schedule - for not only had he run 15 miles the previous day, but also 8 on the morning of the race. Add to this a two mile run from home to the start, the race itself, and an 11½ mile run home and you can see how this great prospect gets in his 140 miles a week.

From THE BELGRAVIAN 25 years ago

1937: The Surrey County Championship was held at Epsom Downs again this year, on the 2nd January. We were well placed at the start, and after about a mile and a half Hines, of Surrey, was leading, with about nine of ours in the first thirty.At three miles Bert Footer was on Hines' shoulder, having apparently done a bit of "snaky" running when out of the sight of spectators. Penny was next, followed by Carter, Cohen, Ring, and Wood (who won the Surrey Junior Race the week after) in the first twenty places. Our hopes of repeating our win of last year were now very bright; and we next saw the field coming round Tattenham Corner led by Bert Footer. Then came Penny, fifth, Carter sixth, Cohen seventh, Ring thirteenth, and Wood seventeenth. This scoring enabled us to win comfortable from Surrey A.C., thus making our tenth win in the last twelve years.

BELS. WIN MITCHAM '15'

Our three 'A' team runners in this event Barry Sawyer, Chas. Walker and Bill Kerr had a most successful afternoon's competition. With three to score for the Jim Peters Trophy our boys beat the Bristol leading trio by 16 points, and in the Fry Trophy competition for six runners our first three boys together with D.W.Jones (55) Len (64) and Alan Bishop (67) beat Epsom & Ewell Harriers by one point after a recount. All round, this was a most satisfactory afternoon's running from the Belgrave viewpoint, and one which we hope heralds in future successes on the road.
