EDITORIAL

How heartening it is to see our youngsters pushing the name of our Club to the fore.

Only eighteen months after the first girls joined Belgrave Harriers the signs are that we have the fastest improving ladies walking team in the country. The great thing is that they become stronger with every week that passes and all have youth on their side. Whilst most of our lady walkers have already made their names well known to us through their deeds on road and track it must be mentioned that another lass, fourteen year old Joann Warnett, is also making a name for herself in the world of cross country running, winning the Wandsworth Schools event, finishing highly placed in the Greater London Schools race and gaining selection to run in the All England Schools Championship.

Do not imagine, however, that our young men are letting the ladies have the stage to themselves. Junior Captain Richard Dorman leads by example and this past winter season has seen him develop into a fine walker, already making his presence felt among the seniors, and as our leading Youth cross country runner has brought the Bels to victory in their Surrey C.C. League events. The cross country team that followed Richard is being viewed with great interest as well, for all still remain Youths next winter.

With the summer season now approaching it will be interesting to see how Mark Shippen continues his progress in the pole vault. Last year while still a youth he ranked =22nd in the Nation's Senior list and 5th among the Juniors. And of course there are quite a few others - jumpers, throwers and runners - who have also already made their mark in Senior competition while still teenagers and who should be ready to improve again with another winter under their belts.

This progress has not come about by pure chance. Three men have put a great deal of their time and energy into organising our young teams' training and competition - Carl Lawton (walking), Ray Hall (Ladies cross country, track and field) and Leo Coy (Youths and Boys). Their tasks can be counted perhaps the most rewarding in our sport; continued at the foot of page 5.

Column · page 4

grapevine

Stuart Masterson has returned to Australian for a couple of years; let us hope that we have not seen the last of this friendly road runner. Stuart would be more than pleased to hear how his old colleagues are getting on so why not drop him a line - 56 Birtwistle Plac[illegible] Poleystone 6111, Western Australia.

We are also very sad to see the return to New Zealand of Graham Seatter but hopefully we will be able to see him in action later this year via our TV screens when the Commonwealth Games at Edmonton, Canada, take place. Graham made many friends while over here - walke[illegible] and road runners - and this is one of the great things about our spor[illegible] our common interest in athletics that serves as a basis to meet and enjoy the company of folk of all nationalities, from all walks of life

However, another Belgravian is planning to return to the UK this summer - Allan Callow. Allan, a past committee member of our club has enjoyed a deal of success while in New Zealand. On October 8th he won an over distance 20 mile Walk at Dunedin beating the NZ 50Km Champion John Rayner in the process, having set a pb of 36:03 for 5 miles the previous week.

Many congratulations to Carl and Barbara Lawton on the birth of son Colin, who arrived on October 23rd weighing 7lb 12oz. And while on the subject of prospective members we must also welcome Faye, daughter of Bernard and Margaret Merry, who was born in December

Charlie Speechley recently received a 90th Birthday Card signed by the President and a number of members. He also received a presentation plaque from the Veterans A.C. He was at the Murphy Shield (10 miles walk) Race and was soon chatting to old friends. He also made the acquaintance of our youngest member, Colin Lawton - 90 years between them!

Surrey County Womens' A.A.A. soon took advantage of our facilities for the ladies. They held their cross country championships there in December. In charge of the proceedings was Win. Shepherd with a number of members helping in the background. We were pleased to hear that Past President of the Assn., Mrs Savage (Win's mother) is still about and enjoying life at eighty plus.

The Boxing Day Meeting usually attracts the odd older member and this year it was F.A. (Mick) Walker, cross country and road runner of the thirties and forties. He scored in a number of Surrey County Championship wins, but he was perhaps best known for his runs at 20 miles and the marathon (nothing longer in those days). During World War II he won the Finchley '20'. He has lived in Cornwall for a number of years.

John Knifton writes from Austin (Texas) where it is much hotter than it is here. Because of the heat he trains late in the evening but even so it is still too hot for speed work. Consequently when he goes to a cooler place for a race (some hundreds of miles away in many cases) the lack of speed work plus Anno Domini begins to tell. However it is good to see his name occasionally in the U.S.A. results. He hopes to visit the U.K. in 1979 - give him a number Carl!

Bernard Eglington informs us that he is retiring this year. He will be cutting down on his forays into Lancashire for the occasional spin. He plans to keep his tracksuit aired regularly on the front at Colwyn Bay. When he first joined us he was a runner with the R.A.F. (he also sported a Derby & County Vest). He switched to walking and soon became a regular member of our teams at all distances gaining a number of winning and place medals.

Merton Schools' have a nice muddy cross country course from their sports ground at Beverley Meads (hard by Beverley Brook and the woods at the foot of Wimbledon Common). A number of Belgravians were representing their schools at this year's championships; star of the meeting was Richard Dorman, Raynes Park High School, who not only won the race for the oldest age group but set a new course record.

Warmest congratulations to our Q.P.H. member, Cyril Gittins, upon being elected President of the Southern Area of the R.W.A. Cyril has also had a spell in the Chair, is a Grade I Timekeeper, and is a regular official at our Battersea Park meetings. A "back-room boy" whose continued service makes our sport possible.

You should have seen them ... past champions Arthur Penny and Tom Carter showing each other their pensioners bus passes while waiting for the runners to reappear after the first lap of our cross country course. We won't insult them by saying how spritely and fit they look, as we know full well that the only reason they don't don a pair of shorts each and set out with the competitors is that they might embarrass the Club by getting among the scorers again!

Meanwhile, one of their peers, Harry Shields, always searching for another cultural pursuit, has taught himself to paint by attending evening classes, and we are pleased to say that some of Harry's work was recently on exhibition. What talent we have in the Club.

Well done Don Maclean, who has managed to get his name on the "Denmark Pot" by winning our Darts Championship. It is particularly appropriate that Don should win this event at last for he was one of its instigators some dozen or so years ago.

At the end of 1977 the total strength of Belgrave Harriers stood at 563 members - 494 men (278 Seniors, 30 Juniors, 26 Youths, 107 Boys, 25 Associate Members, 7 Honorary Members and 21 Second-claim Members) and 69 women (9 Seniors, 6 Juniors, 49 Girls, 2 Associate Members and 3 Second-claim Members).

WALKING

Carl Lawton, Walking Hon. Secretary.

National Medals for a Belgrave Ladies Team. - Not an impossible dream but almost a reality. In the National Women's 5Kms walk championship at Birmingham in December we finished 5th only 7 points from 2nd place and that was with the misfortune of a disqualification without which we would have been placed.

Since the last edition we have increased our ladies section by a sufficient number to give us a team to be reckoned with. It took eighteen months to gently suggest to Shirley Rook that it would be the best policy for her to join us and eventually she made her decision and is now our number 1. Also joining us recently was Linda Nicholls who will be able to compete in the team in a few months. (She was quickly followed by her brother Michael).

As to the men - 2nd overall in the National rankings - we would have won it had we started a Youths team in '76. But this year we have already started off on the wrong foot. The National, Junior, Youths and Boys at Wimbledon, was the first time that the Boys championship was to be included in the tables. Fortunately we just started a boy[illegible] team but not the team it should have been and we finished last. In the Youths we did not start a team although one was entered. Fourth in the Juniors was the best we could do with what we had but if..... Thanks are extended to all who turned up to help make the event a success.

Graham Seatter has returned home and will leave a gap difficult to fill. He managed to fit into 12 months what most of us would do in three years. He arrived as a "useful" walker, he went through bad spells, foot injury, anaemia, a hospital operation, had holidays in Scotland, Ireland, Cornwall and the Isle of Man but still managed to win the National Junior, Leicester 7, Lambeth 10Kms, Enfield 20Km[illegible] and finally his parting triumph the Belgrave 7 and of course he was part of the many Belgrave team victories during the year.

One of the dangers of signing a new walker who will immediately fill a team place is that he will push out one of the old brigade. This method has been tried for years but Ray Middleton still comes back!! Always a stalwart, always there in the Open team wins, and first man in the National 50Kms, Hastings and Brighton.

John Hall is a hard worker who arrived on the Belgrave scene in 1971 as an average mid field man. Over the years he trained, he raced and slowly improved, then '77 was the year that he made the grade. Six years to get there but now, in the first 10 in the opens, an equivalent of sub 52 minutes for 7 miles in the 11Kms at Imber Court, a sub 70 minutes 15Kms and then the Sussex 10 miles title in '78. He also did the Hastings and beat a few notable distance men.

Looking to 1978, well by the time you read this the National 10 will have passed by. National Titles are not impossible but............ There is always a team placed to be filled - Bob Picton at Highgate, Jon Dunsford at Luton, Richard Dorman (in his first outing as a team member) at Imber Court. We have a few new faces in the club and the future is rosy if the backing can be provided - a turn out of four starters in the 50 kilos will not win the team of eight!

Carl Lawton, Walking Hon. Secretary.

SEPTEMBER 3rd 1977 - LONDON TO BRIGHTON. LEICESTER'S TURN!

G.S.D.

This year saw 69 starters in the Open London to Brighton Walk, five of whom were ours. The weather was kind, a little breeze and a little sun but no rain. In the early stages Ray was with the leaders with Dave and Ron walking together not too far down the field. The two Johns completed our team and, if all went well, one of these would be our fourth scorer. By twenty miles Ray had lost a few minutes on the leaders who were moving very well. About halfway John Morris retired; our remaining four were still going strong. Dave was able to open up a gap on Ron (suffering the effects of a recent cold) but Ron always had Dave in sight on the long stretches - it's always good to have someone in view on these long efforts!

Thus the race had developed and history was made when John Lees became the first Brighton walker to win this event, his time of 7h.54m. 52s being very good indeed. He was well ahead of Alan Moulinet the Frenchman who had won the Hastings to Brighton a few weeks earlier. Ray was beaten into fourth place by Ken Harding (Royal Sutton Coldfield) who this year broke Pat Duncan's Manchester to Blackpool record. Soon the blue vests of Leicester came rolling in and they had three men home before our second man, Dave, came in and before Ron came in a fourth Leicester man appeared to give them the team title. Congratulations to Leicester who won the race thirty years ago. The battle for placings in the team race was now on, Ron soon came in followed by a Brighton man and with three men home from each team Brighton had a two points advantage. A stolid John Keown arrived four places ahead of our rivals and so we took second medals. This was John's 17th consecutive Brighton!

Thanks again to the usual band of helpers, particularly Richard Dorman who cycled down. G.S.D.

NOVEMBER 12th 1977 - ENFIELD. DOUBLE EVENT

G.S.D.

On November 12th Club history was made at Enfield for not only did we notch up another scratch team in their 54th Open 7 miles walk but our girls won the team race in the Senior over fifteen 5,000 metres. The meeting comprised five events and we were represented in four of them.

Taking the afternoon's events in order, first the Girls (11-15)2,500m and amongst those lined up was Tracey McDermot. This was her first race and wisely she let the more experienced ones go away, nevertheless she persevered with a fine style and with more experience is going to be challenging the others.

Next the Senior Women's 5,000m which turned out to be between Epsom and ourselves. Anna soon established herself with the leaders with Diane not too far behind and Jane putting up her usual fight to keep us in contention. With vocal support our trio maintained their positions to give us a one point victory. Congratulations to them - may this be the first of many.

Meanwhile the Boys (13-15) 5,000, had got under way and Kevin McDermot was already making his presence felt, but as the race progressed some of his speedier rivals pulled away, but he showed his fighting qualities to bring out a personal best.

The main event, the 7 miles saw the Mexican E.Vera in action, for two of the three laps he led Bob Dobson (Southend), but on the third lap he was away on his own to finish in 48:44 with Dobson second. Easily the best of the remainder was Graham, whilst Carl was showing young Colin what its' all about (he slept through the proceedings). John was in good form and soon appeared and it was not long before Ray came in to give us another team win.

Shortly before the hour was up Trevor McDermott crossed the line - only 16 years of age and '7 in the hour' in his first season. Trevor and Kevin are both strong with good styles and are training under the eye of the old master himself, Ray Middleton. Another improving youngster showing his paces was Peter Morris who is showing some of the Seniors a clean pair of heels.

Feature · page 18

MONEY MATTERS £

K.W. Miller.

At the A.G.M. last September Ken Miller, one of our co-Auditors incisively outlined the Club's financial position. The Committee regards it as important that all our members should be aware of the constant struggle to make ends meet and we therefore reproduce below Ken's comments.

"The Accounts for the year to 30th June 1977 show a loss of £663 of which £246 represents a provision for depreciation. There was thus a cash loss of £417. Apart from a very small deficit in 1975, this is the only occasion since before 1970 that the Club has suffered an outflow of cash. As explained by the Treasurer, the reason in part for this outflow, has been the restricted use of the Hall for social activities during the building programme.

The Balance Sheet at 30th June 1977 shows that after meeting our immediate liabilities, we have surplus cash of £867. Please remember this figure. But what of the future? If we assume that for the year to June 1978, expenditure will increase by 10%; subs. will be static[illegible] Building Society interest will reduce to a small figure; other income (football scheme, hire of hall, socials, etc.) will increase by £500 - the Club will have a cash loss of £500 for the year. But the position is worse than this, for we have already received £400 advance subscriptions. Thus our cash position will deteriorate by £900 during the current year on these assumptions. If we refer back to the fact that we had at June 1977 £867 surplus cash, we could find that the money has run out by next June.

But the problems do not stop at June 1978 for we have already received and effectively spent £400 of subscriptions for the year 1978/79.

A Club with the responsibility of a building such as Belgrave Hall, must in my view have some reserves upon which to fall back for major repairs. We have therefore not just to seek to cover our cash outgoings but also to cover a charge for depreciation to provide a build up of cash for this purpose. A sensible depreciation charge each year on the improved Belgrave Hall would be in the region of £350-£400.

To summarise, we need to improve the financial position of Belgrave Harriers by at least £1,000 per annum. This can only be achieved in two ways -

(1) Cutting costs (marginal scope) (2) Increasing income - from socials, hire of hall, etc. - from subscriptions.

Under the Rules of the Club, subscriptions cannot be increased until 1st July 1978 but it is interesting to note that currently subs. only just cover the cost of printing, stationery and postage (including the "Belgravian").

It is the Auditors' prime function to report on the past but my co-Auditor and I felt we would be failing in our duty if we did not underline this evening the potentially serious financial future facing the Club."

K.W. Miller.

YOUTHS AND BOYS

Leo Coy - Youths, Boys and Colts Secretary

Cross Country Season.

After an initial shock over the resignation of Alan Perriam, who has moved north, the season proved to be one of our best for some years. Alan has already been greatly missed and we hope that he will return in the not too distant future.

The highlight of the season was our team victory in the Surrey League Our result at Wimbledon, like the Seniors, set us up for the rest of the season. This was our first win in any cross country race for a number of years and is an encouraging sign for the future. Of our scoring teams in the league only Richard Dorman won't be eligible for next year. So we intend to show the seniors again how it should be done by winning Div. I in 1978/9.

Another achievement was our good turnout in the Surrey Championships where we completed a team, despite a number of absentees in every age group. Next year with David North eligible to run for us we stand a great chance of being in the medals in the Youths race.

Our turnout in the HHH league at Brockwell Park was very disappointing. We could have won this if everybody had turned out. Hopefully we can improve next year.

Track Season.

The coming track season has a new feature in that we shall be competing in the Rosenheim League for the first time. There is a separate section for a youths team, with one scoring in each event and any number of non-scorers. The league is held over five matches with the first three teams going into the final.

The Boys/Colts will again be competing in the Ebbisham League but included for the first time are second year boys.

If any young athletes want to take part in the above leagues please see me at Battersea Park or give me a ring.

Note to older Members and Parents.

I still need help to run this section of the club. If you feel that you can help in any way please get in touch with me. The club has a better gathering of young athletes now than at any time before so it is up to everyone to make sure that they become the seniors of the future.

Training.

Club training sessions will be held on Sunday morning, Tuesday and Thursday evenings throughout the spring and early summer for all events.

... and 40 years ago: R.W.A. CHAMPIONSHIPS.......1938

The accomplishment which must command our attention above all other matters, and merits our sincerest congratulation, is that of our walkers in winning the R.W.A. 10 miles "Junior" Championship, R.W.A. 20 Miles Championship and R.W.A. 50 Kilos. Championship. No other club has ever at any time won all three championships and to do so in the space of three months is almost miraculous. This record of success is one that may never again be approached by any club, and we most heartily congratulate and thank the members of the winning teams.

The performance of Colin Sutton in appearing in all three championship teams is one that may never be equalled. He was sixth in the "Junior", fourth in the 20 Miles Championship, and fifth in the 50 Kilos.Championship - a trio of performances that reflect the greatest credit on Colin's conscientious training and determined efforts to help the Club to a treble victory.

Others deserving our congratulations are Fred Bentley, who was placed second in both 20 Miles and 50 Kilos events; and Joe Coleman and Len Coleman, who dead-heated in the "Junior" Championship for first place.

It is interesting to note that as a result of the Winter season's activities, all five trophies which the Road Walking Association offer for competition (the "National" Shield, "Garnett" Cup, "Horlick" Cup, "Nijmegan" Shield and "Berman" Cup) are in the possession of Belgrave Harriers!

FEBRUARY 18th 1978 - MILFORD - SURREY C.C. LEAGUE DIV.2. GOING UP!

G.S.D.

A year ago we were bemoaning the fact that we had finished bottom of Division I of the N.Surrey League and would be relegated. "Only for a year" we said. On February 18th we travelled to Milford for the final fixture of the season. We were 80 points up on Ranelagh H and we knew that they were fielding a very strong team, so a keen race was expected. The course was over sandy paths over heathland; the recent weather had covered them with snow, making them slippery in places.

Our Youths had already shown their views on being in Division II by winning their section (they do not have as many races as the Seniors) Our hosts, Guildford & Godalming A.C.) had put on a race for them. David North scored a convincing win, with the improving Mark Sinclair fourth and in eighth position the diminutive Gary Williscroft. Later a blood-stained Ray McCann (he had had a fall) stormed in. Scoring three a side we won from the local club. Great running from great prospects, keep it up!

The Senior race, being over three laps, gave us a chance to assess our chances. Right from the first lap we had Ranelagh vests prominent in the first half dozen or so, with John Abberton in second place. Then Belgrave vests came through thick and fast and the necessary ten scorers, with a few to spare, were soon through. Second time round showed our team to be holding their positions, and so to the finish.

Ranelagh provided the winner, and with about a hundred yards to go John and another Ranelagh runner were neck and neck, but our man out sprinted his rival to gain a valuable place. The next three were solid Ranelagh, then Pat O'Connor led in a group containing many Belgrave vests. Particular mention for a newcomer from New Zealand (actually born in Britain), Lyn Jones whose forceful running split Lionel Mann and Jim Rimmer. Our scoring men, mostly "the old gang" were anchored by Steve Williams with Peter Hardwick and Simon Holmes hot on his heels - these three are soon going to worry our established seniors. It was pleasing to see Brian Marmion further up the field - let's hope he's on his way to showing his true potential.

A quick tot-up showed Ranelagh to have beaten us on the day's event, but they were well short of wiping out our initial advantage. So, next year it's Division I again, let's stay there! There was general elation as we got into the cars which sped us back home.

Finally, our thanks to the man whose efforts were largely responsible for our prowess - no, he didn't actually run, but he wrote to you, 'phoned you and conveyed some of you to the venue and got you to the start. Yes! Thank you Gordon Biscoe!

G.S.D.

ATHLETICS: A GROWTH INDUSTRY

Eric Hall

As one who has competed, officiated and spectated but is now, alas confined by distance to tele bashing I have noticed some changes in the sport over the years. All my early experience of meetings was, I now realise, giving a surface impression. Now it is possible to see things in depth.

Some years ago at a fairly important meeting the centre of the arena was crowded with athletes, officials and hangers on - mainly the latter. The referee appealed for all non-essential personnel to leave the arena - no one moved. He repeated the appeal with the same result, he cajoled, he threatened but still after an hour or so it was the same - packed. It was the usual thing then. Get out in the middle and look important. However, during the mile the rains came. A cloudburst in fact and the result was just what the referee wanted - all but essential personnel left the arena. Not only left but rath[illegible] fled! Drowned! Only the scorers and timekeepers remained. The Almighty supported the referee.

Now all is different. A 22" picture (in colour naturally) of Crysta[illegible] Palace, Meadowbank or Cosford shows the crowds and we hear them described by Ron P., Stuart S., or Barry D., and next year ??? "The crowd numbers X thousand". "The crowd is knowledgeable" - that mea[illegible] they know how to get to Crystal Palace etc. "They are enthusiastic" that means not all have been able to get complimentary tickets. "They are right behind the team" or "the world record holder" or their "favourite" etc., and now "they are boosting them" or "lifting them" - that means the crowd are looking into the possibility of shipping them out by using man powered flight. Another source of fun is to try to spot the commentators word for the year. We have had "adrenalin" followed by "pain barrier" then came "fight back" and "long ha[illegible] season" but this year it has been "above themselves" and "world beaters" with the mind boggling "surpassing themselves" - this means you meet yourself coming back never having been.

Enough of the media and we have all had that I am sure. What about the centre of the arena? On a 22" screen it is still crowded. At times it outnumbers the crowd in the stands but in contrast to my earlier picture this time they are all essential. We now have carefully groomed, white trousered, white haired or balding officials carrying out a wide range of duties. I now spend some of the viewing time spotting old friends - Theo, Julian, Brutus - not their real names of course I have no wish to be sued! One we saw recently didn[illegible] move for well over an hour - he was either playing "Statues" or Madame Tussauds had done a damn good job! I digress, back to the officials - each man has his set duties, computer prepared and each vital to the success of the whole. To name them all would make for a paper shortage but I have selected those concerned with starting a race. We have spotted the starter, the deputy starter, the ammunition keeper, the assistant starter, the gun holder and deputy, the microphone holder, electrician and assistant plus deputies; the lane marshals, generals, majors and sergeants; the block carriers, but alas I have noticed the passing of the nail carriers and that most important official - the hammer holder - Tartan meant redundancy.

The proliferation of officials is not restricted to the meeting itself. Athletes need coaches, advisers, technical assistants, to say nothing of psychiatrists, psychologists, masseurs etc.etc. I have yet to actually see the jock strap carrier but there was a bloke with Litesome on his tracksuit at a recent meeting! In my day we had one piece of soap for the whole team but now I assume they each have their own and have a gold plated container.

The big question for the future is where will it end? You will recall that London hosts the Olympics every forty years and 1988 is not too far away. What the hell is the connection you ask? It should be apparent. By 1988 given the growth in the number of officials needed for a meeting and the decline in the population of London there will not be enough people in the area in 1988 to provide officials.

On a much more serious note though, they don't seem to need the same number of officials at foreign venues and they know when a world record has been broken too!

Eric Hall
