EDITORIAL

This issue will be reaching you about two weeks later than usual but it's delay in going to the printers has enabled us to feature the highlight of the edition as you now find it---WE HAVE FOUND OUR FIRST NATIONAL CHAMPION OF THE 70'S! WILL WESCH, whose popularity soon overflowed from the Walking Section into the rest of the Club when he joined us only 18 months ago, is the new National R.W.A. '10' and Inter-Counties Champion. Belgravians everywhere offer him their hearty congratulations. Pat Duncan's account of the Liverpool race on another page covers the details of his great effort and tells of the hard work Will had to put in to reap his success. It was truly "a tremendous performance by a man who has worked hard for every second and every yard, earning our respect in the process, and making us very proud to have him as a clubmate".

Our joy at finding our first National Champion since 1965 (Ray Middleton) received a further boost when Carl Lawton, Ray Middleton and Len Duquemin produced top form to clinch the team title too---thus winning the only National R.W.A. title to elude them last year. Our congratulations go out to the team and the officials who have served us so well. Our walkers have certainly gone into the 70's on the crest of a wave; but they are only a whisker ahead of our Youth runners who have just completed a cross-country season deserving of the highest praise. Second in the 'Southern' and fourth in the "National" were performances of high merit, but our lads are not yet ready to leave the Youth class until they have made a real mark and, astonishingly, all six of our National team qualify for this category again next year. The weight of favourites is an unkind burden for Youths to bear, but there is no doubt that it is in this section that Belgrave's main hopes lie for emulating the walkers.

A word too for our Senior cross-country runners who nowadays have to face a thousand opponents in National Championships with standards rising at a remarkable rate. They had struggled rather through the season and it is to their credit that they produced their best effort on Mational day. In finishing sixth they beat the holders, Tipton Harriers, and had a points score low enough to have given them bronze medals last year.

So the picture at the beginning of a new decade bodes well for Belgrave at running and walking---but one part of the picture is not too clear. Will our track and field men win that Southern League and get us back into the National Divisions where we belong?.

Will WeschPat DuncanRay MiddletonCarl LawtonLen Duquemin

HON. SECRETARY'S NOTES

Owing to a change of job involving difficult hours, Bill True has been obliged to resign from the office of Hon. Handicapper (walking), a position he has held for many many years and in which he has given universal satisiaction. Our Vice President would have liked to carry on for a few more years yet but circumstances will not permit and we extend our warm thanks to Bill for all his hard work and our congratulations on the high standard of handicapping he has always given us.

Our hearty congratulations go to Track Captain, Peter Hilliar, on his recent marriage and we wish Mr. and Mrs. Hilliar years of happiness and good health ahead.

Congratulations to CHARLIE SPEECHLEY who had his 82nd birthday recently and is still very active indeed - and in a competitive way at that! He confides that he covered 1,100 miles in training and racing last year! Charlie is one of our oldest members, having competed in the first Belgrave Open Walk in 1919.

The following dates have been allocated to us by the GLC for our track meetings and these will, of course, be supplemented by many other fixtures. All members, athletes and officials alike are asked to note the dates given below and give full support so ensuring a successful Summer season.

April 25 (Sat) Battersea Park May 6 (Wed) Battersea Park May 20 (Wed) Battersea Park June 6 (Sat) Club Track and Field Championships at Hurlingham Park (2.30 p.m.) June 10 (Wed) Battersea Park June 24 (Wed) Battersea Park July 1 (Wed) Invitation Inter Club Walks - Battersea Park July 15 (Wed) Battersea Park July 27 (Mon) Battersea Park Aug 5 (Wed) Battersea Park

Other dates to note are:-

April 4 (Sat) Southern and Belgrave 12 Stage Road Relay (Wimbledon) July 11 (Sat) Belgrave 20 Miles Open Run (Wimbledon) Oct 17 (Sat) Belgrave Open Youths Road Relay (Wimbledon) Nov 28 (Sat) Belgrave 51st Annual Open 7m Walk (Wimbledon)

The GLC are altering the track at Battersea Park to a 400 metres lap which may assist our mathematics - or will it? New hurdles are promised and also improved facilities in the jump pits.

The Five Star Award Scheme for young athletes at school and within the clubs has proved a tremendous success and last year 85,000 certificates and 24,000 badges were awarded in the South. It is anticipated that even greater numbers will qualify this coming season and no doubt we as a Club will gain considerable benefit from the scheme. Several of our younger members have already qualified in the highest grade and we hope that each season will add to their number. We also hope that many young athletes will continue with the sport and join our ranks when they will receive a very cordial welcome.

The Commonwealth Games are to be held at Edinburgh on July 16 to 25 and we hope that we shall be well represented in the competing teams.

Congratulations to our Youth's team on their second placing in the Southern Cross Country Championships at Parliament Hill Fields. They ran really well and showed that our teams of the future will rise above all opposition. We have yet to win this championship inaugurated in 1948 but we may be sure it won't be long now. We were second in 1950 at Eastbourne and again in 1954 (Parliament Hill Fields). Third place was ours at Ewell in 1961 and the following year at Reading and now we are among the medals again! The trophy for this event 'The Walter C. Jewell Trophy' should naturally gravitate towards us for in pre-War days this rather handsome cup was known as the 'Jockey Cup' and was won three years in succession by Belgrave in a two mile team race on the track and was on permanent display at our mid-week quarters of those days 'The Rising Sun', Ebury Bridge Road. During the War we held it in safe keeping and later when the clouds cleared it was re-claimed and adopted for use as a Cross Country trophy. We had a sentimental regard for this cup for it was the first open team trophy to be won by the Club. Let's hope our young runners will take a year's lease on it when the 1971 championships come round.

We pay tribute to Ken O'Kell's continuing generosity in keeping up-to-date our Honours Boards showing our 'Presidents' and 'Internationals' which he kindly presented to the Club some years ago. Ken, a Club champion of the '20's, manages to keep very fit thanks to his walks in Epping Forest and an interest in bowls.

ARTHUR KENDALL, of Shaftesbury Harriers, and Hon. Secretary of the Southern Counties AAA for many years past (and previously of the Middlesex County AAA) is resigning this year. It is impossible to even begin to assess his value and competence over so many years to the Association and its affiliated Clubs and we can only express grateful thanks for all his efforts, and good health in his retirement.

No less than 35 years have passed since Belgrave won the ECCU Championship at Beaconsfield, our team consisting of BERT FOOTER (11), DAN PATIENCE (14), JIM GINTY (15), ALF TAYLOR (18), TOM CARTER (19) and HARRY SHIELDS (20). Other wins came in 1939, 1946 and 1948 and we had a sequence of being 'in the medals' from 1933-1948, War years omitted. We have striven hard in subsequent years and accomplished some excellent performances but team medals have eluded us. This is written ahead of this year's championship and let's hope this statement proves false when it comes to be read! It is interesting to note that since our initial win in 1935 no Club has a record superior to our four wins. Birchfield H. have recorded three, Bolton U.H. one, Coventry Godiva two, Derby & County A.C. three, Mitcham A.C. one, North Staffs and Stone one, Portsmouth A.C. three, Sheffield U.H. two, South London H. three, Sutton Harriers and A.C. four, Tipton H. one and Victoria Park A.A.C. (Scotland) one. Time we went ahead again! There's always Birchfields' 28 wins between the years 1880 - 1953 to overtake and surpass!

CHILDRENS PARTY

On Sunday 11th January the good ship Belgrave paid a return visit to Wimbledon and Captain Hoppy and his motley crew welcomed aboard a handsome bunch of girls and boys; the galley provided a fine tea, cakes, sandwiches, jelly, orangeade etc., etc. This was soon polished off and the decks were cleared for action when pirates came aboard. These were soon subdued and clapped in irons, a promise of good behaviour was gained from them and games were organised. Crew and passengers all had great fun.

It was then reported by the look-outs that the ship was crossing the Equator. The ship was hove to and who should climb over the side but King Neptune himself. The crew were then subjected to the crossing-the-line ceremony, much to the delight of our young passengers; Captain Hoppy, Pete the pirate, Blackdog Paddy and young Jim Gillings and the rest hastily abandoned ship.

All the Company now retired to the main lounge where the Purser, Bill Laws, had arranged a film show. Soon the whole ship was filled with laughter, children's and adult's, at the antics of Tom and Gerry and company. Ice cream was distributed and soon consumed.

All eyes were now turned upwards when an old friend made his appearance, without his trousers, but in his normal long flowing red robe. Yes, Father Christmas had returned and it was apparent from his stooping shoulders and slow pace that his sack was heavy.

Nobody had been forgotten, he had a toy for all.

Eight bells now being sounded it was time for the good ship to make its way over the horizon and all good children to be on their way home, but not without saying thanks to all the crew, ladies and gentlemen who served in the galley and those who swabbed the decks later.

STANDARDS 1970

STANDARDS 1970

The A.A.A. Senior Standards for 1970 show many changes, all of course for the tougher. The Juniors are let off lightly with only the long jump being revised, the three grades being increased by 12",6" and 3" respectively. The Youths get off scot free with all marks remaining as last year.

It is the Seniors that take a hammering---no less than ten events being upgraded. Grade I in the 200 and 400 are cut by a tenth of a second only, but in the 800 all three grades are cut by 0.9, 1.2 and 0.3 respectively. The 1500 cut is 1.0, 1.0, 2.0 but the 3,000 Steeplechase is slashed by a mammoth 10, 10, 20 seconds!

The marathon men do not escape by any means, the cuts in their three standards being 5 mins, 5 mins, and 10 mins.

In the Field, the shot mark is increased by an imposing 10%, the biggest change of all; the increases in each grade are 5'-0", 4'-0" and 3"-0. The hammer goes up by 10'-0", 15'-0",

As for our own Club marks, which are unchanged, except for a 1" increase in the Senior and Junior high jumps, it looks as though they are due for a big overhaul.

YOUTHS & BOYS

One of the most pleasing aspects of Belgrave Club life today is the quality of our Youths. Not just their fine performances on track, road and country, but also their spirit, enthusiasm and general behaviour. A lot is said daily in the National Press about the youth of today but generally it portrays only one side of the coin. In Belgrave we seem to have the other. It is not just running ability that has brought the results enjoyed this last cross country season. Nor is it just due to the number of miles put in during those many dark, cold winter evenings. It is also due to the character and general approach of the Youths themselves. This is not something they were necessarily born with of course and it is probably fair to say that they have benefitted a great deal from the influence of our Club structure generally and from their section officials in particular.

Reg Hopkins and Tom Carter have played a large part over the years in building up this important part of our Club. The Youths and Boys mean a great deal to Reg and it is doubtful whether anyone could quite share the joys and sorrows as he does. Sometimes it looks as though he gets his emotions the wrong way round. He looked like crying and was lost for words after the Southern and National performances which pleased him so much, yet when a disappointing result comes along now and again he is the chirpiest, cheerful man around. This latter quality must be a great help to young runners who can get dismayed so easily. In this era of Bedford-type training and 200 miles a week it is easy for youngsters to be badly influenced by the publicity and made to believe that other people's schedules will make them champion runners too. It is not easy to understand that one man's meat is another man's poison and that there is a mental approach to training as well as a physical one.

The moderating influence of Reg and Tom in training policy has made itself felt. The Youths' successes have been chieved on a maximum of 50 miles a week. They have many years ahead of them on which to build up their training in both quality and quantity----and they realise it.

Belgrave are proud of their Youths and Boys, but the Youths and Boys will tell you that no pride could be greater than that which they have in Belgrave Harriers.

Reg HopkinsTom Carter

Youths Win County Title

Milford Heath---January 3rd

Everything went very much to form in this first Championship of the 70's and it was nice to know that that meant Belgrave were to run out easy team winners with the remarkably low score of 19 points.

The Aldershot pair Ford and Adams were reckoned to be certainities for medals but our own Bill Curtin, the reigning County Boys Champion, pushed them hard in the early stages, but he had to give way in the second mile as Ford proved invincible. Peter Carton moved up on Bill and got away in the final stages for an excellent third place (how this lad improves!) while Bill was outkicked by his almost inseparable rival from Mitcham AC, M. Fromant. John Stowe was only 120 yards further back with Tony Binda a similar distance behind John, but closing fast.

Our Press Honorary Secretary, Don Maclean, was an interested spectator as he was a winner of this race in the early 1950's. We went into the 50's winning this Championship in 1950,51, and 52. Will we do the same in the 1970's?

R.H.

Morden Park, February 14th (4X2 Miles Relay)

This race included juniors under 20 years of age. Not having any juniors available on this occasion our team was composed of our youths team minus Bill Curtin who was competing in Ireland. We did not, in these circumstances, expect our lads to fare very well thinking that they would be outclassed.

Stanley Park, Blackpool---February 28th

What a performance! The future holds no bounds for these lads. Every outing supercedes the previous one and in asking "Where will it all end?" one hopes the answer will be "In England's Senior team of 1975!"

One cannot really single out any of the scoring four. They all ran at their best and are such good prospects that the order of finishing could change yet again next season. THE MEN BEHIND THE TROPHIES

AAH

HARRY PARKER

Harry Parker was born in Westminster and lived and worked for the greater part of his life in this area which will always be associated with the Club's beginnings. In 1910 Harry joined the Westminster Harriers and here he met many sportsmen he was to know for the rest of his life. A dispute in the Westminster club, which was an organisation of many sections, led to the formation of the Chelsea Harriers but in 1912 this club was absorbed into the Belgrave Harriers. Harry and several others quickly settled down and devoted themselves to a lifetime with the 'Bels', and this continued for 38 years until Harry passed on in 1950.

Harry was a keen and enthusiastic team man in his active days and thereafter proved himself to be one of the finest Belgravians ever by reason of his unremitting work for the Club. He rarely missed a fixture. He served in many capacities at various times. He was in turn our Running Hon. Secretary, Assistant Hon. Secretary, Hon. Treasurer, President and a Life Member and working all the time for the Club he held so dear. We joked about his little black memo book, in and out of his pocket with staccato notes entered therein, the innumerable postcards he sent to rally the faithful, his bias towards everything Belgravian, his contemptuous feeling for those who had sneered at Belgrave in earlier days and made use of the club for their own purposes only. His greatest work, however, was undoubtedly done during War years when almost single handed he kept in touch with hundreds of members distributed throughout the world and managed to act as a clearing house for all the news he gathered. To Harry, as much as anyone, goes the credit that Belgrave was a real club, very much in being, and financially sound when we re-assembled to resume, more or less, where we had left off.

Harry had served as President of the South of the Thames C.C. Association, as an official at the 1948 Olympic Games, as an AAA Handicapper and in 1950 became President of the English Cross Country Union (and Southern Counties C.C. Association) - the highest honour to be accorded in cross country running councils, and the first Belgrave Harrier to attain this position - only to die in his year of office, much to our sorrow.

So passed a great Belgravian. Harry had earlier presented us with the 'Pyramid' Cup for our Weight Put Championship and on his death his many friends subscribed to present to the Club 'The Harry Parker Memorial Bowl' which is awarded to the first Club man to finish in the ECCU Senior Championship.

These two trophies will assuredly remind us as long as the Club shall exist of one of its finest workers and of one who never gave anything other than the best that was in him to the Belgrave Harriers.

FROM 'The Belgravian' 25 years ago ●●●●● and 40 years ago

25 years ago

As we go to press so comes the good news that we have all waited for, Victory in Europe. Amidst the rejoicing and thanksgiving, members' thoughts may have been turned towards the Club, and how it has fared during these last five odd years. The Club has many good reasons to be proud of its services to athletics and to its members. Belgrave Hall was open not only to our own members but to members of clubs less fortunate and to visiting athletes and servicemen. Much could be written of the fortunes of our many members. A large number, probably the majority, of our active members was called up for service in the Forces. We had, however, many members in reserved occupations, the Police and Civil Defence. These, in spite of long working hours and extra duties and less leisure time, have been able to keep the Club going. We were also pleased to welcome services members when on leave, and they proved valuable helps to the Club's teams.

We have also been fortunate in having a live and efficient Committee of hard working officers. They gave up much valuable time in the interests of the Club. It is perhaps little realised how much work is done behind the scenes.

We do not know how long it will be before athletics are restored to their pre-war scale and scope but we can confidently say that when they are Belgrave will be to the fore. The past winter has shown us that we can still lead the way and we congratulate our runners and walkers on a good season's work. Once again Derrick Burfitt has merited special mention. He won the National Youth's Cross-Country Race by a convincing margin. He also took other junior titles in his stride.

Soon, many of our Services men will be coming back to us and we look forward to seeing them line up with our teams. Others will be drafted to the Far Eastern zone where we already have a number of members. To those who will still be serving we send our best wishes for a speedy and safe return. Until that much-awaited day, they will often be in our thoughts.

and 40 years ago

The past month, the climax of the winter season, has been wonderfully successful for our runners and walkers. The cross-country men have been placed in the 'Southern' for the first time in the Club's history and followed this up by what is, perhaps, an even better performance - sixth in the National - another 'best yet'.

The walkers have won the Junior Championship again and now Belgrave hold both Senior and Junior Championships. Incidentally, we are the first Club to have won the Junior title three times. A team went to Birmingham on March 22 and brought back the 'Bishop of Birmingham's Cup' - our first visit to the Midland town for this race since 1921, when we won the trophy for the first time.

Feature · page 20

THINK METRIC NOW !

It will not be long before British athletics goes metric completely and no more will our field events men and statisticians have to get involved with fractions of an inch when recording performances. Once we have become familiar with the new set of "barriers" this change will bring, the metric result will mean more to us tan it does at present.

To the non-specialist each field event has a mark he regards as a barrier. The mark is usually determined by the roundness of the figure rather than the difficulty in obtaining it, with the result that some events have no convenient barrier for the spectator. The long jump fills the latter category perhaps. Twenty feet is commonplace, but thirty feet is unattainable In the javelin and hammer 250 feet doesn't sound quite so magical as 200 feet did and 300 feet will. In domestic triple jumping 50 feet means something, just as 60 feet does in the shot.

Soon it will all change. Those events which had convenient "barriers" are likely to lose them and those that were stuck in no man's land will have a new lease of life. In the long jump for instance our club men will aim for 7 metres (23'-0") and the internationals the already magical 8 metres (26'-3"). For Britons at any rate the popular high jump target will be 2 metres (6'-6½"). The shot will become tougher; the old 60 feet barrier will now stretch out to 20 metres (65'-7½"). And the javelin throwers will have to take their choice in 10 metre steps until someone achieves the 100 metres throw, then they'll really have something to throw for! The 4 metre and 5 metre marks are conveniently situated for Britain's pole-vaulters, the imperial equivalents being 13'-1½" and 16'-5" respectively.

So one could go on; but the moral should be clear. THINK METRIC FROM THE BEGINNING. Fix in your mind new salient points for each event and work around them---just as mile times are evaluated on the number of seconds either side of 4 minutes.

For your guidance we are reproducing a metric conversion table, but we hope you will soon overcome the desire to convert metric into imperial. The table converts imperial into metric (you can read it in reverse of course) and you will notice that the metric figures are given in millimetres. The third decimal place will not apply in athletic results as marks are recorded to the nearest 10 millimetres. At this point it is worth mentioning that the metric system being adopted is the new Systeme Internationale (S.I. units) which will result in the Engineering and Science world adopting a completely new terminology which will be just as new to other European countries as ourselves. As far as linear measurement is concerned our European colleagues have a head start, as the metre and millimetre have been retained. However, THE CENTIMETRE BECOMES AS EXTINCT AS THE INCH. It would therefore be quite wrong for us to fall into the habit of using a non-standard term and, in this respect, it is unfortunate that the A.A.A. should have chosen to amend their measurement rules this year by introducing the centimetre. They could quite easily have ruled that measurements be taken in ten millimetre increments instead of introducing a revision to the wording later on. Someone didn't do his homework!

The centimetre markings on the present metric tapes will eventually be changed from 1,2,3,4 etc. to 10,20,30,40 etc.---all helping to confuse those who have just digested the centimetre!

The metric system is upon us. THINK METRIC NOW.

Five Bels at Edinburgh ?

John Keown

THE COMMONWEALTH WALK by John Keown

The 9th British Commonwealth Games will be held in Edinburgh from the 16th to 25th July. Included in the programme will be a Men's 20 Miles Road Walk. This event will start at 10.30 a.m. on Saturday July 18th.

The inclusion of such an event in the Games owes much to the hard work and tenacity of the British Athletic Authorities, especially the officials of the Race Walking Association. The fight to get a walk included in the Games began in 1934 when Ernest Neville proposed and the late Jack Crump seconded a motion at a meeting of the Southern Area R.W.A., calling upon that body to urge the Road Walking Association (title changed in 1954), to appoint a special sub-committee to take such steps, on behalf of the Association, as it might deem expedient to obtain the inclusion in the next Empire Games of a 50 Kms. Road Walking Race. Such a sub-committee was set up. Despite the enthusiasm of the R.W.A. for the idea it was to be over 30 years before a walk was included in the Games. During the late 1950's and the early 1960's the Association, through their Hon Secretary, L.W. 'Timber' Woodcock, and the British representative on the Walking Commission of the I.A.A.F., Harold Whitlock, continually pressed athletic authorities in Commonwealth countries to support the appeal for the inclusion of a walk in the Games.

On 6th August, 1966 the first Commonwealth 20 Miles Road Walk was held at Kingston, Jamaica. Walkers from England filled the first two places. Ronnie Wallwork from Lancashire Walking Club took the gold medal in 2 hours 44 minutes 42.8 seconds whilst the silver medal was taken by our own Ray Middleton in 2 hours 45 minutes 19.0 seconds. Norman Read, and exile Englishman from Steyning, walking in the colours of New Zealand, took the bronze medal in 2 hours 46 minutes 48.2 seconds.

This year Belgrave could have a greater interest in the walk. We could have four, possibly five, club walkers representing between them three different countries.

Both Gerry Cameron and Bob Coates have as their ambition representing the host country Scotland. Gerry, who did much work behind the scenes to get race walking re-established in Scotland a couple of years ago, will find it very difficult to make the team. Business commitments and a growing family curtail the amount of time he can devote to training and racing. Bob, on the other hand, has an excellent chance of m king the Scottish team. He is one of Belgrave's brightest hopes for the future. It is only two years since Bob competed in his first walking race, the 1968 Belgrave 20 Miles Championship. Prior to this he was an amateur racing cyclist for four years. During 1969 this young Scotsman made steady progress from being an average club walker to a top class one. Two performances of note during the summer of 1969 were a 3,000 Metres at Brighton when he clocked 13 minutes 16.4 seconds and an 8th place in the 'Birmingham 25' when he clocked 3 hours 49 minutes 18 seconds. In the last few months when Bob has been training five days a week, despite some trouble with the judges, he has recorded some very fast times over 7 and 10 miles. Now we await with interest to see what will happen when he tackles the 20 mile races. Here, we hope his mode of progression will make the judges happy. He should be capable of a time inside 2 hours 50 minutes. His greatest rivals for places in the Scottish team will be Bill Sutherland (Highgate H.) and Alan Buchanan (Brighton & Hove A.C.).

In 1967 Len Duquemin won the Southern 'Garnet' 10 Miles Championship and lead Sarnia Walking Club from Guernsey to success in the team championship. Another member of the winning team was John Moullin who finished third in the race. Shortly afterwards these two walkers came to work in the London area and joined Belgrave Harriers. Their arrival came at an opportune moment when the club was struggling to rebuild its walking section. WESCH WINS NATIONAL 10

WESCH WINS NATIONAL 10

BELS Take Team Title

March 21st 1970 - LIVERPOOL - The National 10 Miles Championship

So just 18 months ago 2 walkers started training in Battersea Park. They were approached by a flabby German called Wesch who reckoned he'd done a bit of walking - naturally being Belgrave men they smashed him into the ground leaving him at the furthest point from the changing rooms. Gradually,'Yer Kraut' as he is known to his close friends, began to improve. Victory in the Surrey 10 mile championship last year followed good results in the Club 10 and the Basingstoke. Then came disaster with disqualification in the National and second place for Belgrave, a setback which Wilf took harder than his clubmates but which was soon forgotten with victories in the Club 20 and some fine track performances. By August it was clear that Belgrave had another champion in the stable but it took a trip to Frankfurt to really show the world. Here before his own crowd Wilf destroyed the East German Junior star Schuster and held off Ray Middleton in his peak Athens form. Throughout the winter Wilf has been putting away 90 - 100 miles a week, in all weathers, preparing himself for the season to come. Victories in the Belgrave 10, the Basingstoke and the Surrey Championship set the stage and on Saturday at Liverpool Belgrave once again had a National Champion to lead the team. Remembering the dying words of one of his sparring partners during a particularly fierce blizzard "I'm not going through all this bloody suffering to see you beaten in the last mile by some scrubber" Wilf caught Ron Wallwork at 9 miles and after a desperate neck and neck struggle for nearly ¾ of a mile the Belgrave man's superior strength and fitness paid off and he surged away for a brilliant 72:07 victory. The Belgrave Team all produced their best. Carl Lawton " I'd have done 74:30 if I had'nt cracked" winning his Honours Badge with a tremendous 75:00 11th position. Then, just 46 seconds later Len Duquemin our fourth scoring man. Ray Middleton produced a good one just 10 years after he first made the Belgrave National 10 Team,in fact it was his second fastest ever. With the addition of Bob Coates, slouching along in 77 minutes the Belgrave boys also took second place in the County result. With such performances the team race was a formality and for all the club the the pleasure came from the front where a lean hard Belgravian defeated some of the finest walkers in the world to take the National Title. A tremendous performance by a man who has worked hard for every second and every yard, earning our respect in the process, and making us very proud to have him as a clubmate.

An Old-un's Reverie

HOW LONG A BELGRAVIAN HAVE I BEEN: WEL SIR, I'D SAY FOR FORTY YEARS OR MORE, I JOINED WHEN MY YEARS WERE JUST NINETEEN, NOW I HAVE SAID GOODBYE TO O'ER SIXTY FOUR.

RACES ON TRACK AND ROAD I HAVE OFT' RUN, AND ACROSS COUNTRY, SO GREATLY ENJOYED; THIS WAS MY PLEASURE; REGRETS HAVE I NONE; FOR THUS WAS MY SPARE TIME MOSTLY EMPLOYED.

WHAT WAS THE FIRST PRIZE I EVER RAN FOR? THE THREE MILE ROAD HANDICAP, I BELIEVE, AND WITH A FAIR START, I MANAGED TO SCORE FOR WHICH A SUGAR BOWL I DID RECEIVE.

RIGHT PROUD OF THIS ACHIEVEMENT I WAS SIR; ELATED WITH THE THOUGHT THAT I HAD WON, I PONDERED, AND TO ME IT DID OCCUR, THAT RUNNING AND RACING COULD BE GOOD FUN..

WITH THIS IN MIND, DOWN TO TRAINING I GOT, AND BEFORE LONG I WAS MAKING THE TEAMS, THAT STARTED OUR WINNING WAYS ON THE TROT, WHICH GAVE US REALITY, NOT DREAMS

'TIS THE COMPANY I VALUE THE MOST, AND SO MANY A FRIENDSHIP'S BEEN MADE, TO WHICH I RAISE MY GLASS AND GIVE A TOAST THAT THEY MAY LAST MANY A DECADE.

FOR MUTUAL WARMTH OF GREETING YOU'LL SEE, WHEN AND WHEREVER BELGRAVIANS MEET, FOR IT IS IN EACH OTHERS COMPANY THE SPIRIT SHOWS, THAT MAKES THE BOND COMPLETE

I REMEMBER THOSE WHO HAVE DEPARTED AND RUNNERS AND WALKERS OF YESTERYEAR, FOR IT WAS THEY WHO HELPED TO GET STARTED ALL THAT HAS BEEN ACHIEVED AND HELD DEAR.

CHAMPIONS GREAT AND CHAMPIONS SMALL WITHIN OUR RANKS, THROUGH THE YEARS, HAVE EMERGED AND MAY THEY ALWAYS PASS THROUGH BELGRAVE HALL AS IF A PLACE FOR THEM HAS BEEN RESERVED

THERE ARE SO MANY WHO HAVE DONE SO MUCH TO PICK BUT ONE, WOULD CHOOSE ANOTHER'S PEER, FOR ALL HAVE NEVER LOST THE COMMON TOUCH THAT HAS BROUGHT US MEMBERS FROM FAR AND NEAR.

TO ALL BELGRAVIANS WHO ARE YET TO BE, LET NOT YOU, THE SPIRIT OF BELGRAVE DIE, FOR WE WILL FADE TO JUST A MEMORY WHEN IT BECOMES OUR TIME TO SAY GOODBYE.

Talking of globe-trotters, CHRIS STEER is just about as far away from Delhi as one could get---Canada---and he sends greetings to all Belgravians. For 5 months he has been roaming around the U.S.A. and Mexico in an old van. He is now in Vancouver "tanned, unfit and broke!" He hopes to be back here in the Summer when he has earned his return fare. He has managed to do a bit of racing and finished 19th in the Pacific A.A.U. cross country championship in San Francisco.

It was U.S. walker Tom Dooley who joined us last summer during his stay in the U.K. with an American team. It's a small world alright, particularly for Chris it seems, for when he was told that Frenchman Guy Monnet had joined us he remarked "That was the guy I accidentally tripped up in a cross-country race here in Canada a few months back". BELS 6th in NATIONAL

BELS 6th in NATIONAL

STANLEY PARK, BLACKPOOL---February 28th

With an indifferent season's results pointing to a bad National for Belgrave; the bearers of claret and gold rose to the occasion and finished one better than last year---6th. In doing so they beat the holders Tipton who finished a struggling 8th.

Once again Gerry North was our spearhead and he set the pattern for the rest of the team to follow. His form had not been too good of late, but here he was on his happy hunting ground of 1962 where he had outsprinted Bruce Tulloh to pull off a rare Inter-Counties/ National double. The course was officially a "figure of eight" route but appeared to be a triple figure of eight with runners on view at all points of the compass seemingly running in all directions.

There were 997 starters(50% more than 1962) and the start seemed even faster than usual. Even so, Gerry actually got his nose to the front after the first mile or so, "I found myself there, rather than pushing for it" he said later. In contrast, John Dear found his first National a struggle and looked unhappy from the beginning. He finished just one place behind former team-mate Mel Thorpe who was Coventry's 7th man.

John Bicourt and Geoff North went well, particularly John, and they soon settled down in the fifties where they remained to the end. Lionel Mann made the first 100 for the first time---the course was obviously to his liking. Adrian Major, Trevor Hart and Bill Dance all ran to current form with Trevor closing in the scoring six, as last year.

Tony Fairclough discovered that you can't go into a National with any hope of a useful run unless you have fully recovered from injury. In February Tony was running even better than last year when he was our 2nd man in the National in 36th place, but a knee injury put him out of the Southern and it had not fully cleared by the time the Blackpool race came round. Result---a poor run and a flare-up of the injury.

The team race was very close and only Stoke and Derby had six men home before Belgrave. There was some consolation in our winning of the 'nine' cup.

The junior team were rather outclassed and finished in 31st position, but they stuck to their task well. John Blair-Fish, who seems to have overcome his poor form of last year, was our first man in 183rd place and seems to be going better every week.

A COUNTRYMAN'S JOTTINGS

Countryman

A COUNTRYMAN'S JOTTINGS

As odd as it may seem, most Belgravians were highly satisfied with our 6th place in the National at Blackpool. Odd, because we were very disappointed with our 4th places in 1966/7/8 and disgusted with our 7th position last year. Why then the pleasure at being 6th? Well, this cross-country season has been a long string of disappointments---poor team form, erratic individual performances, the tailing off of several good runners and a multitude of other depressing circumstances. Apart from the Surrey League we were becoming known as Champions of Nothing and our abysmal Southern performance seemed to be the last nail in the coffin.

As the National approached it became apparent that our second and third scorers of last year, Fairclough (36th) and O'Connor (81st) would probably be out of the scoring six this time through injury and loss of form respectively. What hope was there then in 1970 when we were only 7th in 1969?

As it happened our lads rose to the occasion like a real team and all deserve the highest praise for turning the tide. Mind you, Bicourt and Mann were the only ones to produce their best National positions, and if the others had equalled their's the positions would have read 1,51,12,98,78,83,87,36,337---the best six giving us a winning total of 261 points! But such hopes are in the realms of fantasy and to be realistic sixth place was a very good effort.

It is interesting to note that our score of 444 points would have placed us third last year.

Our best performance in recent years was our third consecutive 4th place at Sutton Coldfield in 1968 when we were only 23 points from silver medals. Our score of 354 points that year would have given us second place last year and this year. That's the way it goes!

* * * * *

What a great performer Gerry North is. He can overcome set-backs and raise himself to unexpected heights like no other runner I know. Gerry's business committments are taking up more and more of his time and involve considerable travelling and the manning of exhibition stands. He managed only one training session in the week prior to the Southern and found standing all day at an exhibition so fatiguing that he couldn't have faced running even if he had had the time. The Southern was one long struggle, but he still pushed himself to the limit and it was only when his legs went rubbery in the closing stages, through all those hours of standing, did his iron-will have to give best, concede about a dozen places and finish a lowly 28th.

(Continued overleaf)

Gerry may have been down but he wasn't out, and he bounced back in the race that counted, the National, with a fine 17th. Some may say "Ah! But he won it once". True that is. But it was eight years ago that he did so, and he had more time for training then.

With twice a day training and 100 to 150 miles a week commonplace amongst the leading runners it is remarkable that Gerry North can keep in touch on his modest 70 a week. "If I had every afternoon available like Mike Turner or had time to train twice a day like many of the others, I would have no trouble making the International team, I know" he told me---- and who would disagree?

Gerry North has the most impressive National record of them all. Not only has he run in every National since 1956 (15 of them) but on only 3 occasions has he failed to make the first 12. In 1958 he retired with "stitch" trouble which plagued him for 18 months; in 1967 he was ill and collapsed after barely making the finish,and this year, despite short training,he was 17th---the only blemishes on a record which will probably never be matched. Here are the positions.

Ignoring 1958, his average National placing is 10th!!

* * * * *

Over the last year or so, the weekly mileage of athletes seems to have shown a sudden jump. Ten years ago we were saying that our runners should be training at least 50 miles a week. Two years ago it was 70. Now, in the space of 2 years it seems to have become the round 100. There have always been the few runners hitting 100 a week but this minority has now moved up to the 150 to 200 bracket, leaving the bulk of the leading club men reaching for that magical 100,week after week. Where will it all end? Dave Bedford and Jack Lane (both Juniors) do 200 and 150 miles a week respectively. Bedford does three, four or even five sessions a day. On the day after winning the North of Thames Championship he ran a steady 25 miles in the morning, a brisk five in the afternoon and an easy run before going to bed in the evening. As a student at Brighton Teachers Training College he has a lot of time for running but what will he do when he has to earn a living?

Dedication has reached such limits these days that we are getting nearer and nearer to a two-tier athletics system. The super athletes will be obliged more than ever to seek full-time training facilities and thus take the first step into professionalism.

Which reminds me of Bruce Tulloh's talk to the R.R.C. on his run across the U.S.A.----- 2,900 miles odd in 66 days. After averaging over 300 miles a week for more than two months Bruce said "The journey showed me that it is possible for a fit athlete to train for up to eight hours a day". We can only hope that Bedford didn't hear him!!

* * * * *

Our Youths produced their finest performance to date at Blackpool. At one point they were lying 2nd, but slipped to 4th by the narrowest of margins. Nevertheless they were the first Southern club to finish, pushing Southern champions T.V.H. back to 9th.

Apart from their excellent running, the most interesting feature of the team is that all six are first year Youths. So next year, with Richard Langley moving up to join them, we could well have a team that will produce the sort of victory Small Heath achieved i.e. 3,7,9 and 51 making 70 points.

Curtin and Carton are showing great progress and seem to be reaching the positions from which most of the top Seniors emerge. It is odd, but invariably the Youth medallists are never heard of as Seniors. Conversely, many of the Senior medallists were virtually unknown as Youths.

Examples are Trevor Wright 59th, Mike Turner 25th and 38th, Mike Baxter 35th and 31st and Mike Tagg 32nd. By placing between 21st and 77th our Youths have shown they are following in distinguished footsteps.

* * * * *

It is only two years since the Editor reproduced [illegible] ranking list, yet already our runners have virtually re-written it. Four races have taken place since March 1968 with the result that the 100th best performance has been cut from 29:08 to 28:50. And I can remember when a sub-30 minutes time would squeeze one into the road relay twelve---as recently as the mid-sixties too!. The revised list is reproduced with these jottings.

Charlie Walker, who in earlier lists had as many as a dozen performances recorded, has had his tally reduced to five. The highest is now eight, by Laurie O'Hara, with Tony Fairclough just one place behind.

The Norths still have a firm grip on the leading times and it is difficult to see them being dislodged over the next two years.

Fairclough and Major are the latest recruits to the exclusive sub-28 club which now numbers eight in all. But the outstanding time in my book is still that magnificent time of 28:25 by Ron Linstead which he ran as a Youth 16 years ago, when Curtin, Carton, Stowe and Binda were new-born babes in their cots! Our Youths have two more opportunities of going one better. I'm sure that Ron would be happy to see them all succeed.

By the time you read these notes we shall be in the throes of the road relay season and, once again, we have to consider our chances in the premier events.

First major event is the Belgrave and Southern Counties Road Relay at Wimbledon on April 4th. Hercules Wimbledon wrested the title from us last year and seem to be even stronger in 1970, so the task of regaining the title could be truly formidable. And yet, we have the ability to do it in a 12 stage race. One of our major attributes has been our quality in depth. We have survived our bad patches because at all times we have had several runners fighting for that last place, and if Gerry North missed out through injury we would not be weakened to the same extent as Hercules Wimbledon's losing Bob Holt. Our rivals, therefore, have to rely on fielding their best three runners race in, race out, and we will be at hand for the slightest slip.

(Continued overleaf)

Of our runners themselves I don't think they have yet peaked out as a team. Adrian Major reached a peak in January but looks set to repeat his form in time for the Southern and National events. John Bicourt ran very well at Blackpool and if he can shake off his catarrh trouble, will be a long-stage force. Gerry North, Lionel Mann and Laurie O'Hara are as reliable as ever, with the latter showing tremendous zip over the short distances. That makes five who are, or have been, running at their best these last few months.

What of the remaining seven or so? Well, in my view they are climbing to peaks and if they are all successful in doing so for the first 18 days of April then the road will fairly hum at the sound of their flying feet.

Dick Piotrowski suffered a family bereavement at the beginning of February and naturally found it difficult to put much into his running; but he is now going great guns and showing his best ever form.

Bob Smith, now permanently resident and in practice as a dentist in Croydon, has just returned after a 3 months spell in his native Australia. He gets fit very quickly and there is little doubt he will produce fast times in the relays if he can keep clear of the injuries that nag him from time to time.

Trevor Hart has not been quite at his best this winter. Trevor's determination is unlikely to let a slip in form last a moment longer than necessary and he could well break through again in time for Leicester. Even so, he is still worth a long stage.

Pat O'Connor is one of "yer actual 100 miles-a-week men", and a few road relays under his belt should provide him with the snap to go with his strength. He is likely to improve his road times each week up to the National.

Geoff North rarely has a bad run but I put him in the fast improving category because he has the ability to build up his form over a long period. The pattern of his running this winter indicates that he is on the verge of running a series of "blinders" on the road which could well embrace the South and National events.

John Dear showed his potential this winter when he finished twelfth in the "Middlesex", ahead of Laurie O'Hara. He is much stronger than in previous years and I expect him to produce a run of best ever times during March and April.

Now to the man who has helped us to so much success in the past but, through pressure of business, has been unable to take his rightful place alongside the Norths---John Thresher. For a few weeks now John has re-planned his training routine and has stepped up the work steadily. He is fast running into form and although he will not reach long leg status in time for the major relays he will be a valuable addition to our short stage men.

If fate is kind to us, and illness and injuries are kept at bay, then our team could well read as follows:-

SHORT STAGES:Bob Smith,Pat O'Connor,John Dear,Dick Piotrowski,John Thresher,Laurie O'Hara.

POOR START TO ROAD RELAYS

POOR START TO ROAD RELAYS

EIGHT-STAGE RELAY---CRAWLEY, MARCH 8TH

Instead of following up our pleasing National cross-country performance with a promising start to the road relay season, we turned out for this Sunday fixture with definite signs of "hangover" amongst the team. Colds were prevalent, aches and pains were rife, and some didn't even appear. The net result was a 4½ minutes defeat by "you know who", with Reading and Mitcham joining in the victory procession for good measure. Even our 'B' team, who usually serve us so well, were pipped in the closing stages by those same unmentionables who, to rub it in, actually closed in three teams.

Adrian and Pat, both suffering with colds, got us off to an indifferent start, over a minute down, and handed over to two more sufferers in Lionel and John. John Dear was 12 secs faster than 'A' man Lionel and they finished almost together in 4th and 6th places. Gerry ("my legs didn't belong to me") held his position but made no ground, while Clive dropped the 'B' team from 6th to 11th. Bill Kerr and Charlie Walker dropped a further place each on stage 4 so at the half-way mark we were 5th (4:17 in arrears) and 12th (first 'B' team 9 seconds ahead of H/W).

Geoff North put in our best run of the day to pass Hillingdon and put Mitcham within reach but it was already obvious that third place was all we could strive for.

Trevor Hart crept up another 21 seconds on Stage 6 and Laurie O'Hara followed with fastest time on his stage, which took him past Mitcham (Wallis) to a seven seconds advantage for the final stage. John Thresher, Still short of training, ran like a man possessed and forced M. Murray of Mitcham to the stage's fastest time before conceding third place by 30secs.

Meanwhile, the 'B' team continued their painstaking battle with Hercules Wimbledon 'B'. After four stages our lead was 9 seconds; after five, 12 seconds, after six, 2 seconds; after seven we were down by 6 seconds while our rivals stretched their lead on the final stage to rob us of our last hope for prizes.

The results were disappointing but the event was a success. The first four stages were run on the Brighton Road and the scene brought back many memories of the classic London-Brighton relay. Traffic was no problem on this stretch and everything moved freely. It was less perfect on the narrow road of the second half where, although they may not have realised it, most congestion was caused by the many runners who would persist in continuing their run beyond the hand-over. On the final two stages it was difficult to pick out the competitors from those on training spins (invariably two abreast) who forced traffic to stop or swing wide ten times more frequently than was really necessary. This disregard of keeping a course clear during a race would not be tolerated at the 'National', nor at the White City. It could well prejudice future promotions by the hardworking Crawley AC.

IT'S PIOTROWSKI'S 'WIMBLEDON CUP'

5¾ Miles Road Championship - Wimbledon Cup, March 14th

A total of 55 runners set out for the first of this years domestic battles of the road, on a rather cold afternoon which resulted in the times being overall slower than usual by some 30-40secs.

After the first lap of the common it was Trevor Hart in the 'drivers seat' with a neat bunch of about 8 gathered around him including a bronzed Bob Smith, recently back from Australia and the most recent addition to our ranks Guy Monnet who hails from France.

Passing the Crooked Billet after 2 laps the leading bunch had broken up slightly with John Thresher and Pat O'Connor losing contact and with Guy having dropped further back. For the climb up Cambridge Road it was Smith, O'Hara, Mann and Piotrowski leading the field leaving the final scene to be enacted over the last lap of the common where a 'return to form' Dick Piotrowski outsprinted fast finisher Laurie O'Hara.

One of the most pleasing aspects of the race was some of the performances put up by the youths namely Messrs Curtin, Stowe, Carton and Binda. It obviously wont be long before some of our established team members find their places in jeopardy.
