EDITORIAL

We are delighted to welcome to Belgrave all those members of Kingston A.C. who have taken the advice of their officers and joined us after the closure of their local club. Our old friend Ben Lullum will still be looking after his charges at Norbiton, but now in the name of Belgrave. Norbiton track is a stronghold of Surrey A.C so Ben will need all the support he can get. The more "claret and gold" we have on show there the better, so those of our members who live in the vicinity would do well to seek out Ben Lullum and make full use of his coaching experience, particularly sprinters, and remember - wear those club vests!

To those members of Kingston A.C who have not yet made up their mind which club to join, we would say that Belgrave's fixture list is second to none (summer and winter) and that no one is refused a run at any inter-club match. So if you want the competition it's there for the taking.

On the subject of club vests, there were several instances this last winter of Belgravians competing in colours other than claret and gold. This is to be deplored. Tommy Robertson, immediate past - President of Surrey County raised this matter at the last Surrey Supper after seeing a relay the previous week where the variety of vests on show completely confused the onlooker. One team had only one member wearing his club vest. Mr. Robertson appealed to the Surrey clubs to reverse this undesirable trend. We in turn ask all Belgravians to take note, and would like to see Section captains and secretaries ensuring that their teams line up properly attired.

Ben LullumTommy Robertson

Obituary · page 3

DAN PATIENCE

It is with real regret that we have to record the death of DAN PATIENCE on February 28th at Inverness. Dan had a leg amputated about 18 months ago, since when his health had deteriorated rapidly. Our sincere sympathy is extended to Dan's widow and his son aged 15, and daughter of 12 years.

Dan Patience, a regular serving soldier in the Scots Guards with his three brothers, was a great favourite with all during the 1930's and was a scoring member in our first winning English Cross-Country Championship team in 1935, and again in 1939. We take our leave of a grand sportsman, a splendid runner and a great Belgravian.

Dan Patience

HON. SEC'S NOTES

A.A.H.

The unfortunate loss of Dan Patience does bring back recollections of that March day at Beaconsfield in 1935 when Belgrave won their first E.C.C.U. Championship. 295 ran and our team scored :- Bert Footer (11) Dan Patience (14) Jim Ginty (15) Alf Taylor (18) Tom Carter (19) Harry Shields (20) - 97 points against 154 of Salford Harriers and 156 of Surrey A.C.. Our team packing was remarkable - six men placed between 11th and 20th scoring positions!

In 1939 we won again, scoring 122 points on this occasion. There were 392 runners in the field and our scoring men were:- Ted Stimpson (3) Arthur Penny (5) Tom Carter(17) Bert Footer (28) Harry Shields (34) Dan Patience (35)

Further wins came in 1946 and 1948, and we had the excellent record of winning or placed team medals in ten consecutive championships, 1933 - 1939 and 1946 - 1948.

* * *

So much for the past! What of the future? In the larger fields of today, and with the changed scoring arrangements now in force, larger team scores are inevitable. It is obvious however, that six men in sixty places can win the Championship - if they are reasonably spread i.e. one in the first 10, one in 11 - 20, one in 21 - 30 and so on. Is this so impossible? We could all name the runners who could take up these positions for us - or bette[illegible] Maybe thirty years after we first won, we can win again!

* * *

Our combined record in Youths, Juniors and Senior E.C.C.U. Championships this year was second only to one other Club - Coventry Godiva H. We were 12t[illegible] in the Youths Race (of 120 Clubs); 9th in the Junior (of 105 Clubs) and 8th in the Senior (of 133 Clubs) - a really splendid combined effort in which we can take great pride. In addition, our congratulations go to Gerry North on his sixth place in the Senior and on International selection; to Tony Fairclough - 5th in the Junior Championship; and to John Bicourt, seventh in the Youths event. All this magnificent running in fields of fantastic size; and its promise for the future.

A Farewell To BARRY SAWYER

by Ron Linstead

By the time this issue of the "Belgravian" appears, Barry Sawyer, our marathon champion and record holder will almost certainly be in South Africa preparing for the 54 mile Durban - Pietermaritzburg Comrades marathon to be run on 30th May. This is just stage I on his overall journey of emigration to New Zealand which is his ultimate destination and where he intends to settle. Certainly in the land of Lydiard and Snell he should have both the climate and the opportunity to train as hard as he likes and to develop his full potential.

B.S's first love was chess and he was introduced to the Bels by an office colleague Alan Bishop back in 1959. Stuck at an office desk for most of his working day and having no physically strenuous hobby or pastime Barry wanted to keep himself reasonably fit. After a brief introduction to Denmark Road and the horserides on Wimbledon Common, he was quickly bitten by the athletic bug. Virtually brainwashed by the teachings of Arthur Newton he began gradually to increase his daily mileage setting himself a target of at least 100 miles per week. As a comparative "scrubber" he ran his first marathon in 1960 clocking an inauspicious 3:28 mins. Last year to illustrate the extent of his improvement he ran exactly an hour faster in the Poly. Windsor - Chiswick race and collected the Club Championship in record time. He continued to improve and by October last year he was in tremendous form when setting a new world best for the strictly unofficial distance of 25 miles track in the R.R.C. Race at Stompond Lane, Walton.

At times Barry, who to the outsider seems a bit of a "cold fish", has been something of an inconsistent competitor and this has perhaps been accentuated by his determined insistence on sticking rigidly to his training schedule. On several occasions he has refused to rest when injured or possibly a little off colour, but a good measure of his enthusiasm is the fact that he commutes regularly between "the Hall" and his Aldershot home to fulfill the 2nd half of his double training session day.

Barry is a much travelled man and apart from competing in the Ben Nevis, Wattstown (Rhondda) and Isle of Man Races, he has visited several European countries including France, Italy, Germany and Russia. In these countries he has had a variety of jobs! He has worked as a snake killer, grape harvester and forester. It was, in fact, whilst in the U.S.S.R. that he met his future wife who was, herself, some way from home as she is a S. African. They have decided that when married they would like to make their home on the other side of the world in New Zealand, where, as we have already said, from the athletic point of view, conditions should be ideal. Whether, in fact, Barry intends to continue with the sport remains to be seen but we shall all look forward to seeing him run well in the Comrades Race and we are hoping to hear from him in the near future. Certainly we all wish him and his fiancee all the very best and would say how good it has been knowing him for the past years. Good luck Barry!

R.L.

A COUNTRYMAN'S Jottings

Countryman

Belgrave's "subsidiary" club, Cadogan AC, whose membership is restricted to Belgravians with Middlesex qualifications, has been getting steadily stronger in recent years. Without the assistance of Laurie O'Hara and Bill Kerr Cadogan AC still managed 5th place in the Middlesex championships, whilst Belgrave's Surrey team could only manage 7th at Coulsdon. This would be an opportune time to resume the old Belgrave/ Cadogan contests in Club Championships, which were such a feature of our earlier hey-days. In 1948, Cadogan won the Middlesex title and Belgrave the Surrey, and rivalry was dead keen; but the custom was dropped when the Club hit its lean spell in the mid-fifties.

Belgrave/Cadogan scores this season have been (6 to score) Club '7'---31/62, Club '10'---46/53, '5¾'--- 41/45.

* * *

How good it is to see Trevor Hart's sudden rise from rags to riches. For several years Trevor has been "scrubbing", despite showing promise as a youth. An increase in mileage, some training sessions at the "Duke of Yorks" track with our leading runners and a determined approach soon produced the long-awaited "break-through", which took Trevor to 3rd place in the '7', 6th in the '10', 12th in the Middlesex Co., 27th in the Sth of Thames Senior, 78th in the 'Southern', 164th in the National and a 7th fastest time on his stage of the Brighton relay.

I wonder just how many of our members could follow suit if they adopted a similar approach.

* * *

The inter-counties championship has long since passed but it was interesting at the time to see how the various clubs were represented. John Moore (157th) ran for Middlesex and was Belgrave's only representative On a five-to-score basis the 'inter-club' result would have been Portsmouth AC 149, Surrey AC 204, Derby 208, Coventry 231 and Bristol 295. As it was, Lancashire (without Gerry North) retained their title with 88 points from Middlesex 209 pts and Warwickshire 261 pts. Surrey could only finish 11th yet we still couldn't get a runner in the team!

* * *

I suppose statistics can be made to prove anything but it was still a fact that for all round strength in the three divisions of the National Championships only Coventry could better us. In the 'Southern' we took the trophy for the lowest aggregate of placings in Senior, Junior and Youths races with (3,2,6,)11pts and we hoped to do likewise in the National, but Coventry (3,1,14)18pts easily beat our (8,9,12)29pts; though there is no trophy for the premier event. Sale (31,2,1)34pts were next, followed by Derby (2,22,15)39pts and our old rivals Birchfield (11,5,34)50pts. Portsmouth's days must surely be numbered, for they have not finished Junior or Youth teams for several years.

To see how realistic the above placings are, a points total of six individuals (2 Sen. 2 Jun. 2 Youths) gave a result of Coventry 193, Belgrave 227, Derby 291, Birchfield 312 and Sale 315; the same five teams in almost similar order. Belgravians our future has never been brighter!

* * *

John Thresher has had a pretty good season. Apart from building up his reputation in the athletics world he also passed his final examinations and is now a Chartered Accountant. Nothing, it would seem, can stand for long in the way of this successful young man. Nonetheless there seems to be one irritating exception----a man he just cannot beat--his driving examiner! John takes his test shortly for the 4th time.

* * *

In this month's "Athletics Arena" a whole page was devoted to an article on "Arthur's Place" (Lauriston Cottage). To scores of cross-country runners Arthur Whitehead's hospitality has long been appreciated. For many years now the doors of Lauriston Cottage have never been closed to runners and this amenity has permitted several thousand additional man-miles to be put in accross the delightful terrain of Wimbledon Common and Richmond Park.

With fixtures now occupying almost every saturday of the year Sunday mornings at Arthur's have become an institution in themselves. The excited chatter about the previous day's fixtures; the exchange of club news with athletes from Wimbledon, Mitcham, Hercules and S.L.H; the pack runs of 10,15, or 22 miles; the return to a hot shower; the seemingly inexhaustible supply of tea and biscuits in Arthur's lounge and his ever-attentive interest in all that goes on, is almost a way of life. The psychological effect of this benevolence enables each runner to go into a new week's training feeling mentally refreshed and full of determination--which is more than many coaches are able to achieve.

* * *

How does Belgrave's name stand in the North? Well, a local Huddersfield paper had this to say just before the National Championships at Leicester,"------it will take a powerful team to dislodge the champions Coventry Godiva, with their seven internationals. Only Portsmouth and the London club, Belgrave, of the 133 teams entered, look capable of presenting a threat to the holders". Perhaps it was written a year too soon for Belgrave!

* * *

In the May 1963 edition of this magazine I listed the year's eleven leading events for cross-country runners, with previous performances and my forecasts for the season just ended. Apart from our abysmal effort in the Surrey Co. Cross-Country my estimates were not far adrift. We recorded five wins as aginst only one in the previous three seasons, and the steady rate of improvement goes on. I won't trouble to compile a similar list for next season as I feel sure we will win them all!

The North Surrey League

The first of this season's four fixtures was reported in our last issue, and we went into the second race with a lead of 79 points over Walton.

Race 2

Walton were hosts for the 2nd event and the race was held over a stiff course at Esher on December 14th. Although we packed fairly well and provided the individual winner in Gerry North, Walton rose to the occasion on home ground and regained all but 13 of our points lead. Cocks ran well but Walker and Morrison were disappointing.

Race 3

Held at Kingston on December 28th, Surrey A.C. were the home club. Our turn-out was not at all good, with most of our leading runners absent. Walton produced a strong team again and we could do nothing to stop them taking an overall points lead in the competition. Fairclough showed us a glimpse of future form but our last 3 scorers were too far down to provide a useful points total.

Race 4

As last year, the final dixture was held at Addington over Herne Hill's course. Walton went into the race with a 39 points lead but, on paper, we were still capable of snatching the title. All of our lads ran to form, apart from Bill Kerr who was currently struggling with a bad patch of form, but Walton won the day yet again and took a winning lead of 81 points. Their victory was earned by taking a coachload of their best men to each fixture - the only way to win an event of this sort. In the four fixtures 19 different runners filled our scoring positions.

This final race produced an excellent win for Gerry North and another sound performance by all-rounder Vaughan Thomas. Although Gerry won three of the races, he did not qualify for the individual award as he did not compete in all 4 races - this went to Walton's Bob Roath for the 2nd successive year.

Bob Glover stepped into the breach to complete our scoring six, but his 89th actual position was too far back to get us anywhere in the team result, and our 7th position must have been a worst ever performance.

DABBS' FINE WIN IN CLUB '10'

This was the first occasion on which Gerry North failed to win a Club race. However, Gerry had just recovered from a septic foot which had put him out of the Inter-Counties and this was his return to racing after a few weeks off. Dabbs and Sawyer set such a furious pace that Gerry eventually cracked, going from 3rd to 13th. Brother Geoff also appearing after a lay-off, retired in the early stages.

The Championship was held over a new lap of 2½ miles and the four circuits required gave our lads a fine knowledge of the course to be used the following week for the South of Thames Senior Championship. As the Club was also responsible for marking the South of Thames course, our marksmen etc. had an ideal dress-rehersal also.

THE NATIONAL C.C. - Bicourt & Fairclough Excell

Bicourt & Fairclough Excell

Tony Fairclough and John Bicourt both chose February 29th 1964 to run their best-ever races. Their respective placings of 5th & 7th were truly great, particularly when one considers that there were about 450 starters in each race.

The Youths were first away and John Bicourt kept clear of the ruck to be handily placed in 10th position at [illegible] mile. The first 3 positions were already determined with that outstanding youth, Aitken, well clear of Romaine & Young. Painfully, over the next 2miles, John moved up one place until, with the finish in sight, he came within striking distance of a pack of four. And strike he did. Hand over fist he closed on the quartet scrambling up to the finish and just managed to split them in two a few yards from the line---another 30yards would have seen him an easy 5th.

Adrian Major had a good run to finish 62nd and Ron Hayes produced his usual sound performance to be our 3rd man. Derek Hurd completed our scoring four. The team ran well, and their 12th place out of the 66 team that closed in deserves high praise.

With Bicourt 7th in the Youths, North 6th in the Senior, what could Fairclough do in the Junior? Could he make us the only club to have a man in the first 10 of each race? Most important of course, could he spearhead our Junior team to victory?---a team quite capable, on their day, of beating all-comers.

As things turned out, it was definitely Tony's day and, even more definitely, not the team's. Fairclough worked terribly hard on his first lap working his way past several star runners to 5th at 2 miles. At this stage Yates & Hammond were shoulder to shoulder at the front, with Walker & Watters also having a personal duel 60 yards behind. Tony set his sights on this latter pair and rounded off a tremendous first lap by streaking past them up the finishing slope to a loud 'ooh!' from the crowd. Shortly afterwards the steepest hill of the circuit had to be climbed and it was here that Tony lost his third place to Walker & Watters The Worcester/Wycombe pair gradually drew away leaving Tony isolated in 5th place with 2 miles to go. Fortunately, his nearest rivals were a good 20 seconds away and although they halved the distance Tony was still a clear-cut 5th----this being 1 up on North and 2 up on Bicourt.

Of the rest, Steer had a good start but never looked happy, losing ground all the way, and although he ran his heart out he conceded at least a dozen places in the finishing straight alone. Alan Morrison also faded, but his 107th was relatively better; whilst Dave Cock's 145th was particularly disappointing. Bob Brown, despite a heavily bandaged hand came down from Derby to complete our six, and although losing a shoe into the bargain finished a gallant, but tired, 372.

This our strongest Junior team for many years ran their last race in this grade of competition. A place would have been a nice note to finish on, but we at least know that our team's potential is the match of the medal-winners', and we must therefore work to see this realised in senior competition.

WANDSWORTH REVISITED

Brian Nott

or Belgrave go inside again!

In the last edition of the Belgravian, Frank Jarvis outlined in great detail our first visit to Wandsworth Prison on 27th November, 1963. However, on that occasion we were unable to complete our talk, neither were we able to snow the film of the Brighton Relay.

At the invitation of the Governor, we paid a second visit to the prison on 14th February, and the same party, with the exception of Jack Bidgood and Ron Langheim, appeared outside the main gates shortly before 5.30p.m. On this occasion we had recruited the services of Bob Sparks of the N.U.T.S. who was to help us during question time.

As Brian Nott was interrupted by the closing of the last meeting, it fell upon him to start the proceedings this time, and he continued with his predictions on what the outcome of our athletes might be as far as medals were concerned in this coming Olympic Games.

Frank Jarvis and Arthur Penny spoke briefly, much on the same lines as before, but it should be recorded that on this visit our audience was probably twice as large, with somewhere in the region of 100 persons present.

After Frank and Arthur had finished their talks, Charlie Manning and Chris Steer then showed the film. Needless to say, this was very well received, particularly the opening captions. One got the impression that the audience were actually witnessing this race as it was being run, judging by the cheering that was sounded when Laurie O'Hara passed the Portsmouth runner, and again when Alan Black reached the finish at Brighton.

We were left with something like half an hour for questions, and among those asked was one to the effect of where could a duplicate of the film be obtained. Another questioner asked Bill Lucas if any members of Belgrave took any form of stimulent to better their athletic performances, and it was pointed out that the drinking of alcholic liquor was the extent to which our runners went on this score. It was also mentioned that judging by recent performances, no adverse effects had been noticed from any over-indulgence in drinking. The questions continued until the meeting closed at 7.30p.m. whereupon we all retired to the local hostelry for some much needed refreshment I think that it is quite true to say that our two visits were very well worth while, and were enjoyed by both the panel and the audience.

Brian Nott.

Five Club Records Go!

by Olympian

Rarely does the weather let us down for London to Brighton Relays, but on this occasion, it could hardly have been worse. How to arrange them to produce the fastest time from London to Brighton was a problem equal only to winning the Treble Chance. Every combination possible was considered before picking the final order and even that selection came under serious reconsideration before the day - who would be a selector?

Stage II. John Thresher (24:12 - 5th fastest) This was John's fourth attempt at this leg and he was anxious and at the same time confident, that he could break the jinx which has beset his previous efforts. Need I say more than that he gained ten places and lowered the club record for this stage by no less than 54 secs. - a great run which brought us back into the picture. However, we were still a minute behind the leaders due to a record breaking run by Adcocks of Coventry who reduced Ken Norris's 8 year old time by 16 seconds and took his club into a 22 sec. record lead over Derby with Portsmouth close behind.

Stage III Trevor Hart ( 20:29 - 7th fastest) The 3rd stage is a new one, as the race is now diverted at Mitcham via Wallington, instead of along the former route up the Purley Way. Trevor, who 12 months ago would not even have been considered for a Brighton team, was selected for this 4 mile leg and he quickly made progress - taking us up to 4th place at one point and eventually handing over in 6th position. This was an extremely plucky run, only 22 secs. slower than the best time of the day by Fudge of Portsmouth.

Stage IV Geoff North (29:30 - 12th fastest) This leg is basically the original but is now over half a mile longer and is tougher by virtue of the uphill 1st mile immediately after the takeover at Sandy Lane, Wallington. This is a stage on which the cream runners perform and Geoff was up against seasoned compaigners like Basil Heatley (Coventry), Jim Hogan (Poly), Tim Briault (Ponders End), 'Kipper' Herring (Blackheath), Shelley (N. Staffs) and John Cooke (Portsmouth) to name but a few. Geoff has been taking his running more seriously lately and is nearing his peak, and we are anticipating great things from him in the future. On this occasion, after quickly disposing of the Birchfield runner, he had to give way to Briault and Shelley and when Merstham was reached we were back to 7th, almost 2 min[illegible] behind Coventry, who were still leading. It was at this point that Portsmouth lost valuable ground when their runner was not ready at the take-over (Editor's note: contrary to several reports in the national press, of delays up to 2, and in some cases 3 minutes, the delay has been calculated at around 40/45 secs.) The pattern of the race was now taking shape - the leading clubs were coming to the fore and the next few legs would decide who were to be the likely winners. Could we pull back to the front as we had done in October?

Stage V, John Moore (21:46 - 8th fastest) John's time lowered the club record by 3 secs. yet even this was not good enough to gain on the leaders or to hold Edmonds of Bristol at bay, and at the end of the stage we were 2 mins 20 secs. behind back to eighth position.

Stage VI, Chas. Walker (13:36 - 3th fastest) This is the shortest leg of all and Charlie, on his 28th consecutive Brighton quickly passed N. Staffs and Ponders End and then set about closing the gap separating us from Bristol and Derby. Once again, the leading clubs had faster runners and the gap was widened to over 2½ minutes between us and Coventry, who had regained the lead after a brief lapse on the 5th leg.

Stage VII, Laurie O'Hara (18:10 - 4th fastest) Laurie is now used to the Gatwick Airport haul and with his quick clipped stride he gained on the Bristol runner eventually passing him to take us up to 5th, but we still could not make any impression on Coventry whose runner put up the fastest time of the day. Nevertheless, Laurie ran extremely well to carve 17 secs. off our own record for this leg.

Stage VIII, Gerry North (28:19 - 1st fastest) Our hopes were now centered on club captain Gerry North running at the peak of his form and determined to knock the record into a cocked hat. He had approached the best time for this leg on earlier attempts both for us and for Blackpool, and on this occasion he made no mistake, lowering the figure from 28:25 to 28:19, and in so doing took us into 4th place, only 2 secs. behind Derby and 13secs. behind Portsmouth who were, themselves, 1:20 secs. behind Coventry. We were back in the hunt, thanks to Gerry's fantastic effort - the battle was now on and our spirits were now restored with the hopes of at least a place. Although Coventry appeared to be invincible, with a bit of luck, we could still finish 2nd.

Stage IX - Bill Kerr (28:57 - 11th fastest) Bill has had a miserable winter through a complete loss of form but has recently been back to his usual self. He now had the invidious task of chasing Tulloh (Portsmouth) and Bullivant (Derby) who soon showed that they were in great form and we were obliged to watch our runner slowly losing the ground that Gerry North had so valiantly made up on stage VIII. The scot ran much faster than in October when he was the fastest for the day, but it was of no avail against those ahead and at Hicksted, we were 2:47 secs. behind the leaders.

Stage X - Tony Fairclough (19:28 - 2nd fastest) Tony, who celebrated his 21st birthday only a few weeks ago, was not the least dismayed to be out of sight of the runners ahead and set about the task of closing the gap like a veteran. Soon he had the Portsmouth runner in his sights and at the end of the leg was only 10 secs. behind the 3rd team and had a new club record under his belt, having lowered Pat Newell's figures by no less than 28 secs. [illegible]nd at the same time, recorded the 2nd fastest time of the day.

[illegible]tage XI Chris Steer (21:03 - 14th fastest) Once again our hopes were raised only to be cruelly shattered when we found that Chris, on his first Brighton, was to pit his strength against Frank Salvat of Pompey. Chris, eager to get going, chased Salvat like a demon up Dale Hill and did in fact, close the gap by 5 secs. but the initial effort was too much for him and once the crown on the hill was reached, the Portsmouth runner leaped away to hand over well clear of a very tired and disappointed Chris. Coventry, still out in front were consolidating their position and Derby appeared to have the 2nd place well in hand, for with one leg to go, they were 68 secs. ahead of Portsmouth.

Stage XII, Alan Black (17:25 - 5th fastest) The last leg, as far as we were concerned was interesting only in seeing that Alan could keep ahead of Bristol who were making great efforts to catch us. This Alan did and reached the Aquarium in a time only 10 secs. outside the club record. Ahead of Alan, however, a dramatic change took place. Dave Cooke picked up over a minute on Derby to take his club into 2nd place and to set up a fantastically fast stage record. In the meantime, Brian Kilby had ensured a 2nd successive win for Coventry.

The times generally were fast for, despite the chilly wet weather, there was a strong following wind. The first 12 clubs each ran faster than they had ever done before. As to our own performance, we can justly be proud of our lads. After a bad start, they fought hard the whole way and there certainly is no disgrace is being beaten by the big three of road relays, all of whom were at their very best. The wheel is turning and we have every prospect of an improved team in 1965 particularly if everyone trains really hard. The three leaders may well falter and give us the chance to step in - we have beaten Portsmouth before, and we can do it again.

FELTHAM '5'----March 30th

On Easter Monday, five days before the "Brighton" relay, we had 3 teams appearing in the Feltham '5'. In addition to John Thresher (holder) we had Gerry North, Newell and Sawyer in the 'A' team.

Our opponents, however, were also strongly represented and this was the highest class field the race had ever seen.

Herring (Blackheath) set a tremendously fast pace right from the start with North and Haith (TVH) at his shoulder. Thresher was not so well away and had to make a great effort for 1½ miles to close with the leaders. The leading group was fast dwindling in size though and at half-way only North, Herring and Haith were left together. Gerry then made a desperate effort to break clear and Haith dropped, but Herring was simply flying along and with half a mile to go he stepped up the pace even more and drew away to a 12 seconds win, easily beating Gerry's own course record. Thresher was only 3 or 4 seconds slower than his win of last year yet was placed 9th. Pat Newell ran very well, was always well placed, and finished only 10secs behind John in 14th position. Barry Sawyer had a poor start, never got going, and although finishing in the twenties was nearly a minute behind Pat. Perhaps this was the moment he should have stepped down in favour of Pat Newell for the big event but 5 days away.

In the team race Belgrave and Ealing had the same score but Ealing won the day on the position of their last scoring man.

DEREK BOOSEY

On January, 10th at the Southern Indoor Championships at Stanmore, Derek Boosey won the triple jump with a leap of 49ft. 2[illegible]ins an excellent effort for so early in the year. Two weeks later the same athlete had a busy day representing the R.A.F. v U.A.U at Cosford. He ran two 60 yard dashes (6.6 and 6.5), was 3rd in the 300 yds (35.0) and won the triple jump with 48ft. 0ins!

Continuing his busy indoor season at Stanmore on February, 8th our International triple jumper won his favourite event yet again (46ft. 10½ins) for Eastern Counties in a 6 sided match. Then a week later had a winning leap of 49ft. 6ins for the R.A.F. v Oxford v Cambridge.

Still striving to achieve a 50ft. 0ins indoor leap, Derek jumped for the R.A.F. Again at Feltwell on March, 7th. He retained his season's unbeaton record but could only do 47ft. 11ins. An improvement, a fortnight later, of 9¼ ins gave Derek his 6th successive win - this time in a 6 sided match at Loughborough.

Providing he can keep free from injury, DEREK BOOSEY is surely Belgrave's best hope for a Tokyo trip.

Derek Boosey
