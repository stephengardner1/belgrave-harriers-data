Editorial · page 2

To all fellow Belgravians, at home and overseas, we send our wishes for a HAPPY AND SUCCESSFUL NEW YEAR.

We would like to continue in a festive, happy vein, but the general apathy in the Club during 1957 has got to be remedied and we would respectfully like to offer some suggestions. Let your new year's resolutions be :-

To turn out in every possible match be it home or away, A or B fixture. A mob match should be supported as such and you cannot call a turn-out of less than 100 a "mob". To take a greater interest in Club affairs; our present officers, even if willing, cannot go on for ever. Young blood, young thought and young ideas are needed.

To see that greater care is taken of Club property, Belgrave Hall is the envy of many clubs, but without proper attention it would soon become a heavy liability. Your subs are low - help to keep them so.

To give the necessary encouragement to all those young members on whose shoulders our future rests. A friendly word of advice or encouragement from a V.I.P. can have a tremendous effect.

To assist in the training of our youngsters; this applies to those whose racing days are over. The Club has given you much, plough some of your experience back.

Above all, remember that we all want Belgrave Harriers to become the leading name in athletics in all its spheres. Let this be our aim for 1958.

ANNUAL GENERAL MEETING

"The Listener"

Held on the 20th September this meeting attracted barely one tenth of the total Club membership. This is the only occasion during the year when members can give their assistance in deciding the management of the Club by taking part in the election of officers, but having left this difficult task to the few who were present, it is hoped that they will show their approval by giving full support to those who were elected. The Hon. Secretary's report, a copy of which you received, was adopted with a few minor amendments. This was followed by the Hon. Treasurer's report which revealed a small profit on the General Account, but he pointed out that with the increase in the cost of postage from 1st October and the general trend of rising prices, there was every need to keep a close watch on expenditure if this state of affairs was to be maintained. The amount outstanding last year in connection with the improvements to the Club premises has now been paid, and allowing for depreciation there was a small loss on the year's working. He next informed the meeting that the cost of maintaining the various services at the hall, including rates etc., was now £350 per annum, and in order to make provision for re-decorating and further improvements in the future it was essential to concentrate on building up a good reserve. Tribute was then paid to the work of Les Cohen in connection with the Supporters Club and to those who had given him valuable assistance during the past year. The accounts were then adopted and a hearty vote of congratulations was accorded to the Hon. Treasurer. In order to facilitate the smooth progress of the meeting the election of officers was then dealt with. The retiring President, Tom Carter, on behalf of the Committee, informed the meeting that their unanimous recommendation for the office of President was Syd Roberts and formally proposed him, giving a brief account of his 21 years as a member. This was supported by Harry Shields and on being put to the meeting was unanimously carried. The newly elected President then took the chair and thanked all members for the honour just received. The election of officers then proceeded in normal rotation, details of which will be found in the new handbook. The Chairman paid tribute to the work of Alf Harley, the General Hon. Secretary, and thanked him for continuing in the office. Whilst the counting of votes for the ten members of the new Committee was being dealt with, Jack Goswell rose on behalf of the outgoing Committee and put forward its recommendation, in accordance with Club Rules, that Eric Hall and Stan Vickers be elected to life-membership, giving a detailed account of their progress in race walking which had culminated in their selection to represent Great Britain in the Olympic Games at Melbourne. After being seconded, there was some discussion on the merits of other members of the Club, following which a vote was taken and the resolution passed by a sufficient majority. After the new committee had been announced the only item left on the agenda was "Other business, if any". The Chairman then proposed a hearty vote of thanks to the retiring President for his work during the past year. This was carried unanimously and the meeting closed with a vote of thanks to the Chairman.

LONDON TO BRIGHTON WALK SEPT. 14TH.

A new record low score in the team race was achieved by the Club when the team finished their four scorers in 1st,3rd,4th and 5th positions for a total of 13 pts., individual positions, of course, being deleted. The fact that the race incorporated the Club Championship for the "Belgrave Cup" no doubt contributed to this remarkable feat, for nothing will spur a Belgrave Harrier on more than the sight of another Club man in front. Indeed, such was the fierceness of the contest that Harry Shields who was 10th in the open race could only get 5th in the Club Championship. We also finished a "B" team, in 4th place - well ahead of many "A" teams . Thanks are due to the many willing helpers, attendants and supporters who helped to make this such a memorable day.

LONDON TO BRIGHTON RELAY (SOUTHERN CLUBS) OCT.12TH.

"Olympian"

Once again we were blessed with the best of weather and no matter what the result our supporters were all set for a good day out. For those who have never followed this race I can fully commend it as the best and most enjoyable in the athletic calendar, both from the competitors and spectators points of view. The changing fortunes of the teams provide a great spirit of friendly rivalry and the quality of the racing gives a piquant spice to a day of many thrills. Those in the know forecast a Thames Valley victory and for once their prognostications proved correct. It would have taken an "Old Moore" to have placed the other teams in the right order. Belgrave were generally considered as being a club with a chance of making the first six with Polytechnic, Woodford Green, Herne Hill, Essex Beagles and St.Albans as the main rivals. Little did we realise the early misfortune that was to befall us and the valiant fight that the rest of the team was to make to recover our prestige. Don MacLean had been given the first leg to Wandsworth Common and the first stroke of ten o'clock sent him on his way with nineteen other runners. At first, all was well, but by the time Chelsea Bridge was reached it was obvious that it was not Don's day and as we have seen so often in the past he fell back and back and our early hopes fell and fell until by the end of the stage we were in eighteenth place and over 1½ mins. behind the leading club (Aylesford P.M.). This was disaster, and a gap which would need a lot of closing. Charlie Walker, however, was spurred on by the responsibility thrown on his shoulders and set out to recover some of the lost ground. By excellent running he passed team after team and the encouraging cheers of the few supporters on this leg kept him at his task. At Mitcham we were up to 9th place but still 1½ mins. behind Poly. who had taken over the lead through a great run by John Evans. Charlie was the second fastest of the day. George Flanagan, one of our youngest team members, had the difficult leg from Mitcham to Purley. His steady start was a wise move, and with the help of Llewellyn of Walton, who caught him after a mile, he made good progress on the field ahead, and by Purley had reduced the overall lead to 69 sec. and had gained a further place - an invaluable effort. Charlie Dabbs took the fourth stage but was obviously not at his best and we were disappointed to see our much improved position slipping away again. Charlie fought hard to keep his place but at Merstham we were back again to 9th and 2¼ min. behind Thames Valley who had gone into what proved to be a winning lead. Jim Masters on the switchback course through Redhill and Earlswood again gave us hope by passing three clubs and taking us up to 6th. Thames Valley, however, were represented by Norris and added 10 more seconds to their lead. Jim produced the second best time and closed up the gap on the intervening clubs - an excellent effort. The next leg was a new one of little more than two miles in length and John Davies, complete with an arm in plaster following a fracture, was entrusted with it. This was John's first experience of this type of racing and he later suffered from an over zealous start which diminished his finishing power. He was forced back to 8th by Portsmouth and Shaftsbury and at this point we were 2¾ min. behind Thames Valley, who were 500 yds. ahead of the second club, Woodford. This run should prove very valuable to John and we shall expect some first class performances from him in future races. Peter Hitchcock had the joy of running over the newly laid stretch of road round Gatwick Airport and although losing ground on the leading clubs he managed to pull us back to 7th with a steady performance which should add to his experience for next year.

We were now well over half way to Brighton and no less than 3 min. 10 sec. behind - a poor prospect and we were wondering whether even a place in the first six was to be gained. Pat Newell, taking over Jack Brown's cloak, proved a worthy successor on the hard grind from Crawley to Handcross, and in gaining two places brought us within sight of all but T.V.H. who had widened their lead,through a grand run by Bert Ingles, to 2½ minutes over the second club, Woodford. Pat, despite an unplanned trip under the subway at Crawley level-crossing, which loses at least ten seconds, was credited with the third best time of the day. Eric Gebbett's turn came next on the long undulating leg from Handcross to "The Castle" at Hickstead. This leg is generally reserved for the best runners of each club, and our difficulty, in recent years, has been to find someone of sufficient calibre to keep on equal terms with other teams. Eric gave of his best and ran a good race, but unfortunately he lost a considerable amount of ground on the leaders, and our sixth position, with only three legs to go, was far from favourable with T.V.H. over five minutes ahead and other clubs well away. Len Bishop, on the tenth stage, thought we still had a chance, and with a run which gave him the fastest time of the day, picked up one place which brought us up to fifth, only 12 seconds behind the third and fourth teams. T.V.H., by this time, were in an invincible position with over three minutes to spare on Woodford, who were still holding on to second place. Dave Baulch, again selected to run up Dale Hill, joined in the spirit of the chase and soon moved up to third place and was gaining fast on Woodford, who had by this time run all their best men and were beginning to flag badly. Dave ran to such purpose that he achieved the best time of the day and brought us to within nine seconds of Woodford - a magnificent performance. Our supporters were wild with excitement and were giving our runners every encouragement possible - never have stop-watches worked so hard in taking time checks. Brian Hallowell set about the last leg in a very workmanlike manner, much to the consternation of the Woodford runner only a short distance ahead - never have I seen anyone look so worried. Across Preston Park in similar manner as in April last, Brian took over second place and went clean away from the Woodford man and reached the Aquarium 36 seconds ahead. He also had the distinction of recording the fastest time of the day. It is doubtful if any team, after such a bad start, has ever recovered in so grand a manner to take one of the major awards and keep the Belgrave flag flying so high. Our record in these races is second to none, and the prospects of even greater improvement are considerable, with a batch of first class material available for selection next April coupled with the solid and experienced strength of our seasoned performers.

FIELD EVENTS GOSSIP

T.C. Jones.

There were no field events notes in the issue of the "Belgravian' just after the track season ended owing to holidays. Normally that contains a review of the season's performances and although a little late we should not forget Percy Porter's fine performances in the hammer, including a 3rd place in the A.A.A. championships and selection as an International reserve.John Powell Jones won the Welsh Shot championship and Charlie (C.R.) Jones put up two fine performances in getting 4th in both Southern and A.A.A. 220 yards hurdles. We had successes in a number of County championships but "Iron Man" Mal Ash had a slightly disappointing season.

Those,and one or two others, have a chance of selection for the Empire Games, so we hope they will get down to serious training early, as the team will be chosen immediately after the Southern Championships in June.

Congratulations to Dave Ansell, our Captain, on his selection for the British team at the Maccabia Games in Tel-a-Viv. He was 7th in the long jump, 6th in the triple jump and gained a medal in the 4 x 100 metres relay.

We should like to extend a special welcome to Deszö Audorka, a very fine International discus thrower from Hungary, and we hope he will be able to show us some of his home form.

It was nice to see Malcolm Draper over Christmas. He tells me he has now left hospital and is convalescing - hopes to be home in about two months but unfortunately no athletics for a couple of years.

I have already mentioned getting into training early and to help in this Thames Valley Harriers and "The Bels" are putting on meetings on the 8th February and 1st of March respectively for some pre-season competition. The Sward Trophy will be round again on the 10th of May at Chiswick when we hope to turn out our best team to win back the trophy we so narrowly lost last year. The usual Club championships and some handicaps will also be held in the Summer.

Details of the Thames Valley meeting can be supplied if anyone is interested.

Our own meeting will be held on Saturday 1st March, at Battersea Park, commencing at 1.30 p.m. Events :-

Shot Discus Javelin High Jump Long Jump Triple Jump Pole Vault

Entries (free) on A.A.A. forms to me not later than 17th Feb. I hope all "Bels" will support our efforts in this meeting.

Best wishes to all for the New Year.

T.C. Jones.

Feature · page 14

PORTRAIT GALLERY

Every club has its characters, Belgrave Harriers being no exception. But we don't propose to cover our canvas with the outline of one of those characters whose performances and history are an open book.

In 1938, there joined Belgrave, one whom we can only assume was as small, lean and sinewy in those days as he is now. Known to us all as "Mac", Joseph MacDonald has had a dareer as distinguished as many who have been fortunate to be called stars.

Under the stern leadership of Footer, Carter and Penny in the immediate post-war years, Mac soon showed he was possessed of great endurance and performed well over the road and country.

In 1948 he was in the 3rd team in the Middlesex C.C. championship, 10th in the Kent '20', 3rd team in the Chichester to Portsmouth 16 and 45th in the South of the Thames Junior C.C. race (noted for its large fields).

Even better in 1949, he was in our winning South of the Thames Senior team, 59th in the Southern Counties C.C. championship and 5th in the Worthing 5 miles road race.

Mac was now established as a road runner and between 1951 and 1953 won the Club Marathon Championship and was placed 2nd and 3rd. But his greatest feats have been in the London to Brighton 52½ miles race where, when twice in dire straits with still a considerable distance to be covered, he has forced himself on in order not to let his team down. Altogether he has been in one third, one second and one winning team in this race in 1952, 1955 and 1956 respectively.

His attendance at road and cross-country events and at training sessions has been an example to all. Week after week at the Duke of York's at Sloane Square, he would come tearing in at the last moment on his bicycle (he wasn't such a plutocrat in those days) and oblivious to the abuse hurled at him by those ready to leave, would hastily don his gear and with feet pattering loud enough to disturb half Sloane Square, would join the pack. Good-hearted and generous, it was often barley sugars all round when sweets were rationed; Mac it seemed had relations who didn't indulge.

He has been with us years and will still be performing well for many to come. Our London to Brighton team can always rely on him.

A true Belgravian, whose performances have so often gone unseen except by the few on the long lonely roads; we are proud to put them on record.

Joseph MacDonaldMacFooterCarterPenny

FROM THE TRACK SECRETARY

D.M. Shepherd.

For the first time I have taken on the task of Track Secretary and I am looking forward to a very successful year for all those members of the Club who are interested in track running

We have a very full programme before us, and it should give all of you plenty of racing.

Arranging this programme has occasioned us a great deal of hard work, so you will be expected to co-operate by replying immediately to all postcards that may be sent to you, stating whether or not you can take part in a particular fixture. By so doing you will be making our task much easier. Try to make the coming track season a success by doing your best to turn out for the Club, and by remembering that you are part of a team and not just an individual.

If I, Bob Taylor, Clive Shippen or Jim Heathfield can help in any way do not hesitate to write to us or see us personally, and we will do our best for you. If you do not help me and the job falls down, through lack of co-operation it will be no good crying about it.

Note especially the date of the Club championships. They will be held on Saturday 14th of June in the evening, and will start at approximately 6.00 p.m. We want to make a special effort this year to have a really first class meeting, and once again it will be up to the competitors, that is yourselves, to SUPPORT these championships. I, on my part, will make sure that the track is there, that the officials are there and that the cups and trophies are also there.

Make it a real day and bring the family.

Yours sincerely.

D.M. Shepherd.

ROAD RUNNING NOTES.

H.A. Bishop,

With the long distance road running season barely two months away it is to be hoped that all distance runners are now getting down to serious training. There are numerous events, ranging from 10 to 53 miles, so that all tastes can be catered for.

I would particularly like to see a very strong Belgrave entry in each of the two main events, namely the Polytechnic marathon and our own Open 20 miles race, both of which carry a Club championship in conjunction.

Scarcely less important is the Finchley 20, which this year incorporates the Southern 20 miles championship. This race presents a good opportunity to secure a Southern standard, as the course is easy and the modest time of 2 hrs. 5 min. should be within everybody's capabilities. There is, of course, no A.A.A. standard for this distance. Southern marathon standard ( 2 hrs.50 mins.) is a trifle more difficult, but on the flat Windsor to Chiswick route it shouldn't present too much trouble provided weather conditions are reasonable. The A.A.A. standard of 2 hrs.35 mins. is a tough proposition on any course.

In many distance events, the promotors stipulate that team runners must make individual entries, and I would suggest that this be done in all cases to avoid any possibility of being left out of a team. If you are not one of the recognised distance men and feel that you would like to take part in a certain race or races just drop me a line and I will include you in the team, assuming, of course, that we have a team going to that particular event.

Wishing you all a successful season with not too many blisters.

H.A. Bishop, 13, Woodside Way, Long Lane, Croydon, Surrey
