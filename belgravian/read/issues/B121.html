EDITORIAL

Your Editor.

Fellow Belgravians,

Xmas has come and gone, a New Year has begun and by the time this magazine reaches you, the reconstruction work at Belgrave Hall will have been completed and I think everyone will agree that the new amenities are a worthy addition to the Club. Alf Harley, who probably knows more about the growth of Belgrave Hall than most of us, has contributed an article in the following pages which should prove interesting to every member.

For the first time since the inception of the "Tommy Green" Trophy, given for the best club performance of the year, the Committee have awarded it to Percy Luxon for his Long Jump of 22ft. 11½ inches. Our Field Events members have made great strides in the past few years and the award of this trophy for a field events performance should be an incentive to even better achievements

Despite the appeal in the last issue of this magazine for better support for club handicaps, the entries this year so far have been slightly less than last. Handicaps whether over the country, on the road, or on the track, are held to encourage the faster men to go even faster and to give the slower men an opportunity to improve and a chance to gain some small reward for their training. I know that Ron Holtum will be looking forward to considerable improvement when the track season starts.

The Open Walk again proved a great success thanks to the hard work of Jack Goswell and his enthusiastic helpers.

There is still a good number of members who are not taking the Belgravian and I hope that you will do your bit towards increasing its circulation.

Your Editor.

Alf HarleyPercy LuxonRon HoltumJack Goswell

THE PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE

Tom Morrell. President.

It surely must be the objective of every true Belgravian to one day become President of this famous Athletic Club, and so, after 27 years of membership, I have gained this coveted prize. I must therefore thank you all most sincerely for electing me to the most senior office in the club for the ensuing year.

My efforts in the past have been only a small part in the efficient running of the club, the results have been reflected in the successes of the past and those that will surely follow in the future. The Club's crowning glory must be the rebuilding of Belgrave Hall, which is without doubt the finest headquarters of any purely amateur club in the country, and I urge you all to make full use of these fine amenities both for training and club social activities.

May I conclude by wishing you and the Club all success for the future and so make my year of office my most enjoyable year in the Club.

Tom Morrell. President.

THE HEADQUARTERS STORY

A.A.H.

The Belgrave Harriers were founded in 1887 at an inaugural meeting held at the "Kings Arms", Ranelagh Road, Westminster and this house continued as the Club Headquarters for 12 years. In these days it was not uncommon for the sprint finals to be held on the Embankment at an hour approaching mid-night and it is said that the bookmakers were usually present in force. In 1899 a change was made to "King William 1V" nearby in Grosvenor Road and Cross Country Headquarters were booked at the "Railway Tavern", Battersea Rise, five minutes from Clapham Junction. This was as far as it was necessary to go in those days and the lonely marshy area south of the Thames - now largely Battersea Park - was no district to traverse with your wages in your pocket and a fog coming up.

In 1903 the Club still retained its Westminster quarters but cross country running was now indulged in from the "Red Lion" Hotel, Barnes. About 1908 headquarters were moved to 180, Kings Road, Chelsea, and the cross country quarters were at the "Spencer Arms", Lower Richmond Road, Barnes. The Licensee here was Tom Sullivan - a well known man in the running world - who just before World War 1 went to Germany as Chief Rowing coach and laid the foundations of this sport in Germany. He continued this work for many years and died comparatively recently at a ripe old age. His departure to Germany meant another change for the Club, and headquarters were established at the "Surprise" Tavern, Christchurch Terrace, Chelsea, with cross country quarters at the Raynes Park Hotel and track facilities were arranged at Stamford Bridge and Battersea Park Cricket Pavilion and ground.

In 1919-20 we used Cain's Hall, Battersea and Pepys House, Westminster as headquarters with Raynes Park Hotel for cross country, but Ashcombe A.C. reviving after the war, asserted their right to their old premises and again we were on the move. This time we alighted on familiar ground and in 1920-21 our cross country runners and walkers stripped off in the skittle alley - now the long lounge bar - at the "Swan" in the Ridgway, Wimbledon. The following season we rented St. John's Hall, Denmark Road, around the corner, changing the name to Belgrave Hall when we acquired the leasehold in 1938. In 1922 our head-quarters were nominated as being at Wimbledon and we were fortun-ate in finding mid-week quarters at the "Rising Sun", Ebury Bridge Road, S.W.1. which were enjoyed until 1938 and contributed much to the Club's successes during this heart warming period that we enjoyed for ten years and more before war again overtook us. Many will remember the very primitive conditions prevailing at the "Rising Sun", but with much pleasure they will recall the training and the joking that went on, the comradeship and enthusiastic club spirit which was such a driving force in all our endeavours.

The coming of the war restricted our activities to the Wimbledon headquarters which remained open throughout and were a rallying point for athletics and all athletes throughout those difficult six years. On resuming full scale activities, we enjoyed midweek hospitality at the Duke of Yorks Headquarters and were able to attain one of our greatest ambitions, the pur-chase of the freehold of Belgrave Hall, and contemplate the improved amenities we had so long desired.

But to hark back to the days following the first World War. Gradually the Club, which had almost gone out of ex-istence for four years, gathered strength and a small cash reserve was built up. It was always thought - but it seemed so far off no one dared to mention it - that this might be the neuclus of a fund to endow our own headquarters in the far off future. Baths at Wimbledon were of the galvanized zinc variety with hot water strictly rationed and everbody contributed, midweek as well as Saturdays, to the "hot water" money appeal. In 1930 our total re-serves were about £500 and although many had shared in accumulating this sum, the late Bob Ricketts, Harry Hare and Wally Bayliss had played the major part. In 1932 Harry Hare launched the "halfpenny" saving scheme and our funds grew apace. Briefly the scheme called on members never to spend a halfpenny but to retain them and pass them over to the Club. Teddy Gordon provided some building brick money boxes in which the surplus halfpennies could be placed each evening. Frank Elson came along with a scheme based on football results which added to our funds and was succeeded by a more ambitious scheme still, organised by Frank Elson and Tom Cotton and assisted by Teddy Gordon and a host of other enthusiasts. In 1938 we acquired the leasehold of Belgrave Hall as it was now renamed and our reserves approached £2,500. In 1941 Les Cohen took over our moneymaking schemes, reframing them on even more ambitious lines and rapidly our available cash reserves increased and the funds available for our normal club activities were also supplemented. No words of praise can be too high for the work of Les Cohen (and of Mrs. Cohen who has also rendered great assistance) and of the many hundreds of willing supporters who placed us in such a strong position financially. This work, fortunately for us, still continues.

In 1950 we took over the freehold of our headquarters and were then free to add to the amenities and comforts of the hall, when the necessary building permits could be obtained. The battle for licences was long sustained and eventually were in a position to go ahead.

As a result we have a two storey addition at the rear end of the hall. Seven showers have been installed with the necessary water heating and storage plant, six hand basins, foot baths and adequate lavatory accommodation are there for the conven-ience of our members and visitors. A servery is supplied from a well equipped kitchen above and also on the first floor is a ladies waiting room and a committee room. An iron stairway externally leads to the first floor and an inner wooden stairway also leads to the first floor.

Visitors from other clubs have commented on the splendid facilities now available to the club, as have older members who remembered the very inadequate allowance of hot water once provided, which was paid for out of pence collections and not provided gratis as now.

We are not, of course, completely satisfied with the improvements made, nor is it likely that we shall ever be. In the course of time we should like to redesign and rebuild our main hall nearer to the heart's desire, but first we must pay for our present improvements. These will cost more than our total funds in reserve and will leave us in debt for a while, but we are quite confident that the old Belgrave spirit will arise and enable us to clear this off and prepare for our next step forward. The spirit which has built so much from such little beginnings is not likely to forsake us now and we certainly have something definite and visible to the eye to inspire us towards generosity and even harder work for Belgrave in the future.

In conclusion it must be said that this major step forward of ours is a victory for team spirit and love of club which can scarcely be rivalled anywhere in the world. So many have shared in the effort which has been continuing for over 30 years; so many who must remain nameless have contributed to our ideals and the translations of them into reality. We are confident that our members of the present and of the future will respect their great inheritance and prove more than worthy of it and the trust reposed in them.

A.A.H.

UNDER NEW MANAGEMENT

Ron. Holtum.

For a number of years the running sections of our club have depended on Ernie Duffett's knowledge of what everyone is doing - and can do - for the naming of teams for our fixtures. We no longer have Ernie Duffett in this more than active position in the administration of the club and as you all know, his work has been split up into the duties of Cross Country and Track Running Secretaries. Over the country, team selection is simpler by virtue of the fact that on any one day there is normally only one event at the one distance. On the track the number of nominations for a meeting are often limited and it has been found that runners' names have been omitted from programmes and runners who are not available have been included.

In order to avoid this sort of thing and to help your new Track Secretary and Captain, a questionnaire has been compiled. A copy is included with this Belgravian and further forms are available for members not yet subscribing to our mag-azine. From the framing of the questions, you will see what we want to know. Don't forget to use the remarks column if you have anything to add. Please fill in the form and see that it is passed to one of the following:-

Track Hon.Sec. R.D.Holtum, 26, Wandsworth Bridge Road, S.W.6. Track Capt. F.Simmons, 69, Monkton Street, S.E.11. Track V/Capt. J.Burr, 46, Rylett Road, W.12.

then we can from the replies received, work out an acceptable programme of fixtures for the 1955 track season - one at which teams worthy of the name "Belgrave" can be expected to attend.

The track season will soon be here, don't delay, send those forms in straight away.

Ron. Holtum.

THE TRACK CAPTAIN'S MESSAGE

F. Simmons.

The track season is steadily drawing near and as Track Captain my thoughts are turning towards the summer evenings and wondering what kind of support I can count on. I know I am safe with our Field Events lads and also the sprinters, but I am not quite happy about the longer distance chaps, maybe they have thought they do not get the appreciation they deserve.

Fixtures are already being arranged and it appears we will have a busy season ahead of us. There will be runs, jumps and throws for everybody, my job is to see that you get some competition at whatever you want to do. I will try very hard for this but if the referees will not allow more to run in a race than those who are nominated, I cannot do anything about it. Always turn up, even if you are a reserve, and bring your kit. Don't be disappointed if you finish last, someone has to be last and I will appreciate your running whatever you do, because I will know you have had a good try. We can't all win, but I know your turn will come.

Some of our fixtures will mean a bit of travelling but as in all sports, the same thing applies to your opponents when they visit you and it is only politeness to attend your away fixtures in the strength at which you attend your home ones. You elected me as your Captain, please make it worth my while contin-uing to try and make a success of the job. I know I have a habit of saying what first comes to my mind, but that is me and I would be false to myself and to you if I tried to be different. Those of you who were at the A.G.M. know of the conditions under which I accepted the office. They still hold good as far as I am concerned.

Good luck and best wishes during the coming season.

F. Simmons.

CROSS COUNTRY 1954-55

Denis Brickwood

What I hope will be a permanent feature of every cross country season is the annual meeting of cross country enthusiasts. To date, to my knowledge there have been two.

Briefly the idea behind it is to give the Captain a chance to issue his statement of policy, to invite suggestions and to stimulate discussion. On this last item, it saves, or should do, club business becoming the property of every club within running distance of Tooting Bec or other focal points. I say that it should do, but from my experience it has not been the case. Team selections, before they have even become official, have been the common property of all and sundry.

The meeting this season, held on the same day as the Club Three Miles, was better attended than the previous year. We were pleased to have the Club President with us and it was good to see a sprinkling of youths and juniors.

Firstly, because it is the most important aspect of the season, the Captain, Eddie Short, gave us a concise idea of his hopes and aspirations. Club spirit was the essential quality required and how could it be resurrected?. The answer was to give the members a break; keep them informed; give them interesting programmes etc. The Captain then got down to training and it was agreed that training could not be done by everyone at the same place; there would have to be two venues, Wimbledon (Club H.Q.) and Tooting Bec Track. Method was then talked about and I found a little uncertainty here. Not by Eddie Short - he went flat out for midweek training at Tooting Bec, fast and slow, fast runs of a quarter or thereabouts and duration variable according to the season's programme. Generally it was agreed that their would be differences in members training according to their number of days training. Perhaps the issue was "miles" or "speed" - perhaps a combination of both. The question of what should be done on the Saturday run was the final matter on training. Surprisingly enough the only possible answer - a pace run - was provided by Bill Lucas, never more than a lukewarm enthusiast.

Next on the agenda was the youths and juniors and what part they had to play in the club. There seemed to be a mystery of "Where do the under 21's go in the winter time". I suppose a good many of them, to the Services. The youths them-selves said a few words and how I agreed with them. They wanted interest; they wanted an experienced man to take them out on Saturdays. It was at this stage it was made known that Charlie Smart and Len Marchant were prepared to help in any connection. It had been previously mentioned that whoever took the lads out would have their hands full with a quartet such as Braine, Flanagan, Chevalier and Dabbs. Both Charlie and Len are well known and experienced, so make the most of it.

The fixtures which had been circulated provision-ally, were discussed and everyone seemed agreed that they were suitable. The Secretary explained that the fixtures with Surrey A.C. and Herne Hill were planned to avoid unnecessary travelling and so encourage a big turn out. It was also mentioned that all fixtures were for seniors, juniors and youths.

As the Secretary of the Cross Country, I made the plea put out by all Secretaries - PLEASE ANSWER THE CARDS WHICH ARE SENT TO YOU - please let me know what you are doing and where you are.

The meeting closed with some people enlightened, some satisfied, a certain coach bewildered, but above all, every one, with one exception, confident that we were dur for a better season.

Denis Brickwood

MARKING CROSS COUNTRY COURSES

Owing to the mass of tracks on the Common and the great number of people about, the task of course marking is probably more difficult than anywhere else in the country. Our members may be expected to know the routes normally used, but visiting runners cannot do so and in no race this season have we had the individual leader to guide the way. Added to these diffifulties is the fact that the marks put out are invariably tampered with by children, false trails being frequently laid and regular helpers are badly needed to assist Arthur Whitehead in this arduous task. Would volunteers please come forward? Please do not wait until you are asked. Nobody likes to worry you in case they might be trespassing too much on your good nature. Only YOU can say whether you have the inclination and opportunity to help your club in this essential way.

Gordon Biscoe has done a wonderful job in making a great number of direction arrows and, with the many flags available, all that is needed is the requisite helpers. In addition to markers, pointsmen are also often needed and everyone who is not actually competing, can help by running his training round the marked course and so keeping an eye on the flags and arrows.

Arthur WhiteheadGordon Biscoe

LONDON TO BRIGHTON RELAY - SOUTHERN CLUBS October 9th 1954

"Olympian"

Following the post war precedent of last April, this race was again started from Westminster and it would appear that unless some unforseen circumstance arises, the original full course will continue to be used.

A new name, Herne Hill Harriers, was added to the ever growing list of clubs who have won this race and although their win was described by a leading journalist as being unex-pected, there can be little doubt in the minds of those who have closer contact with Herne Hill that they went to the starting post with something more than an excellent chance of success. A quick perusal of their entry was sufficient to see that they had a well balanced team with above average athletes such as Mick Maynard and Jack Heywood to weigh the balance in their favour. And so it proved for they romped home with almost two minutes to spare from Thames Valley Harriers with Belgrave third a further forty seconds behind.

As far as the race itself was concerned, there was very little to cause great excitement amongst the leading positions for after the first three legs, Herne Hill had built up a reasonable lead which they continued to widen on the 6th to 9th stages until they had almost three minutes to spare. Thames Valley had run themselves into an almost certain 2nd place by the fourth stage and Belgrave never really looked like being deprived of 3rd position although first Blackheath and later Essex Beagles, caused us some anxiety. Thus it will be seen that the race had developed into a procession, with the greater battles for position amongst the lesser lights.

Charlie Walker performed quite creditably on the first leg to give us third place. John Bromley kept this place on the run to Mitcham although having to give way to Herne Hill who moved up into 2nd place. I thought John looked a little slug-ish and in need of a race or two to pep up his pace. Charlie Smart on the third stage gave of his best and once again produced an excellent performance for a man of his age. Our new Cross Country Captain, Eddie Short, then took over at Purley for the long grind to Earlswood and I suppose no one could wish for a less envious task than to chase Alec Olney of T.V.H. who must be considered one of the best road runners of all time. Nevertheless Eddie stuck to his task and although losing ground on the leaders, maintained our 3rd position. Denis Brickwood, who came into the team at the last minute due to Chris Roffey being unfit, again took the Redhill stage but unfortunately had to contend with Withers of Blackheath, a very promising performer who produced the fastest time of the day on this leg and in so doing took his team into 3rd place. Ray Tooby, looking most casual in his running and no doubt obeying the orders of his coach, kept our 4th place on the sixth stage at the end of which we were over two minutes behind the leaders. We were however still hopeful of a recovery for Jack Brown had been entrusted with the long leg to Handcross. Unfortunately he was not in his usual devastating road form, perhaps due to lack of racing in the weeks prior to this race. Jack was also in the invidious position of having two first class athletes in the persons of Mick Maynard, Herne Hill, and Ingles of T.V.H. ahead of him and not even in sight. When Len Herbert took over at Handcross, we were again third, but over three minutes in arrears. Len set about his task in his usual workmanlike manner but fireworks cannot be expected from the over forties in races such as this and he had to give further ground and it was certain when he arrived at the Castle at Hickstead that 2nd was the best position for which we could hope. The 9th stage had been given to John Burr running in his first Brighton and so well did he run that he produced the best time of the day on this leg - definitely an encouraging start to what should be a long career on the Brighton Road. Archie Bedford on the 10th also did the fastest time of the day and brought us within striking distance of T.V.H. but Len Bishop on the last leg could make no impression on Eddie Ellis, T.V.H's International Steeplechaser, and the positions remained unchanged to the end.

Once again it was forcibly brought home to us that we have too many old men in our team and that until we can produce at least two above average runners who can hold their own with the leading athletes on the long six mile stages, we must resign our-selves to taking lower positions.

Next April when we compete in the National Relays, we shall have to contend with the star studded South London Team in addition to this rapidly improving Herne Hill side and the strong opposition of T.V.H. We must in the meantime find new blood, possibly in the form of Jumbo Masters and Ron Linstead to strengthen our team and replace our veterans who must inevitably become slower with each succeeding race and who have borne the brunt of this race for so long.

INTERNATIONAL 100 Km. WALK - ITALY 4-11-54

Our Long Distance Walking Champion Norman Guilmant, was once again selected to represent Great Britain in this Inter-national walking race of just over 60 miles, his co-competitor being Vic Stone of the Poly H. whilst our old friend "Timber" Woodcock of Surrey W.C. accompanied them as Manager and we are indebted to the latter for this report on the race.

The party left London at mid-day on Monday, 1st of November and arrived in Milan at mid-day on Tuesday in a minor heatwave, moving on to Lecco, the venue for this year's race, the following morning. The time in Milan was spent sightseeing, coupled with Vic Stone doing his utmost to purchase a collar stud, but apparently such things are unknown in Italy. This was slightly co-incidental as just before leaving, Vic had been awarded the "Studd" Trophy.

Lecco turned out to be a holiday centre on Lake Como and all agreed that it was a fine town, Norman being partic-ularly interested in the postcards (don't ask what kind). In the afternoon our two competitors attended a medical examination required by the promoters of the event. Then followed vetting of permits to compete, issuing of numbers and allocation of attendants, all quite a business. One interesting point at this stage was that each competitor had a wire fixed to his wrist with a lead seal bearing his number in the race (rather like the pigeons). This is done so that there can be no question of a substitute imper-sonating a competitor during the race. All these formalities having been completed, we returned to our hotel for a meal and linked up with Brian Morris, Met. W.C. who had taken a few days holiday to see the race. He managed to get hold of a cycle which later proved very useful. Finally we worked out a system of sign language with the two attendants allotted to Norman and Vic for the next day and turned in at an early hour as we had to be up at 2am for a 4 o'clock start. Norman proved a great asset to the party as far as food was concerned, having brought his own grape fruit and bacon for breakfast which proved to be sufficient for the whole party. We could have had porridge as well if only we could have made the locals understand what it was and how to make it.

The day of the race, 4th November, we learned is the Italian Armistice Day and a national holiday. This resulted in large crowds collecting all along the route and at times causing some difficulty to the competitors, both Norman and Vic having to elbow their way through the crowd at some points, whilst at the finish there was a crowd of about 30,000 held back by crush barriers. And so to the race.

There was a field of 59 competitors from six countries, Great Britain 2, France 2, Holland 1, Sweden 3, Switzerland 3 and Italy 47. The course was completely different to last year and in addition to being more difficult, the weather conditions were against the competitors. The race started at 4am in a cold damp mist which lasted seven hours but when it cleared, the temperature rose quickly to 70. At 5Km. the lead had been taken by Charriere (Switz) Norman was walking in a bunch of six in 3rd place, with Vic Stone in 15th position. Charriere continued to walk very fast and at 15 Km. led by 4 minutes. At Seregno, 33Km. the field began to open out but Charriere was still leading in 3hrs 18mins. followed by Ljunggren (Sweden), Soderland (Sweden), Mazza (Italy), Guilmant (G.B.) 3hrs. 24mins. with Vic Stone 6th in 3hrs 28mins. About this time the cold mist began to affect some of the walkers and some changes took place and at 44 Km. Ljunggren had taken the lead with Norman and Vic still in the same positions, 4th and 6th.

At Monza, 59Km. Ljunggren passed in 5hrs. 58mins. and while Vic Stone had come up to 4th position in 6hrs. 13 mins. Norman had dropped considerably to 8th, 5 minutes behind Vic. Usmate, 71Km. was passed by Ljunggren in 7hrs. 25 mins. and although Soderland was still 2nd, he was troubled with cramp in his legs and was being rapidly overhauled by Vic Stone who was now in 3rd place. On reaching Airuno, 85Km. Vic had gone up to 2nd place, and Norman who had recovered from a bout of sickness, had moved up to 5th place. Over the remaining 15 Km. Ljunggren maintained his lead but Vic Stone reduced the lead by a further 8 minutes. A. Reste, Italy, came through well over the final stages to beat Norman for 3rd place.

Column · page 10

WITH THE WALKERS

Jack.

Time passes very quickly and once again I have to pen some notes for a worried editor. I am going to start with an appeal to you all, and that includes every member of the Club. Please help your Editor and the Sectional Secretaries by sending YOUR contribution to YOUR magazine. Some of you see more of the events on the road and across the country, than the officials who are organising them as they are often far too busy to take note of what is going on round the course. In addition it would make a marked addition to the variety of the magazine articles if they come from different members of the club. Also perhaps some of you would like to start a discussion on some of the aspects of the club, which could be continued or answered in a following issue. Judging by the noise that goes on in the dressing rooms and under the showers, there should be plenty of material to write about, so why not get down to it and not leave it to the few for the next issue.

And now back to Walking. Since the winter pro-gramme has started our lads have taken heart from their distance successes and have really begun to show up well. Gradually they have moved up to make their presence felt in the Open 7's. With the backing of a few more, who have been conspicuous by their absence, right well will the Belgrave colours be at the front when the Championships start. I have said before that Championships will only be won by training round our own courses, so come along you lads who have been stopping at home on Saturdays and Tuesdays. The more out, the merrier. Now that we have such magnificent quarters, it is a crime not to use them. I have been elected "Tea Boy" on Tuesday evenings so come along and enjoy my poison. I shall be pleased to serve you.

Eric Hall takes pride of place as the most improved walker this season so far. He annexed the Civil Service title in a new Championship record of 54m. 21s. and followed this up by winning the Horwood Cup in excellent time. Bob Newson has shown considerably improvement, so also has "The Demon King", I mean Len Demmon. With these three we have the basis of a fine "Garnet" Cup team and as I expect this Championship will be over by the time you receive this issue, I hope we shall have the cup back in our possession by that time.

This year the National 10 is to be held at Southport, only 220 miles away. Let us hope the travelling arrangements will be better than they were for Macclesfield. Can we break the monotony of Sheffield United Harriers taking this Championship year after year. Of course we can, but only if you all get out together and TRAIN, TRAIN, TRAIN. Our last visit north for the Bradford 50 Kilos. was a huge success, why not this one?.

The Surrey 10 Miles Championship this year is being held from Surrey W.C. headquarters, The Stag and Hounds and this should result in another dual title. Our Middlesex lads will also no doubt be out in force.

It should be noted that the Regents Park 15 miles will be used as the basis of selection for the Union of London Walking Clubs match in Paris a few weeks later so its up to you to do your best if you want to go to "Gay Paree".

This year, for the first time, we shall be staging a National Championship from our headquarters, the Race Walking Association having accepted our offer for the 20 miles on 4th May. We shall be using the same course of three laps as our own Championship on April 16th, which should prove of considerable advantage in helping us to retain our hard won title of last year.

We shall need lots of help on this most important day, if we are to make this, our first National Championship, one to be remembered. I have already been promised the help and co-operation of Arthur Whitehead who knows the details of the course so well. Cars will be needed to transport judges and other officials round the course, so please let me have your names as early as possible so that I can be sure of a list of willing helpers, not only for transport, but for sponging and feeding stations, pointsmen and all the other incidental jobs that are necessary in an important race. COACHING CHAT

F. Simmons.

During the week end of November 27th 28th I attended a coaching course promoted by the Surrey County A.A.A. It was disappointing to me that I was the only qualified coach from Belgrave who attended. It is not enough to have gained your Honorary Coach's badge. Surely there is more to learn so that we can use that knowledge for the benefit of our club members. A demonstration of weight training by the expert Oscar State was given. I watched this most carefully and also had an attempt at some of the demonstrations. I was convinced that these could assist the not so well endowed in physique, this after being sceptical on the subject for a long time. These exercises are also of great assistance to sprinters, jumpers and throwers and can also help middle and long distance men.

The loop films on the subject which you were taking were most helpful and gave you as a coach a clear idea of what to look for and how to spot a bad habit in an athletds action. We were also given advice on how to help athletes to cure this, stressing that it is not always wise to alter the athlete's natural action.

Geof Dyson gave a lecture on "Mechanics without tears". I thought I should probably fall asleep but strangely enough I was absorbed by the lecture and followed it closely and hope that I have learned something from it to help me when I go for my test on the Jumping events in February.

After tea on Sunday we were shown the film on the European Games and some shots of the various stars in action. This was followed by a general discussion and it was decided to have another course in January. More details will be available later so if you are keen to go please contact me. It was also arranged to have a County Coaches Meeting at which all County and Club Coaches will be welcomed.

We are the only club in the County to have a Coaches' panel but to be quite honest it is not functioning as I think it should be. We could make our presence felt if more attended these courses put on by the County so that we all have at least the fundamental knowledge of events at which we do not specialize.

Lastly I would like, if it is at all possible, for Club Coaches to work in closer union with Franz Stampfl. This can easily be done without losing your individuality. He is most anxious to help any member of Belgrave, whether a coach or an active athlete.

F. Simmons.
