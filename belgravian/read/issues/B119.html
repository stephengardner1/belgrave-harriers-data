EDITORIAL

Your Editor.

Dear Fellow Belgravians,

Since our last issue of the Club Magazine, a major tragedy has befallen the Club in the loss of our President, Frank Elson. It is difficult to realise how great a loss the Club has sustained as Frank was something more than an ordinary Club member. As my Co-editor, I shall miss his very great assistance and I am sure the magazine will suffer to a degree without his help and co-operation. Any further tribute I will leave to the pen of Alf Harley who has known him considerably longer than I have but I know you will all join in a few moments of silent tribute to the memory of a great sportsman, a first class club man and a gentleman.

The long illness of our late President has been partly responsible for the delay in coming to a decision over the future of the Magazine and you now have before you the first issue of the Belgravian in its new form.

After much consideration and bearing in mind the suggestions put forward at the last Annual General Meeting, your committee have arrived at the following conclusions:

1.That there is a general desire for a Club Magazine. 2.That such a magazine should be published at least once a quarter. 3.That the cost of four issues a year would be too great to be met wholly out of club funds.

It was therefore decided to publish a first issue for circulation to every member and thereafter to introduce a charge of 2/- a year for four issues, the second and subsequent issues being sent only to those who indicated their desire to be associated with the scheme by completing and returning the form which is enclosed. It must of course be made quite clear that the proposed charge of 2/- a year would only meet part of the cost of production and distribution the balance being borne by Club Funds. Your response will really show if you want a club magazine and it is hoped that every member will support this new venture and thus ensure its success. The yearly charge will also apply to members who are engaged on their compulsory period of National Service and are not called upon for ordinary subscriptions.

If you wish to receive the next issue,please don't put the enclosed form away and forget about it. Complete it now and forward it with P.O. for 2/- to the Hon. Treasurer: S.C. Roberts, 4, Derwent Avenue, London,N.18.

The future of the Belgravian depends on you. Your Editor will do the utmost to provide you with an interesting and topical magazine.

Owing to the lapse of time since our last issue and the fact that space is restricted, it is not possible to give as full results of some of the races as we would wish, but I hope that with a regular quarterly issue in the future,I shall not have to resort to any cutting of copy sent to me

Your Editor.

Frank ElsonAlf HarleyS.C. Roberts

Obituary · page 3

FRANK H. ELSON

A.A.H.

We mourn the loss of our President who passed away on 26th April at the early age of 47 years and extend our heartfelt sympathies to Mrs. Elson and daughter June.

We congratulate ourselves on having had Frank as an enthusiastic member for 27 years but we are profoundly sorry that he has not been spared to see the fruition of much that he worked for within the Club and to give us the continuing benefits of his wise guidance, his courageous and pleasing personality and enthusiastic zeal for anything to do with Belgrave. His great work for our Building Fund will ever be remembered.

Frank joined the Club in October 1927, and quickly endeared himself to all members. In a very short time he started showing the most promising form in Walking events, and in his first "Open" road event finished 29th and was a member of our team which won the handicap. In his second open race he was again a member of the winning team and for several years was a first class performer both on road and track. On scores of occasions Frank figured in our winning and placed teams and special mention must be made of his second place in the R.W.A. National Junior Championship of 1930 at Enfield when he led our team to victory.

He served the Club at various times as Captain, Walking Hon. Secretary, Hon. Handicapper and Joint Hon. Editor and his hard work for the Club had deservedly gained him Life Membership. He became our President last October, and many will recall his courageous and inspiring speech at our Dinner a few weeks later.

For 27 years, Frank gave of his enthusiastic best for the Club - a sustained and ever zealous effort throughout - and his pleasing personality and devotion to Belgrave's interest over so many years can well be an inspiration to all.

R. I. P.

A.A.H.

The following is an extract from Frank's will:- "My very deep appreciation of the many happy hours spent in the company of very fine fellows. The Club has always been one of the greatest things of my life and my feelings towards the Club and those who have been and still are true Belgravians are those of sincere pride and gratitude".

........

Well over twenty members and friends paid their last respect to our President and there were numerous messages of condolence and floral tributes from other Athletic Clubs and Associations.

Frank H. ElsonMrs. ElsonA.A.H.

INTRODUCING YOUR NEW TRACK CAPTAIN

Frank Simmons.

Due to the enforced resignation of Len Marchant owing to domestic difficulties (house hunting), I was persuaded by your Committee to accept the post of Track Captain. In taking on this position I realise what a tremendous job I have, but I am determined to try and make a success of it. I can if you will all give me your unstinted support throughout the Summer Season. We have a full programme as you will see by your Fixture List, and everybody has a chance of running or partaking in the various field events to enable them to enjoy their athletics.

There will be no "Closed shop" as has been alleged in the past. Everybody has the same opportunity to run in the big events but they must show that they are worthy.. There is ample proof of the talent in the Club and I, for one, want to see the younger members making our first teams. Teams will be picked on current merit and not on past reputations and reserves must always remember to bring their kit with them, even although they may not get a run. Remember too that it will make my job much easier if all turn up at the correct time and not an hour after the majority. I can then say "My team is here, let's get to town Belgrave". We have had a lot of glamour on the country and on the road, now lets get some on the track.

My sincere thanks to Bill Lucas and Tom Carter for their promised assistance when needed.

One last word. I should be most happy if more of you, especially the half and the one milers who cannot go to Albert Shaw, would make more use of Franz Stampfl. He is most anxious to help you and his advice is given wholeheartedly,with great charming and understanding, but with the accent on hard work.

Good luck, good racing and Up the "Bels".

Frank Simmons.

LEYTON FLOODLIGHT MEETING

Bystander.

A cold blustery evening on May 5th saw a grand crowd of club members at this meeting, the first big attraction of the season. Those who were not competing, were cheering the club members on.

Dennis Merrett ran in the 100 yards, but it was very evident that he had not recovered from an attack of the flu. Ted Gaworski gave a good account of himself in the Shot and was most unlucky not to gain a place. His put was 42" 8". Some of you could take a lesson from Ted in Clubmanship. J. Butterfield was also in the shot put and managed over 40', good performances on such an evening.

John Bromley was lying third in the 3000 metres steeplechase when he gave his ankle a nasty bang on a hurdle and had to retire. He however ran again in the two mile team race so that we could field a full team, another example of good Belgrave spirit, although we did not fare too well in this event. R.Taylor ran an excellent half mile considering he was the victim of a lot of unnecessary rough tactics on the first bend.

The highlight of the evening for us was the mile medley relay. Bob Wolton's 660 yards cannot be praised too much and the magnificent way in which the rest of the team rose to the occasion to give us a handsome win in the time of 3mins. 26.3secs was terrific. The team was R. Wolton, D. Merrett, R. Holtum, Ian Mayers and Jock Cameron.

Jock Cameron paid our bus fares back to the railway station and everybody was extremely happy about the evening.

LONDON TO BRIGHTON NATIONAL RELAY RACE

By Olympian.

SATURDAY APRIL 10TH 1954 By Olympian.

This Classic race promoted by the "News of the World" has come to be recognised as one of the major events in the Athletic Calendar and on this occasion history was again made when the course from Westminster to Brighton as instituted in 1924 when the race was originally staged was once more experimentally adopted for the first time since 19[illegible]. In the intervening years the start has been from Mitcham but it is hoped that by the restriction of supporters coaches that the full distance of 54¾ miles can be reinstated as the recognised route for future races.

The race started at 10am. precisely by the first stroke of Big Ben and after the twenty runners had sorted themselves out from the conglomeration of press cameramen, who had formed an almost impassable barrier in the first twenty yards, the battle was on in earnest. A fast start along the embankment to Chelsea Bridge soon strung out the field which was led by Wood of Sheffield United Harriers who had recently performed creditably in the International Cross Country closely followed by Parmenter of Birchfield and Wood of Surrey A.C. Our own Bill Lucas was not far away in 10th position The three leading teams remained unchanged to Clapham Common where we were seventh, some fifty seconds behind the Sheffield Club with all but Birchfield of the favoured clubs behind us. John Bromley on the second leg to Mitcham maintained the position with a steady run but Blackheath and Essex Beagles had come more into the picture and Sheffield were over a minute ahead with Bolton also prominent. Jack Brown on his favourite stage from Mitcham to Purley produced another of his excellent performances on this leg and with a well judged effort took Belgrave into a very valuable lead of twenty one seconds from Surrey A.C. and Sheffield. The lead was held by Archie Bedford on the fourth stage to Merstham at which point we had 16 seconds over Essex Beagles and 28 over South London who had been slowly moving through the field. The remainder of the teams had begun to fall away - Birchfield had not maintained their earlier promise and Thames Valley, a well fancied team, had not made up for their poor start.

Our Cross Country Captain Dennis Brickwood, who in 1937 had started Mitcham A.C. on a winning sequence in this event by an inches decision over our own Arthur Allum, was performing for the first time in this race in Belgrave colours and although chased by two very promising runners in the shape of Douglas of Essex Beagles and Lyne of S.L.H. retained our lead by a small margin over the latter club. Another veteran, Len Herbert, who is now 40 years of age took over at Earlswood with the Beagles and South London Harriers breathing down his neck, but with a nicely judged effort and one of his well renowned sprint finishes opened up a few seconds gap from those clubs.

Now began one of the crucial stages of the race the newly arranged leg of well over six miles which constituted the combined Nos. 5 and 6 stages of the old course. Charlie Smart who at the age of 44 is running better than ever set off with the Belgrave baton with Gordon Pirie of S.L.H. some 15 seconds behind. As one would expect it was not long before Gordon caught Charlie ,but it was not without a fight that he eventually got away. In fact Charles actually repassed Pirie twice before having to relinquish his lead and by finishing only a minute behind at Handcross,can be said to have put up an excellent performance. The Beagles were still in third place but had lost a little ground.

The next stage was another vital long leg over which many battles have been fought in the past. S.L.H. had given it to Peter Driver another of their young but very talented members and he set up for them what proved to be an unassailable lead with a time of 28.52 which beat by 17 seconds the October effort of Pirie and Olney which it will be remembered was considered to be an almost fantastic effort. However even Driver's time was not unbeatable, for first Jim Peters lowered it by 10 seconds and in doing so passed Charlie Walker and took Essex Beagles into 2nd place, and later by Alec Olney who reduced it by another 7 seconds without bringing his club T.V.H. anywhere near the front.

The race by this time had definitely resolved itself into a battle between the leading three clubs with S. L. H. almost certain winners. Bob Taylor set about reducing the lead and with the fastest time of the day on his leg gained 43secs. on the Beagles but made only a small impression on S.L.H. who were still some 2½ minutes ahead. Stan Gawler carried on the good work and also returned the fastest time of the day on his stage and in so doing he brought us back to second place. S.L.H. were still keeping up the pace and with only the last leg to be run had over two minutes to spare. Dave Baulch a newcomer to Belgrave and on his first Brighton run put up a creditable performance which kept us in second place and also gave him the second fastest time for the stage.This member has definitely come to stay and should be an asset to our teams for some time to come

Although we had to be content with only second place on this occasion the team performance was extremely good and it will take an exceptionally fast team to defeat S.L.H. in this type of race over the next few years. Providing Belgrave can produce two runners to undertake the vital 7th and 8th stages which now more than ever are the crucial points of the race, I feel that we can match South London's strength. In Jack Brown we have one of those runners but at present it is difficult to see who else can bear this mantle to combat S.L.H.'s Pirie and Driver. It is time our younger runners trained hard enough to keep our ageing athletes out of the team - this is not a race for the over 35's, yet in this year's team we had five in that group and without doubt were the oldest team on the road. We cannot much longer depend on these runners who have carried the long distance, track and road running on their shoulders since the war ended.

In passing it should be mentioned that the old record for the full course which had stood since 1932 was beaten by no less than 8 minutes by S.L.H. and six by Belgrave and the Mitcham/Brighton time made by Mitcham A.C. in 1938 by approximately two minutes by both clubs. Perhaps the perfect weather with a slight following breeze had something to do with it.

NATIONAL CROSS COUNTRY CHAMPIONSHIPS

Observer.

This year Arrowe Park, Birkenhead was the venue for the Championships which were held on March 13th. The course which everyone had expected to be easy, turned out to be quite sticky.

The first event was the Youths' race over 3 Miles. There, our lads expected to do well, bearing in mind their surprise 2nd place in the "Southern". This was not to be however, for Ron Linstead and Jim Edgar never seemed to get going and, finishing together in 30th and 31st places, were a minute down on the winner. D. Pettican did not seem at home on the sticky ground and was 85th G. Flanagan, 99th failed to produce his Southern form but nevertheless ran well for an athlete of only three months experience. When the team positions had been worked out it was found that we had come eighth.

The Senior Race over a course of just under 10 miles was the next on. Our team was weakened by Jack Brown's absence and it is interesting to note that G. Olding, our Second Claim member, who was beaten by Jack in the Club "7", finished 19th. Although we did not expect anything brilliant from our runners, they did not run too badly and certainly bettered their "Southern" performance In coming 31st, Chas Walker certainly pulled one out of the bag and so becomes holder of the "Harry Parker" Memorial Trophy, for the ensuing year. Not far behind Chas. was "[illegible]umbo" Masters in 41st place, a good performance especially in view of his recent foot injury. WITH THE WALKERS

Jack Goswell.

This being the first issue of the Belgravian for some months, the activities of the Walking Section have had to be tightly compressed, so if I have inadvertently overlooked any of the unusually good performances, my humble apologies.

We can be pleased with our progress,during the winter we made continued improvement in the "Open" sevens and some of our younger members began to show their paces. I hope it will continue during the track season. Our distance walkers are going from strength to strength. To gauge the spirit, one only needs to go on one of our trips away from home, a better days outing you could not wish for.The biggest triumph of all was, of course, our win in the National 20, a big surprise for many and a credit to the Club.

Our all day strolls are thoroughly enjoyed by all who take part, even if "Big head" will argue with the Captain (alias Fog horn), over the birth and names of trees. Those who want to improve their knowledge of Natural History or English History would always be most welcome and would soon learn something about "Silent pools" "Good Queen Bess", "Old manors", etc. Last Whitsun we learnt that they feed the north on rhubarb - acres and acres of it, in fact the country outside Leeds is covered with it.

There is a very good attendance at Battersea Track on Tuesday evenings and I am sure the lads will benefit from the advice and assistance of our coaches Len Fletcher and Don Brown.Unfortunately Len is now working quite a bit outside London and is not able to spend so much time at the track as last year and whilst we miss you Len,we are fortunate to have one of your pupils in Don Brown.

We must endeavour to enrol more youngsters to keep our strength going and I should like to see a good field battling for the "Olympic" Cup next year. By the time this is being read, the Inter-Club meeting at Battersea will be over and I am sure we can look for a good result. The National 50 Kilos will also have been held at Sheffield and I hope will result in another National Win.The lads are all fighting fit, particularly after their win at Bradford in the Northern 50 Kilos on Whit Monday and I am sure we all wish them luck, and continued progress in the coming months. May the name of Belgrave be seen many times in our National papers.

Yours sincerely Jack Goswell.

RACE WALKING NEWS SINCE THE LAST ISSUE

Before starting on the winter season's results, some mention should be made of the efforts of our distance walkers during August and September last.

In the Hastings to Brighton race on 15th August last Norman Guilmant put up a grand performance by winning the race in a new record time and to crown it, won the handicap also. The team were second in the team race, our best performance for many years.

In the following month, Norman again excelled himself by gaining second place in the London to Brighton Walk and of course won the Club Long Distance Championship. Hugh McGreechan and Harry Shields walked their best distances to date, both being inside 8. 40. but unfortunately Fred Rolfe had shoe trouble and was obliged to fall out at Dale Hill. However Jack Wakeley who had only intended making it a stroll, being his first long walk following his operation, made a great effort to push through and finish as our fourth man to make us second in the team race, only five points behind the winners. Our other walkers, Bernard Eglington, Joe Wilson, Percy Wilson, all gave a good account of themselves. The usual enthusiastic support was in evidence and much appreciated by the walkers.

Following Norman's fine performance in the Brighton Walk we were very pleased to hear that he had been selected to go to Milan for an International 100 kilometres race. After lying 6th at 80 kilometres, he suddenly collapsed and of course was out of the race. He was subsequently taken to hospital, and our old friend of Surrey Walking Club, Timber Woodcock, who was acting as manager, in order to stay with Norman, was obliged to become a casualty also, and be put to bed with "flu". Fortunately Norman has suffered no serious after effects and is now almost back to last year's form. Hard luck Norman and better luck next time.

In the St. Dunstan's Hour Walks at Parliament Hill Fields, 23 of our lads competed, six of whom qualified for "Six mile" badges and no doubt they are all now striving for the "Seven". This was virtually the end of the summer season and we now go over to Wimbledon.

After the race the general opinion of all the walkers was that the course was an improvement on the old one.

Norman Guilmant, 4th, Bob Newson, 5th, George Beecham 6th and Fred Rolfe 7th all completed the course inside 3hrs. 15mins. Outstanding performance was that of Bob Newson on his first 20. Our thanks are due to all the helpers, the ladies who manned a sponge and drink station at the bottom of the hill, to Ron Garwood, Arthur Skinner and their ladies at the top of Kingston Hill, the helpers at the top of Lauriston Road and all those who assisted with cars and enabled the judges to get round the course.

As last year, we arranged to hold our Junior 5 miles race on the same day, for the "Olympic" Cup. Unfortunately Johnny Haynes was unable to get away from duty to defend his title and only Ray Middleton turned up and had a walk over for this very fine cup. This shows the necessity for enrolling some more junior members and thus ensure that next year we shall have a proper race.

NATIONAL 20 MILES CHAMPIONSHIP - May 8th.

The Championship was held at Birmingham this year and what a grand day it turned out to be for the Club. Once again we are holders of a National title and it was grand to see in a weekly paper "Belgrave National Champions".

It was quite a good field which faced the starter on a fair two lap course although there was fairly heavy traffic in many places. From the start the lads were content to settle down and the following table shows the positions of the team throughout the race.

As one can see, not only did the lads consolidate their positions, but gradually improved on them, although Geogge Checkley did drop one place in the last stages. Sheffield made a dashing start and were only 29 points at 3 miles, whilst we were then 78 and thereafter at every check we were improving our position. The last few miles was a drive to strengthen our position and this we certainly did.

The only real disappointment was that we did not win the Nimegen Shield, although we had seven men.in before Woodford's 5th man, but unfortunately Fred Rolfe developed a bad spell as will be seen from the table above between 9 and 14 miles otherwise we should certainly have scored the double, in fact we only missed it by one place, the shield going to the first team to close in their full team of 8.

Our heartiest congratulations to all, particularly to George Checkley who celebrated his birthday by gaining his Honours badge. Bernard Eglington, 13th, missed his by one position. George Beecham gained his first National winning team medal, Hugh McGreechan the Sydney Bayliss Cup and Bob Newson his first (2nd Class) National Standard medal. In fact the four members of the winning team were all inside first class standard and three out of the remaining founr were inside 2nd class.

To Joe Wilson, Gordon Doubleday, Len Demmon and Billie True and the ladies who came along to give their help and support ,we say thank you very much. Needless to say the hard working Hon. Sec. Jack Goswell, covered quite 50 miles on his iron horse dashing from one end of the field to the other to attend to the wants of the men. All this went along way towards attaining our aim and ultimate posas -BELGRAVE - NATIONAL CHAMPIONS.

Column · page 19

ON THE TRACK

Our two miles track handicaps held to date have been well supported. The first was won by "Pothunter" Harry Shields, with Eric Hall 2nd and Doub 3rd. The second was won by Maurice Nye who we had not seen since the early winter, but who must have been doing plenty of secret training. We look forward to seeing him more with the lads in the future. Doub improved on his first effort by coming 2nd and Percy Wilson was third, slowly getting back to fitness after a spell of ill health. We were also pleased to see Charlie Speechley having a go again after a period in hospital. One minute covered fourteen competitors in this race - good marks to the handicapper.

News has just been received that Johnny Haynes has made up his mind to resign from the club as he is now permanently posted a long way away and is hoping to join another club nearer at hand. We can ill afford to lose our younger members but thank him for his help in the past and wish him well in the future.

We understand the "Baron" has bought a nice big car. We have missed him out racing this past winter, but when he is qualified to drive we feel sure he will be out regularly particularly on the distance walks when he can be of great help.

Gerry Warr has gone back to rejoin our Southampton-cum-Eastleigh contingent having taken up residence at Southampton, so we hope he will be seeing something of Percy Wright.

For many years Belgrave Walkers have been trying to win the Police Barking to Southend Walk for four years in succession, and this year we hoped Hugh McGreechan would be the first to do it. Fred Rickards, Fred Bentley and Hugh have all won on three successive occasions and Hugh looked all set for it this year. However that very improved young walker, McFarlane of the Poly, who incidentally seems to spend more time with us than his own club, beat Mc.to it and so the battle for this honour still goes on.

A piece of news concerning our long distance walkers has just come to hand and whilst we hope to give a fuller report of the race in the next issue, we feel that brief details should be included here.

Four walkers and the usual hard working Jack Goswell went up to Bradford for the Northern 50 Kilometres Walk on Whit Monday and came away well loaded up with prizes. Hugh McGreechan was 3rd in the race, Norman Guilmant 5th, Bernard Eglington 9th and Harry Shields 10th, all inside 5 hours. In addition Harry took the second handicap prize and Norman the 3rd. It looks well for the R.W.A.50 Kilometres.
